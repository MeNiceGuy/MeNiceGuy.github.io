Virginia Home Care Startup Checklist, Fees and Timeline

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA

Starting a home care agency in Virginia means getting a Home Care Organization license from the Virginia Department of Health, Office of Licensure and Certification. The license fee is $1,500, the term is three years, and the state expects a complete application at least 60 days before your planned opening date, with an on-site survey before the license issues. The fee is not the hard part. The hard part is the sequence: reserve funds, insurance, an administrator who meets the rule, and a three-month operating budget all have to be in place before the application counts as complete.

Virginia licenses an organization that provides home health services, personal care services, or pharmaceutical services (Code of Virginia 32.1-162.7). If you plan to offer only homemaker, chore, or companion services, you are not a Home Care Organization and this license is not yours to get. Everything below is the Home Care Organization path, run in the state's order, under 12VAC5-381 and Code of Virginia 32.1-162.9.

The licensing process, step by step

  1. Confirm the license applies to you. If your service list includes personal care or home health services, you need the Home Care Organization license before you operate. Operating without it is a Class 6 felony under Code of Virginia 32.1-162.15.
  2. Designate an administrator who meets 12VAC5-381-170: an employee of the organization with at least one year of training and experience in direct health care service delivery, plus at least one year within the last five years of supervisory or administrative management experience in home health care or a related health program.
  3. Prepare the money file. Your application includes a detailed operating budget for the three-month period after the license issues, and proof of reserve operating funds for that period: a current balance sheet, a bank or financial institution letter, or a line-of-credit letter (Code of Virginia 32.1-162.9 D; 12VAC5-381-190 A).
  4. Bind the insurance the rule requires at all times (12VAC5-381-210 B): malpractice insurance consistent with Code of Virginia 8.01-581.15; general liability of at least $1,000,000 per occurrence; and third-party crime insurance or a blanket fidelity bond of at least $50,000. Note what Virginia does not require: a surety bond. The $50,000 figure is fidelity coverage, not a surety bond.
  5. Apply online through the VDH Office of Licensure and Certification licensing portal and pay the $1,500 fee. The application must be complete at least 60 days before your planned opening date (12VAC5-381-40 D).
  6. Pass the prelicensure survey. An on-site survey is conducted before the initial license issues, and if deficiencies are cited you have 15 days to correct them and submit an acceptable Plan of Correction.
  7. Receive the license. The term is three years, and the renewal fee is the same $1,500.

Renewal fee$1,500
License term3 years
Application lead timeComplete application at least 60 days before the planned opening date
Department processingUp to 60 days from receipt: review, pre-conference, initial survey, deficiency report
Deficiency correction window15 days to submit an acceptable Plan of Correction
Incomplete applicationsGo inactive 6 months after receipt
Reserve operating fundsProof of funds for the first 3 months of operation, based on your projected budget

The $1,500 fee took effect May 22, 2025, replacing the old $500 fee. Any source still quoting $500 is out of date. Fee and term: Code of Virginia 32.1-162.9 and the VDH Office of Licensure and Certification fee notice.
