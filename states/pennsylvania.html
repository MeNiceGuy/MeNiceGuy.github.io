Pennsylvania Home Care Startup Checklist, Fees and Timeline

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · Boswell Consulting Agency

Pennsylvania licenses home care agencies and home care registries under 28 Pa. Code Chapter 611, and it licenses them per physical location. An agency employs its caregivers; a registry refers independent contractors. The model you choose changes your duties to workers and to consumers, so it is the first decision, not a detail.

The Pennsylvania fee is the lowest in the region at $100, and the state trades fee complexity for process: an online application, an inspection before the license issues, specific hiring rules, and a competency program the agency designs itself. Here is the order.

The licensing process, step by step

  1. Decide agency or registry. Chapter 611 draws a real line between the two models, including who employs the caregiver and who carries the duties to the consumer. Pick the model your operation will actually run.
  2. License every physical location separately. The mandate runs per location, so a second office needs its own license and its own file. There is no branch coverage under another office's license.
  3. File the application online and pay the $100 fee, payable to the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania. The same $100 covers renewal, which keeps renewal budgeting simple.
  4. Pass the pre-licensure inspection. The license does not issue until the Department inspects, so the office, the files, and the policies have to be survey ready before you schedule clients.
  5. Build the hiring wall first. Sections 611.51 through 611.54 set the screening, health, and documentation requirements for the people you hire and refer. No caregiver starts before the file is complete.
  6. Design your competency program. Section 611.55 names 16 competency topics and mandates zero training hours, so the agency builds the program, delivers it, and documents it. Done well, this is a selling point with referral sources.
  7. Honor the consumer rules from day one, including the notice periods. Section 611.57 requires 10 days notice before a consumer's services end, and your agreements should say so plainly.
