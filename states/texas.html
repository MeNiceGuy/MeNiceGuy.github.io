Texas Home Care Startup Checklist, Fees and Timeline

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA

Texas licenses home care agencies as Home and Community Support Services Agencies, and a non-medical startup applies in the Personal Assistance Services category only. The initial license fee for a parent agency is $2,625 for a three-year term, every fee is non-refundable, and the Health and Human Services Commission has up to 45 days to process a thoroughly complete application. The sequence matters more than the fee: the presurvey training and the administrator training come before the application is accepted, and a second clock, the initial survey, starts the day the license issues.

The rulebook is 26 TAC Chapter 558, under Texas Health and Safety Code Chapter 142, and applications run through TULIP, the Texas Unified Licensure Information Portal. A Personal Assistance Services agency does not need a supervising nurse. The moment your service list touches the skilled side (nursing, therapies, medical social services, and the rest of the Licensed Home Health trigger list), you are in a different category with different staffing, so draw that line before you apply, not after.

The licensing process, step by step

  1. Choose your category on the application. A non-medical startup designates Personal Assistance Services (PAS) only.
  2. Designate an administrator and an alternate administrator who meet 26 TAC 558.244. For a PAS-only agency: a high school diploma or GED with at least one year of experience or training in caring for individuals with functional disabilities, or two years of full-time study at an accredited college or university in a health-related field. First-time administrators complete 8 clock hours of training in agency administration before designation, and 16 more within the first 12 months (26 TAC 558.259).
  3. Complete the HHSC presurvey computer-based training. Both the administrator and the alternate must complete it, and the signed participation certificates go in with the application. Packets are not accepted without them.
  4. Build the policies Chapter 558 requires, including the PAS standards at 558.404, client care and rights, infection control, emergency preparedness, and staffing and employability verification (558.245, 558.247).
  5. Submit Form 2021 (parent agency application) in TULIP with all documents and the license fee, and confirm the portal shows payment received. That status starts the processing clock.
  6. Answer deficiencies inside the window. If the application is incomplete, HHSC notifies you in TULIP and you have 30 days to respond with complete and correct information or the application is denied; the license is then issued or denied within 45 days.
  7. Start services once the initial license issues, enroll your first client, and file HHSC Form 2020, Notification of Readiness for Initial Survey, with the regional office no later than six months after the license effective date. Then prepare for and complete the initial survey.

Processing, thoroughly complete applicationUp to 45 days (26 TAC 558.31)
Deficiency response window30 days
Decision after deficiency responseIssued or denied within 45 days
Initial survey request deadlineNo later than 6 months after the license effective date (Form 2020)

Before anyone is hired, the background work is set by rule: criminal history checks under Texas Health and Safety Code Chapter 250, and the Employee Misconduct Registry and Nurse Aide Registry checked before hire and annually. Fees and time frames: HHSC, How to Become a Licensed HCSSA Provider and 26 TAC 558.3 and 558.31. One more boundary: the license authorizes operation only. Texas Medicaid enrollment is a separate application through the Provider Enrollment and Management System, and EVV applies to Medicaid personal care services, not to private-pay work.
