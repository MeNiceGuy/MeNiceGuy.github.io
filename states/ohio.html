Ohio Home Care Startup Checklist, Fees and Timeline

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · Boswell Consulting Agency

Ohio licenses non-medical home care. An agency that provides hands-on personal care and household support to clients in their homes needs a Nonmedical Home Health Services license from the Ohio Department of Health under Revised Code Chapter 3740, and the same requirement reaches self-employed providers working alone.

The Ohio application is small but exact: a nonrefundable fee that has to arrive in a specific form, a surety bond for newer businesses, owner fingerprinting, and a written criminal records check policy. This page walks the process in the order the Department runs it.

The licensing process, step by step

  1. Confirm the license covers you. R.C. 3740.02 requires a license to provide, or arrange to provide, nonmedical home health services, defined at 3740.01(Q) around the nonmedical definition at 3740.01(N). Agency or solo provider, the mandate is the same.
  2. Form the business and settle ownership first. The application fingerprinting runs on the owners, so the ownership structure has to be final before you file.
  3. Line up the surety bond. Applicants who were not in business before September 30, 2021 post a $20,000 surety bond. Price the bond premium into your startup budget alongside the fee.
  4. Write the criminal records check policy before the first hire. Ohio requires a graded policy that applies the disqualifying offenses in the rules to every hire, not a case-by-case judgment call.
  5. File the application with the $250 nonrefundable fee. The fee is payable to Treasurer, State of Ohio, and the Department takes it by cashier's check or postal money order only. A personal check or card payment stalls the file.
  6. Run the clocks after filing and after licensure. Information the Department requests is due back within 14 days. Renewal runs on the 90-day rule, and changes to the business are reported within 10 days.
  7. Stay on the non-medical lane. Ohio sets zero statutory training hours for non-medical services, which makes your own documented training standard your quality story. Operating without the license is a misdemeanor under R.C. 3740.99, and it is charged per day.
