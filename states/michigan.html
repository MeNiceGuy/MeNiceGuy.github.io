Michigan Home Care Startup Checklist, Fees and Timeline

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · Boswell Consulting Agency

Michigan is the largest senior market in the country that does not license non-medical home care. About 2.08 million Michigan residents are 65 or older, roughly one in five people in the state, and personal care, companionship, homemaking, meal preparation, transportation, medication reminders, and respite care carry no state license requirement.

No license does not mean no work. The agencies that win in Michigan build the same foundation voluntarily: a registered business, liability insurance, written policies, and trained caregivers, because referral sources and families ask for proof even when the state does not.

The licensing process, step by step

  1. Draw the non-medical line and stay inside it. The no-license lane covers personal care and household support. The moment services turn skilled or medical, different rules and licenses apply.
  2. Register the business. Form the entity, get an EIN, and complete the ordinary state and local business registrations that any Michigan employer carries.
  3. Carry liability insurance anyway. The state will not ask for it on a license application that does not exist; a discharge planner, a family, or a landlord still might. Insurance is part of looking like an agency, not a startup.
  4. Write the policies before the first client: service agreements, caregiver standards, scheduling and backup coverage, incident handling, and how client records are kept. In a no-license state, your written standards are your credential.
  5. Hire and train to your own documented standard, with background checks in every file. Set the bar and keep the records; that file is what you show when a referral source vets you.
  6. Price from the market. CareScout 2025 puts Michigan non-medical care at about $35 an hour, about $79,508 a year at full-time hours. Build rates from caregiver pay and margin, not from the agency down the road.
