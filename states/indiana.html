Indiana Home Care Startup Checklist, Fees and Timeline

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · Boswell Consulting Agency

Indiana requires a license for non-medical home care. The license is the Personal Services Agency license, issued by the Indiana Department of Health under Indiana Code 16-27, and it is required before you operate, not something you pick up after the first referrals arrive.

The market case is solid: about 1.28 million Hoosiers are 65 or older, and the CareScout 2025 survey puts Indiana non-medical care at about $35 an hour, right at the national median. The license fee is modest. What matters is the order: license first, then clients.

The licensing process, step by step

  1. Confirm the license applies. Personal services, meaning assistance with activities of daily living in the home, run through a licensed Personal Services Agency under IC 16-27. If the service is personal care, the license is not optional.
  2. Form the business and settle ownership before filing, so the application names the final structure.
  3. File the initial license application with the Indiana Department of Health and pay the $250 nonrefundable application fee.
  4. Build the operating shelf while the application is in: written policies and procedures, caregiver screening and personnel files, and service documentation that matches the standard the license sets.
  5. Open priced from the market. CareScout 2025 puts Indiana homemaker and companion care at about $35 an hour, about $80,080 a year at full-time hours. Set rates from your costs, not from a competitor's flyer.
