Georgia Home Care Startup Checklist, Fees and Timeline

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · Boswell Consulting Agency

Georgia licenses non-medical home care as a Private Home Care Provider, under Department of Community Health Rule 111-8-65. The license is built around service categories: you apply for the categories you will actually deliver, and the fee follows the count.

Georgia is also the state where the paperwork packet decides your timeline. A registered nurse has to be on the application, the packet runs to 15 documents filed through the GAHLES portal, and the first license you get is provisional, with a short clock and a hard client cap. Here is the order the process runs in.

The licensing process, step by step

  1. Choose your service categories. Rule 111-8-65 licenses three categories of private home care services, and the boundary at .03 keeps skilled services out of this license. Apply for the categories you will deliver and no more.
  2. Put a registered nurse on the application. The Healthcare Facility Regulation Division checklist requires an RN on every application, so that relationship has to be in place before the packet goes in.
  3. Build the 15-document packet for GAHLES. The application is filed through GAHLES, the state's online licensing system, and the packet is where applications stall. Assemble all 15 documents before you open the portal.
  4. Pay the fees. Georgia charges a $300 processing fee plus $250 per service category, so a standard two-service agency pays $800. The fees are nonrefundable, which is why the packet gets finished first.
  5. Work the review. The Department targets 30 business days to review a complete packet. Documents requested during review go back fast; the provisional clock does not start until the license issues.
  6. Open on the provisional license. The provisional period runs 3 months and up to 3 clients, and it voids automatically if the marks are missed. Treat those first clients as your survey rehearsal.
  7. Train caregivers to the rule. Personal care assistants complete 40 hours of training, plus 8 hours every year after, with TB screening in the file. Keep the records; Georgia checks them.
