Florida Home Care Startup Checklist, Fees and Timeline

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · Boswell Consulting Agency

Florida splits home care into licensed care and registered care, and picking the wrong side of that line is the most common mistake I see. If your agency will provide companionship and homemaker services only, with no hands-on personal care, Florida does not license you. Section 400.509, Florida Statutes, exempts that work from licensure, and the Agency for Health Care Administration (AHCA) registers you instead.

Registration is cheaper and faster than a home health agency license, but it has its own traps: a naming rule, a screening regime, a cure window that refunds nothing, and a hard scope boundary. This page walks the registration process in order.

The licensing process, step by step

  1. Draw the service line first. Companionship, homemaking, meal preparation, errands, and respite sit on the registration side. Hands-on personal care such as bathing, feeding, and changing does not; that work requires a home health agency license or a nurse registry. Build your service list against the line before you file anything.
  2. Form the business and check the name. Register the entity with the Division of Corporations, or register as a sole proprietor under your own legal name. The business name cannot include words like home health, personal care, nursing, medical, or health care, and a fictitious name, if you use one, is registered too.
  3. Start Level 2 background screening early. The managing employee and the financial officer complete Level 2 screening before registration, and every new homemaker or companion is screened before hire, with rescreening every 5 years.
  4. Name your service area. The application lists the counties you will serve inside your health service planning district, under Rule 59A-8.025. Counties inside your area can be added later at no charge; counties outside it cannot.
  5. File the registration. Registration is required before you operate, even if you are a one-person business working alone. File AHCA Form 3110-1003 through AHCA's online licensing system and pay the registration fee listed below.
  6. Work the AHCA clocks. AHCA notifies you of errors or omissions within 30 days of receiving the application. You have 21 days to fix them; miss the window and the application is withdrawn and the fee is not refunded. On a complete application, AHCA must approve or deny within 60 days.
  7. Operate inside the registration. Print your registration number in all advertising. Obtain and verify employment history for your caregivers. Track the renewal date: registrations run no more than 2 years, renewal is due at least 60 days before expiration, and an expired registration cannot be renewed. If it lapses, you stop operating and start over.
