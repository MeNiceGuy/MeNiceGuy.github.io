Maryland Home Care Startup Checklist, Fees and Timeline

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · Boswell Consulting Agency

Maryland licenses non-medical personal care as a Residential Service Agency. The license comes from the Office of Health Care Quality (OHCQ) in the Maryland Department of Health, and the rulebook is COMAR 10.07.05. Skilled nursing and therapy sit in other sections; this page is the personal care lane only.

Maryland's process is document heavy: a typed application, five required attachments, and policies that have to match the regulation before the survey. One caution up front, because the two governing documents do not agree: COMAR lists a license fee, and the current OHCQ application says there is no fee to apply. Both are stated exactly on this page, the same way my Maryland pack states them.

The licensing process, step by step

  1. Confirm the license type and scope. A Residential Service Agency license under COMAR 10.07.05 covers non-medical personal care. The skilled, IV, and ventilator sections of the regulation are a different lane with different rules.
  2. Check ownership eligibility before you spend anything. Section .04B carries a 10-year ownership bar that keeps certain prior owners out, so eligibility gets confirmed first, not discovered at filing.
  3. Assemble the typed application and its five Required Attachments, including the organizational chart, the SDAT Letter of Good Standing, and workers' compensation proof on one of the accepted forms.
  4. Build the policies to the regulation. COMAR 10.07.05 sets the policy content, and it also sets the survey machinery you will live under later, down to the 10-calendar-day window for a plan of correction at .07E. Write the policies as if the surveyor is reading them, because one will.
  5. File with OHCQ and work the prelicensure process. If the Department requires an additional prelicensure visit, COMAR prices it at $250 under .04K. A provisional license exists under .04J when the Department uses it.
  6. Operate on the term. The license runs 3 years under .04H, renewal sits at .04I, and an amended license costs $50. Operating without the license carries a penalty of up to $10,000, which is the most expensive shortcut in Maryland home care.
