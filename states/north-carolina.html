North Carolina Home Care Startup Checklist, Fees and Timeline

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA

North Carolina requires a license to operate a home care agency, and the lighter businesses are not exempt: companion, sitter, and homemaker services run under the Home Assistance Services Provider license instead. The license fee is $510, licenses renew every year, and every North Carolina home care license expires at midnight on December 31. What North Carolina does not publish is a processing timeline, so the deadline that controls your project is the state's own: finish every step within 12 months of your initial application and fee, or the process restarts.

Licensure runs through the Department of Health and Human Services, Division of Health Service Regulation (DHSR), Acute and Home Care Licensure and Certification Section, under N.C.G.S. 131E-135 through 131E-142 and the rules at 10A NCAC 13J. A license cannot be issued until you have completed DHSR-approved provider training or proved prior ownership or operation of a home care agency, so training is step one, not an errand to run while you wait.

The licensing process, step by step

  1. Pick your track. Hands-on care (in-home aide services, nursing, therapy, and the other services at N.C.G.S. 131E-136(3)) takes the full home care agency license. Hands-off companion, sitter, respite, and homemaker work takes the Home Assistance Services Provider license. Operating either one without a license is prohibited by N.C.G.S. 131E-138(a).
  2. Study the Initial Home Care Survey Checklist and the 10A NCAC 13J rules first. DHSR states plainly that not understanding them is the primary reason applicants are denied.
  3. Complete DHSR-approved provider training, or assemble proof that you previously owned or operated a home care agency (10A NCAC 13J .0903(a)).
  4. Mail the completed application, the $510 fee (check, money order, or certified check payable to the N.C. Division of Health Service Regulation), and your training proof to the Division of Health Service Regulation, 2712 Mail Service Center, Raleigh, NC 27699-2712. Each site is licensed separately.
  5. Complete the desk review. DHSR confirms receipt by email, requests your policies, procedures, and personnel documents, and conducts the initial survey as a desk review of those documents. Send copies: submitted documents are discarded after the process and are not returned.
  6. Receive the approval letter and the instructions to print your license from the Enterprise system. Keep the 12-month window in view the whole way: everything must be complete within one year of the initial application and fee receipt.

Fees and timeline

Initial license fee$510, nonrefundable
Annual renewal fee$510, nonrefundable
Change of ownership fee$510
License term1 year; every license expires at midnight on December 31
Processing timelineDHSR publishes no specific time frames
Completion windowAll steps complete within 12 months of the initial application and fee, or the process restarts
InspectionsEvery 3 years, unannounced
Late renewalTakes effect on the date received, which leaves a gap of operating unlicensed

Staffing rules that shape your startup budget: an RN must be available for supervision and consultation during every hour in-home aide services are provided, and in-home aides work under the task rules at 10A NCAC 13J .1107. Background checks run within five business days of a conditional offer, and no one with a substantiated finding on the Health Care Personnel Registry may be hired. Sources: the DHSR home care licensure page and the DHSR fee schedule.

One boundary to plan around: the license is not Medicaid enrollment. The Division of Health Benefits runs North Carolina Medicaid, and provider enrollment runs through NCTracks as a separate track after licensure. Personal care services under Medicaid carry their own staffing rules in the same 13J subchapter, including the competency requirements at 10A NCAC 13J .1110 and the Nurse Aide Registry line at .1107(b) for extensive assistance.
