Government Contracting

The vendor systems changed in 2026. The old guides did not.

States replaced vendor portals, shut down certification programs, and rewrote their rules all through 2026. If you learned how to sell to your state from a guide written before this year, parts of it are wrong now, and some of it will get an application kicked back.

I rebuilt the process from the ground up: a federal guide for the SAM.gov registration every government seller needs first, and a procurement pack for every state. Each pack was built from that state's own official pages, and a separate check compared every fee, date, percentage, and form name against the live official page before the pack went on sale. Anything that could not be verified on an official page came out rather than shipping as a guess.

Start with the federal guide. Add your state. Work the steps in order.

Why the Old Guides Fail

Six changes from 2026 alone

These are the kinds of moves that quietly broke the older material. Every one of them is in the packs, sourced to the state that made it.

Arkansas: new system, program repealed

Supplier registration moved to SAP Ariba on July 11, 2026, and the state's MWBE certification program was repealed effective March 16, 2026. Guides still sending you to that program are sending you nowhere.

Georgia: a new marketplace

GA@WORK Marketplace replaced Team Georgia Marketplace on July 1, 2026. The old portal no longer registers anyone.

New Mexico: a new network

The state moved supplier registration to the Euna Supplier Network on September 1, 2026, and its preference numbers changed with the move.

North Carolina: HUB eliminated

The HUB program was eliminated on July 7, 2026. State contracts no longer recognize HUB certifications; the live certification is the state's small business enterprise program.

Indiana: certifications suspended

MBE and WBE certifications were suspended effective July 14, 2026, and replaced by the Indiana Small Business Program.

Texas: VetHUB only

Texas has run VetHUB only since May 12, 2026. Certifications granted on race, ethnicity, or sex were revoked, and the old HUB rules no longer apply.
