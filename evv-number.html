Most Home Care Owners Do Not Know Their EVV Number. Their State Does.

Every Medicaid visit your agency delivers gets graded, and not on the care. On the data. What share of your visits were captured electronically, in real time, with no hand touching them afterward. What share of your claims line up with an EVV record behind them. States compute those percentages inside their own systems, and more of them now publish the line your percentage has to clear.

Here is what the published lines look like, from the states' own documents. Minnesota requires a minimum EVV compliance rate of 80 percent as of July 1, 2026, reviewed quarterly, with corrective action, payment withholds, and enrollment loss behind a miss. Louisiana requires 80 percent of in-home services to carry a complete EVV record and blocks payment on manual entries past a 20 percent allowance. Illinois requires 75 percent overall EVV visit compliance per quarter, on a ladder that runs from required training to a corrective action plan to referral to the Inspector General. Texas scores providers against an 80 percent EVV Usage Score per quarter. Wyoming runs at 70 percent today and rises to 85 percent on January 1, 2027.

On the manual edit side, Pennsylvania caps manual entries at 15 percent of visits per quarter, down from 50 percent. Michigan expects 85 percent of verified visits to carry no manual edits, per payer, per quarter, from April 1, 2026. North Carolina publishes the same shape as guidance: manual entries at 15 percent or less, at least 85 percent captured electronically.

If your state is not on that list, do not relax. Most states publish no percentage at all. Most of them simply deny the claim when the EVV record behind it does not match. North Carolina pends a claim with no EVV record for 14 days, then denies it. Missouri validates five elements between claim and visit: participant, date of service, provider, procedure code and modifier, and units.

The federal auditors are running the same math at scale. A New York State Comptroller audit (November 2024) found Medicaid paid for 82 million personal care services with no matching EVV record. Federal auditors in Colorado recommended an $8.0 million refund over EVV failures and set $45.7 million more aside for review (HHS OIG, A-07-24-03260).

Your number takes four counts

Total EVV visits in the period. Visits captured electronically in real time, no manual touch. Visits created by hand after the fact. Visits edited by hand after capture. Compliance rate is the second count over the first. Manual visit rate is the third and fourth counts together over the first. Then the claim side: claims billed, claims with a matching EVV record, claims denied for an EVV reason.

Two warnings. Count compliant the way your state counts it: in Minnesota a corrected visit counts against you even when the correction was right, and in Illinois a manual visit is always non-compliant. And run the manual rate by caregiver. In almost every agency, a small number of caregivers produce most of the manual visits. That is a training problem with a name on it.
