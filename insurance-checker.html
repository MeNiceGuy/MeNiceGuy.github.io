Insurance readiness checker

For home care, home health, and hospice agency owners in Texas, Ohio, Georgia, Florida, and North Carolina. Pick your state and agency type and see three things: what your state license actually requires, what Medicare or Medicaid enrollment adds, and what no rule requires at all, whatever a seller told you. Every line carries the rule it came from. Where a rule requires coverage but sets no dollar amount, this checker says so instead of inventing one. Everything updates as you change the inputs, and nothing you enter leaves your browser.

Not required, but have it ready

Nothing in this section is a legal requirement. It is the document packet brokers and underwriters actually ask a brand-new agency for before they will quote, taken from real insurer application forms. An incomplete packet is the usual reason an application sits unquoted for weeks.

  1. Completed, signed carrier application, plus ACORD forms for each line of coverage requested.
  2. Ownership and entity documents the applications ask for: legal entity name, ownership, years in business, and your state license, which is a listed attachment on the senior care form.
  3. Policies and procedures evidence: incident reporting, informed consent, and your screening practices (background checks, drug tests, motor vehicle record checks on caregivers) and abuse-prevention training.
  4. Staff roster: every provider and staff member to be insured, with roles and your service mix (skilled, non-medical, hospice).
  5. Prior insurance declarations pages. A brand-new agency has none; state no prior coverage on the form instead.
  6. Loss runs, 5 to 6 years, currently valued within the last 60 to 90 days. A new agency substitutes a written no-loss, no-prior-coverage statement.
  7. Most recent financial statement, audited if available (asked on facility and senior care forms).
  8. Advertising copy, brochure, and website address.

Industry practice, not law. Sources: Kinsale Insurance Company, Allied Health - Home Health and Staffing Application; Societies Insurance, Home Health Care Supplemental Application; CRC Group, Senior Care Liability Supplemental Application. All read November 24, 2026.

Methodology

I built this checker the way I build every tool on this site: primary sources only, read in full, on November 24, 2026. For each state, the licensing statute and the full licensing rule chapter were read or word-searched for "insurance," "bond," "surety," and "liability," and the Medicaid enrollment sources (provider manuals, enrollment policies, and the agreements a provider actually signs) were checked the same way. Federal rules were read in the Code of Federal Regulations. A negative finding is a real finding here: when this checker says your license rule sets no insurance requirement, that means the word search and section review of the official text produced none, not that I did not look.

Three layers are kept separate on purpose, because mixing them is how owners get sold coverage they do not need yet, and miss coverage they do. The license layer is what your state requires to issue and keep the license. The enrollment layer is what Medicare and Medicaid add. Contract terms, like a managed care plan's insurance schedule, are shown as contract terms, never dressed up as state rules. Where a source requires coverage but sets no amount, the checker prints "No amount set in the cited source." That honesty is the product: a number with no rule behind it is worse than no number.

What I could not verify, stated plainly

Texas: the wording of 1 TAC 354.1033(c), reported to require a $50,000 Texas Medicaid home health bond, could not be read on the state's official site in this pass, so the Texas Medicaid home health bond in this checker rests on the federal rule, 42 CFR 441.16, which independently sets $50,000. The provider-type list TMHP applies the 1 TAC 352.15 bond to was not read on a TMHP page; the list described in the checker comes from secondary reproductions of TMHP material. A full-text insurance scan of the Texas PHC contracting chapters (26 TAC Chapters 52 and 277) was not completed, and Texas Medicaid managed care contract insurance minimums were not read at all, so none are stated. Texas vehicle financial responsibility minimums were not verified from a primary source and are not stated.

Ohio: the general Medicaid provider agreement rules (OAC 5160-1-17 and 5160-1-17.2) were reviewed in part only; no insurance or bond item was found in the portions reviewed. Whether ODA's certification application requires the certificate of insurance itself to be uploaded at application was not confirmed from the application form. Ohio managed care plan credentialing insurance demands were not read.

Georgia: the home health enrollment findings were verified in the October 2022, January 2024, and July 2024 editions of the DCH home health manual; the current GAMMIS-posted edition was not opened, so a later amendment adding an insurance or bond item cannot be ruled out. Insurance conditions in the NOW/COMP, ICWP, and GAPP manuals were not reviewed; the EDWP numbers in this checker belong to EDWP (CCSP and SOURCE) only. The workers compensation threshold was read in a reproduction of the Georgia Code, not the General Assembly's own publication.

Florida: the nurse registry licensure rule was amended effective August 17, 2026, and the amended text could not be read in this pass; the finding of no insurance requirement rests on the statute, read in full, and the rule text current through June 24, 2025. The homemaker and companion registration rule was read through subsection (8); its remaining subsections were not read word for word, and the governing statute was read in full.

North Carolina: whether NC Medicaid actually collects the federal home health bond (42 CFR 441.16) during NCTracks enrollment was not confirmed from a North Carolina source; the checker presents the federal rule and flags the collection step. Standard Plan managed care insurance amounts were not pulled; only the Alliance Health Tailored Plan terms are shown, as one plan's example. The section text of N.C.G.S. 97-93 was not separately opened; the three-employee duty is stated by the NC Industrial Commission. Which form DHSR currently uses for home assistance services provider applicants was not verified; the statutory duties are verified.

Florida workers compensation was researched separately on November 24, 2026, from F.S. 440.02 and 440.05 and the Division of Workers' Compensation coverage and exemption pages, and the four-employee threshold is stated in the workers compensation section above. One nuance in that research was not resolved: F.S. 440.02(20)(c)1 excludes domestic servants in private homes from "employment," and whether that exclusion could ever reach an agency's own caregivers was not confirmed from a primary source, so this checker does not rely on it. The DMEPOS bond figure was read in the 2020 CFR annual edition and CMS transmittals; the live eCFR page for 42 CFR 424.57 was not re-read in this pass.

Read this before you buy anything

This tool is general information for planning. It is not legal advice, it is not insurance advice, and it is not a guaranteed statement of what your agency needs: licensing rules and enrollment manuals change, managed care contracts set their own terms, and your signed agreements control over any summary, including this one. Verify your requirements with your state licensing agency and your insurer or broker before you buy coverage, and keep the certificates and bonds you file current. If the checker shows a gap between what you carry and what your layer requires, closing that gap in the right order is the work I do with agencies at Boswell Consulting Agency services.
