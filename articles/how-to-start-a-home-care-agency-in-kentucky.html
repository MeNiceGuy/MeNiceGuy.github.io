How to Start a Home Care Agency in Kentucky (Non-Medical Care Is Certified, Not Licensed, at $500)

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 19, 2026

The Kentucky story starts with a founder in Louisville who was told two wrong things in the same week. One guide told her Kentucky requires no approval at all for non-medical home care. Another pointed her at a residential rule and called it the home care license. Both were wrong, in opposite directions. The first thing I tell owners looking at Kentucky is that the default door for non-medical care is real, and it is a certification, not a license: personal services agency certification from the Office of Inspector General, at $500. The second thing I tell them is that the skilled doors in this state sit behind a certificate of need with a patient-count test, so the home health timeline is set by a need determination before it is set by a license application. Here is the full picture, lane by lane, in the order a founder actually meets it.

Kentucky runs four separate doors inside one cabinet, and the founders who do well here are the ones who name each door correctly on the first filing. The sections below follow the Kentucky packet, which is built from Kentucky primary sources checked on November 16, 2026.

The demand: about 866,000 seniors across the Commonwealth

Start with why owners keep looking at Kentucky. The Census Bureau puts the state population at 4,606,864 as of July 1, 2025, and 18.8 percent of residents are age 65 or older. Run the math and you get about 866,000 seniors. The same Census QuickFacts page for Kentucky puts median household income at $63,726 for 2020 to 2024, which is the income base a private pay agency sells into. A senior base that size, split between the Louisville and Lexington metros and a long rural tail, supports private pay work in the metros and waiver work almost everywhere, and it rewards an owner who picks a service area with the payer mix in mind from the start.

The license question, answered directly: non-medical care is certified under 906 KAR 1:180

Here is the searched question this article exists to answer. Do you need a license to start a non-medical home care agency in Kentucky? You need certification, and the distinction is the state own frame, not a technicality. Under KRS 216.710 to 216.716 and 906 KAR 1:180, to operate a personal services agency, a person or entity shall obtain certification from the Office of Inspector General, and no person or entity shall provide personal services prior to obtaining certification. The initial application fee is $500 and the annual recertification fee is $350, each on Form OIG-1180, with recertification filed at least sixty calendar days before expiration. A health facility or health service already licensed under KRS Chapter 216B is not required to hold this certification to provide personal services inside its own license.

The other doors are licenses, and each stands alone. Skilled home health is the home health agency license under KRS Chapter 216B and 902 KAR 20:081, behind a certificate of need. Hospice is the hospice license under 902 KAR 20:140, also behind a certificate of need. Private duty nursing carries its own agency license. No one door covers another door services, and Medicaid enrollment does not cure a credential gap: a personal services agency that enrolls and bills outside the services its certification covers carries the overpayment and the survey record. Sources that say Kentucky requires no approval for non-medical home care missed 906 KAR 1:180, and sources that point to 902 KAR 20:036 are describing personal care homes, a residential license.

The non-medical lane: personal services agency certification at $500

The sequence for the default door is short. Confirm the service menu is personal services, the non-medical help the certification covers, and settle the ownership record, because a change of ownership above twenty-five percent carries its own $350 fee on this door. Form the business and hold the EIN, the insurance, and the operating accounts. File Form OIG-1180 with the Office of Inspector General and pay the $500 initial application fee. Hold services until certification issues, because the rule bars providing personal services before it. Then run the annual rhythm: $350 recertification, filed at least sixty calendar days before expiration, calendared as a standing cost of the door.

The Medicaid door on this track runs through Kentucky Medicaid, administered by the Department for Medicaid Services, with provider enrollment, revalidation, and maintenance handled electronically, and the enrollment process may take up to sixty days or longer when documentation is incomplete. Managed care contracting carries no state fee, and the five current plans are Aetna Better Health of Kentucky, Humana Healthy Horizons, Passport Health Plan by Molina Healthcare, UnitedHealthcare Community Plan, and WellCare of Kentucky. Anthem Blue Cross and Blue Shield Medicaid left Kentucky Medicaid on January 1, 2025, and directories still listing it are stale. The state EVV system is Therap, offered at no cost, with third-party systems integrating into it, on the services the Kentucky EVV frame covers. Sequence the credential first, the enrollment second, and the first claim last.

The skilled lane: the home health license behind a certificate of need with a 250-patient test

A home health agency in Kentucky provides part-time or intermittent health and health-related services to a patient in the place of residence, as required by a plan of care prescribed by a licensed physician, advanced practice registered nurse, or physician assistant. The license sits under KRS Chapter 216B and 902 KAR 20:081, and the initial and annual licensure fee is $500 under 902 KAR 20:008. In front of the license stands the certificate of need, under KRS 216B.061 and the State Health Plan at 900 KAR 5:020. Establishment of a new home health service is tested at a projected need for at least 250 additional patients in the county, and expansion of an existing service is tested at 125 additional patients. That two-tier test, verified for this build as finding KY-1, is the fact that sets the skilled timeline: the need determination runs on its own statutory calendar ahead of licensure, so budget the CON calendar first, because the license application follows it.

No CON application fee figure is printed in this packet, because the fee schedule at 900 KAR 6:020 was not read line by line for this build, and no figure is supplied; verify the current CON fee with the Office of Inspector General, Division of Certificate of Need, before budgeting a CON filing. The general licensure frame at 902 KAR 20:008 adds a late renewal penalty of twenty percent of the renewal fee or $25, whichever is greater, and plans and specifications review at $0.10 per square foot with a $200 minimum where a licensed facility build is in the plan. The licensure inspection frame is an unannounced on-site inspection within three months of the provisional license. Certificate of need is a gate, not a pause: no Kentucky licensure moratorium and no Department for Medicaid Services enrollment pause was located for any track on November 16, 2026. Medicare certification layers on the license through enrollment on Form CMS-855A through PECOS to Palmetto GBA, Jurisdiction M, the Home Health and Hospice MAC for Kentucky, with the CY2026 federal fee of $750. The A/B MAC for Kentucky is CGS Administrators, Jurisdiction 15, and its home health and hospice desk covers a different state list and does not include Kentucky. Initial Medicare enrollment on the certified tracks is gated by the federal enrollment moratoria, CMS-6101-N2 and CMS-6102-N2, extended and modified effective November 12, 2026 (the extension is a six-month period, running to about May 12, 2027).

The hospice lane: a $500 license behind the same need test

A hospice in Kentucky is a centrally administered program of palliative and supportive services, including skilled nursing services, for a terminally ill person and the family, on a twenty-four hour, seven day a week on-call basis, provided in the home or in an inpatient health care facility. A hospice program seeks licensure as a freestanding hospice or as a hospice operated by another licensed health care facility or service, under 902 KAR 20:140. The initial and annual licensure fee is $500 under 902 KAR 20:008, and a new hospice service is tested against the county need frame in the State Health Plan, the same certificate of need gate that fronts the home health door. A residential hospice facility license also prints at $500 under 902 KAR 20:008, on its own credential. The practical point for a founder is sequencing: the need determination, then the license, then Medicare certification and Medicaid enrollment, in that order, with no step billed before its door opens.

The residential doors: group homes, personal care homes, and assisted living

Founders who want a home meet a licensed Kentucky map, and two of its doors explain the confusion at the top of this article. Group homes for individuals with intellectual or developmental disabilities are licensed by the Office of Inspector General under 902 KAR 20:078, homelike homes of four to eight residents on individualized habilitation plans, at a license fee of $100. Personal care homes are licensed under 902 KAR 20:036 at $100 plus $5 per bed, twenty-four hour supervision and personal care for residents who live in the home; that residential license is the rule sloppy guides cite as the home care license, and it authorizes no in-home agency work. Family care homes are licensed under 902 KAR 20:041 at $42, for three or fewer residents in the operator home. Assisted living communities are licensed long-term care facilities under 902 KAR 20:480 in three categories, social model, with basic health services, and dementia care, at a license fee by unit count plus $40 per unit; the old certification rule at 910 KAR 1:240 was repealed because the model became a license, and any template still saying assisted living certification in Kentucky is stale. Supports for Community Living residential settings are certified through DBHDID under 907 KAR 12:010, printed as NO FEE PUBLISHED.

Background checks and the personnel file

The personnel file the packet builds for each Kentucky worker holds the application and role description for the position held, the criminal record check result completed before the start date where the credential requires it, the nurse aide and home health aide abuse registry check result, the caregiver misconduct registry check result, the substance abuse test result, the tuberculosis risk assessment, the orientation record and competency documentation before independent assignment, the dementia-specific training record for staff serving clients with dementia under KRS 216.713, license or certification verification for any licensed role on the home health tracks, and the annual performance review with any corrective action record. That is a longer list than most states print, and it is the list a Kentucky surveyor will sample. Build it at hire, in that order, and the file stops being a project.

What you still do today: form the business the Kentucky way

Formation is the ordinary employer load, done in the right order: form the entity, get an EIN from the IRS, put liability insurance in place before the first client, adopt written policies and procedures covering the services your credential actually covers, set up the registry checks, substance abuse testing, and training records as a system rather than a scramble, and use a service agreement that says clearly what your agency does and does not do, with skilled tasks on the does not do side of the line for a certified personal services agency. No state formation fee figure is printed in the sources checked for this build, so none is quoted here. The Kentucky difference is the credential order: certification or license first, enrollment second, first claim last, on every door in this article.

The practical warning

Three Kentucky traps cost new owners money. The first is the certificate that guides call a license, in both directions: the founder told no approval exists starts unlicensed work the rule bars, and the founder pointed at 902 KAR 20:036 files for a residential personal care home license she does not need. The second is the $500 that belongs to three different doors: 902 KAR 20:008 prints $500 for a home health agency, $500 for a hospice, and $500 for a private duty nursing agency, and 906 KAR 1:180 prints $500 for personal services agency certification, so a budget that carries one $500 line for a multi-door plan is short. The third is the MAC desk trap: CGS Administrators holds two Kentucky contracts, Jurisdiction 15 as the A/B MAC and Jurisdiction B as the DME MAC, and neither is the home health or hospice enrollment desk, which is Palmetto GBA, Jurisdiction M. Enrollment correspondence sent to the wrong desk is time a new agency does not get back.
