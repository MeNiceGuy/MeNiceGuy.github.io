How to Start a Group Home in Pennsylvania

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Pennsylvania. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Pennsylvania licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Pennsylvania licenses

Personal Care Home (PCH)

Licensed by: Pennsylvania Department of Human Services (DHS), Office of Long-Term Living, Bureau of Human Services Licensing (BHSL). Serves: Four or more persons who need personal care services, assistance and supervision. Residential facilities that offer personal care services, assistance and supervision to four or more persons, inspected and licensed by DHS under 55 Pa. Code Chapter 2600. A PCH must have a license to operate in Pennsylvania. DHS FAQ: there are no federal regulations for PCHs and ALRs, and generally no third party reimbursement, but many accept low-income residents who receive Supplemental Security Income. PCH administrators must complete a 100-hour administrator training course and pass the competency test, and attend PCH Administrator Orientation, per 55 Pa. Code section 2600.64(a).

Assisted Living Residence (ALR)

Licensed by: Pennsylvania Department of Human Services, Office of Long-Term Living, Bureau of Human Services Licensing. Serves: Four or more persons who need personal care services, assistance and supervision, in a residence licensed to provide assisted living services. Licensed by DHS under 55 Pa. Code Chapter 2800, grouped with PCHs in DHS licensing, training and FAQ material. ALR administrators complete the same 100-hour course plus a supplemental 15-hour ALR course and ALR Administrator Orientation. On renewal, a per bed fee is also required before the renewal Certificate of Compliance is issued, per the DHS application for license page.

Community Home for Individuals with Intellectual Disability or Autism, also called Community Living Home or Community Living Arrangement

Licensed by: Pennsylvania Department of Human Services, Office of Developmental Programs (ODP). Serves: Individuals with an intellectual disability or autism. Defined at 55 Pa. Code Chapter 6400 as a building or separate dwelling unit in which residential care is provided to one or more individuals with an intellectual disability or autism; in licensed community homes, services may be provided up to the approved program capacity. ODP service descriptions list Residential Habilitation in licensed Community Homes under the Consolidated Waiver and Base funding, and ODPs intellectual disability and autism waivers are the Consolidated, Community Living and Person/Family Directed Support waivers.

Family Living Home

Licensed by: Pennsylvania Department of Human Services, Office of Developmental Programs. Serves: Individuals with an intellectual disability, in a family living setting. Listed by DHS among its licensed residential facilities for adults at 55 Pa. Code Chapter 6500, alongside Community Homes at Chapter 6400 and ICF/IID at Chapter 6600.

Intermediate Care Facility for Individuals with Intellectual Disabilities (ICF/IID)

Licensed by: Pennsylvania Department of Human Services, Office of Developmental Programs, 55 Pa. Code Chapter 6600. Serves: Individuals with an intellectual disability requiring intermediate care. Listed by DHS in the ODP fee table at Chapter 6600, with the certificate fee based on the amount in each individual fee letter.

Community Residential Rehabilitation Services (CRRS)

Licensed by: Pennsylvania Department of Human Services, Office of Mental Health and Substance Abuse Services, 55 Pa. Code Chapter 5310. Serves: Persons with chronic psychiatric disability; described for the dual-diagnosed in DHS service material. Characterized in DHS Home and Community Services material as transitional residential programs in community settings for persons with chronic psychiatric disability; full-care CRRS is for adults and excludes host homes.

Long Term Structured Residence (LTSR)

Licensed by: Pennsylvania Department of Human Services, 55 Pa. Code Chapter 5320. Serves: Adults requiring long-term structured residential mental health treatment. Listed by DHS among its licensed residential facilities for adults at 55 Pa. Code Chapter 5320, and in the DHS licensing fee table with the fee based on the amount in each individual fee letter.

Domiciliary Care Home

Licensed by: Pennsylvania Department of Aging, 6 Pa. Code Chapter 21. Serves: Adults in domiciliary care. Listed in Pennsylvania protective services material as a facility type defined under the Older Adults Protective Services Act framework, licensed through the Department of Aging, distinct from DHS-licensed personal care homes.

How to start a group home in Pennsylvania, step by step

  1. For DHS human services licensure generally, complete the HS 633 application form to become a licensed provider for the services DHS lists, including Personal Care Homes and Assisted Living Residences under Long-Term Care
  2. Note the DHS exception: the Office of Child Development and Early Learning and the Office of Developmental Programs do not use the HS 633 form; ODP directs applicants to MyODP for Community Homes information
  3. For a PCH or ALR initial application, the initial application cannot be made electronically and only a check or money order is accepted with the initial application, per the DHS application for license page

Pennsylvania group home license fees and costs

  • PCH and ALR renewal: BHSL requires an application fee when the renewal application is due, with the amount included in the renewal application packet; for an ALR a per bed fee is also required before issuance of the renewal Certificate of Compliance; no fixed initial fee amount was printed on the DHS pages fetched, so no amount is stated
  • ODP certificate fees, including Chapter 6400 Community Living Homes, Chapter 6600 ICF/IID and Chapter 5320 LTSR: for-profit providers pay a certificate fee before a Certificate of Compliance can be issued, based on the amount specified on each individual fee letter, and payment should not be submitted before the fee letter is received

How long does it take in Pennsylvania?

  • PCH and ALR renewal application packet is issued 130 days prior to the expiration date of the current Certificate of Compliance, per the DHS application for license page

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to open a group home in Pennsylvania?

The figures Pennsylvania publishes are these: PCH and ALR renewal: BHSL requires an application fee when the renewal application is due, with the amount included in the renewal application packet; for an ALR a per bed fee is also required before issuance of the renewal Certificate of Compliance; no fixed initial fee amount was printed on the DHS pages fetched, so no amount is stated ODP certificate fees, including Chapter 6400 Community Living Homes, Chapter 6600 ICF/IID and Chapter 5320 LTSR: for-profit providers pay a certificate fee before a Certificate of Compliance can be issued, based on the amount specified on each individual fee letter, and payment should not be submitted before the fee letter is received The license fee is rarely the largest cost. The home itself, life safety and accessibility work, insurance, and staffing hired before the first placement carry the project.

How long does it take to get a group home license in Pennsylvania?

PCH and ALR renewal application packet is issued 130 days prior to the expiration date of the current Certificate of Compliance, per the DHS application for license page

What are the requirements to open a group home in Pennsylvania?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Pennsylvania?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
