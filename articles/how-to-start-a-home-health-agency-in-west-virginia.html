How to Start a Home Health Agency in West Virginia

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

West Virginia licenses home health agencies as a Home Health Agency - Certificate of Need approval to establish the agency, plus Medicare certification; a distinct state home health licensure fee and rule were not printed in the official OHFLAC fee schedule reviewed, through the Office of Health Facility Licensure and Certification (OHFLAC), within the Office of Inspector General, for facility licensure and certification and Medicare survey functions; West Virginia Health Care Authority for Certificate of Need. The governing law is W. Va. Code 16-2D-8(b)(21), establishing a home health agency requires a certificate of need regardless of minimum expenditure, and 16-2D-8(a)(11), expansion of the service area for a home health agency requires a certificate of need; CON procedures and fees at W. Va. Code 16-2D-13; home care agency registration at W. Va. Code 15-2C-7 in the code text fetched, recodified for legally unlicensed homes at 16B-6-3. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the West Virginia license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The West Virginia license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in West Virginia, step by step

  1. Submit a letter of intent ten days prior to submitting the Certificate of Need application, per 16-2D-13(b)(1) as reproduced in legislative text
  2. Submit the Certificate of Need application to the West Virginia Health Care Authority before offering or developing the proposed health service, with the appropriate tiered application fee, per 16-2D-13
  3. Obtain Certificate of Need approval to establish a home health agency under 16-2D-8(b)(21); an expansion of a home health agency service area also requires a Certificate of Need under 16-2D-8(a)(11)
  4. For a licensed center process documented by the state for new agencies, contact the OHFLAC Life Safety Program for any new construction or renovation, with building sites and architectural plans approved before construction and occupancy inspected and approved before occupancy, then submit the licensure application with policies and procedures developed and schedule the initial licensure survey after the State Fire Marshal recommendation - these steps are printed in the Bureau for Behavioral Health Steps for New Agencies document for behavioral health centers and are not printed on an OHFLAC home health specific page in the sources fetched

West Virginia home health license fees and costs

  • Certificate of Need application fee under 16-2D-13(b)(2), as printed in the Bureau for Behavioral Health Steps for New Agencies document citing that section: up to $1,500,000 a fee of $1,500; from $1,500,001 to $5,000,000 a fee of $5,000; from $5,000,001 to $25,000,000 a fee of $25,000; from $25,000,001 and above a fee of $35,000
  • Request for a determination of reviewability under 16-2D-7: $100, in the same Steps for New Agencies document
  • Registration of home care service providers not otherwise licensed: no fee may be charged for registration, per W. Va. Code 15-2C-7(a) text fetched from code.wvlegislature.gov

How long does it take in West Virginia?

  • Certificate of Need letter of intent is due ten days prior to submitting the CON application, per 16-2D-13
  • The Health Care Authority has forty five days to review an exemption request, per the Steps for New Agencies document summary of 16-2D-11(a) procedures

What catches West Virginia applicants

  • Verification is thin for a standalone state home health license: the OHFLAC Facility Fee Schedule effective June 1, 2025 lists hospitals, nursing homes, assisted living residences, residential care communities, recovery residences, hospice, behavioral health and other types, but lists no home health agency category or fee
  • The clear state gate verified from primary sources is Certificate of Need: establishing a home health agency and expanding its service area both require a CON from the West Virginia Health Care Authority
  • OHFLAC is described in official and code sources as the Office of Health Facility Licensure and Certification within the Office of Inspector General, and its programs include Home Health and Hospice oversight for Medicare certification purposes; do not treat the 15-2C-7 registration, which is for providers not otherwise licensed and carries no fee, as a home health operating license

The Medicare layer, after the West Virginia license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

How long does it take to get a home health license in West Virginia?

Certificate of Need letter of intent is due ten days prior to submitting the CON application, per 16-2D-13 The Health Care Authority has forty five days to review an exemption request, per the Steps for New Agencies document summary of 16-2D-11(a) procedures The federal certification that follows runs on the survey calendar, and new Medicare enrollment is under a CMS moratorium to about mid-May 2027.

What do I need to start a home health agency in West Virginia?

The Home Health Agency - Certificate of Need approval to establish the agency, plus Medicare certification; a distinct state home health licensure fee and rule were not printed in the official OHFLAC fee schedule reviewed (Office of Health Facility Licensure and Certification (OHFLAC), within the Office of Inspector General, for facility licensure and certification and Medicare survey functions; West Virginia Health Care Authority for Certificate of Need), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet West Virginia rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.
