How to Start a Group Home in Arkansas

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Arkansas. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Arkansas licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Arkansas licenses

Assisted Living Facility Level I

Licensed by: Arkansas Department of Human Services, Division of Provider Services and Quality Assurance (DPSQA), Office of Long Term Care. Serves: Elderly and disabled persons in a homelike, apartment style setting who are not medically eligible for nursing home level of care. Level I does not provide the more complex assisted living care involving limited nursing services provided in Level II. A Level I license is authority to house and serve only individuals who are not medically eligible for nursing home level of care and who do not receive services through the Medicaid 1915(c) waiver for assisted living. No license without proof of a valid, current Permit of Approval.

Assisted Living Facility Level II

Licensed by: Arkansas Department of Human Services, DPSQA, Office of Long Term Care, under Arkansas Code 20-10-1701 et seq. and 20 CAR part 411. Serves: Elderly and disabled persons, including individuals medically eligible for nursing home level of care and individuals receiving Medicaid 1915(c) Home and Community Based Services Waiver services for assisted living. Provides housing, meals, laundry, social activities, transportation assistance, direct care, health care, 24 hour supervision and limited nursing services for more than three adult residents. A Level II license also grants Level I licensure, but Level II level of care residents must be housed in physically distinct parts or wings from Level I residents. No license without proof of a valid, current Permit of Approval.

Residential Care Facility (RCF)

Licensed by: Arkansas Department of Human Services, DPSQA, Office of Long Term Care, 20 CAR part 401. Serves: Three or more adults whose functional capabilities may have been impaired but who do not require hospital or nursing home care on a daily basis and may require other assistance with activities of daily living. Provides, for pay on a 24 hour basis, a place of residence and board. An RCF does not provide nursing or medical services, and its services are for independently mobile adult residents. Listed by the Arkansas Health Services Permit Agency as a health facility type in the Permit of Approval process.

Intermediate Care Facility for Individuals with Intellectual Disabilities (ICF/IID)

Licensed by: Arkansas Department of Human Services, DPSQA (licensing); Division of Developmental Disabilities Services operates five Human Development Centers that are intermediate care facilities. Serves: Arkansans with intellectual and developmental disabilities needing 24 hour care. DPSQA lists ICF/IID among the provider types it licenses. The Arkansas Health Services Permit Agency lists ICF-ID among the health facility types in the Permit of Approval process. DDS operates five Human Development Centers providing 24 hour care.

CES Waiver supportive living and provider owned group homes

Licensed by: Arkansas Department of Human Services, Division of Developmental Disabilities Services (DDS) for the Community and Employment Support (CES) Waiver; DPSQA licenses Community Support Systems Providers. Serves: Medicaid eligible Arkansans of all ages with intellectual or developmental disabilities. CES Waiver supportive living services help clients live in their own home, with family, or in a setting such as an apartment or provider owned group home, as an alternative to an institutional setting such as a nursing home or intermediate care facility. CES Waiver services are delivered through the Provider Led Arkansas Shared Savings Entity (PASSE) program.

Psychiatric Residential Treatment Facility (PRTF)

Licensed by: Arkansas Department of Human Services, DPSQA. Serves: Children and youth served in psychiatric residential treatment, as a DPSQA licensed provider type. DPSQA lists Psychiatric Residential Treatment Facilities among the provider types it licenses, and the Arkansas Health Services Permit Agency lists PRTF among the health facility types in the Permit of Approval process. No defining bed or service detail beyond that listing was printed on the official pages fetched for this sheet.

Therapeutic Communities Level 1 and Level 2, and Behavioral Health Agencies (certified, not licensed by DPSQA list)

Licensed by: Arkansas Department of Human Services, DPSQA (certification). Serves: People receiving behavioral health and substance use services. DPSQA lists Therapeutic Communities Level 1 and Level 2 and Behavioral Health Agencies among the programs it certifies, separate from the provider types it licenses. DPSQA also licenses Alcohol and Other Drug Abuse Treatment Programs.

How to start a group home in Arkansas, step by step

  1. Determine the facility type and check the AHSPA bed need book for the county, as in the Permit of Approval process steps
  2. Obtain a Permit of Approval where the rules require one; assisted living rules state no license shall be issued without proof of a valid, current Permit of Approval
  3. File the licensure application on Department of Human Services forms for the facility type; a facility shall not admit residents until the license has been issued (RCF and Level II assisted living rules)

Arkansas group home license fees and costs

  • Residential Care Facility: license fee of $5.00 per bed, paid in advance, for initial license, renewal or relicensure after change of ownership (20 CAR 401-205)
  • Assisted Living Facility Level II: initial license fee of $10.00 per bed plus a $250 nonrefundable application fee; annual licensure fees are $250 plus $10.00 per bed (20 CAR 411-205)
  • ICF/IID: annual license fee of $10.00 multiplied by the total licensed resident beds or maximum licensed client population (20 CAR 414-202)

How long does it take in Arkansas?

  • Residential Care Facility licenses are effective on the state fiscal year, July 1 through June 30, and expire June 30 each year, with annual renewal required (20 CAR 401-205)
  • Long term care facility licenses, including assisted living, are time limited, nontransferable permits issued for a maximum period of 12 months (definitions in 20 CAR parts 401 and 411)

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to open a group home in Arkansas?

The figures Arkansas publishes are these: Residential Care Facility: license fee of $5.00 per bed, paid in advance, for initial license, renewal or relicensure after change of ownership (20 CAR 401-205) Assisted Living Facility Level II: initial license fee of $10.00 per bed plus a $250 nonrefundable application fee; annual licensure fees are $250 plus $10.00 per bed (20 CAR 411-205) ICF/IID: annual license fee of $10.00 multiplied by the total licensed resident beds or maximum licensed client population (20 CAR 414-202) The license fee is rarely the largest cost. The home itself, life safety and accessibility work, insurance, and staffing hired before the first placement carry the project.

How long does it take to get a group home license in Arkansas?

Residential Care Facility licenses are effective on the state fiscal year, July 1 through June 30, and expire June 30 each year, with annual renewal required (20 CAR 401-205) Long term care facility licenses, including assisted living, are time limited, nontransferable permits issued for a maximum period of 12 months (definitions in 20 CAR parts 401 and 411)

What are the requirements to open a group home in Arkansas?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Arkansas?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
