How to Start a Group Home in Wisconsin

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Wisconsin. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Wisconsin licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Wisconsin licenses

Community-Based Residential Facility (CBRF)

Licensed by: Wisconsin Department of Health Services, Division of Quality Assurance, Bureau of Assisted Living. Serves: Five or more unrelated adults living together in a community setting, including frail elderly people and people with developmental, mental health, physical or sensory disabilities or substance use problems. Licensed under Wis. Admin. Code ch. DHS 83 and Wis. Stat. ch. 50, definition at Wis. Stat. section 50.01(1g). Services are room and board, supervision and support services, with up to three hours of nursing care per week. Residents do not require care above intermediate level nursing care. Residents may be eligible for waiver funding through county departments or Family Care managed care organizations. Licensing fee is $389 plus $50.25 per resident based on capacity, charged every two years.

Adult Family Home, 3 to 4 beds (licensed)

Licensed by: Wisconsin Department of Health Services, Division of Quality Assurance, Bureau of Assisted Living. Serves: Three or four adults who are not related to the operator and who receive care, treatment or services above the level of room and board. Licensed under Wis. Admin. Code ch. DHS 88 and Wis. Stat. ch. 50, definition at Wis. Stat. section 50.01(1)(b). May include up to seven hours per week of nursing care per resident. Residents may be eligible for waiver funding through local public health or human services departments, Family Care or IRIS. Licensing fee is $171 charged every two years, and fees are non-refundable.

Adult Family Home, 1 to 2 beds (certified)

Licensed by: Certified by a managed care organization, a county human service agency or the IRIS program, with oversight described by DHS Division of Medicaid Services in the DHS licensure crosswalk. Serves: One or two adults who are not related to the operator and who receive care, treatment or services above the level of room and board. Must be certified before serving residents, and an AFH can only be certified by one agency. Certified AFHs are an allowable setting for Family Care members and IRIS participants. The DHS crosswalk lists Wis. Admin. Code ch. DHS 82 and Wisconsin Medicaid Standards P-00638 for this type.

Residential Care Apartment Complex (RCAC)

Licensed by: Wisconsin Department of Health Services, Division of Quality Assurance, Bureau of Assisted Living. Serves: Five or more adults residing in independent apartments. Registered or certified under Wis. Admin. Code ch. DHS 89 and Wis. Stat. ch. 50. Apartments have an individual lockable entrance and exit, kitchen with stove, individual bathroom, sleeping area and living area, and the facility provides no more than 28 hours per week of supportive, personal and nursing services. Registered RCACs serve only private pay tenants. Certified RCACs may also serve tenants who receive public funding including Medicaid waiver reimbursement, and certified RCACs are an allowable setting for Family Care and IRIS, while registered RCACs are not.

How to start a group home in Wisconsin, step by step

  1. Determine whether licensure, certification or registration is required for the setting first; DHS states this determination is the first step and affects what Medicaid funding is allowable
  2. Review the applicable law (DHS 83 for a CBRF, DHS 88 for a 3 to 4 bed AFH, DHS 89 for an RCAC) and connect with the local municipality on ordinances and local laws
  3. For a CBRF, RCAC or 3 to 4 bed AFH, submit the application through the DHS DQA Provider Portal; for a 1 to 2 bed AFH, contact and request certification from the preferred certifying agency
  4. Complete the applicant compliance statement, upload required documents such as program statement, floor plan and service agreement as applicable, and complete caregiver background checks
  5. DHS schedules an initial licensing or certification visit when the application is complete; for Medicaid waiver funding, upload a completed HCBS Compliance Review Request F-02138 (listed as F-20138 on the DHS Medicaid assisted living page)
  6. Providers interested in enrolling in Wisconsin Medicaid review requirements and enroll in ForwardHealth

Wisconsin group home license fees and costs

  • CBRF: $389 plus $50.25 per resident based on capacity, charged every two years; the fee for the 12 month probationary license is half this amount and fees are non-refundable
  • Adult Family Home 3 to 4 beds: $171 licensing fee charged every two years, non-refundable
  • RCAC: no fee for registration; certification base fee is $445 plus $7.60 for each apartment unit every year, non-refundable

How long does it take in Wisconsin?

  • DHS has 70 days to approve or deny a license for an AFH and for an RCAC; the period begins after a complete application packet is received and DHS completes the initial licensing survey
  • CBRF, AFH and RCAC applications not completed within six months of submission will be closed due to inactivity
  • Certified RCAC duration is one year; registered RCAC registration does not expire unless terminated or revoked

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to open a group home in Wisconsin?

The figures Wisconsin publishes are these: CBRF: $389 plus $50.25 per resident based on capacity, charged every two years; the fee for the 12 month probationary license is half this amount and fees are non-refundable Adult Family Home 3 to 4 beds: $171 licensing fee charged every two years, non-refundable RCAC: no fee for registration; certification base fee is $445 plus $7.60 for each apartment unit every year, non-refundable The license fee is rarely the largest cost. The home itself, life safety and accessibility work, insurance, and staffing hired before the first placement carry the project.

How long does it take to get a group home license in Wisconsin?

DHS has 70 days to approve or deny a license for an AFH and for an RCAC; the period begins after a complete application packet is received and DHS completes the initial licensing survey CBRF, AFH and RCAC applications not completed within six months of submission will be closed due to inactivity Certified RCAC duration is one year; registered RCAC registration does not expire unless terminated or revoked

What are the requirements to open a group home in Wisconsin?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Wisconsin?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
