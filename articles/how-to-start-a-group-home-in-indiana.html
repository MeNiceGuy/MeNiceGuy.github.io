How to Start a Group Home in Indiana

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Indiana. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Indiana licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Indiana licenses

Residential Care Facility (RCF)

Licensed by: Indiana Department of Health. Serves: Residents in an assisted living or housing with services setting who need residential nursing care or administration of medication prescribed by a physician. An assisted living facility registered as a Housing with Services Establishment that provides only room, meals and limited assistance with activities of daily living is not required to be licensed; a facility that provides residential nursing care or administers physician prescribed medication must be licensed as a residential care facility under IC 16-28. IDOH residential rules are cited as 410 IAC 16.2-5 in state survey documents. The state funded Residential Care Assistance Program pays an approved per diem for eligible residents of IDOH licensed residential care facilities and county homes under contract with the FSSA Division of Aging, under IC 12-10-6-1 and 455 IAC 1-4.

Housing with Services Establishment (HWSE)

Licensed by: Indiana Family and Social Services Administration (FSSA), Division of Aging. Serves: At least five residents provided sleeping accommodation who are offered, for a fee, at least one regularly scheduled health related service or at least two regularly scheduled supportive services. Defined at IC 12-10-15-3. Facilities meeting the HWSE criteria, including assisted living facilities, must submit an annual Disclosure for Housing with Services Establishments (State Form 49028) to the Division of Aging under IC 12-10-15-10; this is a disclosure filing, not a license. A separate Alzheimer and dementia special care unit disclosure (State Form 48896) is required under IC 12-10-5.5 for facilities that provide or offer that care.

Supervised Group Living (SGL) for intellectual and developmental disabilities

Licensed by: FSSA Bureau of Disabilities Services (BDS), within the Division of Disability, Aging and Rehabilitative Services; homes are also surveyed by the Indiana Department of Health. Serves: Eligible individuals with intellectual or developmental disabilities who need residential services. BDS describes Supervised Group Living as a group home and a residential option and alternative to waiver placements. There are almost 500 SGL homes in Indiana with capacity to serve over 3,000 individuals. Homes are licensed and governed by state and federal regulations and have an annual recertification that includes two IDOH recertification surveys, one on care of individuals in the home and one on Life Safety. IDOH also describes intermediate care facilities for individuals with intellectual disabilities (ICF/IID) as group homes in its complaint information. BDS administers the Community Integration and Habilitation Waiver and the Family Supports Waiver for waiver funded services.

Supervised Group Living Facility for psychiatric disorders or addictions (DMHA)

Licensed by: FSSA Division of Mental Health and Addiction (DMHA). Serves: Persons with a psychiatric disorder or addiction who need a group living arrangement as a post psychiatric hospitalization intervention or as an alternative to hospitalization. DMHA certifies this residential facility type under 440 IAC 7.5; it provides a therapeutic environment in a home-like setting. Each SGL location must be added to the program accreditation within twelve months of obtaining certification. A provider cannot have residents in the facility until it has received supervised group living certification from DMHA. The DMHA application runs through the DMHA Provider Portal and requires items including zoning paperwork and Department of Homeland Security construction design release or exemption paperwork.

Other DMHA residential living facility types under 440 IAC 7.5

Licensed by: FSSA Division of Mental Health and Addiction (DMHA). Serves: Individuals with psychiatric disorders or addictions, by facility type. DMHA lists 440 IAC 7.5 as covering Alternative Families for Adults, Semi Independent Living Programs, Transitional Living Facilities, Supervised Group Living Facilities and Sub Acute Facilities. DMHA describes a Sub-Acute Stabilization Facility separately as a twenty-four hour facility for treatment of psychiatric disorders or addictions that is more restrictive than a supervised group living facility and less restrictive than an inpatient facility, also under 440 IAC 7.5. A residential care provider that operates a residential setting under 440 IAC 7.5 must also be certified as a Residential Care Provider under 440 IAC 6 unless an exception in the DMHA page applies.

How to start a group home in Indiana, step by step

  1. Identify the population and the correct route first: BDS for intellectual and developmental disabilities group living, DMHA for psychiatric or addiction residential settings, or IDOH for a residential care facility
  2. For BDS Supervised Group Living, contact the local BDS district office, which can provide the application and help with other services the individual may be eligible to receive (per IN.gov FAQs)
  3. For DMHA Supervised Group Living, apply in the DMHA Provider Portal with the policies, services and supervision information and the uploaded documents the certification page lists, working with the Department of Homeland Security, zoning authorities, IDOH and DMHA, which the page notes can be done in tandem
  4. For a residential care facility, file the Housing with Services Establishment disclosure with the Division of Aging if the facility meets the HWSE criteria, and obtain the IDOH residential care facility license if the facility will provide residential nursing care or administer physician prescribed medication

How long does it take in Indiana?

  • DMHA Supervised Group Living: each location must be added to the program accreditation within twelve months of obtaining certification
  • BDS Supervised Group Living: annual recertification, including two IDOH recertification surveys per year as described by BDS

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get a group home license in Indiana?

DMHA Supervised Group Living: each location must be added to the program accreditation within twelve months of obtaining certification BDS Supervised Group Living: annual recertification, including two IDOH recertification surveys per year as described by BDS

What are the requirements to open a group home in Indiana?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Indiana?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
