How to Start a Group Home in Utah

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Utah. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Utah licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Utah licenses

Assisted Living Facility, Type I

Licensed by: Utah Department of Health and Human Services, Division of Licensing and Background Checks, Office of Licensing. Serves: Two or more residents who are ambulatory or mobile, capable of taking life-saving action in an emergency without the assistance of another person, with stable health and no or limited assistance with activities of daily living. Licensed under Utah Administrative Code R432-270. Under R432-270-5 as summarized in the official rule text, a Type I license applies if the individuals under care are capable of achieving enough mobility to exit the facility without the assistance of another person, and the licensee provides social care. Size bands in Utah building code definitions at Utah Code Title 15A: limited capacity 2 to 5 residents, small 6 to 16 residents, large more than 16 residents, for Type I and Type II. Utah Medicaid New Choices Waiver covers Adult Residential Services in assisted living facilities, with a separate Attachment B for ALF Type 1 and 2 and for secure memory care.

Assisted Living Facility, Type II

Licensed by: Utah Department of Health and Human Services, Division of Licensing and Background Checks, Office of Licensing. Serves: Two or more residents who may require total assistance in more than three activities of daily living, are physically disabled but able to direct their own care, or are cognitively impaired or physically disabled but able to evacuate with the limited assistance of one person. Licensed under Utah Administrative Code R432-270. A Type II licensee provides care in a home-like setting with an array of coordinated supportive personal and health care services available 24 hours a day (R432-270-5). Same size bands as Type I: limited capacity 2 to 5, small 6 to 16, large more than 16 residents.

Small Health Care Facility, Four to Sixteen Beds

Licensed by: Utah Department of Health and Human Services, Division of Licensing and Background Checks, Office of Licensing. Serves: Four to 16 individuals, by level, including individuals who require 24-hour staff coverage and licensed therapy or specialized program and support care. Licensed under Utah Administrative Code R432-200. The rule analysis on utah.gov describes Level III as a facility providing 24-hour staff coverage and licensed therapy to four to 16 individuals who are ambulatory, and Level IV as specialized program and support care to four to 16 individuals who require more supervision than a residential care facility provides. Supplement A at R432-201 covers an intellectual disabilities facility under this small health care facility rule, and R432-152 covers an Intermediate Care Facility for Individuals with Intellectual Disabilities as a separate DLBC license type.

Small Health Care Facility, Type N

Licensed by: Utah Department of Health and Human Services, Division of Licensing and Background Checks, Office of Licensing. Serves: Individuals who receive nursing care in privately owned homes. Licensed under Utah Administrative Code R432-300. The utah.gov rule analysis states the rule provides health and safety standards for individuals who receive nursing care in privately owned homes, a license is required to operate a Small Health Care Facility Type N, and criteria include providing care in a residence where the licensee lives full time, meeting local zoning, and obtaining a certificate of fire clearance annually. Utah Medicaid New Choices Waiver lists licensed small health care Type N facilities among its Adult Residential Services settings.

Residential Support Program

Licensed by: Utah Department of Health and Human Services, Division of Licensing and Background Checks, Office of Licensing (human services licensing), with Division of Services for People with Disabilities certification rules at R501-23. Serves: People with disabilities receiving residential support, under the human services licensing rules listed by DLBC. Licensed under Utah Administrative Code R501-22, Residential Support Programs, listed on the DLBC Human Services Rules page, with a separate listing for Residential Support Programs, Homeless Facilities at R501-22A. General human services rules R380-80, R380-600, R501-1, and R501-14 apply to all human services programs. Utah Medicaid Community Supports Waiver is the 1915(c) waiver for people with intellectual disabilities and related conditions at ICF/ID level of care and funds residential services; DLBC lists DSPD certification under R501-23.

Residential Treatment Program and Recovery Residence

Licensed by: Utah Department of Health and Human Services, Division of Licensing and Background Checks, Office of Licensing (human services licensing). Serves: Persons in residential treatment, including behavioral health and substance use treatment, and persons in recovery residence services. DLBC Human Services Rules page lists Residential Treatment Programs at R501-19, Residential Treatment, Congregate Care at R501-19A, and Recovery Residence Services at R501-18, plus Social Detoxification Programs at R501-11 and Day Treatment at R501-20. No bed bands or fees were printed on the DLBC rules index page fetched.

How to start a group home in Utah, step by step

  1. Use the DLBC Human Services and Health Facilities rules pages to identify the license category for the population served: health facility categories are listed at R432, and human services categories are listed at R501 (DLBC pages fetched)
  2. For a health facility license, follow the DLBC Health Facilities Applications process and contact DLBC before completing forms, then submit the application, fee, documentation, and clearances as the application packet requires
  3. For a human services license, follow the DLBC Human Services Applications page, which outlines applying for and renewing a license, certificate, and approval, including applying for a DSPD certificate or license (DLBC search result summary)

Frequently asked questions

What are the requirements to open a group home in Utah?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Utah?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
