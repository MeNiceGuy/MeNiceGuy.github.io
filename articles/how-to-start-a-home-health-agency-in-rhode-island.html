How to Start a Home Health Agency in Rhode Island

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Rhode Island licenses home health agencies as a Home Nursing Care Provider license (skilled track; the non-medical track is the Home Care Provider license, under the same regulation), through the Rhode Island Department of Health (RIDOH), Center for Health Facilities Regulation (Division of Facilities Regulation). The governing law is R.I. Gen. Laws chapter 23-17, including section 23-17-4; 216-RICR-40-10-17, Licensing Home Nursing Care Providers and Home Care Providers, promulgated under R.I. Gen. Laws sections 23-17-10 and 23-17-42. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the Rhode Island license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The Rhode Island license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Rhode Island, step by step

  1. Obtain a certificate of need as a precondition to establishment and initial licensure, with prior review by the Health Services Council and approval of the licensing agency (216-RICR-40-10-17 section 17.4)
  2. File the application for certificate of need on forms provided by the licensing agency, with the application fee specified in the Department of Health fee structure rules, Part 10-05-2 of Title 216
  3. Obtain the Home Nursing Care Provider license from RIDOH before establishing, conducting, or maintaining the provider

Rhode Island home health license fees and costs

  • Home Nursing Care Provider license: $650 fee, as referenced in RIDOH application materials stating that a secondary Nursing Service Agency license carries no fee if the applicant has paid a $650 fee for a Home Care Provider or Home Nursing Care Provider license

What catches Rhode Island applicants

  • No person shall establish, conduct, or maintain a home nursing care provider or home care provider in Rhode Island without a license in accordance with R.I. Gen. Laws section 23-17-4 and 216-RICR-40-10-17, so a state license is required before operation
  • The regulation as published on rules.sos.ri.gov still requires a certificate of need as a precondition to establishment and initial licensure; a June 2026 repeal of CON review for these provider types was reported only in secondary sources and was not verified in the official rule text reviewed, so it is flagged here rather than stated as fact
  • Part 17 adopts 42 C.F.R. section 484 by reference, tying the state license to the federal home health Conditions of Participation
  • A home nursing care provider includes skilled nursing services and may also include activities allowed as a home care provider or as a nursing service agency, per the health care facility definition in 216-RICR-10-10-3 on rules.sos.ri.gov
  • RIDOH lists licensed Home Nursing Care Providers in its provider data; 69 records were displayed in the listing reviewed

The Medicare layer, after the Rhode Island license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a home health agency in Rhode Island?

The figures Rhode Island publishes are these: Home Nursing Care Provider license: $650 fee, as referenced in RIDOH application materials stating that a secondary Nursing Service Agency license carries no fee if the applicant has paid a $650 fee for a Home Care Provider or Home Nursing Care Provider license Beyond the published figures, the operating costs (clinical payroll before certification, insurance, and the reserve operating funds required by 42 CFR 489.28) are larger than every fee on the list.

What do I need to start a home health agency in Rhode Island?

The Home Nursing Care Provider license (skilled track; the non-medical track is the Home Care Provider license, under the same regulation) (Rhode Island Department of Health (RIDOH), Center for Health Facilities Regulation (Division of Facilities Regulation)), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet Rhode Island rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in Rhode Island?

Apply to the Rhode Island Department of Health (RIDOH), Center for Health Facilities Regulation (Division of Facilities Regulation) under R.I. Gen. Laws chapter 23-17, including section 23-17-4; 216-RICR-40-10-17, Licensing Home Nursing Care Providers and Home Care Providers, promulgated under R.I. Gen. Laws sections 23-17-10 and 23-17-42. The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
