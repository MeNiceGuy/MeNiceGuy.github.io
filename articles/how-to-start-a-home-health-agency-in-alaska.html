How to Start a Home Health Agency in Alaska

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Alaska licenses home health agencies as a Home Health Agency license - provisional license first, then biennial license, through the Alaska Department of Health, Division of Health Care Services, Health Facility Licensing and Certification (HFLC). The governing law is AS 47.32.010(b)(7) lists home health agencies as entities under centralized licensing and AS 47.32.020 requires a license. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the Alaska license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The Alaska license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Alaska, step by step

  1. Complete the HHA Licensing Application packet
  2. Pay the associated provisional licensing fee, based on the number of FTEs and number of branches or subunits
  3. Provide an electronic copy of all policies and procedures
  4. Submit all requested information to Health Facility Licensing and Certification
  5. Application review by HFLC unit staff, working with the applicant to meet licensing requirements
  6. Onsite inspection

Alaska home health license fees and costs

  • Provisional licensing fee $1,000 for 5 or fewer full-time employees (FTEs)
  • Provisional licensing fee $1,500 for 6 to 15 FTEs
  • Provisional licensing fee $2,000 for 16 to 50 FTEs
  • Provisional licensing fee $3,000 for 51 or more FTEs
  • Plus $500 per branch or subunit for provisional licensing
  • Biennial renewal fee $2,000 for 5 or fewer FTEs, $3,000 for 6 to 15 FTEs, $4,000 for 16 to 50 FTEs, $6,000 for 51 or more FTEs, plus $1,000 per branch or subunit
  • Relocation fee $150 and miscellaneous modification fee $250 as printed on the HFLC page

How long does it take in Alaska?

  • Renewal information must be submitted to HFLC before April 1 of even-numbered years, consistent with the biennial renewal cycle printed on the HFLC page

What catches Alaska applicants

  • HHAs are required to be licensed in the state of Alaska, per the official HFLC page.
  • HHAs that meet federal requirements may also be eligible for federal certification. The official page presents federal certification as additional to state licensure, not a substitute for it.
  • HHA services listed by HFLC include part-time skilled nursing care, physical therapy, occupational therapy, speech-language therapy, home health aide services, and medical social services, and may also include durable medical equipment and medical supplies.
  • The official HFLC home health page fetched for this sheet does not list a certificate of need step for home health agencies.
  • Verification note: the specific Alaska Administrative Code chapter for home health standards was not read from an official regulation text in this pass, so no AAC citation beyond the statute above is stated here.

The Medicare layer, after the Alaska license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a home health agency in Alaska?

The figures Alaska publishes are these: Provisional licensing fee $1,000 for 5 or fewer full-time employees (FTEs) Provisional licensing fee $1,500 for 6 to 15 FTEs Provisional licensing fee $2,000 for 16 to 50 FTEs Provisional licensing fee $3,000 for 51 or more FTEs Plus $500 per branch or subunit for provisional licensing Biennial renewal fee $2,000 for 5 or fewer FTEs, $3,000 for 6 to 15 FTEs, $4,000 for 16 to 50 FTEs, $6,000 for 51 or more FTEs, plus $1,000 per branch or subunit Relocation fee $150 and miscellaneous modification fee $250 as printed on the HFLC page Beyond the published figures, the operating costs (clinical payroll before certification, insurance, and the reserve operating funds required by 42 CFR 489.28) are larger than every fee on the list.

How long does it take to get a home health license in Alaska?

Renewal information must be submitted to HFLC before April 1 of even-numbered years, consistent with the biennial renewal cycle printed on the HFLC page The federal certification that follows runs on the survey calendar, and new Medicare enrollment is under a CMS moratorium to about mid-May 2027.

What do I need to start a home health agency in Alaska?

The Home Health Agency license - provisional license first, then biennial license (Alaska Department of Health, Division of Health Care Services, Health Facility Licensing and Certification (HFLC)), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet Alaska rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in Alaska?

Apply to the Alaska Department of Health, Division of Health Care Services, Health Facility Licensing and Certification (HFLC) under AS 47.32.010(b)(7) lists home health agencies as entities under centralized licensing and AS 47.32.020 requires a license. The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
