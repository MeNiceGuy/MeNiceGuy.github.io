How to Start a Group Home in Kentucky

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Kentucky. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Kentucky licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Kentucky licenses

Assisted Living Community (ALC)

Licensed by: Cabinet for Health and Family Services, Office of Inspector General, Division of Health Care - listed under Long-Term Care and under Assisted Living Community on the OIG applications page, with a License Fee Schedule for long-term care facilities. Serves: Five or more adult persons not related within the third degree of consanguinity to the owner or manager. Licensed facility that provides sleeping accommodations and assisted living services set forth in the assisted living community lease and policies for five or more adult persons not related within the third degree of consanguinity to the owner or manager - definition in KRS 194A.700 as shown in Kentucky Legislature bill text. Current OIG rule cited on the CHFS long-term care page is 902 KAR 20:480, Assisted living communities.

Personal Care Home (PCH), including Specialized Personal Care Home

Licensed by: Cabinet for Health and Family Services, Office of Inspector General. Serves: Residents admitted in accordance with KRS 216.765, who are ambulatory or mobile nonambulatory unless the facility elects otherwise under 902 KAR 20:036 section 3. Establishment located in a permanent building that has resident beds and provides supervision of residents, basic health and health-related services, personal care services, residential care services, and social and recreational activities - definition of personal care home at KRS 216.597(1)(b) as quoted in 902 KAR 20:036. The regulation also covers specialized personal care homes. Certificate of need is listed for personal care homes at KRS 216B.020 on the CHFS long-term care page.

Family Care Home

Licensed by: Cabinet for Health and Family Services, Office of Inspector General. Serves: Residents of a licensed long-term care facility type overseen by OIG; the OIG applications and long-term care pages list family care homes among licensed long-term care facilities. Regulation is 902 KAR 20:041, Operation and services. Family care homes are the stated exception to the rule that long-term care facilities must have a certificate of need prior to licensure. License fee printed by OIG is $42.

Intermediate Care Facility for Individuals with Intellectual Disabilities (ICF/IID)

Licensed by: Cabinet for Health and Family Services, Office of Inspector General. Serves: Individuals with intellectual disabilities. Regulation is 902 KAR 20:086, Operation and service. KRS 216B.020 Certificate of Need is listed for this facility type on the CHFS long-term care page.

Intermediate Care Facility

Licensed by: Cabinet for Health and Family Services, Office of Inspector General. Serves: Persons needing intermediate care services as a licensed long-term care facility type. Regulation is 902 KAR 20:051, Operation and services. KRS 216B.020 Certificate of Need is listed for intermediate care on the CHFS long-term care page.

Group Home (MR/DD Group Home)

Licensed by: Cabinet for Health and Family Services, Office of Inspector General, Division of Health Care. Serves: Persons with intellectual or developmental disabilities, as the MR/DD group home type listed by OIG. OIG applications page lists an MR/DD Group Home Application among applications for license to operate health care facilities. The Cabinet Keys Amendment summary for 2028 states OIG, Division of Health Care is the designated authority for regulatory standards for health facilities and services including group homes, and that no changes were made in the past calendar year to standards for group homes.

Supports for Community Living (SCL) residential supports

Licensed by: Certification by Cabinet for Health and Family Services, Department for Behavioral Health, Developmental and Intellectual Disabilities; waiver operated with Department for Medicaid Services. Serves: Individuals with an intellectual or developmental disability who meet the requirements for residence in an ICF/IID, at 907 KAR 1:022, and meet Medicaid financial qualifications. SCL is a 1915(c) home and community-based waiver that enables individuals with an intellectual or developmental disability to reside and receive services in a community setting rather than in an ICF/IID. SCL providers must be certified with DBHDID. Standards are at 907 KAR 12:010. Covered services include residential support services, and SCL is the only one of SCL and Michelle P. that includes residential support - CHFS SCL provider page states residential support services, SCL only. There is currently a waiting list for SCL - CHFS SCL waiver page.

Psychiatric Residential Treatment Facility and Residential Crisis Stabilization Unit

Licensed by: Cabinet for Health and Family Services, Office of Inspector General. Serves: Behavioral health residential populations served by the licensed facility types named; detail not printed on the OIG applications page fetched. Both types appear as separate license applications on the OIG applications page for other health care facilities: Psychiatric Residential Treatment Facility Application and Residential Crisis Stabilization Unit Application. No population, bed size, fee or timeline for these types was printed in the OIG pages fetched, so none is stated here.

How to start a group home in Kentucky, step by step

  1. For long-term care facilities, obtain a certificate of need prior to licensure, with the stated exception that family care homes do not require a certificate of need - CHFS OIG applications page, Long Term Care Facilities section
  2. For other health care facilities, contact the OIG office before submitting an application to determine whether a certificate of need or fire marshal approval applies to the facility - CHFS OIG applications page, Other Health Care Facilities section
  3. Submit the facility-type application listed by OIG, for example Application for Assisted Living Community License, Application for Family Care Home License, or the MR/DD Group Home Application - CHFS OIG applications page

Kentucky group home license fees and costs

  • Family Care Home license fees: $42 - CHFS OIG applications page
  • Assisted Living Community, from the OIG fee schedule revised 10/15/2026: for ALCs with 25 units or less, $500 plus $40 per unit; for ALCs with 75 to 99 units, $1,750 plus $40 per unit; for ALCs with 100 or more units, $2,000 plus $40 per unit; $500 fee for change of ownership - the middle tier text was truncated in the official snippet reviewed, so it is not listed here

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to open a group home in Kentucky?

The figures Kentucky publishes are these: Family Care Home license fees: $42 - CHFS OIG applications page Assisted Living Community, from the OIG fee schedule revised 10/15/2026: for ALCs with 25 units or less, $500 plus $40 per unit; for ALCs with 75 to 99 units, $1,750 plus $40 per unit; for ALCs with 100 or more units, $2,000 plus $40 per unit; $500 fee for change of ownership - the middle tier text was truncated in the official snippet reviewed, so it is not listed here The license fee is rarely the largest cost. The home itself, life safety and accessibility work, insurance, and staffing hired before the first placement carry the project.

What are the requirements to open a group home in Kentucky?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Kentucky?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
