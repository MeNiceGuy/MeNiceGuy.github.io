How to Start a Group Home in Mississippi

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Mississippi. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Mississippi licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Mississippi licenses

Personal Care Home - Residential Living

Licensed by: Mississippi State Department of Health, Division of Health Facilities Licensure and Certification, Title 15, Part 16, Subpart 1, chapter 48. Serves: Individuals who require personal care, in a facility operating 24 hours a day, seven days a week. Defined at Rule 48.5.1 as any place or facility operating 24 hours a day, seven days a week, accepting individuals who require personal care. Rules are adopted under Miss. Code Ann. section 43-11-13. No facility may be licensed until it shows conformance to fire safety regulations (Rule 48.1.3).

Personal Care Home - Assisted Living

Licensed by: Mississippi State Department of Health, Division of Health Facilities Licensure and Certification, Title 15, Part 16, Subpart 1, chapter 48 (with a separate assisted living chapter in the same subpart). Serves: Individuals who require assisted living services, in a facility operating 24 hours a day, seven days a week. Defined at Rule 48.5.2. Assisted living at Rule 48.2.2 means the provision of personal care and the addition of supplemental services including medical services such as medication procedures and medication administration, and emergency response services. The Mississippi Assisted Living Waiver, administered and operated by the Division of Medicaid, is targeted to qualifying individuals age 21 and older living in Medicaid enrolled assisted living facilities.

ID/DD Waiver Supervised Living

Licensed by: Mississippi Department of Mental Health (DMH), Bureau of Intellectual and Developmental Disabilities, with the ID/DD Waiver administered by the Division of Medicaid. Serves: People with intellectual and developmental disabilities enrolled in the ID/DD Home and Community Based Waiver. DMH describes supervised community living as a staff person on site 24 hours per day, seven days per week. The ID/DD Waiver provides individualized, Medicaid funded supports as an alternative to institutional care, including Supervised Residential Habilitation, Supported Living, Home and Community Supports and respite.

ID/DD Waiver Supported Living

Licensed by: Mississippi Department of Mental Health (DMH), with the ID/DD Waiver administered by the Division of Medicaid. Serves: People with intellectual and developmental disabilities who can live independently in their own home or apartment. DMH describes Supported Living as limited support, maximum of eight hours per day, to access the community, pay bills, shop for groceries, access medical care and other personal assistance as needed.

Intermediate Care Facility for Individuals with Intellectual Disabilities (ICF/IID), regional center programs

Licensed by: Mississippi Department of Mental Health (DMH) regional programs for persons with intellectual or developmental disabilities. Serves: People with intellectual or developmental disabilities needing residential services. DMH operates regional programs including Boswell Regional Center, Ellisville State School, Hudspeth Regional Center, North Mississippi Regional Center and South Mississippi Regional Center, which provide residential services and licensed homes for community living, per the DMH frequently asked questions page.

Mental health group homes and supervised housing (DMH certified community network)

Licensed by: Mississippi Department of Mental Health (DMH) certification of community providers. Serves: Adults with mental illness served through community mental health centers and other DMH certified providers. DMH lists Group Home Services, Supervised Housing and Therapeutic Group Home among services for adults with mental illness in its service system materials. Detail on bed size and licensure steps for this type was not printed on the official pages fetched, so verification is thin.

How to start a group home in Mississippi, step by step

  1. Apply for a personal care home license or renewal in writing to the licensing agency, MSDH, on forms provided by the licensing agency (Title 15, Part 16, chapter 48, Rule 48.7.1)
  2. Show conformance to fire safety regulations before licensure (Rule 48.1.3)

Frequently asked questions

What are the requirements to open a group home in Mississippi?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Mississippi?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
