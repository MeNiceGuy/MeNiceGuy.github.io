How to Start a Group Home in Massachusetts

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Massachusetts. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Massachusetts licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Massachusetts licenses

Assisted Living Residence (ALR) - certified, not licensed

Licensed by: Executive Office of Aging and Independence (EOAI). Serves: Older adults and persons with disabilities (651 CMR 12.00 purpose: services for older adults or persons with disabilities). Massachusetts certifies, rather than licenses, assisted living residences. No person shall advertise, operate or maintain an assisted living residence without the certification required under M.G.L. c.19D. Certification procedures and standards are at 651 CMR 12.00. Certification is issued for a term of 2 years and the fee is based on the number of units. Certification indicates whether the residence is approved to provide basic health services.

Rest Home (Residential Care Facility)

Licensed by: Massachusetts Department of Public Health (DPH), Division of Health Care Facility Licensure and Certification. Serves: Adults who may need 24-hour supervision but do not need ongoing nursing or medical care. Rest homes provide 24-hour supervision and supportive services for individuals who do not routinely need nursing or medical care, in a supportive, family-like living environment. Standards for long-term care facilities, including rest homes, are at 105 CMR 150.00, under M.G.L. c.111 Sections 3, 71 and 72. Rest homes are licensed and monitored by DPH.

DDS Residential Services - 24-hour group homes (Residential Habilitation)

Licensed by: Massachusetts Department of Developmental Services (DDS). Serves: Adults with intellectual disabilities, including those requiring 24-hour supervision and support due to significant behavioral, medical and/or physical support needs. DDS licensure and certification of providers is governed by 115 CMR 7.00 (standards for all services and supports) and 115 CMR 8.00 (licensure and certification of providers), under M.G.L. c.19B. DDS operates three HCBS waivers for adults with intellectual disabilities through MassHealth: the Adult Supports Waiver and Community Living Waiver (participants live in family homes, adult foster care, with a live-in caregiver or independently and do not require 24-hour care) and the Intensive Supports Waiver (supervision and support 24 hours a day, seven days a week). Waiver services include Residential Habilitation and Placement Services (Shared Living) - 24 Hour Supports, and all waiver services are contracted through DDS.

Placement Services (Shared Living) and Adult Foster Care (AFC)

Licensed by: Massachusetts Department of Developmental Services (DDS) for placement and shared living; MassHealth for Adult Foster Care. Serves: Adults with intellectual disabilities (DDS shared living) and MassHealth members with a medical or mental condition who can live safely in a home setting with a caregiver (AFC). Placement Services (Shared Living) - 24 Hour Supports is a listed service in the DDS HCBS waivers for adults with intellectual disabilities. The MassHealth Adult Foster Care program is a separate state plan program: the member and a qualified live-in caregiver live together in the member's or caregiver's home, with nursing oversight and care management; full eligibility is at 130 CMR 408.000. Chapter 180 of the Acts of 2026 references the MassHealth adult foster care program definition at 130 CMR 408.402 in the home care definitions.

Group Adult Foster Care (GAFC)

Licensed by: MassHealth (Executive Office of Health and Human Services). Serves: Adults 22 years or older who require physical assistance or cueing and supervision with at least one activity of daily living and who qualify for MassHealth Standard or CommonHealth. GAFC provides personal care services by direct care workers employed by a GAFC agency to eligible individuals residing in a home setting, helping them remain in their communities. A direct care aide provides daily physical assistance or cueing and supervision in the member's home.

DMH Community Residential Programs

Licensed by: Massachusetts Department of Mental Health (DMH), Community Licensing Division. Serves: Mentally ill persons in a residential treatment, rehabilitation, support and supervision program. A program license is required for any program, including a program not operated or contracted by DMH, that operates a residential site and is organized primarily to provide treatment, rehabilitation, support and supervision for mentally ill persons. Licensing and operational standards for community services are at 104 CMR 28.00 and for mental health facilities at 104 CMR 27.00.

Residential Rehabilitation Services - substance use disorder (BSAS)

Licensed by: Massachusetts Department of Public Health (DPH), Bureau of Substance Addiction Services (BSAS). Serves: Persons receiving substance use disorder treatment in a 24-hour residential setting. Residential rehabilitation services are organized substance use disorder treatment and education services featuring a planned program of care in a 24-hour residential setting, under 105 CMR 164.400. Initial licensure requires a suitability review and a Notice of Intent to Apply under 105 CMR 164.000.

How to start a group home in Massachusetts, step by step

  1. Assisted Living Residence: file an application for initial certification with the Executive Office of Aging and Independence, pay the unit-based fee, and file an operating plan covering the number of units, residents per unit, location of units and common spaces and egresses by floor, the base fee per unit, services to be offered including basic health services if applicable and arrangements and linkages, and the number of staff to be employed (M.G.L. c.19D Section 4)
  2. DDS residential and placement services: services in the three DDS HCBS waivers are contracted through the Department of Developmental Services, and providers enroll with DDS to provide waiver services

Massachusetts group home license fees and costs

  • Assisted Living Residence certification: no dollar amount is printed in the sources fetched; M.G.L. c.19D Section 4 states the fee is based on the number of units at the residence, established by the secretary of administration and finance under Section 3B of chapter 7, with a minimum fee

How long does it take in Massachusetts?

  • Assisted Living Residence certification term: 2 years, renewable for like terms (M.G.L. c.19D Section 4)
  • DDS licensure: DDS provider survey reports fetched show a Two-Year License for the Residential Service Grouping

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to open a group home in Massachusetts?

The figures Massachusetts publishes are these: Assisted Living Residence certification: no dollar amount is printed in the sources fetched; M.G.L. c.19D Section 4 states the fee is based on the number of units at the residence, established by the secretary of administration and finance under Section 3B of chapter 7, with a minimum fee The license fee is rarely the largest cost. The home itself, life safety and accessibility work, insurance, and staffing hired before the first placement carry the project.

How long does it take to get a group home license in Massachusetts?

Assisted Living Residence certification term: 2 years, renewable for like terms (M.G.L. c.19D Section 4) DDS licensure: DDS provider survey reports fetched show a Two-Year License for the Residential Service Grouping

What are the requirements to open a group home in Massachusetts?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Massachusetts?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
