How to Start a Home Health Agency in New York

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

New York licenses home health agencies as a Certified Home Health Agency (CHHA) certificate of approval under Article 36; a Licensed Home Care Services Agency (LHCSA) license is the separate, non-certified home care track, through the New York State Department of Health (NYSDOH). The governing law is Public Health Law Article 36; Title 10 NYCRR Part 763 and Part 766 for CHHA and LHCSA requirements as cited by NYSDOH, and Subpart 765-1, Title 10 NYCRR Part 765, for LHCSA licensure, public need and financial feasibility. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the New York license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The New York license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in New York, step by step

  1. For a CHHA, apply for establishment through the NYSDOH Certificate of Need and establishment process: CHHA Establish and Construct applications are listed for review by the Public Health and Health Planning Council (PHHPC) Establishment and Project Review Committee in NYSDOH council agendas
  2. PHHPC establishment review and approval, shown in NYSDOH agendas as contingent approval or approval for CHHA establishment applications
  3. Obtain a valid certificate of approval issued pursuant to Article 36 of the Public Health Law, which NYSDOH Medicaid material states a home care services provider must possess
  4. For a LHCSA, complete licensure review under Title 10 NYCRR Part 765, which NYSDOH states includes the public need determination and review of financial feasibility

New York home health license fees and costs

  • Licensed Home Care Services Agency (LHCSA) application fee $2,000, from the published Boswell Consulting Agency article provided for this task; a CHHA establishment fee amount was not printed on the NYSDOH pages fetched in this run, so no CHHA fee amount is stated

What catches New York applicants

  • CHHA services as described in NYSDOH Medicaid State Plan material: medically necessary, physician-ordered, part-time and intermittent skilled services in the home and community, including nursing, home health aide, physical therapy, occupational therapy and speech therapy, under a written plan of care
  • Certificate of need: NYSDOH Health Care Worker Bonus FAQ lists Certified Home Health Agencies among employer types subject to the certificate of need process
  • Public need test: the LHCSA track carries a public need determination. NYSDOH 2020 PHHPC reporting states Part 765 amendments implement the statutory public need determination for LHCSAs, financial feasibility review, and the application review process, under Section 9-b, Part B of Chapter 57 of the Laws of 2018
  • The LHCSA $2,000 fee, the public need test and the CHHA distinction are taken from the published article at the local file listed in sources, as directed for this task; the Article 36 and Part 765 points above are corroborated by NYSDOH pages fetched
  • No statement that a state license must precede Medicare certification was printed on the NYSDOH CHHA pages fetched; a CHHA is by definition the certified track, while a LHCSA is licensed only and is not the Medicare-certified agency type

The Medicare layer, after the New York license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a home health agency in New York?

The figures New York publishes are these: Licensed Home Care Services Agency (LHCSA) application fee $2,000, from the published Boswell Consulting Agency article provided for this task; a CHHA establishment fee amount was not printed on the NYSDOH pages fetched in this run, so no CHHA fee amount is stated Beyond the published figures, the operating costs (clinical payroll before certification, insurance, and the reserve operating funds required by 42 CFR 489.28) are larger than every fee on the list.

What do I need to start a home health agency in New York?

The Certified Home Health Agency (CHHA) certificate of approval under Article 36; a Licensed Home Care Services Agency (LHCSA) license is the separate, non-certified home care track (New York State Department of Health (NYSDOH)), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet New York rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in New York?

Apply to the New York State Department of Health (NYSDOH) under Public Health Law Article 36; Title 10 NYCRR Part 763 and Part 766 for CHHA and LHCSA requirements as cited by NYSDOH, and Subpart 765-1, Title 10 NYCRR Part 765, for LHCSA licensure, public need and financial feasibility. The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
