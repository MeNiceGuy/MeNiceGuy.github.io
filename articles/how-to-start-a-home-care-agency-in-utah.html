How to Start a Home Care Agency in Utah (Non-Medical Care Needs the Personal Care Agency License)

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 19, 2026

The Utah story starts with a founder who trusted a national guide. The guide said no license required for non-medical home care in Utah, in a tidy table, and she built her launch plan on that line: no application, no fee, no survey, open when ready. The line was wrong. Utah licenses non-medical home care, and it separates Utah from the states where the default door is unlicensed. An agency of two or more individuals providing personal care services on a visiting basis holds the personal care agency license under Utah Administrative Code R432-725, issued by the Division of Licensing and Background Checks, the Office of Licensing inside the Utah Department of Health and Human Services, at a first time total of $3,244 that the state's own fee schedule builds from three printed parts. The founder who reads the rule before the guide files once. The founder who reads the guide first finds the license at the worst possible time, usually after the first client. Here is the full picture, lane by lane, in the order a founder actually meets it.

The demand: about 460,000 seniors, the youngest senior share in this series

Start with why owners keep looking at Utah. The Census Bureau puts the state population at 3,538,904 as of July 1, 2025, and 13.0 percent of residents are age 65 or older. Run the math and you get about 460,000 seniors. The same Census QuickFacts page for Utah puts median household income at $95,166 for 2020 to 2024, the highest income base in this series, which is the base a private pay agency sells into.

Utah's senior share is the smallest in this batch of states and its population growth is among the fastest, which frames the opportunity honestly: the senior count is the floor the market grows from, not the ceiling. The agencies positioned for that growth are the ones holding the license, the background screening clearance, and the aide training record before the growth arrives at their door, because in Utah those are filing requirements, not marketing claims.

The license question, answered directly: personal care agencies are licensed under R432-725

Here is the searched question this article exists to answer. Do you need a license to start a non-medical home care agency in Utah? Yes, once the business is an agency. The personal care agency license at R432-725 applies to an agency of two or more individuals who provide personal care services on a visiting basis. Personal care services at R432-725-4 are help with self-administration of medication, transferring, personal grooming and dressing, eating and meal preparation, oral hygiene and denture care, toileting and toilet hygiene, bathing, taking and recording temperatures and weights, emergency first aid, and providing transportation. Services that require a licensed nurse or another licensed health care professional sit outside this license. A solo provider with no agency sits outside the agency definition; document the determination either way, because the definition is the first question a survey asks.

The three home-based licenses come from one office, the Division of Licensing and Background Checks, and they do not substitute for each other. Non-medical personal care sits on the personal care agency license at R432-725. Skilled home health sits on the home health agency license at R432-700, under Utah Code Title 26B, Chapter 2, Part 2. Hospice sits on its own license at R432-750. The residential doors are separate licenses and certifications again: assisted living facilities Type I and Type II under R432-270, small health care facilities under R432-300, nursing care facilities under R432-150, group homes certified under R501-23, and adult day care under R501-13. No one license covers another license's services.

The non-medical lane: $3,244 the first time, built from three printed parts

Utah health facility licenses run on a two year cycle built from parts, and the personal care math is exact. The two year licensing base fee is $743.83. The personal care agency type fee is $1,430.45. A new provider or change of ownership application fee of $1,069.26 applies to a first time applicant. Add the parts and a new personal care agency pays $3,243.54, which the schedule rounds to a first time total of $3,244, and a renewal pays $2,174.28, the base plus the type fee with no new provider fee. Each branch location adds a branch fee of $371.92 on the schedule. Fee schedule amounts reset each July 1, so re-read the schedule on the day of filing: the figures here are the FY2027 Division of Licensing and Background Checks schedule, verified line by line against the legislative schedule.

The sequence runs in filing order. First, confirm the service menu is non-skilled and the agency meets the R432-725 agency definition, two or more individuals providing personal care services on a visiting basis, with the R432-725-4 service list as the scope test. Second, form the business with the Utah Division of Corporations, hold the EIN and tax registrations, and put the operating accounts and insurance in place. Third, apply through the Office of Licensing Provider Portal with the fee schedule total for a new personal care agency. Fourth, build the screening and file systems before the first visit, covered in the background check section below. Fifth, complete the licensure survey, correct any deficiencies through the plan of correction process, and hold the license before providing covered services. The rule carries the operating standards with it: the administrator standard, the aide training and competency record, the supervision frame, and six hours of annual in-service.

The skilled lane and the hospice lane: R432-700 and R432-750

A home health agency is a health care facility that provides licensed nursing or therapy services through its own employees in client homes, licensed at R432-700. The fee schedule builds its license from the same base: $743.83 plus the home health agency type fee of $2,138.52, a two year license total of $2,882.35, and $3,951.61 for a first time applicant with the new provider fee. A home health agency may also deliver personal care services; the personal care agency license carries no skilled service, and that one-way bridge is the scope line to hold. No certificate of need requirement for a home health agency was located in the sources checked for this article. Medicare certification layers on the license under 42 CFR Part 484, on Form CMS-855A through PECOS to CGS Administrators, Jurisdiction 15, the Home Health and Hospice MAC for Utah, with the CY2026 institutional enrollment fee of $750. The A/B MAC is Noridian Healthcare Solutions, Jurisdiction F, and Noridian Jurisdiction D is the DME MAC under the same contractor name, a different desk.

Hospice holds its own license at R432-750: a hospice licensee provides support and care for individuals with a limited life expectancy so that they might live as fully and comfortably as possible, in the home and in an inpatient setting, through an interdisciplinary team. The file includes the governing body and administrator record at R432-750-4, personnel licensure proof within 45 days of hire at R432-750-5, and the quality assurance program at R432-750-10. On fees, the position is stated plainly: NO FEE PUBLISHED for a hospice specific figure, because no hospice type fee was located in the sources checked, and no other license type figure is borrowed for this track. Confirm the type fee with the Office of Licensing on the day of filing and hold the confirmation in the startup file. The nonprofit volunteer end of life care exemption at R432-32 is not a hospice license. Medicare certification for hospice runs under 42 CFR Part 418, and on both certified tracks the federal Medicare enrollment moratorium gates initial enrollment only (the extension is a six-month period, running to about May 12, 2027); it blocks no Utah state license.

Background checks: Direct Access Clearance before direct access care

Build the screening and file systems before the first visit: background screening clearance through the Direct Access Clearance System, at $100 initial and $200 renewal on the schedule, the OIG and SAM exclusion checks, tuberculosis screening, the aide competency evaluation, and the supervision record. The deficiency pattern in this state is staff providing direct access care before background screening clearance is documented, and aide in-service hours drifting below the six hour annual record the personal care rule prints. Sequence clearance before assignment, file the result in the personnel record, and calendar the annual in-service the same week the first aide is hired, not at the first renewal.

The Medicaid lane: PRISM enrollment on top of the license, EVV by provider choice

Utah Medicaid sits inside the Department of Health and Human Services, and provider enrollment, screening, and revalidation run through PRISM. Personal care services under Medicaid may be delivered by a licensed personal care agency or a licensed home health agency; the license comes first, the PRISM enrollment second, and enrollment runs for each waiver served, including Aging Waiver personal attendant services for people age 65 or older, New Choices Waiver attendant care, and the Physical Disabilities and Acquired Brain Injury Waiver services. Electronic visit verification is mandatory for Medicaid personal care and home health services on a provider choice model: each provider selects its own EVV vendor, no state vendor is mandated and no state system is provided, and EVV records are submitted to Utah Medicaid to accompany each claim within three months of submitting the claim, feeding the state aggregator. Providers without EVV data are subject to recoupment or penalties, and any guide that names a mandated Utah EVV vendor is describing another state.

One Utah Medicaid moratorium exists, and its scope is narrow enough to state in one sentence. Medicaid Information Bulletin item 26-60 prints a CMS approved temporary moratorium on new fee-for-service enrollments of substance use disorder and mental health rehabilitation providers, effective July 1, 2026, for an initial period of six months, running to about January 1, 2027. That pause governs the residential treatment payer doors in the all-homes tracks. No Utah Medicaid moratorium on home health, personal care, hospice, or facility enrollments was located, and none applies to the home-based tracks in this article. Do not import the behavioral health pause into a home-based plan.

What you still do today: form the business the Utah way

Form the business with the Utah Division of Corporations, hold the EIN and tax registrations, and put the operating accounts and insurance in place before the portal application goes in. Then do the ordinary employer load in the right order: liability insurance in place before the first client, the policy set built to R432-725, the Direct Access Clearance System screening running before the first assignment, aide training and competency records filed per aide, and a service agreement that states what the agency does and does not do, with services requiring a licensed nurse or another licensed health care professional on the does not do side of the line. No Utah formation fee is printed in the sources checked for this article, so I print none: confirm the current filing fee on the day of filing.

The practical warning

Utah's traps start with the guide trap: national guides that print no license required for Utah non-medical home care are wrong, the rule and the Division of Licensing and Background Checks license index both print this license, and an agency opened on the no-license assumption finds out at the first survey referral or the first payer application. The second trap is scope: skilled services marketed or delivered on the personal care agency license are the most expensive scope error in Utah home-based care. The third trap is contractor geography: Palmetto GBA holds no Utah desk, and a Medicare enrollment built for the wrong MAC goes nowhere. The fourth is the calendar: fee schedule amounts reset each July 1, so a budget built on last year's schedule is stale on the first day of the new fiscal year. Read the rule, the schedule in effect on the filing date, and the bulletin scope, and this state files clean.
