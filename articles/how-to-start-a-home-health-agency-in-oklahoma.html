How to Start a Home Health Agency in Oklahoma

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Oklahoma licenses home health agencies as a License to Operate a Home Care Agency, ODH Form 757, through the Oklahoma State Department of Health (OSDH), Medical Facilities Service, Home Services Division. The governing law is Home Care Act, 63 O.S. 1-1960 et seq.; Oklahoma Administrative Code Title 310, Chapter 662, including 310:662-2-1. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the Oklahoma license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The Oklahoma license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Oklahoma, step by step

  1. State in the cover letter for an initial application whether the agency is planning to become Medicare certified - OSDH How to Submit a Successful Application
  2. Use entity and DBA names exactly as registered with the Oklahoma Secretary of State, and provide Secretary of State authority to operate - OSDH application guide and ODH Form 757
  3. Complete ODH Form 757, Application for License to Operate a Home Care Agency, and mail it with the nonrefundable license fee payable to the Oklahoma State Department of Health - ODH Form 757
  4. Include required attachments listed in OSDH guidance: application fee, Secretary of State authority, administrator certification for home care agencies, nurse license verification for supervising and alternate supervising nurses, financial and insurance verification, narrative summary, and Plan of Delivery describing scope and range of services - OSDH How to Submit a Successful Application

Oklahoma home health license fees and costs

  • Initial License and Application Fee $1,000, nonrefundable - ODH Form 757
  • Renewal license fee $500 per year; prorated fee $125 per quarter for a license prorated to expire on July 31 - ODH Form 757
  • Branch renewal fee $25 for each branch - ODH Form 757 prorated fee note

How long does it take in Oklahoma?

  • Home care agency license expires July 31; renewal Form 757 and required attachments and fee must be returned no later than June 30 - OSDH Home Care Renewal Letter
  • A change of ownership must be filed at least 30 days prior to the effective date of the change, per Title 310:662-2-1(F)(2) - OSDH Home Care Change of Ownership guidance

What catches Oklahoma applicants

  • Medicare note from an official OSDH application guide: an initial applicant should include in the cover letter whether the applicant is planning to become Medicare certified, and that instruction applies to Home Care and Hospices only - OSDH How to Submit a Successful Application. The guide does not state a full Medicare certification sequence, so verification of the state-license-before-Medicare order for Oklahoma is thin.
  • Certificate of need: no certificate of need requirement for a home care agency was printed in the OSDH Form 757, renewal, change of ownership, or application-guide documents fetched. No CON fact is asserted here.
  • The Home Services Division phone number printed in OSDH application guidance is (405) 426-8470.

The Medicare layer, after the Oklahoma license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a home health agency in Oklahoma?

The figures Oklahoma publishes are these: Initial License and Application Fee $1,000, nonrefundable - ODH Form 757 Renewal license fee $500 per year; prorated fee $125 per quarter for a license prorated to expire on July 31 - ODH Form 757 Branch renewal fee $25 for each branch - ODH Form 757 prorated fee note Beyond the published figures, the operating costs (clinical payroll before certification, insurance, and the reserve operating funds required by 42 CFR 489.28) are larger than every fee on the list.

How long does it take to get a home health license in Oklahoma?

Home care agency license expires July 31; renewal Form 757 and required attachments and fee must be returned no later than June 30 - OSDH Home Care Renewal Letter A change of ownership must be filed at least 30 days prior to the effective date of the change, per Title 310:662-2-1(F)(2) - OSDH Home Care Change of Ownership guidance The federal certification that follows runs on the survey calendar, and new Medicare enrollment is under a CMS moratorium to about mid-May 2027.

What do I need to start a home health agency in Oklahoma?

The License to Operate a Home Care Agency, ODH Form 757 (Oklahoma State Department of Health (OSDH), Medical Facilities Service, Home Services Division), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet Oklahoma rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in Oklahoma?

Apply to the Oklahoma State Department of Health (OSDH), Medical Facilities Service, Home Services Division under Home Care Act, 63 O.S. 1-1960 et seq.; Oklahoma Administrative Code Title 310, Chapter 662, including 310:662-2-1. The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
