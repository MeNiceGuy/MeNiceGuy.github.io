How to Start a Home Health Agency in New Hampshire

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

New Hampshire licenses home health agencies as a Home Health Care Provider license (He-P 809), through the New Hampshire Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS), Health Facilities Administration, Office of Legal and Regulatory Services. The governing law is RSA 151; He-P 809 for Home Health Care Providers, and He-P 822 for Home Care Service Provider Agencies. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the New Hampshire license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The New Hampshire license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in New Hampshire, step by step

  1. Apply for licensure to the DHHS Health Facilities Administration; home health care providers and home care service providers are both listed as facility types under its jurisdiction
  2. For Medicare or Medicaid participation, the agency is treated as a certified facility through the DHHS Health Facility Certification Unit, separate from state licensure

New Hampshire home health license fees and costs

  • Home Health Care Providers (He-P 809): $250.00, per the DHHS Health Facilities Administration fee list

What catches New Hampshire applicants

  • New Hampshire licenses both skilled home health care providers under He-P 809 and home care service provider agencies under He-P 822 through the same Health Facilities Administration
  • A certified facility, in DHHS terms, is any facility such as a home health agency that participates in the Medicare/Medicaid programs; the Health Facility Certification Unit handles certified facilities and the Health Facilities Licensing Unit handles licensed facilities, both at 129 Pleasant Street, Concord
  • Fee conflict to flag: the DHHS Health Facilities Administration page fetched for this sheet lists Personal Care Providers (822) at less than 10 clients $25.00 and 10 or more clients $100.00, while secondary summaries of RSA 151:5 print $250 for 10 or more clients; the DHHS page figure is used in this sheet and the conflict should be rechecked before publication
  • No certificate of need step or processing timeline is printed on the DHHS Health Facilities Administration page reviewed, so none is listed here

The Medicare layer, after the New Hampshire license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a home health agency in New Hampshire?

The figures New Hampshire publishes are these: Home Health Care Providers (He-P 809): $250.00, per the DHHS Health Facilities Administration fee list Beyond the published figures, the operating costs (clinical payroll before certification, insurance, and the reserve operating funds required by 42 CFR 489.28) are larger than every fee on the list.

What do I need to start a home health agency in New Hampshire?

The Home Health Care Provider license (He-P 809) (New Hampshire Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS), Health Facilities Administration, Office of Legal and Regulatory Services), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet New Hampshire rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in New Hampshire?

Apply to the New Hampshire Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS), Health Facilities Administration, Office of Legal and Regulatory Services under RSA 151; He-P 809 for Home Health Care Providers, and He-P 822 for Home Care Service Provider Agencies. The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
