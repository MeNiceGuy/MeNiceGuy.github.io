How to Start a Group Home in Illinois

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Illinois. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Illinois licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Illinois licenses

Community-Integrated Living Arrangement (CILA)

Licensed by: Illinois Department of Human Services (IDHS), Division of Developmental Disabilities (DDD); licensure surveys by the Bureau of Accreditation, Licensure and Certification (BALC). Serves: Adults age 18 and older with a developmental disability; 8 or fewer unrelated adults per site. 210 ILCS 135 and 59 Ill. Adm. Code Part 115; the agency is licensed and each site is certified; variants under one license are 24-Hour CILA, Intermittent CILA, Family CILA, and Host Family CILA (Traditional and Shared Living models, Part 115 Subpart E); payer is the Adults with Developmental Disabilities 1915(c) waiver IL.0350 plus state-only DDD funding; no Medicare door

Child Group Home (Children's Group Home)

Licensed by: Waiver service under HFS and IDHS-DDD; underlying home licensed by DCFS. Serves: Children and young adults ages 3 through 21 with developmental disabilities at risk of ICF/IID placement. A residential service in the 1915(c) Residential Waiver for Children and Young Adults with Developmental Disabilities, waiver IL.0473, defined at 59 Ill. Adm. Code 120.10; not a freestanding license, the home is licensed by DCFS under Part 403 or Part 404 by size; no separate CGH fee

Intermediate Care Facility for the Developmentally Disabled (ICF/DD, federally ICF/IID)

Licensed by: IDPH, Long-Term Care licensure. Serves: 3 or more persons with developmental disabilities needing intermediate care and active treatment. 210 ILCS 47 (ID/DD Community Care Act) and 77 Ill. Adm. Code Part 350; the 16-or-less form (Subpart P) is one building with a maximum of 16 residents and site-separation rules; a Health Facilities Planning Board permit is required to establish one; Medicaid only, no Medicare door; federal Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 483 Subpart I

Medically Complex for the Developmentally Disabled facility (MC/DD)

Licensed by: IDPH license. Serves: 3 or more persons who are medically complex with developmental disabilities, with a pediatric and under-22 core. 210 ILCS 46 and 77 Ill. Adm. Code Part 390; created by P.A. 99-180 (2015), which removed the under-22 population from the ID/DD Community Care Act into MC/DD licensure; Medicaid door including HFS exceptional-care payments under 89 Ill. Adm. Code 146.1015; no Medicare door found

Community Living Facility (CLF)

Licensed by: IDPH license (a different license from CILA). Serves: Persons with a mild or moderate developmental disability, in a transitional setting aimed at more independent living. 210 ILCS 35 and 77 Ill. Adm. Code Part 370; license cap is 20 residents excluding staff (35/3 and 370.240); a waiver figure of 16 appears in DDD waiver guidance but was not re-verified as the license cap, so 20 is the license cap in the research; payer is Adults with DD waiver residential habilitation when waiver-enrolled, plus state and private funding; no Medicare door

Continuum of Care Facility

Licensed by: Secretary of Human Services, acting through or in coordination with the Director of Public Health. Serves: Individuals with developmental disabilities served by a campus-based continuum organization. 210 ILCS 42; a single license whose constituent elements (for example CILA and ICF/DD elements) must each meet the otherwise applicable law for that element; no fee published (proven negative); register entry inside the CILA and ICF/DD tracks, not a startup group-home route

DCFS Group Home

Licensed by: Illinois Department of Children and Family Services (DCFS) license. Serves: No more than 10 children placed by and under the supervision of a licensed child welfare agency. 225 ILCS 10 (Child Care Act of 1969) and 89 Ill. Adm. Code Part 403; the home must be owned or rented, staffed, maintained, and operated by the licensed child welfare agency, there is no standalone individual group-home license under Part 403; payer is primarily DCFS purchase-of-service and Title IV-E, with the Child Group Home waiver service and Rule 132 clinical services as the Medicaid entries; no Medicare door

DCFS Child Care Institution (and Maternity Center)

Licensed by: DCFS license. Serves: More than 7 children received and maintained for care or training; children under 18 and persons under 21 referred as the rule provides. 225 ILCS 10 and 89 Ill. Adm. Code Part 404; no upper bed cap is printed in 404.2, capacity is the licensed capacity on the license; payer is DCFS purchase-of-service and Title IV-E subject to the QRTP designation; Medicaid clinical services only via separately enrolled providers; not an ICF/IID and not a PRTF

Qualified Residential Treatment Program (QRTP)

Licensed by: Federal Title IV-E designation layered on a DCFS-licensed child care institution (DCFS). Serves: Children in foster care placements in a child care institution. Not a license and not a separate facility type; 89 Ill. Adm. Code 359.11 provides that from the third week, federal foster care maintenance payments require a QRTP or another printed exception, and the QRTP placement assessment must be completed within 30 days; register inside the child care institution track; no QRTP fee

Secure Child Care Facility

Licensed by: DCFS license under the Child Care Act of 1969, 225 ILCS 10. Serves: Children and youth age 13 or older and less than 18 placed under the Children and Family Services Act and not subject to Department of Corrections placement. 89 Ill. Adm. Code Part 411; a stacked track only: the applicant must already be licensed as a child care institution under Part 404, be accredited, be certified and enrolled in good standing under Rule 132 (59 Ill. Adm. Code Part 132), and have a written DCFS notification of need for that region; fee is a gap, no fee found and the packet states it as unpublished, never as $0

Specialized Mental Health Rehabilitation Facility (SMHRF)

Licensed by: IDPH license. Serves: Individuals with a serious mental illness in long-term care facilities federally designated as institutions for mental diseases (IMDs) on July 22, 2013. 210 ILCS 49 and 77 Ill. Adm. Code Part 380; closed class: no more than 24 licenses statewide (380.700(g)) and total beds capped at the July 22, 2013 number, so a new operator cannot open a new SMHRF and the track is acquisition or change-of-ownership only; service levels inside the license include crisis stabilization, transitional living, and recovery and rehabilitation supports units; Medicaid payer door

Rule 132 Certified Community Mental Health Center or Specialty Provider (mental health community residential door)

Licensed by: Certification, not licensure, by a Certifying State Agency (IDHS BALC or DCFS), now administered within IDHS Division of Behavioral Health and Recovery. Serves: Persons receiving Medicaid community mental health services, including residential services. 59 Ill. Adm. Code Part 132 under the Community Services Act, 405 ILCS 30; Illinois has no freestanding mental-health facility license for community residential settings (proven negative); Rule 132 certifies the provider and its sites, including Community Support - Residential, which may only be provided in Supervised Residential, CILA, or Crisis Residential settings; the DBHR grant-funded levels 820 Supported Residential, 830 Supervised Residential, and 860 Crisis Residential are registers inside this track and recent cycles were non-competitive and limited to current awardees; proposed Title 59 Part 140 (2013) was never adopted and is a named exclusion; no fee published (proven negative)

Substance Use Disorder residential treatment (Levels 3.1 and 3.5 and withdrawal management)

Licensed by: IDHS Division of Behavioral Health and Recovery (DBHR) license; rule text reads DHS/SUPR. Serves: Adults and adolescents by license category, level of care, and population, needing residential substance use disorder treatment. 20 ILCS 301 and 77 Ill. Adm. Code Part 2060 (new Part effective April 2, 2025); Level 3.1 is clinically-managed low-intensity residential with staff awake 24 hours a day and clinical services at least 5 hours per week, Level 3.5 is clinically-managed medium-to-high-intensity residential with clinical services averaging a minimum of 25 hours per week, and withdrawal management levels are registers inside this track; no statewide bed cap, capacity is the licensed capacity for the site and level; the current Medicaid SUD enrollment rule citation was not verified in the research (gap)

Recovery Home

Licensed by: IDHS-DBHR intervention license. Serves: Persons in substance use disorder recovery. 20 ILCS 301 and 77 Ill. Adm. Code 2060.540; a service under the intervention license with a credentialed recovery home operator and on-site manager and a requirement tied to two months of operating expenses in the rule text as retrieved; no bed cap printed in 2060.540 in the sections retrieved; payer includes DBHR contract or referral and resident fees

Oxford House and sober living home

Licensed by: No state credential; registry listing only under 20 ILCS 301/55-40. Serves: Persons in substance use disorder recovery in peer-run, alcohol- and drug-free homes. Expressly non-licensed in IDHS materials; a Recovery Home is the licensed form, while Oxford Houses and sober living homes are non-licensed recovery residences; resident self-pay only; named exclusion with the registry inside the Recovery Home track

Assisted Living Establishment

Licensed by: IDPH license. Serves: At least 3 unrelated adults, at least 80 percent age 55 or older. 210 ILCS 9 and 77 Ill. Adm. Code Part 295; no statutory maximum in the definition; an Alzheimer's Disease and Related Dementias Special Care Disclosure form is a required application item; private pay, with no Medicaid or Medicare door through the 210 ILCS 9 license itself (the Medicaid model is the Supportive Living Program)

Shared Housing Establishment

Licensed by: IDPH license. Serves: 16 or fewer persons, at least 80 percent age 55 or older, unrelated to the owners. 210 ILCS 9 and 77 Ill. Adm. Code Part 295, in the same license family as assisted living establishments; private pay, with no Medicaid or Medicare door through the license itself

Supportive Living Program (SLP) setting

Licensed by: Healthcare and Family Services (HFS) certification, not an IDPH license. Serves: Medicaid-eligible residents in a supportive living setting, including dementia care units under Subpart E certification. 305 ILCS 5/5-5.01a and 89 Ill. Adm. Code Part 146 Subpart B (and Subpart E for dementia units); waiver IL.0326 operated by HFS itself; no fee published (proven negative); a conflict is named in the research: the HFS Provider Fact Sheet prints $90 per month retained income while 305 ILCS 5/5-5.01a(h) prints a $120 personal needs allowance from January 1, 2025, and neither is printed as the settled figure

IDOA Community Care Program (CCP) provider certification (priority track)

Licensed by: Illinois Department on Aging (IDOA). Serves: Participants age 60 and older assessed at risk of nursing facility placement; services are in-home, adult day, Emergency Home Response Service, and Automated Medication Dispenser service. 20 ILCS 105 and 89 Ill. Adm. Code Part 240; CCP is the Persons Who Are Elderly 1915(c) waiver (renewal IL.0143.R08.00 effective October 1, 2026) plus a non-Medicaid component; there is no IDPH adult day license, adult day routes only through CCP Adult Day Service certification; Emergency Certification under 240.1605 is non-renewable and is a register only, never a normal door; no Medicare door

How to start a group home in Illinois, step by step

  1. Main group home track (CILA): attend New Provider Orientation (currently three sessions, attendance verified via WebEx, at least one person from the agency for the entire sessions)
  2. After New Provider Orientation (or meeting exemption requirements), request the CILA Licensure Application from BALC, 401 S. Clinton, 7th Floor, Chicago, IL 60607 (DHS.BALC@illinois.gov); under 59 Ill. Adm. Code 115.410 the agency must fulfill new provider requirements within one year of requesting the application
  3. Submit the application with documents and the $200 nonrefundable application fee to BALC, with electronic fingerprint background check through IDPH for owners, authorized representatives, officers, and the administrator
  4. Complete the initial interview with BALC; DHS guidance states that an applicant who does not pass may not reapply for one calendar year, and the same bar applies if policies and procedures are not approved (guidance, not rule text)
  5. Submit policies and procedures, a projected two-year budget, sample case files, and sample implementation strategies
  6. Receive the provisional license if approved: up to one year, allowing operation of one CILA site serving up to eight individuals (115.430(c))
  7. Notify BALC when the property is ready; complete the Office of the State Fire Marshal inspection and the BALC property inspection before occupancy and before individuals are accepted
  8. Complete the BALC site visit within six months after the first admission and, if passed, receive the permanent license for 3 years; the Bureau of Quality Management makes an unannounced visit within the first year after first admission
  9. Enroll for payment after licensure: obtain a Type 2 organizational NPI with taxonomy 320900000X, submit enrollment documents to DHS-DDD Provider Enrollment, enroll in IMPACT as a Facility/Agency/Organization with the CILA specialty, complete ROCS billing setup, and obtain DSP and QIDP training program approval by DDD and the Bureau of Quality Management

Illinois group home license fees and costs

  • CILA: $200 nonrefundable application fee (IDHS Steps for CILA and New Provider Guide 2026; statutory cap $200 at 210 ILCS 135/4(d) and renewal cap $200 at 4(h); Part 115 prints no dollar figure)
  • ICF/DD: $995 annual license or $1,990 for a two-year license, exempt for facilities paying the Article V-C Public Aid Code assessment (210 ILCS 47/3-103(2))
  • MC/DD: $995 annual license or double for a two-year license, exempt for facilities paying the Article V-C assessment (210 ILCS 46/3-103(2))
  • Community Living Facility: $200 application fee (210 ILCS 35/7(1) and 77 Ill. Adm. Code 370.120(c)); no separate renewal dollar figure was printed in the sections checked
  • DCFS group home and child care institution: $0, the rules print that there shall be no fee or charge for the license (89 Ill. Adm. Code 403.6(i); 404.6(g) and 404.7(h))
  • Secure child care facility: no fee found (gap; the underlying Part 404 license is $0, but the packet states the Part 411 fee as unpublished, never as $0)
  • SMHRF: $5,700 non-refundable license fee (77 Ill. Adm. Code 380.700 and 380.740(a))
  • Rule 132 certification: no fee published (proven negative; no fee section exists in Part 132)
  • SUD residential and Recovery Home: no application fee (77 Ill. Adm. Code 2060.220, except as specified in 2060.260 and 2060.265); the legacy $200 DASA fee belongs to the repealed Part and must never be printed
  • Assisted living establishment: $2,000 plus $20 per licensed unit, nonrefundable; shared housing establishment: $1,000, nonrefundable (77 Ill. Adm. Code 295.500(e); the same fee applies on renewal under 295.700(a))
  • Supportive Living Program: no fee published (proven negative in 89 Ill. Adm. Code 146.215, Part 146 Subparts B and E, 305 ILCS 5/5-5.01a, and the HFS SLP Provider Fact Sheet)
  • CCP provider certification: no fee published (proven negative in 89 Ill. Adm. Code 240.1505, 240.1600, 240.1605, 240.1607 and every IDOA application, certification, and FAQ page checked)
  • Continuum of Care Facility: no fee published (proven negative in the 210 ILCS 42 text checked)
  • Nursing facility (facility context in the research): $1,990 annual or $3,980 two-year, flat per facility, exempt if paying the Article V-C assessment (210 ILCS 45/3-103(2))

How long does it take in Illinois?

  • CILA: license term 3 years (210 ILCS 135/4(e)); provisional license up to 1 year for one site up to 8 individuals; agency must be operational within 18 months or the process is abandoned (115.410(e)); Department inspections of each certified CILA at least once every 2 years
  • ICF/DD license term: not less than 6 nor more than 18 months for annual licenses, and not less than 18 nor more than 30 months for two-year licenses (77 Ill. Adm. Code 350.110(b))
  • Community Living Facility: probationary license not to exceed 6 months, with a final on-site evaluation within 30 days before probationary expiry (210 ILCS 35/7(5))
  • DCFS group home and child care institution: license valid for 4 years, with a 6-month permit stage (license issued within the permit period on compliance)
  • SMHRF: provisional period 3 years under the Act, full license 1 year renewable annually; national accreditation is required to move from provisional to full licensure
  • Rule 132: provisional certificate in effect for 12 months unless extended; on-site certification review within the provisional period; at minimum one-third of providers reviewed annually and all reviewed within 3 years; specialty recertification is annual and DMH reviews a specialty submission within 90 days
  • SUD license term: up to 3 years (2060.225(a)); the applicant has 60 calendar days to submit requested information or corrections or the application is returned (2060.240, as retrieved)
  • Assisted living and shared housing: IDPH issues a regular license within 120 days of a complete application (295.600(b)); initial regular license 1 year; renewal filed 120 to 150 days before expiration and may be granted for 2 years on request (295.700(a))
  • Supportive Living Program: site must be operational within 24 months of approval (89 Ill. Adm. Code 146.215(c)(1)(E))
  • CCP: certification for no more than 3 years, recertification no less frequently than every 3 years, Provider Agreements generally 3 years and entered no more frequently than semiannually after initial certification; provisional certification not to exceed 2 years; if IDOA asks for more material and the agency does not respond, after 60 calendar days the request is closed and all information must be resubmitted (240.1600(c)); no IDOA-published application-to-contract processing time was found and the stale 3 to 4 month Fiscal Year 2010 estimate must not be used
  • Hospice (facility context): comprehensive initial $500 and volunteer initial $250 with a 1-year term, hospice residence certificate 2 years plus 1 renewal, statewide cap 48 residences

What are the requirements to open a group home in Illinois?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Illinois?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
