How to Start a Group Home in Virginia

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Virginia. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Virginia licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Virginia licenses

DBHDS adult group home and community residential

Licensed by: Department of Behavioral Health and Developmental Services (DBHDS), Office of Licensing. Serves: Adults with developmental disabilities (service code 01-001) or mental illness (code 01-004). Licensable service at 12VAC35-105-30 item 12; definition at 12VAC35-105-20 is 24-hour supervision in a community-based home with eight or fewer residents; group home residential is covered only in the Community Living waiver at 12VAC30-122-390, with a Medicaid reimbursement cap of six licensed beds for new settings

DBHDS sponsored residential home

Licensed by: DBHDS, Office of Licensing. Serves: Adults and children with developmental disabilities and adults with mental illness, living in a sponsor family home. Service at 12VAC35-105-30 item 28, Part VI Article 4 sections 1160 to 1235; maximum two individuals per sponsored home and seven occupants in the home (section 1230); mutually exclusive with a group home per home (12VAC35-105-1180.G); codes 08-011 DD adults Non-Priority, 08-013 DD children Priority 1, 08-014 MH Priority 1

DBHDS supervised living residential

Licensed by: DBHDS, Office of Licensing. Serves: Adults with developmental disabilities or mental illness in supervised living settings. Service at 12VAC35-105-30 item 32; codes 01-011 DD and 01-012 MH, both Priority 2; DD waiver supervised living service; HCBS compliance entry rule applies

ICF/IID

Licensed by: DBHDS license plus DMAS Medicaid certification. Serves: Individuals with intellectual or developmental disabilities needing intermediate care. Service at 12VAC35-105-30 item 13, code 01-005 Non-Priority; federal conditions at 42 CFR Part 483 Subpart I and provider agreements at Part 442; DMAS enrollment through MES; no Medicare benefit category exists for ICF/IID

Children's group home / children's residential facility (DBHDS)

Licensed by: DBHDS, Office of Licensing, under Title 37.2. Serves: Children and adolescents, including children with developmental disabilities. Licensed under 12VAC35-46, not 12VAC35-105; DD group home service for children routes here per 12VAC30-122-390.D.2; prioritization code 01-035 Non-Priority; fees at 12VAC35-46-100 are kept strictly separate from the adult track

DSS children's residential facility / group home for children

Licensed by: Department of Social Services (DSS), Division of Licensing Programs. Serves: Children in child welfare placements, including children separated from parents for full-time care. Licensed under 22VAC40-151, definition at 22VAC40-151-10; independent living services for ages 18 to 21 sit inside this license and are not a separate license; fees at 22VAC40-151-60

ASAM residential services

Licensed by: DBHDS, same 12VAC35-105 service license system. Serves: People receiving substance use disorder residential treatment. Clinically managed low-intensity residential (3.1, item 4), population-specific high-intensity residential (3.3, item 5), high-intensity residential (3.5, item 3), and residential treatment (item 26); no separate home license class, registered inside the DBHDS group home track

Crisis residential classes

Licensed by: DBHDS, same 12VAC35-105 service license system. Serves: People in behavioral health crisis, including geriatric psychiatric and crisis stabilization populations. Community gero-psychiatric residential (item 6), community-based crisis stabilization (item 7), crisis receiving center (item 8), crisis stabilization unit (item 9), and REACH Crisis Therapeutic Home (item 24); specialized programs, several state-operated or regionally contracted, registered inside the DBHDS group home track

Assisted Living Facility

Licensed by: DSS, Division of Licensing Programs. Serves: Adults needing residential assisted living care. Licensed under 22VAC40-73 with fees on the capacity schedule at 22VAC40-160-10; license term up to three years under Code sections 63.2-1701 and 63.2-1707; Medicaid does not pay ALF room and board, and the payer sequence in the research is private pay and Auxiliary Grant

Nursing facility

Licensed by: VDH, Office of Licensure and Certification. Serves: Residents needing skilled nursing facility care. Licensed under 12VAC5-371; Certificate of Public Need required before licensure (12VAC5-371-40.E); one-year license stating operator, location, and maximum beds; Medicare SNF and Medicaid NF certification under 42 CFR Part 483

Adult day center (adult day care)

Licensed by: DSS, Division of Licensing Programs. Serves: Adults who reside elsewhere and receive part-day care, four or more adults. Licensed under 22VAC40-61, definition at 22VAC40-61-10, standards revised effective August 14, 2024; fees on the 22VAC40-160-10 capacity schedule; adult day health care is a CCC Plus waiver service door

Adult foster care

Licensed by: Local department of social services program under Title 63.2. Serves: Adults placed in a local foster care arrangement. A local DSS placement program for adults, not a state facility license an owner applies for; registered inside the adult day care track in the research

How to start a group home in Virginia, step by step

  1. Check the current DBHDS Prioritization List and the Application Document Matrix before starting; adult group home codes 01-001 and 01-004 are Non-Priority under the July 18, 2025 memorandum effective July 30, 2025
  2. For Priority 1 and Priority 2 services only, complete the DBHDS Initial Applicant Orientation Training and pass the Comprehensive Knowledge Exam at 85 percent or higher (November 7, 2025 memorandum); the memo does not impose the exam on Non-Priority adult group home codes
  3. Submit the initial application in the DBHDS CONNECT Provider Portal within 90 days of beginning the application process or it is administratively closed, with the contents required by Code section 37.2-405.2 and 12VAC35-105-40, including a first-year working budget, evidence of at least 90 days of operating capital, staffing plan, service description, and records policy
  4. Wait on the CONNECT waitlist for policy review assignment; for Non-Priority services at least one waitlisted applicant is assigned to policy review each month
  5. Complete criminal background checks and registry searches under 12VAC35-105-400 and Code sections 37.2-416, 37.2-416.1, 37.2-506, 37.2-506.1, and 37.2-607, with outcomes delivered through CONNECT
  6. Pass the DBHDS onsite review of the physical plant and program under 12VAC35-105-70 and Part III Article 3, including bedroom, bathroom, fire inspection, and emergency preparedness standards; food service inspection provisions do not apply to group homes under section 290
  7. Receive a conditional license first, limited for group homes to a single location serving no more than four individuals, then a full license on full compliance under 12VAC35-105-50
  8. For DD waiver billing, hold a DMAS HCBS compliance letter before completing DMAS enrollment, then enroll through the MES Provider Resources site with an NPI and a signed DMAS participation agreement
  9. File any change to services, locations, or capacity as a written modification application at least 30 days in advance under 12VAC35-105-60, and confirm renewal intent before the license expires

Virginia group home license fees and costs

  • DBHDS licenses under 12VAC35-105 (adult group home, sponsored residential, supervised living, and ICF/IID): no application fee and no renewal fee; proven negative, no fee section exists in the chapter and Code section 37.2-410 is permissive only
  • DBHDS children's residential license (12VAC35-46-100): $500 nonrefundable initial application fee, a further $500 on reapplication after closure, denial, or withdrawal, and $100 nonrefundable renewal application fee; no renewal fee directly following a conditional license; state and local government facilities exempt
  • DSS children's residential license (22VAC40-151-60): $500 nonrefundable initial application fee, a further $500 on reapplication, and $100 nonrefundable renewal application fee; no renewal fee directly following a conditional license; government facilities exempt
  • Nursing facility license (12VAC5-371-45): initial or renewal base fee $150 plus a per-bed fee of $29.00; change of licensure fee $250, waivable by the Commissioner on documented justification
  • Adult day center and assisted living fees use the DSS capacity schedule at 22VAC40-160-10, collected annually: capacity 1 to 12 is $14 for one year, $28 for two years, $42 for three years; 13 to 25 is $35 / $70 / $105; 26 to 50 is $70 / $140 / $210; 51 to 75 is $105 / $210 / $315; 76 to 200 is $140 / $280 / $420; 201 and up is $200 / $400 / $600; no fee directly following a conditional license
  • DMAS enrollment for DD waiver provider types: no fixed figure published; the DD Waivers Provider Manual Chapter II states an institutional provider may be required to pay a federally required fee and the amount, if any, is stated in the enrollment application or revalidation notice itself
  • Federal institutional provider enrollment fee on the nursing facility Medicare door only: $750 for calendar year 2026

How long does it take in Virginia?

  • Initial and modification applications must be submitted within 90 days of beginning the process or they are administratively closed
  • Policy review assignment targets: Priority 1 within 5 to 10 business days, Priority 2 within 21 business days, Non-Priority at least one waitlisted applicant assigned each month
  • Review and potential licensure within 90 days is the published target for Priority 1 and Priority 2 complete applications only; DBHDS publishes no licensure decision deadline for Non-Priority group home services and states licensure may not occur within that 90-day window
  • Conditional license: maximum 6 months, renewable up to 12 successive months total; provisional license: maximum 6 months, renewals capped at 12 months total
  • Full license: up to three years, and the first full license after a conditional or provisional period is capped at one year
  • Corrective action plan due within 15 business days of a licensing report, with one extension of up to 10 additional business days if requested before the due date
  • Children's residential initial applications not complete within 12 months are closed (12VAC35-46-20), and a six-month reapplication wait follows a denial under Code section 37.2-418.C

What are the requirements to open a group home in Virginia?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Virginia?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
