How to Start a Group Home in Rhode Island

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Rhode Island. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Rhode Island licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Rhode Island licenses

Assisted Living Residence, including Special Care Unit and Limited Health Services licenses

Licensed by: Rhode Island Department of Health (RIDOH), Center for Health Facilities Regulation. Serves: Seniors and other residents who need housing, meals, and services in a residential setting, including residents with dementia in special care units. Licensed under R.I. Gen. Laws chapter 23-17.4. The license has three parts on the RIDOH application: the base Assisted Living Residence license, a Special Care Unit license, and a Limited Health Services license. Basic assisted living is not a skilled nursing facility; it is designed to be the senior's home in a more secure and social environment that can provide meals and services. Special care and memory care communities focus daily life around residents with Alzheimer's disease or another form of dementia. A Limited Health Services license allows the community to provide more nursing services by appropriately trained clinical staff, and to care for residents on hospice who become bed bound (RIDOH assisted living page).

Residential Habilitation / Group Home for adults with developmental disabilities

Licensed by: Rhode Island Department of Behavioral Healthcare, Developmental Disabilities and Hospitals (BHDDH), Office of Licensure and Standards, Division of Developmental Disabilities. Serves: Adults with intellectual and developmental disabilities who need 24-hour residential support. Residential habilitation, or group home, is a service provided by a licensed DD provider: the person lives in a home owned by the provider with other adults with disabilities, gets help from staff all day every day as needed, has their own bedroom or shares a bedroom, and shares a bathroom, living room, and kitchen, and pays room and board (BHDDH DD Guidebook and DD Service Spotlight Cards). The BHDDH license application covers Residential Supports Services with bed capacity, Community Residence Support Service, Non-Congregate Residential Support Service, and Shared Living Arrangement Service. Eligibility runs through BHDDH Division of Developmental Disabilities approval and Medicaid Long-Term Services and Supports approval through the Department of Human Services.

Shared Living Arrangement and Whole Life Shared Living Arrangement

Licensed by: Rhode Island Department of Behavioral Healthcare, Developmental Disabilities and Hospitals (BHDDH), Division of Developmental Disabilities. Serves: Adults with intellectual and developmental disabilities living with a host or in a shared living setting. The Division of Developmental Disabilities names three residential services: Shared Living Arrangement, Whole Life Shared Living Arrangement, and Residential Habilitation or Group Home services. People who live in a Shared Living Arrangement, Whole Life Shared Living Arrangement, or Group Home pay rent and help with other home costs (BHDDH DD Guidebook). Detailed license terms for Shared Living were not printed beyond its listing as a Residential Supports Service on the BHDDH license application, so no fee or bed limit is listed.

How to start a group home in Rhode Island, step by step

  1. Developmental disabilities services: file the BHDDH Office of Licensure and Standards Application for License to Provide Services to Adults with Developmental Disabilities or Cognitive Disabilities, selecting Residential Supports Services and the setting type, and attach evidence of compliance with the licensure requirements in Section 1.5 of the BHDDH licensing procedure rules
  2. Assisted living: file the RIDOH Licensing Application for Assisted Living Residences, with the sections for the base ALR license and, if applicable, the Special Care Unit license or the Limited Health Services license

Rhode Island group home license fees and costs

  • Assisted Living Residence license, initial, change of ownership, or change of address: $330.00 plus $70.00 per licensed bed; $70 per bed for increases in existing capacity; no fee for a name change (RIDOH Assisted Living Residences application)
  • Assisted Living Residence Special Care Unit license: additional $600.00 (RIDOH application)
  • Assisted Living Residence Limited Health Services license: additional $600.00 (RIDOH application)

How long does it take in Rhode Island?

  • Assisted Living Residence increases in services provided may only be made one time per annual licensing period, and decreases require notice of 30 days or more prior to ceasing to offer a licensed service (RIDOH application)

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to open a group home in Rhode Island?

The figures Rhode Island publishes are these: Assisted Living Residence license, initial, change of ownership, or change of address: $330.00 plus $70.00 per licensed bed; $70 per bed for increases in existing capacity; no fee for a name change (RIDOH Assisted Living Residences application) Assisted Living Residence Special Care Unit license: additional $600.00 (RIDOH application) Assisted Living Residence Limited Health Services license: additional $600.00 (RIDOH application) The license fee is rarely the largest cost. The home itself, life safety and accessibility work, insurance, and staffing hired before the first placement carry the project.

How long does it take to get a group home license in Rhode Island?

Assisted Living Residence increases in services provided may only be made one time per annual licensing period, and decreases require notice of 30 days or more prior to ceasing to offer a licensed service (RIDOH application)

What are the requirements to open a group home in Rhode Island?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Rhode Island?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
