How to Start a Home Health Agency in Arizona

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Arizona licenses home health agencies as a Home health agency license (health care institution license), through the Arizona Department of Health Services (ADHS), Bureau of Medical Facilities Licensing. The governing law is A.R.S. 36-151; A.R.S. 36-425.01; Arizona Administrative Code Title 9, Chapter 10, Article 12 (R9-10-1201 to 1211); general licensing at A.R.S. 36-422 and 36-425 and AAC R9-10-102(A)(19), R9-10-105, R9-10-108. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the Arizona license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The Arizona license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Arizona, step by step

  1. File the health care institution application through the ADHS Licensing Management System (LMS) at least 60 but not more than 120 days before the anticipated date of operation; ADHS no longer accepts paper forms
  2. Complete the application contents under A.R.S. 36-422 and R9-10-105, including owner and controlling person disclosures, governing authority, chief administrative officer, proposed scope of services, and the application fee
  3. File the Article 12 supplemental application (R9-10-1202): branch office names and addresses, geographic region served, and a fingerprint clearance card copy for the applicant or for each individual with 10 percent or greater ownership
  4. Set up administration under R9-10-1203: governing authority sets the written scope of services and administrator qualifications and designates the administrator in writing, an advisory group of at least 4 members including a physician is in place, and a home health services director is designated in writing
  5. Meet personnel requirements under R9-10-1206, including verification of qualifications before services, tuberculosis evidence on or before the first day for personnel with direct patient interaction, and fingerprint clearance under A.R.S. 36-411 where it applies
  6. Notify ADHS the institution is ready for licensing inspection; the application packet is not complete until that notice, and ADHS inspects on a properly completed application
  7. Receive the license after inspection and fee payment and post it in the reception area; the initial licensing fee is due within 60 calendar days after the written approval notice
  8. For Medicare, enroll separately through PECOS on the CMS-855A to the Medicare Administrative Contractor (Noridian Healthcare Solutions, Jurisdiction F) and complete the certification survey by ADHS as state survey agency or by a CMS-approved accreditation organization (ACHC, CHAP, The Joint Commission)
  9. For Medicaid, enroll separately through the AHCCCS Provider Enrollment Portal (APEP), sign the Provider Participation Agreement, and complete the APEP checklist and document uploads; registration alone does not produce ALTCS members, which requires contracting with the ALTCS program contractor for the county

Arizona home health license fees and costs

  • Application fee: $50, nonrefundable (R9-10-106(A))
  • Annual licensing fee, home health agency: $482 (Table 1.3; no licensed capacity, so the per-capacity addition does not apply to an agency without beds)
  • Late payment fee: $250 (R9-10-106(D))
  • AHCCCS and federal institutional provider enrollment fee: $750 for calendar year 2026, also collected at revalidation and reactivation
  • Home health agency surety bond of at least $50,000 is required under 42 CFR 489.67 (a bond requirement, not a state fee)

How long does it take in Arizona?

  • Overall licensing time-frame: 120 calendar days (30 days administrative completeness review plus 90 days substantive review) under R9-10-108 and Table 1.1
  • File at least 60 but not more than 120 days before the anticipated date of operation (A.R.S. 36-422)
  • ADHS must approve or deny within 90 days after receiving a complete initial application (A.R.S. 36-425(L)(1))
  • Initial licensing fee due within 60 calendar days after the written approval notice, or within 90 days with the late fee (R9-10-107)
  • The health care institution license does not expire and remains valid unless revoked, suspended, surrendered, or void for nonpayment; annual fees apply, with compliance inspection at least annually and no repeat survey for 24 months for a deficiency-free facility (A.R.S. 36-425(C), (D), (E))
  • Provisional license of not more than 1 year, with no consecutive provisional licenses (A.R.S. 36-425(G))
  • AHCCCS APEP application must be completed within 30 calendar days once started
  • Direct Care Workers serving ALTCS members must pass the required standardized competency tests within 90 days of hire

What catches Arizona applicants

  • No certificate of need is required for any class in this build; no facility certificate of need exists in A.R.S. Title 36 Chapter 4 or 9 A.A.C. 10
  • State license, Medicare certification, and AHCCCS (Medicaid) enrollment are separate doors; the state license comes first, then PECOS enrollment and the certification survey
  • Arizona has no state license for a non-medical or personal care home care agency; the only home-delivered license classes are the home health agency and the hospice service agency, and non-medical agencies serving ALTCS members register with AHCCCS and meet the Direct Care Worker standards instead
  • A licensed home health agency may include supportive services, including personal care services, in its scope under R9-10-1211; private duty nursing as a standalone agency type has no ADHS class and runs on the home health agency license
  • For ALTCS HCBS, a home health agency serving members must be Medicare-certified and licensed under 9 A.A.C. 10 (AAC R9-28-504)
  • Fingerprinting under A.R.S. 36-411 names residential care institutions, nursing care institutions, and home health agencies; hospice service agencies are not named in that statute as written
  • One license per facility; no separate license is required for an HHA off-site home services or an administrative office (R9-10-103)

The Medicare layer, after the Arizona license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a home health agency in Arizona?

The figures Arizona publishes are these: Application fee: $50, nonrefundable (R9-10-106(A)) Annual licensing fee, home health agency: $482 (Table 1.3; no licensed capacity, so the per-capacity addition does not apply to an agency without beds) Late payment fee: $250 (R9-10-106(D)) AHCCCS and federal institutional provider enrollment fee: $750 for calendar year 2026, also collected at revalidation and reactivation Home health agency surety bond of at least $50,000 is required under 42 CFR 489.67 (a bond requirement, not a state fee) Beyond the published figures, the operating costs (clinical payroll before certification, insurance, and the reserve operating funds required by 42 CFR 489.28) are larger than every fee on the list.

How long does it take to get a home health license in Arizona?

Overall licensing time-frame: 120 calendar days (30 days administrative completeness review plus 90 days substantive review) under R9-10-108 and Table 1.1 File at least 60 but not more than 120 days before the anticipated date of operation (A.R.S. 36-422) ADHS must approve or deny within 90 days after receiving a complete initial application (A.R.S. 36-425(L)(1)) Initial licensing fee due within 60 calendar days after the written approval notice, or within 90 days with the late fee (R9-10-107) The health care institution license does not expire and remains valid unless revoked, suspended, surrendered, or void for nonpayment; annual fees apply, with compliance inspection at least annually and no repeat survey for 24 months for a deficiency-free facility (A.R.S. 36-425(C), (D), (E)) Provisional license of not more than 1 year, with no consecutive provisional licenses (A.R.S. 36-425(G)) AHCCCS APEP application must be completed within 30 calendar days once started Direct Care Workers serving ALTCS members must pass the required standardized competency tests within 90 days of hire The federal certification that follows runs on the survey calendar, and new Medicare enrollment is under a CMS moratorium to about mid-May 2027.

What do I need to start a home health agency in Arizona?

The Home health agency license (health care institution license) (Arizona Department of Health Services (ADHS), Bureau of Medical Facilities Licensing), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet Arizona rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in Arizona?

Apply to the Arizona Department of Health Services (ADHS), Bureau of Medical Facilities Licensing under A.R.S. 36-151; A.R.S. 36-425.01; Arizona Administrative Code Title 9, Chapter 10, Article 12 (R9-10-1201 to 1211); general licensing at A.R.S. 36-422 and 36-425 and AAC R9-10-102(A)(19), R9-10-105, R9-10-108. The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
