How to Start a Group Home in Ohio

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Ohio. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Ohio licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Ohio licenses

DODD licensed residential facility

Licensed by: Ohio Department of Developmental Disabilities (DODD). Serves: People with developmental disabilities in non-ICF group homes and residential facilities. License under ORC 5123.19, rules at OAC Chapter 5123:2-3; statewide licensed bed cap under ORC 5123.196 (10,838 minus beds lost and beds converted to supported living since July 1, 2003); DODD guidance states capacity has been reached and new licenses are not being issued, with relocation of an existing license and acquisition of an unused license as the remaining routes

ICF/IID

Licensed by: DODD license, Ohio Department of Health certification of federal compliance findings, Ohio Department of Medicaid provider agreement. Serves: People with developmental disabilities needing intermediate care. Included in the residential facility license at ORC 5123.19(A)(5)(a); Medicaid participation at OAC 5123-7-02 requires the DODD license, ODH certification findings, a minimum of four certified beds, and an ODM provider agreement; federal rules at 42 CFR Part 483 Subpart I and Part 442; Medicaid only, no Medicare door

DODD supported living and waiver group living

Licensed by: DODD certification, Ohio Department of Medicaid PNM enrollment. Serves: People with developmental disabilities on the Individual Options, Level One, and SELF waivers living in provider-owned or arranged homes that are not licensed facilities. Supported living certificate under ORC 5123.161 et seq. and provider certification under ORC 5123.045 and OAC 5123-2-08 and 5123-2-09; waiver services at OAC Chapter 5123-9; PNM provider types 45 (organization) and 55 (individual); no facility license

Ohio Shared Living

Licensed by: DODD, Individual Options waiver service. Serves: Adults with developmental disabilities living in a caregiver home. Adult foster style waiver service delivered in the caregiver home; registered inside the supported living track, not a separate license

DBH Class 1 residential facility

Licensed by: Ohio Department of Behavioral Health (DBH, formerly OhioMHAS, renamed October 1, 2025). Serves: Adults with mental illness, or children and adolescents with severe emotional disturbances. License under ORC 5119.34(B)(1)(a) and OAC Chapter 5122-30, providing accommodations, supervision, personal care, and mental health services; a Class 1 facility accepting children for placement also carries the QRTP overlay at OAC 5122-30-32

DBH Class 2 residential facility

Licensed by: Ohio Department of Behavioral Health (DBH). Serves: The Ohio small adult group home: 1 to 2 persons with mental illness, 1 to 2 Residential State Supplement adults, or 3 to 16 unrelated adults. License under ORC 5119.34(B)(1)(b) and OAC Chapter 5122-30; the primary living arrangement for the Residential State Supplement program under ORC 5119.41; former adult family homes (3 to 5 adults) and adult group homes (6 to 16 adults) under former ORC Chapter 3722 now route here where they meet the Class 2 definition

DBH Class 3 residential facility

Licensed by: Ohio Department of Behavioral Health (DBH). Serves: Five or more unrelated adults with mental illness needing room and board. License under ORC 5119.34(B)(1)(c) and OAC Chapter 5122-30, room and board only class

Substance use disorder residential treatment and withdrawal management

Licensed by: Ohio Department of Behavioral Health (DBH) certification. Serves: People receiving residential SUD treatment at ASAM levels. Certification of community behavioral health services under ORC 5119.36, residential and withdrawal management standards at OAC 5122-29-09, certification fees at OAC 5122-25-08, and national accreditation required by ORC 5119.36; certification, not a 5122-30 facility license

Recovery housing residence

Licensed by: Ohio Department of Behavioral Health (DBH). Serves: People in recovery living in certified sober living residences. ORC 5119.391 to 5119.397; DBH certification under OAC Chapter 5122-31 effective August 10, 2026; registry at ORC 5119.394, advertising bar at 5119.395, referral bar at 5119.396; expressly not subject to residential facility licensure under ORC 5119.34; operation, advertising, and referral bars for uncertified residences apply from January 1, 2025

Group home for children

Licensed by: Ohio Department of Children and Youth (DCY). Serves: 10 or fewer children in a certified group home. Certification under ORC 5103.05(A)(6), agency certification functions at OAC 5180:2-5-03, residential standards at OAC Chapter 5180:2-9; staffing ratio floor at ORC 5103.0520 is at least one staff per seven children, or per six where the home accepts fewer than seven

Children's residential center

Licensed by: Ohio Department of Children and Youth (DCY). Serves: 11 or more children in a certified residential center. Certification under ORC 5103.05(A)(1) in the same DCY system as group homes; the QRTP designation at OAC 5180:2-9-42 is a federal Title IV-E overlay requiring accreditation by CARF, The Joint Commission, or COA, a trauma-informed model, and nursing and clinical staff, not a separate license

Children's crisis care facility

Licensed by: Ohio Department of Children and Youth (DCY). Serves: Preteens in crisis or emergency placement. Certificate under ORC 5103.13; no more than 120 days of residential care per preteen per calendar year

Residential parenting facility

Licensed by: Ohio Department of Children and Youth (DCY). Serves: Teenage mothers and their children residing together. Certification under ORC 5103.05(A)(9); residential infant care centers under ORC 5103.602 are registered inside this track

Certified foster family home

Licensed by: DCY system, agency-recommended certification. Serves: Children placed in a certified foster family home. Certification under the ORC 5103.03 system with standards at OAC Chapter 5180:2-7; a foster home is not a residential facility (ORC 5103.05(A)(8)); certification track with no facility license of its own

Residential care facility (assisted living)

Licensed by: Ohio Department of Health (ODH). Serves: Adults needing residential care and assisted living services. License under ORC Chapter 3721 and OAC Chapter 3701-16 on the same ORC 3721.02(E) fee schedule as nursing homes; home for the aging is a third Chapter 3721 category on the same schedule; Assisted Living Waiver and PASSPORT door runs through Ohio Department of Aging certification under ORC 173.391

Nursing home

Licensed by: Ohio Department of Health (ODH). Serves: Residents needing skilled nursing home care. License under ORC Chapter 3721 and OAC Chapter 3701-17; Medicare SNF and Medicaid NF certification under 42 CFR Part 483 with CMS-855A enrollment through PECOS, Ohio MAC CGS Administrators Jurisdiction 15

Private psychiatric hospital

Licensed by: Ohio Department of Behavioral Health (DBH). Serves: Patients receiving inpatient psychiatric hospital care. License under ORC 5119.33 and OAC Chapter 5122-14; full license term is one year and an interim license is 90 days; Medicare psychiatric hospital conditions at 42 CFR Part 482 Subpart E where the hospital seeks Medicare payment

How to start a group home in Ohio, step by step

  1. Confirm licensed bed capacity with DODD before committing funds: ORC 5123.196 caps total licensed beds by formula and DODD guidance states capacity has been reached, so a new license is not a routine filing and relocation or acquisition of an existing license may be the only routes
  2. Apply for the DODD residential facility license under ORC 5123.19 and OAC Chapter 5123:2-3 for the specific facility and bed count
  3. Pay the license fee for the bed count and term (see gh_fees)
  4. Operate on the initial license term of up to one year, then renew for a term of up to three years under ORC 5123.19
  5. For ICF/IID operation, add the Medicaid layer: ODH certification of federal compliance findings, a minimum of four certified beds, and an ODM provider agreement under OAC 5123-7-02 and 42 CFR Part 483 Subpart I and Part 442
  6. For unlicensed waiver homes instead of a facility, use the supported living route: provider certification under ORC 5123.045 and OAC 5123-2-08 or 5123-2-09, application through the PSM system, Director of Operations orientation, submission of documents inside the 30-day document window described by county board provider pages, an ODM site visit, and PNM enrollment as provider type 45 or 55

Ohio group home license fees and costs

  • DODD residential facility license, initial or one-year, 15 or fewer beds: $100; 16 or more beds: $500 (ORC 5123.19(G)(3) and OAC 5123:2-3-06(J)(3)(b), per the Legislative Service Commission DODD Agency Fees inventory of August 20, 2025)
  • DODD residential facility license, three-year renewal, 15 or fewer beds: $300; 16 or more beds: $1,500 (OAC 5123:2-3-06(J)(3)(a))
  • DODD residential facility interim license: $0 (ORC 5123.19(Q) and OAC 5123:2-3-08)
  • Supported living certificate and provider certification, independent provider: $125 initial and $125 renewal on a three-year term, $25 to add a service; small agency serving 50 or fewer individuals: $800 initial and renewal, $75 to add a service; large agency serving 51 or more: $1,600 initial and renewal, $150 to add a service
  • DBH residential facility license, Class 1: $150, non-refundable and non-waivable, full license three years; Class 2 or Class 3: $75, non-refundable and non-waivable, full license two years (OAC 5122-30-04(A)(1)(e) and 5122-30-05(A)(2))
  • DBH community behavioral health certification for residential and inpatient SUD services and each listed high-risk service: $200 per service with a $1,000 minimum for services that are not accredited, non-refundable; all other services $100 per service with no minimum (OAC 5122-25-08(C))
  • Recovery housing DBH certification: $15 per bed, non-refundable and non-waivable (OAC 5122-31-03, effective August 10, 2026); Ohio Recovery Housing certification, the accreditor's own schedule reported in provider sources, is $800 application plus $250 per house over a two-year cycle and is not a state fee
  • Nursing home and residential care facility license application and annual renewal: $320 for each 50 persons or part thereof of licensed capacity (ORC 3721.02(E)(1)(c)); nursing home change of operator $3,200 and expedited initial inspection $2,250 (OAC 3701-17-03)
  • Private psychiatric hospital annual license fee: $750 for 25 or fewer beds, $1,350 for 26 to 50, $1,650 for 51 to 75, $1,950 for 76 to 100, $2,250 for over 100 (OAC 5122-14-08(D))
  • DCY agency and facility certification (group home, residential center, crisis care, parenting facility, foster home): no application fee figure is published in ORC 5103.05, ORC 5103.13, or OAC 5180:2-5-03 as read on 2026-11-12, and the research quotes none
  • Federal institutional provider enrollment fee: $750 for calendar year 2026

How long does it take in Ohio?

  • DODD residential facility license: initial term up to one year, renewal term up to three years (ORC 5123.19)
  • Nursing home initial application: not less than 45 days before the proposed opening under the current OAC 3701-17-03 text on codes.ohio.gov, with six months of operating capital or reserve, renewal in January, and a $100 per week late fee; residential care facility initial application: not less than 60 days before the proposed opening (OAC 3701-16-03), renewal in January, $100 per week late fee
  • DBH residential facilities: on-site inspection before occupancy and license before admission (OAC 5122-30-04)
  • Children's crisis care: no more than 120 days of residential care per preteen per calendar year (ORC 5103.13)
  • Group home staffing ratios: ORC 5103.0520, effective September 30, 2025, directs DCY to adopt ratio rules within 270 days; the statutory floor in the research is one staff per seven children, or per six in a home accepting fewer than seven
  • Recovery housing rules at OAC Chapter 5122-31 took effect August 10, 2026 as an emergency filing running up to 120 days, to about December 8, 2026, while DBH writes permanent rules

What are the requirements to open a group home in Ohio?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Ohio?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
