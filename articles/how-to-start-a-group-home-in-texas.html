How to Start a Group Home in Texas

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Texas. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Texas licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Texas licenses

HCS residential, supervised living and residential support services in 3- and 4-person residences

Licensed by: HHSC HCS Medicaid 1915(c) waiver; 26 TAC Chapter 263 and Chapter 565; provider contract under 26 TAC Chapter 52. Serves: HCS waiver participants in 3- and 4-person residences. Waiver contract and PEMS enrollment, not a facility license; Section 52.31 requires Provider Applicant Training and a competency exam

HCS host home / companion care

Licensed by: HHSC HCS waiver and contract system; 26 TAC Chapters 52, 263 and 565. Serves: HCS participants living in the host provider home. Enrollment track, no own license; the HCS program provider holds the contract and the provider number

ICF/IID group homes and facilities

Licensed by: HHSC; Health and Safety Code Chapter 252, 26 TAC Chapter 551. Serves: Individuals with intellectual or developmental disabilities. Medicaid certification under 42 CFR Part 483 Subpart I and Part 442; Medicaid bed allocation required under 26 TAC 261.220; Medicaid only, no Medicare door

General Residential Operation (GRO), including Residential Treatment Center class

Licensed by: HHSC Child Care Regulation; Human Resources Code Chapter 42, 26 TAC Chapter 748. Serves: 7 or more children 17 and younger in 24-hour residential child care. RTC is a GRO class under Section 748.5; GRO services include treatment services, emergency care, therapeutic camps and transitional living; at least one unannounced CCR inspection per year

Child-Placing Agency (CPA)

Licensed by: HHSC Child Care Regulation; Human Resources Code Chapter 42, 26 TAC Chapter 749. Serves: Children placed in foster homes verified by the agency. An agency license, not a residence; a CPA places children and verifies foster homes

CPA foster group home, 7 to 12 children

Licensed by: Verification through a licensed CPA under 26 TAC Chapter 749. Serves: 7 to 12 children in a foster group home. Verification track, no own state license; distinct from the CPA foster family home of 6 or fewer children, which is a register entry inside the CPA and foster group home tracks

Adult Foster Care home

Licensed by: HHSC; 26 TAC Chapter 278 Subchapter B; HHSC Community Care Services Eligibility Handbook Section 4100, Revision 25-2, effective March 31, 2025. Serves: Up to 3 adults without licensure in an enrolled foster home; small group home variant up to 8 adults. Enrolled, no license for up to 3 adults; the provider lives in the household and is the primary caregiver; a 4th person requires an assisted living license, Type A or Type B for a new operator because HHSC no longer issues the legacy Type C named in the handbook

Mental health community residential services

Licensed by: HHSC community mental health standards, 26 TAC Chapter 301 Subchapter G, delivered or contracted through Local Mental Health Authorities and Local Behavioral Health Authorities. Serves: People served through LMHA and LBHA contracts, including crisis residential. Contract enrollment track, no own facility license; Texas has no standalone facility license for a freestanding outpatient or community mental health residential program, a proven negative in the research

Substance use residential, residential treatment and residential detoxification / withdrawal management

Licensed by: HHSC; Health and Safety Code Chapter 464, Chemical Dependency Treatment Facility license, 26 TAC Chapter 564. Serves: People needing residential chemical dependency treatment or detoxification. One license per physical location under 26 TAC 564.401; rules moved from 25 TAC Chapter 448 on April 30, 2024; sober living and recovery residences have no Texas license, certification or registration for non-clinical residences, a register entry inside this track

Psychiatric Residential Youth Treatment Facility (PRYTF) certification

Licensed by: HHSC; Health and Safety Code Chapter 577A. Serves: Youth in a GRO licensed setting. A voluntary HHSC certification on top of a GRO license, not a license itself; register entry inside the GRO track

Nursing Facility

Licensed by: HHSC; Health and Safety Code Chapter 242, 26 TAC Chapter 554. Serves: Residents needing skilled nursing facility care. Medicare SNF under 42 CFR Part 483 Subpart B and Part 488 on CMS-855A; Medicaid NF through HHSC CAPM and PEMS; Medicaid bed allocation required

Day Activity and Health Services (DAHS) facility, including Individualized Skills and Socialization (ISS)

Licensed by: HHSC; Human Resources Code Chapter 103, 26 TAC Chapter 559, ISS at Subchapter H. Serves: 4 or more adults in a daytime program. Daytime, not overnight

Prescribed Pediatric Extended Care Center (PPECC)

Licensed by: HHSC; Health and Safety Code Chapter 248A, 26 TAC Chapter 550. Serves: 4 or more medically or technologically dependent minors in nonresidential day care. Nonresidential day care; requires a $250,000 letter of credit from an FDIC-insured bank and administrator pre-licensing computer based training under 26 TAC 550.105 and 550.305(b)

Private psychiatric hospital and Crisis Stabilization Unit

Licensed by: HHSC; Health and Safety Code Chapter 577, 26 TAC Chapter 510. Serves: Patients needing private psychiatric hospital or crisis stabilization care. CSU is licensed under the same chapter; physician in charge must have 3 years of psychiatry experience in a mental hospital or board certification under HSC 577.008

How to start a group home in Texas, step by step

  1. For the main group home track, HCS residential: confirm HCS is a waiver contract door, not a license; complete Provider Applicant Training online plus the in-person competency exam under 26 TAC 52.31
  2. Meet the HCS program manager standard in the HHSC source material: program manager resume with at least 3 years of HCS or similar experience and a competency exam score of at least 90 percent, with 3 reference letters and criminal history receipts
  3. File through the CAMP portal using the Form 5873 checklist and Form 2031, with NPI and EIN documents and entity documents by form; HCS open enrollment is year-round and noncompetitive on the HHSC How to Become an HCS Provider page read November 12, 2026
  4. Complete PEMS enrollment through TMHP under the TMHP HCS Enrollment Checklist revised February 12, 2025: practice locations are added individually, each with a site visit, and an application fee is required for a first HCS enrollment and for revalidation, with the current amount directed to tmhp.com
  5. Operate the residence as a 3- or 4-person residence delivering supervised living or residential support services under 26 TAC Chapter 565, or as host home / companion care where the person lives in the host provider home
  6. For ICF/IID as the alternative licensed group home route: obtain a Medicaid bed allocation under 26 TAC 261.220 first, file in TULIP with ownership disclosure, complete Life Safety Code and health inspections, and obtain Licensure and Credentialing Unit approval, then contract for Medicaid through HHSC CAPM

Texas group home license fees and costs

  • HCS provider contract: no HHSC contract application fee is published on the HHSC How to Become an HCS Provider page or in 26 TAC 52.31 as read; packets quote none for the contract itself
  • HCS PEMS enrollment: an application fee is required for a first HCS enrollment and for revalidation per the TMHP checklist, which directs the filer to tmhp.com for the current amount; the research prints the federal figure of $750 for calendar year 2026 and never a Texas-invented amount
  • ICF/IID initial and renewal (3-year): $225 plus $7.50 per unit of capacity; capacity increase $7.50 per unit approved; change of administrator $20
  • GRO: application fee $35 nonrefundable; initial license $35; annual license fee $35 plus $1 per child of licensed capacity (Human Resources Code 42.054)
  • CPA: application fee $35 nonrefundable; initial license $50; annual license fee $100 (Human Resources Code 42.054)
  • Foster group home verification: no separate state fee for CPA verification is published in HRC 42.054 or Chapter 749 as read; packets quote no verification fee
  • Adult Foster Care enrollment: no enrollment fee figure is published in 26 TAC Chapter 278 Subchapter B or the HHSC AFC handbook sections read; packets quote none
  • Mental health residential (LMHA contract): no facility license exists and no enrollment fee figure is published in the sources read; packets quote none
  • CDTF license: base $1,200 plus $125 per site plus $35 per bed (residential beds), 2-year term (26 TAC 564.408 and 564.404)
  • Nursing facility initial and renewal (3-year standard): $375 plus $15 per bed; probationary (1-year, never licensed in Texas) $125 plus $5 per bed; Alzheimer certification $300 per 3-year certification; background information fee $50; change of administrator $20
  • DAHS license: $75 for a 3-year license; $50 for a 2-year license; $25 late fee
  • PPECC initial, renewal, change of ownership and relocation: $2,625; capacity increase $1,312.50; late renewal $50 per day, maximum $500
  • Private psychiatric hospital / CSU license: $200 per bed per 24 months, minimum $6,000 per 24 months (26 TAC 510.26(b)(1))

How long does it take in Texas?

  • ICF/IID: review up to 30 days, deficient items answered within 30 days, license within 45 days of a complete application with passing surveys, per the HHSC ICF/IID provider page
  • Nursing facility: application 60 days before anticipated opening; change of ownership 30 days ahead with a 90-day temporary license; renewal 45 days ahead, late fee half the basic fee
  • DAHS: initial application 45 days before the building is expected to conform; change of ownership 30 days; renewal 45 days
  • PPECC: renewal 60 days ahead
  • CDTF: completeness review 45 days, inspection 45 days, application held 6 months then denied with a 6-month wait to reapply (26 TAC 564.403); two year term, renewal at least 60 days before expiration
  • HCS provider enrollment: open enrollment year-round, noncompetitive, per the HHSC page read November 12, 2026

What are the requirements to open a group home in Texas?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Texas?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
