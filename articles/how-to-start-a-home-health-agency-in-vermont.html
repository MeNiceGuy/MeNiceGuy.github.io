How to Start a Home Health Agency in Vermont

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Vermont designates home health agencies rather than licensing them. The Department of Disabilities, Aging and Independent Living (DAIL), Division of Licensing and Protection, designates agencies under 33 V.S.A. sections 6301 to 6304, and the sequence the state publishes runs Certificate of Need first (Green Mountain Care Board), then Medicare certification, then DAIL designation, in four year terms. This page follows that order, then the federal layer inside it.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The state-side entry work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Vermont, step by step

  1. Apply for and obtain a Certificate of Need from the Green Mountain Care Board prior to filing an application for designation
  2. Obtain and maintain federal CMS (Medicare) certification as a home health agency
  3. Apply for and obtain designation by DAIL for a defined geographic service area

How long does it take in Vermont?

  • Designation of home health agencies runs in 4-year terms under 33 V.S.A. section 6304
  • Revised Regulations for the Designation and Operation of Home Health Agencies took effect July 1, 2026

What catches Vermont applicants

  • Vermont designates home health agencies by geographic service area rather than licensing them on an open-entry basis; no agency may render home health services in an area without being designated for that area
  • Designation runs in 4-year terms under 33 V.S.A. section 6304
  • A Certificate of Need is required before designation, so a state approval step comes before operation; Medicare certification must be obtained and maintained before designation under the designation rules as summarized from the official rule text
  • Unskilled services, meaning personal care, respite, companionship, and homemaker services, are not home health or hospice services; they are non-medical services provided by unlicensed individuals through the Medicaid Choices for Care program (testimony filed with the Legislative Committee on Administrative Rules, legislature.vermont.gov)
  • No designation fee was printed on the official pages reviewed, so no fee is listed here; verification of the designation sequence rests on the official rule citations as summarized in state filings, and the full 2026 rule PDF was not directly readable in this pass, so this section is thinner than the other states

The Medicare layer

With the state-side entry handled and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get a home health license in Vermont?

Designation of home health agencies runs in 4-year terms under 33 V.S.A. section 6304 Revised Regulations for the Designation and Operation of Home Health Agencies took effect July 1, 2026 The federal certification that follows runs on the survey calendar, and new Medicare enrollment is under a CMS moratorium to about mid-May 2027.

What do I need to start a home health agency in Vermont?

The Home Health Agency designation (Vermont designates home health agencies rather than issuing a conventional license) (Vermont Department of Disabilities, Aging and Independent Living (DAIL), Division of Licensing and Protection, for designation; Green Mountain Care Board, for Certificate of Need), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet Vermont rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in Vermont?

Apply to the Vermont Department of Disabilities, Aging and Independent Living (DAIL), Division of Licensing and Protection, for designation; Green Mountain Care Board, for Certificate of Need under 33 V.S.A. sections 6301 to 6304; Regulations for the Designation and Operation of Home Health Agencies, effective July 1, 2026; Certificate of Need under 18 V.S.A. section 9434 as cited for home health services. The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
