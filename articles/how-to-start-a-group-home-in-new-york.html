How to Start a Group Home in New York

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in New York. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type New York licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types New York licenses

Individualized Residential Alternative (IRA), also called a group home

Licensed by: Office for People With Developmental Disabilities (OPWDD). Serves: People with developmental disabilities determined eligible by OPWDD. Small, community-based homes certified by OPWDD. Supervised IRAs provide 24/7 support, typically support 3 to 8 people per house but can go up to 14 residents, and help with daily living, meal preparation, medication management and community integration. Supportive IRAs are staffed based on individual needs for people who need less than 24-hour supervision. OPWDD glossary: an IRA is a certified home for 1 to 14 people that provides room, board and individualized protective oversight, and is a type of Community Residence.

Family Care Home

Licensed by: OPWDD. Serves: People with developmental disabilities who enjoy living in a family home and require daily support that cannot be accommodated living on their own. A certified residential opportunity where the person lives with another family in their home; the Family Care Provider offers support, guidance and companionship in a stable home environment, receives training and background checks, the home undergoes a complete safety inspection, and the provider is reimbursed for room and board and residential habilitation.

Intermediate Care Facility (ICF)

Licensed by: OPWDD. Serves: People with developmental disabilities and higher support needs who may require intermittent skilled nursing care. Provides 24/7 higher level residential services, comprehensive and individualized supportive care and habilitative services, and bundled clinical services including medical nursing, psychological, nutritional, occupational, physical and recreation, with 24/7 direct support staff.

Community Residence (CR)

Licensed by: OPWDD. Serves: People with developmental disabilities in a semi-independent living situation. OPWDD glossary: a certified semi-independent living situation with two types, Supervised with staffing available 24 hours, and Supportive limited to three people with staff support varying by individual need. Both are referred to as Congregate Care Level II by Social Security.

Adult Home (Adult Care Facility)

Licensed by: New York State Department of Health, Division of Adult Care Facility and Assisted Living Surveillance. Serves: Adults residing in adult care facilities. Adult homes, enriched housing programs and residences for adults are together called Adult Care Facilities or ACFs in NYSDOH material, citing 18 NYCRR section 487.7 for adult homes, section 488.7 for enriched housing, and section 490.7 for residences for adults. Adult Care Facilities are licensed under Article 7 of the Social Services Law, per NYSDOH correspondence fetched.

Enriched Housing Program (Adult Care Facility)

Licensed by: New York State Department of Health. Serves: Adults in enriched housing programs. One of the three Adult Care Facility types named by NYSDOH with adult homes and residences for adults, under 18 NYCRR Part 488.

Residence for Adults (Adult Care Facility)

Licensed by: New York State Department of Health. Serves: Adults in residences for adults. One of the three Adult Care Facility types named by NYSDOH, under 18 NYCRR Part 490.

Assisted Living Residence (ALR), including Enhanced and Special Needs ALR

Licensed by: New York State Department of Health. Serves: Adults who need assisted living services. Assisted living services are provided in accordance with Article 46-B of the Public Health Law, as referenced in NYSDOH Public Health Law material. NYSDOH needs assessments distinguish basic ALR, enhanced ALR and special needs ALR bed capacity within adult care facilities. An Assisted Living Program (ALP) is the related Medicaid program operating inside an adult care facility.

OMH Community Residence and Licensed Housing Programs

Licensed by: New York State Office of Mental Health (OMH), under Title 14 NYCRR. Serves: Adults, and children and adolescents, with mental illness or serious emotional disturbance, by program type. OMH licensed residential types listed by OMH: Apartment Support Community Residence and Apartment Treatment Community Residence (apartment in the community with staff visits); Congregate Support and Congregate Treatment Community Residence (single site group living, 3 meals per day, 24-hour supervision); Children and Youth Community Residence (group living for 6 to 8 children or adolescents in a supervised therapeutic environment); Crisis Residence (24-hour supervision, generally up to 30 days, to avoid hospitalization); Teaching Family Home (up to 4 children or adolescents with serious emotional disturbances in a family setting); and Service Enriched Single Room Occupancy.

How to start a group home in New York, step by step

For OPWDD certified residential opportunities, OPWDD states the access process starts by looking at the most integrated and least restrictive living arrangement that will meet the persons needs, including remaining in their own home or family home with additional supports, and the persons Care Manager helps walk them through the process
  2. No provider-side licensing step list, fee or timeline for OPWDD, adult care facility or OMH residential licensure was printed on the official pages fetched in this run, so no provider startup sequence is stated for New York group homes here

Frequently asked questions

What are the requirements to open a group home in New York?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in New York?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
