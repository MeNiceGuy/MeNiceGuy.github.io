How to Start a Home Health Agency in Indiana

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Indiana licenses home health agencies as a License to Operate a Home Health Agency (initial application State Form 4008; renewal State Form 48851), through the Indiana Department of Health (IDOH), Consumer Services and Healthcare Regulation Commission, Home Health Agency Licensing and Certification Program, Division of Home and Community-Based Care. The governing law is Indiana Code 16-27 (including IC 16-27-1-2 on skilled nursing and IC 16-27-2 provisions cited in the IDOH instruction letter) and 410 IAC 17 (including 410 IAC 17-11-3 on renewal and 410 IAC 17-12-1 on clinical supervision); Medicare Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR 484. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the Indiana license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The Indiana license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Indiana, step by step

  1. Complete the Application for License to Operate a Home Health Agency (State Form 4008) in its entirety and submit it to IDOH with supporting documents and the $250 non-refundable fee, including a cover letter, Secretary of State organizational documents, an IRS document showing the legal name and EIN, criminal history checks for the administrator, alternate administrator, nursing supervisor, alternate nursing supervisor and owners or officers, and resumes showing required qualifications
  2. IDOH receives and approves the initial application for licensure and the paperwork for initial certification
  3. IDOH issues the provisional license
  4. The provider notifies IDOH in writing when it has provided care to three patients and is ready for the initial licensure survey; IDOH notes the federal survey readiness standard is ten skilled patients with seven active at the time of survey if an initial Medicaid survey is done in tandem
  5. IDOH conducts the initial licensure survey; if there are no deficiencies, or a Plan of Correction is accepted and a Post Survey Revisit verifies corrections, a full license is issued
  6. For Medicare enrollment, a licensed agency applies through PECOS or by paper CMS-855A; the Medicare Administrative Contractor reviews the application and refers it to IDOH or the accrediting organization, and the agency also enrolls with Indiana Health Coverage Programs for Medicaid
  7. Submit the remaining certification documents IDOH must receive, including an approved CMS-855A, Health Insurance Benefits Agreement Form CMS-1561 and the Office of Civil Rights attestation of compliance, plus a survey from an accrediting organization with deemed status where that route is used
  8. IDOH assembles and forwards the completed initial certification survey, CMS-855A and certification forms to CMS and recommends certification if the survey meets the Conditions of Participation
  9. CMS sends a confirmation letter assigning a CMS Certification Number with the effective date of participation and grants final approval of Medicare certification

Indiana home health license fees and costs

  • $250 non-refundable license application fee for initial licensure, payable to IDOH
  • $250 non-refundable licensure fee for annual renewal (State Form 48851)

How long does it take in Indiana?

  • A home health agency license expires one year after the date of issuance of the initial license and must be renewed annually
  • Renewal application, documentation and the $250 fee must be submitted at least 60 days, but not sooner than 90 days, before the expiration date of the current license
  • Applicants have 30 days to provide corrections or additions requested by IDOH, and IDOH will only request corrections or additions three times before an incomplete application is denied (per the IDOH instruction letter)
  • FSSA announced June 26, 2025 that all Medicaid home health providers will need to be dually certified as Medicare and Medicaid providers by July 1, 2026 (per the IDOH instruction letter)

What catches Indiana applicants

  • Indiana has mandatory state licensure for home health agencies: an HHA must be licensed in Indiana as a home health agency in order to become a Medicare Certified Provider, per the IDOH Process of Initial Medicare Certification document
  • IDOH licenses agencies that provide nursing, physical therapy, occupational therapy, speech therapy, medical social services, home health aide and other therapeutic services at the patient residence, and it monitors agencies on behalf of CMS
  • Acceptance and clearance of the licensure fee check alone does not allow an applicant to begin admitting patients, per the IDOH instruction letter
  • The IDOH instruction letter and certification process documents list no certificate of need step for a home health agency
  • Separate non-medical track noted for context only: Indiana also licenses Personal Services Agencies under IC 16-27, which is a different license from the home health agency license

The Medicare layer, after the Indiana license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a home health agency in Indiana?

The figures Indiana publishes are these: $250 non-refundable license application fee for initial licensure, payable to IDOH $250 non-refundable licensure fee for annual renewal (State Form 48851) Beyond the published figures, the operating costs (clinical payroll before certification, insurance, and the reserve operating funds required by 42 CFR 489.28) are larger than every fee on the list.

How long does it take to get a home health license in Indiana?

A home health agency license expires one year after the date of issuance of the initial license and must be renewed annually Renewal application, documentation and the $250 fee must be submitted at least 60 days, but not sooner than 90 days, before the expiration date of the current license Applicants have 30 days to provide corrections or additions requested by IDOH, and IDOH will only request corrections or additions three times before an incomplete application is denied (per the IDOH instruction letter) FSSA announced June 26, 2025 that all Medicaid home health providers will need to be dually certified as Medicare and Medicaid providers by July 1, 2026 (per the IDOH instruction letter) The federal certification that follows runs on the survey calendar, and new Medicare enrollment is under a CMS moratorium to about mid-May 2027.

What do I need to start a home health agency in Indiana?

The License to Operate a Home Health Agency (initial application State Form 4008; renewal State Form 48851) (Indiana Department of Health (IDOH), Consumer Services and Healthcare Regulation Commission, Home Health Agency Licensing and Certification Program, Division of Home and Community-Based Care), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet Indiana rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in Indiana?

Apply to the Indiana Department of Health (IDOH), Consumer Services and Healthcare Regulation Commission, Home Health Agency Licensing and Certification Program, Division of Home and Community-Based Care under Indiana Code 16-27 (including IC 16-27-1-2 on skilled nursing and IC 16-27-2 provisions cited in the IDOH instruction letter) and 410 IAC 17 (including 410 IAC 17-11-3 on renewal and 410 IAC 17-12-1 on clinical supervision); Medicare Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR 484. The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
