How to Start a Home Care Agency in Mississippi (No License for Non-Medical Care, a Certificate of Need Wall for Home Health)

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 19, 2026

The Mississippi story starts with a founder who found the license and still got it wrong. She searched the state site, found a personal care home license, and built her whole opening plan around applying for it. A personal care home in Mississippi is a residential facility, a place where clients live. It authorizes no in-home agency work, and no in-home agency needs it. Mississippi prints no license at all for an agency sending personal care workers into client homes. When owners come to me with a Mississippi plan, the first job is to separate the three doors that share vocabulary and nothing else: unlicensed non-medical care, licensed home health behind a certificate of need, and hospice on its own license with new licensure suspended by statute.

The licensing authority for the facility doors is the Mississippi State Department of Health, called MSDH. Medicaid is administered by the Mississippi Division of Medicaid, called DOM. Mississippi home-based care runs on those three doors, and no one door covers another door's services. Here is the full picture, lane by lane, in the order a founder actually meets it.

The demand: about 570,000 seniors, nearly one in five residents

Start with why owners keep looking at Mississippi. The Census Bureau puts the state population at 2,954,160 as of July 1, 2025, and 19.3 percent of residents are age 65 or older. Run the math and you get about 570,000 seniors, nearly one in five people in the state. The same Census QuickFacts page for Mississippi puts median household income at $56,447 for 2020 to 2024, the lowest income base in this batch of states, which is the honest frame for Mississippi pricing: the private pay market is thin, and the Medicaid waiver doors carry much of the real volume.

That income figure is why the sequencing in this article matters. A Mississippi founder who builds only for private pay is building for the smaller payer, and a founder who plans a skilled agency without testing the certificate of need posture first is planning a business the state gate may not let open. Both mistakes are common, and both are avoidable on day one.

The license question, answered directly: no Mississippi license exists for non-medical home care

Here is the searched question this article exists to answer. Do you need a license to start a non-medical home care agency in Mississippi? No state license exists to get. No MSDH chapter licenses an agency for sending personal care workers into client homes, no application is filed for private pay personal care, and no fee is published for a license that does not exist. The absence is the finding: the MSDH facility standards list home health agencies, hospices, personal care homes, and the facility types, and no in-home non-medical category appears. A private pay personal care agency forms as a business, carries its insurance and screening systems, and operates on the business record.

The regulated door for that same work is Medicaid. Personal care services are a covered service of the Elderly and Disabled Waiver at Title 23, Part 208, Rule 1.6, delivered by providers enrolled with the Division of Medicaid under the provider standards at Rule 1.3 of the same chapter, and personal care attendant services also run under the Independent Living Waiver and the Traumatic Brain Injury and Spinal Cord Injury Waiver, operated with the Mississippi Department of Rehabilitation Services. The residential models in the generator, personal care homes for residential living or assisted living, DMH supervised living settings, adult foster care, nursing facilities, ICF/IID settings, and the other all-homes tracks, each carry their own Mississippi credential. Checked negative, printed because owners budget against it: no DOM payer enrollment moratorium was located in the primary sources checked for the packet, and no Medicaid pause is printed for Mississippi. The state gates that do exist sit on the skilled tracks, and the next two sections name them.

The Medicaid lane on the unlicensed door: waiver enrollment under Rule 1.3

For Elderly and Disabled Waiver personal care, the provider standards at Title 23, Part 208, Rule 1.3 are the operating bar, and the packet carries the standards the summaries of that rule print: at least one year providing the service to private pay clients before enrollment, DOM orientation with a scored examination, fingerprint based background checks on a two year cycle, and the Nurse Aide Abuse Registry and exclusion checks. Rule 1.6 itself carries a supervision fact worth building around: the Elderly and Disabled Waiver personal care supervisor standard in that rule carries a twenty-PCA ceiling, a limit on how many personal care attendants one supervisor covers. That ceiling is Mississippi's own rule for this waiver service, and it sets the supervision hiring math for an agency growing a waiver book.

Enrollment runs under Title 23, Part 200, Chapter 4, with revalidation at least once every five years. Managed care runs through MississippiCAN, whose coordinated care organizations on the DOM comparison chart revised August 11, 2026 are Magnolia Health, Molina Healthcare, and TrueCare; plan lists naming UnitedHealthcare Community Plan are stale, because that plan exited MississippiCAN effective July 1, 2025. DOM enrollment carries the federal screening frame at Rule 4.2, including the provider application fee provision at 42 CFR 455.460 where it applies to the provider type; no separate Mississippi dollar figure for waiver personal care enrollment was located in the sources checked, and the packet prints none.

The skilled lane: the home health license behind the certificate of need gate

The moment your plan includes skilled nursing or therapy, you need the home health agency license under sections 41-71-1 and following and Title 15, Part 16, Chapter 46. The definition at Rule 46.3.20 is the scope line: skilled nursing provided by or under the supervision of a registered nurse licensed in Mississippi, plus one or more of the listed part-time intermittent services, in the individual place of residence, at the written direction of a licensed physician or another authorized practitioner the rule names.

The gate in front of the license is a certificate of need. Offering home health services that have not been provided on a regular basis by the proposed provider within the prior twelve months requires a certificate of need under section 41-7-191(1)(d)(ix), and the license application carries the certificate of need evidence with it. House Bill 1622 of the 2026 Regular Session carried legislative findings continuing the moratorium on home health agencies, among other facility types, and directs MSDH and the Division of Medicaid to report on capacity and need by December 1, 2026, and every second year after. A certificate of need is a standing gate, and the moratorium is the current state of that gate for new home health agencies; neither is a temporary processing pause. The certificate of need processing fee at Title 15, Part 9, Rule 9-91-3.11 is one half of one percent of the proposed capital expenditure, with a minimum fee of $5,000 and a maximum fee of $25,000, paid with the application and nonrefundable. That fee is the certificate of need fee, not a license fee: no home health license fee figure was located in the Chapter 46 text read for the packet, and guides that print a $500 home health license fee cite superseded rule numbering that does not appear in the current text. A new agency may hold a provisional license before opening, valid until a regular license issues or June 30 following issuance, whichever is sooner, under Rule 46.7.2, and the plan of treatment is signed and reviewed at least every sixty days under Rule 46.3.44. Medicare certification layers on this license through Form CMS-855A to Palmetto GBA, Jurisdiction M, with the CY2026 fee of $750 under 42 CFR Part 484, gated for initial enrollment by CMS-6101-N2 on the federal terms the next sections note.

The hospice lane: its own license, with new licensure suspended until the July 1, 2027 repeal

Hospice sits on its own license under sections 41-85-1 through 41-85-25 and Title 15, Part 16, Chapter 1, administered by MSDH under section 41-85-7. The fee is $1,000 for each site or location at application and at renewal, plus a fee of $18 per bed for the review of inpatient hospice care. The suspension that governs new entry is statutory and dated: section 41-85-7(3) directs that MSDH shall not process any new applications for hospice licensure or issue any new hospice licenses, except renewals, outside the closed windows that subsection prints, and the subsection stands repealed on July 1, 2027. Section 41-85-7(4) excepts a license reinstatement application from a hospice whose license was temporarily suspended after a federal audit that concluded without penalty. A new hospice planned today sequences against that repeal date and the renewal and acquisition doors, not against an open application window. The Medicare component runs under 42 CFR Part 418, gated for initial enrollment by CMS-6102-N2, effective November 12, 2026, with no calendar end date printed in the notice (the extension is a six-month period, running to about May 12, 2027).

Background checks and visit verification on the Mississippi doors

The personnel file on every Mississippi track carries the Nurse Aide Abuse Registry check through MSDH before hire and on the cycle the applicable standards set, OIG and SAM exclusion checks before hire and monthly, and the criminal history and fingerprint record where the door requires it; the Chapter 46 fingerprint processing fee is capped at fifty dollars. The waiver standards add fingerprint based background checks on a two year cycle for Elderly and Disabled Waiver personal care. Electronic visit verification is mandatory under Title 23, Part 200, Rule 1.14, effective December 1, 2025, for the personal care and home health services the rule lists, including Elderly and Disabled Waiver personal care and in-home respite, the Independent Living and Traumatic Brain Injury and Spinal Cord Injury attendant services, and State Plan home health. A provider uses the state provided EVV portal via HHAeXchange, or a DOM approved provider contracted solution that submits the collected data to the state EVV aggregator within fourteen days of collection. EVV exceptions left unworked past that fourteen day window are a named deficiency in the packet for a reason.

What you still do today: form the business the Mississippi way

On the unlicensed door, formation is the filing: form the business with the Mississippi Secretary of State, hold the EIN and the tax registrations, and put the operating accounts and insurance in place, with no MSDH facility application filed for this door. Then build the screening and file systems before the first visit, the registry and exclusion checks, the competency file for every attendant, and the service agreements with the Plan of Services and Supports documented for each client. Run a full private pay year with clean records if Medicaid waiver work is the goal, because the Rule 1.3 standards ask for that year before enrollment. On the skilled tracks, formation is only the first step: test the certificate of need posture for the proposed counties before any other filing, and test the section 41-85-7(3) suspension posture before any hospice opening date is published.

The practical warning

Three Mississippi traps cost new owners money. The first is the name trap run in either direction: treating a personal care home license as an in-home credential, or presenting an in-home agency as licensed when no license exists for the door. The second is sequencing a home health filing before the certificate of need posture is tested, against the moratorium findings House Bill 1622 carried; the $5,000 minimum certificate of need fee is nonrefundable, and the older $500 minimum in the statute text at section 41-7-188 is not the operative figure, because the current rule sets the $5,000 minimum and the $25,000 maximum and the rule controls. The third is building a hospice plan as if new licensure were open, against the section 41-85-7(3) suspension that stands until its July 1, 2027 repeal. None of these gates is a processing delay. Each one decides whether the business model can open at all, and each one can be tested before real money moves.
