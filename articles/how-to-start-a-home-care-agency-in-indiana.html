How to Start a Home Care Agency in Indiana (Separate $250 Licenses, and a Waiver Enrollment Pause)

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 19, 2026

The Indiana story starts with a founder near Indianapolis who filed the wrong $250. She wanted to send caregivers into homes for bathing, meals, and companionship, and she filed for the license a search result called the Indiana home care license, which turned out to be the skilled home health license. Same department, same fee, wrong door, and a filing she had to redo. The first thing I tell owners looking at Indiana is that this state runs on separate licenses, not one door: a personal services agency license for non-medical care, a home health agency license for skilled care, and a hospice license, each with its own form. The second thing I tell them is newer: Indiana Medicaid is running a CMS-approved pause on new HCBS waiver enrollment, and it pauses the payer door only, never the licenses. Here is the full picture, lane by lane, in the order a founder actually meets it.

Indiana rewards the founder who sequences. License first, enrollment second, first claim last is not a slogan in this state, it is the order the doors open. The sections below follow the Indiana packet, which is built from Indiana primary sources checked on November 16, 2026.

The demand: about 1.28 million seniors across a large Midwest state

Start with why owners keep looking at Indiana. The Census Bureau puts the state population at 6,973,333 as of July 1, 2025, and 18.4 percent of residents are age 65 or older. Run the math and you get about 1.28 million seniors. The same Census QuickFacts page for Indiana puts median household income at $71,957 for 2020 to 2024, which is the income base a private pay agency sells into. A senior base that size, spread across metro, suburban, and rural counties, supports both private pay agencies and the Medicaid waiver work the state pays for at scale.

The license question, answered directly: non-medical care is a personal services agency license

Here is the searched question this article exists to answer. Do you need a license to start a non-medical home care agency in Indiana? Yes, and its name is the personal services agency license, issued by the Indiana Department of Health under IC 16-27-4. A personal services agency provides attendant care, homemaker services, and companion services at the client residence. To operate one, a person must obtain a license from the state health commissioner under IC 16-27-4-6(a). The application is State Form 53391, filed with policies and procedures through the IDOH Gateway, and the fee is $250 for each license application under IC 16-27-4-6(d). The license lasts one year and renewal is also $250, and a change of ownership is a new $250 application.

The boundary on this license is statutory and worth memorizing. A personal services agency may not serve a client with an unstable health condition requiring skilled intervention; it must refer that client to an appropriately licensed provider, under IC 16-27-4-8. Skilled home health is the home health agency license issued by the same department under IC 16-27-1 and 410 IAC 17. Hospice is the license issued under IC 16-25-3. No one license covers another license services, and IHCP enrollment never substitutes for the state license, just as the license never substitutes for enrollment.

The non-medical lane: the personal services agency license at $250, one-year term

The sequence for the non-medical door is short and exact. Confirm the service menu is attendant care, homemaker services, and companion services only, with the referral duty for unstable clients built into the intake process from day one. Form the business and hold the EIN, the insurance, and the operating accounts. File State Form 53391 with policies and procedures through the IDOH Gateway and pay the $250 fee. Operate on the one-year term, and calendar the $250 renewal as an annual cost of the door, not a surprise. The trap on this track is reading the license as a skilled license: IC 16-27-4 licenses attendant care, homemaker, and companion services only, and skilled nursing and therapy are home health license work under IC 16-27-1 and 410 IAC 17.

The Medicaid door on this track runs through the Indiana Health Coverage Programs, called IHCP, administered by the Family and Social Services Administration through the Office of Medicaid Policy and Planning, with Gainwell Technologies as fiscal agent. Home and community based waiver services for people 60 and over run through Indiana PathWays for Aging and its three managed care entities: Anthem, Humana, and UnitedHealthcare. The PathWays sequence runs OMPP certification, IHCP enrollment, then contracting with the managed care entities, and a contract with one entity is not an IHCP enrollment, just as an enrollment is not a contract with any of the three. Electronic visit verification runs on an open model: Indiana uses Sandata as the state-sponsored system at no cost, or an alternate system that exports visit data to the Sandata Aggregator, and EVV applies to personal care services from January 1, 2021 and home health services from January 1, 2024. A personal care agency that skips the Sandata setup has built a billing door with no clock behind it.

The waiver pause: IHCP BT2026124 pauses enrollment, not licensure

Indiana carries a state payer pause, and stating it exactly matters. Under IHCP Bulletin BT2026124, effective August 1, 2026, a CMS-approved statewide moratorium pauses certification and enrollment for specified HCBS waiver services, including Attendant Care, Adult Family Care, Participant Assistance and Care, and Residential Habilitation and Support, across the PathWays, Health and Wellness, TBI, CIH, and Family Supports waivers, for an initial period of six months that the IHCP may extend in six-month increments. The pause blocks new certification and enrollment, changes of ownership, adding counties, and adding restricted services. It does not pause the IDOH licenses in this article. The ledger for this build re-verified the pause in its November 19, 2026 sweep: BT2026124 remains in effect, running to about February 1, 2027 on its initial term.

Both misreadings of the pause cost money. Read as a license moratorium, it freezes the wrong door: an applicant that stops its license filing because of a payer pause has stalled the one filing that is still open. Read as nothing, it produces a licensed agency with no waiver door to bill through while the pause runs. The working plan sequences the license now, builds the private pay and non-restricted work first, and times waiver enrollment against the bulletin, not against a rumor of its end.

The skilled lane: the home health agency license under 410 IAC 17

No home health agency may be opened, operated, managed, or maintained in Indiana without a license issued by the Department of Health, under 410 IAC 17-10-1. The application is State Form 4008 with a nonrefundable fee of $250. The license expires one year after issuance. Renewal runs on State Form 48851, with the $250 fee, filed at least 60 days before, and not sooner than 90 days before, expiration, under 410 IAC 17-11-3. The department may issue a provisional license valid 90 days after a complete application, under 410 IAC 17-10-1(e). No IDOH source checked for this build publishes an overall approval promise for these doors, and the packet estimates none; the real clock on the certified track is the survey cycle, the initial survey scheduling, any statement of deficiency, and the plan of correction.

Medicare certification layers on this license through enrollment on Form CMS-855A through PECOS, with the CY2026 federal institutional enrollment fee of $750. The Home Health and Hospice MAC for Indiana is Palmetto GBA, Jurisdiction M. The A/B MAC is Wisconsin Physicians Service, Jurisdiction 8, and CGS Administrators Jurisdiction B is the DME MAC, never the enrollment desk. Initial Medicare enrollment on the certified home health and hospice tracks is gated by the federal enrollment moratoria, CMS-6101-N2 and CMS-6102-N2, extended and modified effective November 12, 2026 (the extension is a six-month period, running to about May 12, 2027). Neither federal notice blocks a state license, and neither belongs in a personal services packet.

The hospice lane: licensed under IC 16-25-3, fee not published

A hospice program in Indiana must be licensed by the Department of Health, must meet the minimum standards for certification under the Medicare program at IC 16-25-3-4, and renews annually. State-licensed hospitals, health facilities, and home health agencies that operate a hospice program are approved by the department under IC 16-25-3-1 and are not required to hold a separate hospice license. The fee handling on this door is the honest kind: IC 16-25-4 is the hospice license fee chapter, and no fee amount was located in the primary sources checked for this build, so the packet prints NO FEE PUBLISHED. The trap figures are the two $250 fees, personal services and home health, carried into hospice; a budget that carries $250 into hospice is built on the wrong license.

The residential doors: group homes and facilities on their own licenses

Founders who want a home meet a separate Indiana map. Supervised group living for developmental disabilities is licensed under 460 IAC 9, in homes of 4 to 8 individuals, printed as NO FEE PUBLISHED. Residential care facilities, the Indiana assisted living door, are licensed by the Department under 410 IAC 16.2 at $200 for the first 50 beds plus $10 for each additional bed, and comprehensive care facilities carry the same bed fee frame with a certificate of need layer for new beds. Group homes for children are licensed by the Department of Child Services under 465 IAC 2-12, with the processing fee capped at $3 per licensed bed and a $150 maximum. Adult family care, homes of up to 4 residents for waiver payment, is certified through the Division of Aging and OMPP at NO FEE PUBLISHED, and it sits inside the BT2026124 pause as a restricted service, while adult day services certification is exempt from that moratorium. Those figures belong to those doors and are never borrowed for a home-based license.

Background checks and the personnel file

The personnel file the packet builds for each Indiana worker holds the application and identity verification, the background check result completed before assignment, license or credential verification for each licensed class, the orientation and training record by topic and date, the competency evaluation for each assigned service, the supervision and annual evaluation record, the health and immunization record the track requires, and the signed confidentiality and conduct acknowledgments. The pattern to avoid is the file built at survey time from memory. In the records I review, the agencies that pass clean are the ones whose files were assembled at hire, in order, with the background check dated before the first assignment it covers.

What you still do today: form the business the Indiana way

Formation is the ordinary employer load, done in the right order: form the entity, get an EIN from the IRS, put liability insurance in place before the first client, adopt written policies and procedures covering the services you will actually sell, and file those policies with the license application the door requires, since the personal services application travels with its policy file through the IDOH Gateway. Set up caregiver training, background screening, and the Sandata EVV configuration as systems rather than scrambles, and use a service agreement that says clearly what your agency does and does not do, with the unstable-client referral duty stated where a family will see it before anyone needs it. No state formation fee figure is printed in the sources checked for this build, so none is quoted here.

The practical warning

Three Indiana traps cost new owners money. The first is the $250 swap: the personal services fee and the home health fee are the same number on different licenses, and the hospice fee is unpublished, so a multi-door budget with one $250 line is short before it starts. The second is the pause misread, in both directions, covered above: BT2026124 pauses waiver certification and enrollment and does not touch IDOH licensure. The third is the plan roster trap: MDwise left the Healthy Indiana Plan and Hoosier Healthwise on January 1, 2026, and the current IHCP managed care table names Anthem, CareSource, Humana, Managed Health Services, and UnitedHealthcare. A contracting plan built on a departed plan is a plan built on a closed door.

Your next step

Work in lane order. File the personal services agency license first if non-medical care is the plan, State Form 53391 at $250 for a one-year term, and keep the home health license at its own $250 with its 60 to 90 day renewal window separate in the budget. Sequence IHCP enrollment behind the license, read BT2026124 before timing any waiver service it restricts, and set up Sandata before the first billed visit.
