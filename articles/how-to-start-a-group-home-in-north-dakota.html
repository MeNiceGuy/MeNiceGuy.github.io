How to Start a Group Home in North Dakota

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in North Dakota. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type North Dakota licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types North Dakota licenses

Basic Care Facility

Licensed by: North Dakota Department of Health and Human Services, Health Facilities Unit. Serves: Five or more residents not related by blood or marriage to the owner or manager who, because of impaired capacity for independent living, require health, social, or personal care services but do not require regular 24-hour medical or nursing services. Licensed under N.D.C.C. chapter 23-09.3 and N.D. Admin. Code chapters 33-03-24.1 and 33-03-24.2. Provides room and board and health, social, and personal care on a 24-hour basis to assist residents to attain or maintain their highest level of functioning, consistent with the resident assessment and care plan. Application must be made to the department prior to opening a facility. North Dakota Medicaid covers services provided by in-state licensed and enrolled basic care facilities.

Assisted Living Facility

Licensed by: North Dakota Department of Health and Human Services, licensed by both Food and Lodging and the Health Response and Licensure Section. Serves: Five or more individuals who are not related by blood, marriage, or guardianship to the owner or manager. A building containing a series of at least five living units operated as one entity, held out to the public as a place that provides or coordinates individualized support services to accommodate the individual's needs and abilities to maintain as much independence as possible. An entity providing assisted living services to five or more individuals in this setting must be licensed. Governing law cited on the HHS assisted living page includes N.D.C.C. 23-09, N.D.C.C. 50-32, N.D. Admin. Code 33-33-09, and N.D. Admin. Code 75-03-34.

Adult Foster Care Home (Adult Foster Home)

Licensed by: North Dakota Department of Health and Human Services, Adults and Aging / Qualified Service Provider (QSP) enrollment. Serves: Adults age 18 or older who are unable, neglect, or refuse to provide for their own care; the home serves four or fewer adults who are not related by blood or marriage to the owner or lessee. An occupied private residence where adult foster care is regularly provided by the owner or lessee for hire or compensation. Adult foster care is the provision of food, shelter, security, safety, guidance, and comfort on a 24-hour per day basis, and includes personal non-medical service to assist a resident with activities of daily living, as defined at N.D.C.C. section 50-11-00.1(5). A provider must enroll as an individual QSP and be licensed as an AFC to provide foster care to more than one adult, in private pay and public pay situations alike, per the HHS QSP Adult Foster Care handbook.

Agency Foster Home for Adults

Licensed by: North Dakota Department of Health and Human Services, Aging Services Section. Serves: Individuals, including individuals with physical disabilities, who are eligible for Residential Habilitation and Community Support services. Licensed under Agency Foster Home for Adults Licensing Policy 670-05-20 and N.D. Admin. Code section 75-03-21.1, with a licensed capacity of 4 in the HHS settings rule summaries reviewed. The setting delivers Residential Habilitation and Community Supports, funded through the Medicaid HCBS waiver for aged and disabled individuals and state programs administered with Medical Services and Adults and Aging Services.

Developmental Disabilities licensed residential services, including Residential Habilitation and Intermediate Care Facility for Individuals with Intellectual Disabilities (ICF/IID)

Licensed by: North Dakota Department of Health and Human Services, Developmental Disabilities Section. Serves: Individuals of all ages with an intellectual disability or developmental disability or related condition who meet the ICF/IID level of care. Under N.D. Admin. Code 75-04-01-17, services subject to licensure through the DD Section include Residential Habilitation, Day Habilitation, Independent Habilitation, ICF/IID, Employment Supports, Prevocational Services, and Family Support Services including In-Home Supports, Extended Home Health Care, Family Care Option, and Respite. Residential placements are funded by the Traditional IID/DD Home and Community Based Services Waiver, which covers residential habilitation, independent habilitation, adult foster care, family care option, host home, and in-home supports for individuals meeting ICF/IID level of care.

How to start a group home in North Dakota, step by step

  1. Basic care: application to operate a facility must be made to the department prior to opening a facility, prior to change in ownership, and annually (N.D. Admin. Code 33-03-24.1-03)
  2. Assisted living: file the HHS license application (SFN forms listed on the HHS assisted living page) for licensure by Food and Lodging and the Health Response and Licensure Section
  3. DD licensed providers: the DD Section New Provider Packet contains a checklist that lays out the required steps for licensure, per the DD provider manual

North Dakota group home license fees and costs

  • Assisted Living Facility license application fee: $120.00, per the Schedule of License Fees on the HHS Food and Lodging Unit assisted living facility license application (SFN 19384)

How long does it take in North Dakota?

  • Basic care initial license is valid for a period not to exceed one year and expires on December 31 of the year issued; licenses are issued on a calendar year basis and expire on December 31 of each year (N.D. Admin. Code 33-03-24.1-03)

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to open a group home in North Dakota?

The figures North Dakota publishes are these: Assisted Living Facility license application fee: $120.00, per the Schedule of License Fees on the HHS Food and Lodging Unit assisted living facility license application (SFN 19384) The license fee is rarely the largest cost. The home itself, life safety and accessibility work, insurance, and staffing hired before the first placement carry the project.

How long does it take to get a group home license in North Dakota?

Basic care initial license is valid for a period not to exceed one year and expires on December 31 of the year issued; licenses are issued on a calendar year basis and expire on December 31 of each year (N.D. Admin. Code 33-03-24.1-03)

What are the requirements to open a group home in North Dakota?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in North Dakota?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
