How to Start a Group Home in New Mexico

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in New Mexico. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type New Mexico licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types New Mexico licenses

Assisted Living Facility for Adults

Licensed by: New Mexico Health Care Authority, Division of Health Improvement. Serves: Two or more adults who need or desire assistance with one or more activities of daily living, per 8.370.14.2 NMAC. Licensure is required: no person or entity shall establish, maintain or operate an assisted living facility without first obtaining a license, per 8.370.14.8 NMAC in the December 23, 2025 rule text. The rule objective is a safe, humane and homelike environment for adults who need assistance or supervision or cueing with activities of daily living but who do not need acute care, continuous nursing care, skilled nursing care or care in an intermediate care facility. Fee is the assisted living base assessment rate of $300 annually under 8.370.3.10 NMAC.

Boarding Home

Licensed by: New Mexico Health Care Authority, Division of Health Improvement. Serves: Two or more adults age 18 or older, not related to the owner, that admits residents discharged from any mental or behavioral health care institution, per the boarding home definition in 8.370.15 NMAC. Non-medical boarding homes required to be licensed under the Public Health Act to provide residential placement to individuals seeking assistance with one or more instrumental activities of daily living or assistance with accessing or coordination of community services. Individuals who need a higher level of services than a boarding home provides shall not be placed in a boarding home. Fee is $300 under 8.370.3 NMAC.

Intermediate Care Facility (in Long Term Care Facilities rule)

Licensed by: New Mexico Health Care Authority, Division of Health Improvement. Serves: Residents needing intermediate nursing care, most with long term illnesses or disabilities that may have reached a relatively stable plateau, per 8.370.16 NMAC definitions. 8.370.16 NMAC defines an intermediate care facility as a nursing home licensed by the authority as an intermediate care facility to provide intermediate nursing care, and intermediate nursing care requires the skill of a licensed nurse for observation and recording under periodic medical supervision. The fee rule lists intermediate care facilities at $12 per bed and intermediate care facilities for persons with intellectual disabilities in the long term care section of 8.370.3 NMAC.

Developmental Disabilities Waiver Living Supports and Residential Habilitation

Licensed by: New Mexico Health Care Authority, Developmental Disabilities Supports Division for the waiver, with Division of Health Improvement oversight of home and community based Medicaid waiver program providers. Serves: Individuals with intellectual disabilities or specific related conditions and developmental disability that occur before age 22, per the Developmental Disabilities Waiver application on hca.nm.gov. This is a Medicaid 1915(c) waiver service, not a facility license in the sources reviewed. The waiver application states the Developmental Disabilities Waiver serves that population with community based services to increase independence, and Appendix C Residential Habilitation, Living Supports language was updated for participant protections from eviction. DHI states it is responsible for oversight of home and community based Medicaid waiver program providers, including the Developmental Disabilities Waiver, Medically Fragile Waiver and Mi Via Waiver.

How to start a group home in New Mexico, step by step

  1. Submit a letter of intent to facility.license@hca.nm.gov, per the HCA How to License a Health Care Facility page
  2. Write a program narrative describing the population, services and care, and anticipated contract services
  3. Receive the application packet after letter of intent approval and submit the application with the nonrefundable fee
  4. Submit annotated policy and procedure manuals with a crosswalk to the rules
  5. Early in planning and before construction, contact Program Operations Bureau at 505-476-9093 to discuss the project and anticipated opening date, and provide building plans for life safety code review
  6. Obtain Board of Pharmacy approval before licensure if the facility will handle drugs
  7. Complete the life safety code inspection, which is required before a temporary license can be issued; upon successful completion a temporary license is issued and the facility may serve a limited number of patients or residents
  8. Request the initial health survey in writing when in full operation; upon successful completion, an annual license is issued

New Mexico group home license fees and costs

  • Assisted living base assessment rate: $300 annually under 8.370.3.10 NMAC
  • Boarding homes: $300 under 8.370.3 NMAC
  • Intermediate care facilities: $12 per bed in the long term care section of 8.370.3 NMAC, as shown in the rule text
  • Amended license: $300 under 8.370.3.11 NMAC

How long does it take in New Mexico?

  • Application, policy and procedure review and plan review are good for one year from receipt of the application fee, per the HCA licensing page
  • Temporary license is issued after successful life safety code inspection and before the initial health survey and annual license, per the HCA licensing page

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to open a group home in New Mexico?

The figures New Mexico publishes are these: Assisted living base assessment rate: $300 annually under 8.370.3.10 NMAC Boarding homes: $300 under 8.370.3 NMAC Intermediate care facilities: $12 per bed in the long term care section of 8.370.3 NMAC, as shown in the rule text Amended license: $300 under 8.370.3.11 NMAC The license fee is rarely the largest cost. The home itself, life safety and accessibility work, insurance, and staffing hired before the first placement carry the project.

How long does it take to get a group home license in New Mexico?

Application, policy and procedure review and plan review are good for one year from receipt of the application fee, per the HCA licensing page Temporary license is issued after successful life safety code inspection and before the initial health survey and annual license, per the HCA licensing page

What are the requirements to open a group home in New Mexico?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in New Mexico?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
