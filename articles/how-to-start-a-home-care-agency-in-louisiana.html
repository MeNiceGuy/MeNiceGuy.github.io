How to Start a Home Care Agency in Louisiana (Every Home-Based Door Is Licensed, Starting With the HCBS License)

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 19, 2026

The Louisiana story starts with a founder who assumed personal care was the easy door. She had worked as a caregiver around Baton Rouge, she had families asking when she would open, and she had read a national guide that told her non-medical home care needed no license at all. That guide was describing another state. Louisiana licenses hands-on personal care, and it licenses it under a name most founders never search for: the Home and Community-Based Services provider license, with a module for each service, and the personal care door is the Personal Care Attendant module. When owners come to me with a Louisiana plan, the first thing I do is rename the door for them, because every fee, every prerequisite, and every survey in this state hangs off getting the credential right first.

Louisiana runs three separate licensed doors for home-based care, and no one door covers another door's services. Non-medical home care sits on the HCBS provider license under R.S. 40:2120.1 et seq. and LAC 48:I Chapter 50. Skilled home health sits on the home health agency license under R.S. 40:2116.31 et seq. and LAC 48:I Chapter 91. Hospice sits on the hospice agency license under R.S. 40:2181 to 2192 and LAC 48:I Chapter 82. All three are issued by the Health Standards Section of the Louisiana Department of Health. Here is the full picture, lane by lane, in the order a founder actually meets it.

The demand: about 868,000 seniors, close to one in five residents

Start with why owners keep looking at Louisiana. The Census Bureau puts the state population at 4,618,189 as of July 1, 2025, and 18.8 percent of residents are age 65 or older. Run the math and you get about 868,000 seniors, close to one in five people in the state. The same Census QuickFacts page for Louisiana puts median household income at $60,756 for 2020 to 2024, which is the income base a private pay agency sells into and the reason the Medicaid waiver doors matter so much in this state.

The payer mix is the Louisiana story underneath the senior count. Louisiana Medicaid runs a large home and community-based waiver system, and the managed care program, Healthy Louisiana, is where most Medicaid home care volume moves. An owner who plans only for private pay in Louisiana is planning for the smaller half of the market. The licensed doors below are the doors both halves run through.

The license question, answered directly: hands-on personal care is licensed as an HCBS provider

Here is the searched question this article exists to answer. Do you need a license to start a non-medical home care agency in Louisiana? Yes, if the work is hands-on personal care, for any payer, private pay included. The credential is the HCBS provider license issued by the LDH Health Standards Section, with a module approved for each service. One license carries the modules the state lists at LAC 48:I.5001: adult day care, family support, personal care attendant, respite, substitute family care, supervised independent living, supported employment, and monitored in-home caregiving. The personal care door is the Personal Care Attendant module.

The exemptions are narrow and worth stating exactly. An entity that solely provides sitter services, or solely housekeeping, meal preparation or delivery, home modifications, or personal emergency response, sits outside this license on the sources checked for the Louisiana packet. The first hands-on personal care task moves the business inside it. The residential models in the generator, adult residential care providers, supervised independent living settings, behavioral health residential programs, therapeutic group homes, nursing facilities, ICF/DD facilities, adult day health care centers, and the other all-homes tracks, each carry their own Louisiana credential and are built as separate tracks. Nothing on a home-based license authorizes a residential setting, and no residential license authorizes agency work in client homes.

The non-medical lane: the HCBS license and the PCA module, with Facility Need Review first

The sequence on this door starts before the license application. Personal care attendant, respite, supervised independent living, and monitored in-home caregiving need Facility Need Review approval before the license application, under LAC 48:I Chapter 125, at a $200 non-refundable Facility Need Review fee. Hold the approval letter for the license packet. Founders who file the license application first have sequenced the door backwards.

Financial viability is a licensure fact at R.S. 40:2120.2: a line of credit of at least $50,000 from a federally insured lending institution, general and professional liability insurance of at least $300,000, and workers compensation insurance, with LDH Health Standards named as certificate holder on the policies the checklist names. The fee facts for this door: the base license fee is $600 and renewal is $600, and the statute caps the base license at $600 per year for providers of in-home services at R.S. 40:2120.4. Adding a module costs $200 per module, $250 for center-based respite with a parent company, and a delinquent renewal costs $100. After the license, Louisiana Medicaid enrollment runs through the Louisiana Medicaid provider enrollment portal as the licensed module provider. Enrollment does not substitute for the license, and the license does not enroll the agency.

The skilled lane: the home health agency license under Chapter 91

The moment your plan includes skilled nursing or therapy in the home, you have left the HCBS door. LDH is the only licensing authority for home health agencies in Louisiana under LAC 48:I.9105, and no agency may open, conduct, manage, or maintain a home health agency without the license under R.S. 40:2116.32. Initial licensure requires documentation of a line of credit of at least $75,000 from a licensed lending agency as proof of adequate finances to sustain an agency for at least six months, and general and professional liability coverage of at least $300,000, with workers compensation insurance. The fee is $600 and renewal is $600, a branch or satellite costs $300, and a delinquent renewal costs $100 on the Health Standards fee schedule. An approved applicant is fully operational, in compliance with the licensing standards, and providing care to only two patients at the time of the initial survey, on the rule text the packet read.

Medicare certification is a federal layer on this license, not a second state filing. It runs on Form CMS-855A through PECOS to Palmetto GBA, Jurisdiction M, the Home Health and Hospice MAC for Louisiana, with the CY2026 institutional enrollment fee of $750, under the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484. Read the federal gate before timing it: initial Medicare enrollment for home health agencies is blocked under the federal moratorium extended and modified by CMS-6101-N2, effective November 12, 2026, with no calendar end date printed in the notice (the extension is a six-month period, running to about May 12, 2027). The state license sequence runs on its own clock while the federal door is gated.

The hospice lane: its own license under Chapter 82, and Louisiana issues no hospice certificate

Hospice sits on its own license under R.S. 40:2181 to 2192 and LAC 48:I Chapter 82, and it is unlawful to operate a hospice without it under LAC 48:I.8203. Facility Need Review approval is required for initial licensure, change of address, and change of ownership. Initial licensing items on the LDH hospice page include a $75,000 line of credit from a federally insured lending agency. The fee is $600 plus $5 per unit; a branch or alternative delivery site costs $300, an inpatient site $600, and a delinquent renewal costs $100. The Medicare component runs on Form CMS-855A to Palmetto GBA, Jurisdiction M, with the CY2026 federal fee of $750 under 42 CFR Part 418, gated for initial enrollment by CMS-6102-N2 on the same federal terms as the home health gate above. Neither federal gate blocks the state license.

Background checks: the LDH Direct Service Worker Registry, before hire and monthly

Louisiana's screening system for direct service workers is the LDH Direct Service Worker Registry under LAC 48:I Chapter 92. The packet builds the personnel file around registry checks completed and filed before assignment, with the registry standing rechecked on the cycle the door requires, alongside license or credential verification for each licensed class, orientation and training records by topic and date, competency evaluation for each assigned service, and the supervision and annual evaluation record. One warning belongs here because the error is common in national guides: Louisiana's check is the Direct Service Worker Registry. A guide that sends a Louisiana founder to a Family Care Safety Registry is describing Missouri's system, not Louisiana's, and a personnel file built on the wrong registry is a survey finding waiting for its date.

Electronic visit verification attaches to the Medicaid doors from the first visit. Louisiana requires EVV for Medicaid personal care and home health services through LaSRS, the Louisiana Service Reporting System operated by Statistical Resources, Inc., or an LDH-approved third-party system that feeds LaSRS. The visit record, the authorized plan, and the claim must agree.

What you still do today: form the business the Louisiana way

The licensed doors sit on top of an ordinary business build, and the order matters. Form the entity and hold the EIN, then build the financial viability record the door requires, the line of credit and the insurance certificates with LDH Health Standards named as certificate holder where the checklist names it, then the Direct Service Worker Registry screening system, the worker training system, the service plans, and the policies LAC 48:I Chapter 50 requires, all before filing, so the review is a submission exercise instead of a rebuild. No separate Louisiana Medicaid enrollment fee figure was located in the LDH sources checked for the packet, and the packet prints none. Managed care runs through Healthy Louisiana: Aetna Better Health of Louisiana, AmeriHealth Caritas Louisiana, Healthy Blue through December 31, 2026, Humana Healthy Horizons in Louisiana, and Louisiana Healthcare Connections. UnitedHealthcare Community Plan ended participation on March 31, 2026, under LDH Informational Bulletin 26-3, and Healthy Blue exits on December 31, 2026, leaving four plans from January 1, 2027. A state enrollment is not a plan contract, and a plan contract is not an enrollment.

The practical warning

Three Louisiana traps cost new owners money. The first is reading the state payer pause as a license moratorium. LDH Informational Bulletin 26-13 states a CMS-approved temporary moratorium on new Louisiana Medicaid enrollments for durable medical equipment suppliers, home health agencies, and hospice providers, effective June 18, 2026 through December 17, 2026, for payer enrollment only. Providers actively enrolled before June 18, 2026 and applications received before that date are not affected. The pause does not touch the LDH licenses, and it does not reach the HCBS door, which it does not name. An applicant who stops a license filing because of a payer pause has frozen the wrong door. The second trap is the expedited fee read as the license fee: LAC 48:I Chapter 41 sets optional expedited licensing process fees of $7,000, $6,000, and $5,000 by tier, which buy a faster process for an applicant that elects it. The license fees are the Health Standards fee schedule figures, $600 for the doors in this article. The third trap is the wrong MAC desk: home health and hospice enrollment goes to Palmetto GBA, Jurisdiction M. Novitas Solutions, Jurisdiction H, is the A/B MAC, and CGS Administrators, Jurisdiction C, is the DME MAC and is never the enrollment desk for these tracks.
