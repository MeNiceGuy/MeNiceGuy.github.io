How to Start a Home Health Agency in Minnesota

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Minnesota licenses home health agencies as a Comprehensive Home Care Provider License, issued first as a Temporary Comprehensive Home Care License; Medicare certified home health agencies must hold a comprehensive home care license, through the Minnesota Department of Health (MDH), Health Regulation Division. The governing law is Minnesota Statutes chapter 144A, sections 144A.43 to 144A.482; home care services defined at section 144A.471 subdivisions 6 and 7. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the Minnesota license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The Minnesota license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Minnesota, step by step

  1. Apply for a temporary comprehensive home care license through the MDH Facility and Provider Licensing System portal, uploading the application and required attachments
  2. MDH acknowledges receipt in the portal; incomplete or inaccurate applications may be rejected and sent back under section 144A.472
  3. Pay the application fee in the portal when MDH determines it has all required information, signatures and attachments and contacts the applicant to request payment
  4. Operate on the temporary license and pass the initial survey; a full comprehensive license follows substantial compliance
  5. For Medicare certification as a licensed comprehensive provider: complete CMS-1572 (a and b), CMS-1561 and the Civil Rights Attestation and Assurance of Compliance form and email them to MDH at Health.HRD-FedLCR@state.mn.us
  6. Submit the CMS-855A Medicare Enrollment Application to the designated Medicare Administrative Contractor (MAC)
  7. Contact a CMS approved National Accrediting Organization to conduct the initial Medicare survey
  8. MDH proceeds after the forms are complete, the CMS-855A is approved by the MAC and the accrediting organization has completed the initial survey, then forwards its recommendation for certification or denial to the MAC, which forwards the final determination

Minnesota home health license fees and costs

  • Temporary Comprehensive Home Care License: $4,200 (MDH application, Minn. Stat. 144A.472 subdivision 7)
  • Temporary Basic Home Care License: $2,100 (MDH application, for the non-skilled level)

How long does it take in Minnesota?

  • 90 day decision clock after a complete home care application (verified context for the Minnesota home care license)

What catches Minnesota applicants

  • State license before Medicare certification: MDH presents Medicare certification as a process for an already licensed comprehensive home care provider, and states all Medicare certified home health agencies must hold a comprehensive home care license
  • Minnesota has no separate home health agency license name in the MDH materials reviewed; skilled nursing and therapy at home sit inside the comprehensive home care license
  • No certificate of need step appears in the MDH home care application or MDH initial Medicare certification process reviewed
  • A provider offering both sleeping accommodations and home care services must be licensed as an assisted living facility under Minn. Stat. 144A.471 subdivision 1a, per the MDH home care application

The Medicare layer, after the Minnesota license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a home health agency in Minnesota?

The figures Minnesota publishes are these: Temporary Comprehensive Home Care License: $4,200 (MDH application, Minn. Stat. 144A.472 subdivision 7) Temporary Basic Home Care License: $2,100 (MDH application, for the non-skilled level) Beyond the published figures, the operating costs (clinical payroll before certification, insurance, and the reserve operating funds required by 42 CFR 489.28) are larger than every fee on the list.

How long does it take to get a home health license in Minnesota?

90 day decision clock after a complete home care application (verified context for the Minnesota home care license) The federal certification that follows runs on the survey calendar, and new Medicare enrollment is under a CMS moratorium to about mid-May 2027.

What do I need to start a home health agency in Minnesota?

The Comprehensive Home Care Provider License, issued first as a Temporary Comprehensive Home Care License; Medicare certified home health agencies must hold a comprehensive home care license (Minnesota Department of Health (MDH), Health Regulation Division), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet Minnesota rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in Minnesota?

Apply to the Minnesota Department of Health (MDH), Health Regulation Division under Minnesota Statutes chapter 144A, sections 144A.43 to 144A.482; home care services defined at section 144A.471 subdivisions 6 and 7. The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
