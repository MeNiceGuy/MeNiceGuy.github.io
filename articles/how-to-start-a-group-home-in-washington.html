How to Start a Group Home in Washington

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Washington. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Washington licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Washington licenses

Adult Family Home (AFH)

Licensed by: Washington Department of Social and Health Services (DSHS), Residential Care Services. Serves: Adults needing personal care, specialty care, supervision, laundry, and room and board in a residential home. Residential home licensed to care for two to six adults not related by blood or marriage to the provider; since June 2020, homes licensed for two years may apply to increase capacity to seven or eight residents if criteria are met. Each home supports two to eight adults on the DSHS personal care page. Chapter 70.128 RCW and Chapter 388-76 WAC. License and processing fee $2,750. Medicaid placements are funded in licensed residential settings through programs including the COPES waiver (see notes).

Assisted Living Facility (ALF)

Licensed by: Washington Department of Social and Health Services (DSHS). Serves: Adults, seven or more per facility, needing personal care and support with daily care needs. Community setting licensed by DSHS to care for seven or more residents. Offers three service packages (Adult Residential Care, Enhanced Adult Residential Care, and Assisted Living) providing personal care, housekeeping, meals, snacks, laundry and activities. Chapter 18.20 RCW and Chapter 388-78A WAC. License fee $381 per bed on the current application form.

Enhanced Services Facility (ESF)

Licensed by: Washington Department of Social and Health Services (DSHS). Serves: People referred from state and community psychiatric hospitals, or with no other placement option, whose complicated personal care and behavioral challenges do not require an institutional setting. Authorized under Chapter 70.97 RCW and Chapter 388-107 WAC. Uses high staffing ratios with a strong focus on behavioral interventions, and offers behavioral health, personal care and nursing at an intensity not generally provided in other licensed long-term care settings. License fee $1,040 per bed. A provider must be both licensed as an ESF and contracted with the Home and Community Services Division to serve ESF residents.

Group Homes and Group Training Homes (DDA)

Licensed by: Washington Department of Social and Health Services (DSHS), Developmental Disabilities Administration (DDA) - Certified Community Residential Services and Supports. Serves: Adults with developmental disabilities. The provider leases or rents a home shared with others, five to 12 people total, with life skills training including community activities, menu planning, cooking, medication management and medical appointment scheduling. Residents pay room and board through an income-based cost-of-care fee. Group Homes are licensed facilities operated by a for-profit or non-profit provider; Group Training Homes are non-licensed facilities operated by a non-profit provider. DDA operates five Home and Community Based Services waivers for people with developmental disabilities: Basic Plus, Children's Intensive In-Home Behavioral Supports, Community Protection, Core, and Individual and Family Services.

Supported Living, Companion Homes and Alternative Living (DDA)

Licensed by: Washington Department of Social and Health Services (DSHS), Developmental Disabilities Administration (DDA) - certified, contracted agencies or State-Operated Living Alternatives. Serves: Adults with developmental disabilities. Provided in a home the person owns, rents or leases with up to three other people; the person pays for food, utilities and personal expenses with their own income and chooses their home, roommates and provider. Companion Homes provide life skills training in the provider's own home in a family setting. Alternative Living is a skills-based service (not personal care) for people in a home they rent or lease.

Residential Treatment Facility (RTF) - behavioral health

Licensed by: Washington State Department of Health (DOH). Serves: Adults and youth receiving residential or inpatient behavioral health services, including substance use disorder and mental health treatment. A current DOH license is required before operating or advertising a residential treatment facility; the license is effective for one year and is not transferable. The application packet also requires behavioral health agency licensing and certification for residential and inpatient services, including residential and inpatient substance use disorder and mental health treatment beds. Administrative processing and initial application fee $490 under WAC 246-337-990.

How to start a group home in Washington, step by step

  1. Adult Family Home: complete the AFH license application and requirements in WAC 388-76-10057 before submitting; DSHS notes an applicant must be Home Care Aide Certified (or exempt), including 75-hour training and the Department of Health certification exam, before applying for an AFH license unless an exemption applies
  2. Assisted Living Facility: submit the application and supporting documents at least 90 days before the anticipated opening date or change of ownership effective date, with the per bed license fee
  3. Enhanced Services Facility: 1. Request an information meeting with the HCS Resource Support and Development Team; 2. Work with HCS and Residential Care Services to develop building and operational plans; 3. Begin the application process with Department of Health Construction Review Services; then read Chapter 388-107 WAC, ensure the building meets it through Construction Review Services, submit the ESF license application and $1,040 per bed fee, work with RCS licensors to meet all requirements, and after licensure submit an ESF contract application to serve DSHS clients
  4. Group Home, Group Training Home and Supported Living (DDA): request the Certified Community Residential Service and Support Initial Application and Instructions from DSHS at ccrssapplicationsrequest@dshs.wa.gov

Washington group home license fees and costs

  • Adult Family Home: $2,750 license and processing fee submitted with the license application; $700 for a change of ownership application (DSHS AFH information sheet)
  • Assisted Living Facility: license fee $381 per bed on the DSHS license application (DSHS 10-591, Rev. 07/2026); a separate DSHS provider letter states the annual per bed fee increased to $383 per bed effective July 1, 2025 - both figures are printed by DSHS in different documents fetched
  • Enhanced Services Facility: license fee $1,040 per bed (DSHS ESF page)
  • Residential Treatment Facility: administrative processing and initial application fee $490 (DOH page, citing WAC 246-337-990)

How long does it take in Washington?

  • Adult Family Home: once an application is complete, processing could take up to 60 days; the AFH licensing FAQ states an application can take 60 days or more to process
  • Assisted Living Facility: application must be submitted at least 90 days before the anticipated opening or ownership change date, and DSHS states current processing may take as long as four to six months
  • Residential Treatment Facility: license is effective for one year; renew annually 30 days before expiration

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to open a group home in Washington?

The figures Washington publishes are these: Adult Family Home: $2,750 license and processing fee submitted with the license application; $700 for a change of ownership application (DSHS AFH information sheet) Assisted Living Facility: license fee $381 per bed on the DSHS license application (DSHS 10-591, Rev. 07/2026); a separate DSHS provider letter states the annual per bed fee increased to $383 per bed effective July 1, 2025 - both figures are printed by DSHS in different documents fetched Enhanced Services Facility: license fee $1,040 per bed (DSHS ESF page) Residential Treatment Facility: administrative processing and initial application fee $490 (DOH page, citing WAC 246-337-990) The license fee is rarely the largest cost. The home itself, life safety and accessibility work, insurance, and staffing hired before the first placement carry the project.

How long does it take to get a group home license in Washington?

Adult Family Home: once an application is complete, processing could take up to 60 days; the AFH licensing FAQ states an application can take 60 days or more to process Assisted Living Facility: application must be submitted at least 90 days before the anticipated opening or ownership change date, and DSHS states current processing may take as long as four to six months Residential Treatment Facility: license is effective for one year; renew annually 30 days before expiration

What are the requirements to open a group home in Washington?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Washington?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
