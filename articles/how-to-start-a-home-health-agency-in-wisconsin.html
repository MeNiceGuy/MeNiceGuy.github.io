How to Start a Home Health Agency in Wisconsin

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Wisconsin licenses home health agencies as a Home Health Agency license - provisional license first, then permanent (continuation) license, through the Wisconsin Department of Health Services (DHS), Division of Quality Assurance (DQA). The governing law is Wis. Stat. section 50.49 and Wis. Admin. Code ch. DHS 133; Medicare certification under 42 CFR Part 484. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the Wisconsin license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The Wisconsin license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Wisconsin, step by step

  1. Review Wis. Admin. Code ch. DHS 133, Wis. Stat. section 50.49 and 42 CFR Part 484 before applying
  2. For the combined accrediting organization route: choose and contact a DQA and CMS approved accrediting organization (ACHC, CHAP or The Joint Commission) and get its letter of acceptance
  3. Apply for a provisional license by sending DQA the accrediting organization acceptance letter, a letter of intent describing the proposed HHA, completed License Application F-62674, administrator credentials under DHS 133.06, the $300 fee, background checks, organizational chart, a proposed 90 day operating budget and financial references, EIN letter and organizational documents; also send the accrediting organization a copy of F-62674 plus the Pre-Licensure Checklist F-62536 with policies and procedures
  4. Wait for review: DQA performs a fit and qualified review under DHS 133.03(i), then the accrediting organization conducts a provisional licensure survey and a provisional license is issued
  5. Operate under the provisional license and serve 10 clients who require skilled nursing services; Medicare and Medicaid funding are not available during this period
  6. Submit Medicare certification documents during provisional licensure, including CMS-855 to the Medicare Administrative Contractor (currently National Government Services), a signed CMS-1561 to DQA and the Office of Civil Rights assurance confirmation
  7. After 10 clients, send a letter with 10 patient care plans to the accrediting organization to trigger the unannounced permanent licensure and Medicare certification survey
  8. On completion of the survey and any corrections, a permanent HHA license can be issued and DQA can recommend Medicare certification to CMS, which approves or denies it and issues the Medicare number
  9. Alternative state survey route presented by DHS: send the same provisional license packet and F-62536 checklist to DQA instead of an accrediting organization, operate provisionally, send DQA a letter with 10 patient care plans, DQA performs the unannounced survey, then permanent licensure and a Medicare recommendation can follow

Wisconsin home health license fees and costs

  • $300 one-time application fee for a provisional license, payable to the Division of Quality Assurance
  • Background check fee of $15 per individual, listed on the DHS application page
  • For permanent HHA licenses, yearly fees are 0.25 percent of patient annual fees, ranging from $500 to $2,500

How long does it take in Wisconsin?

  • Provisional licensure is good for three months and is extendable up to three times, providing one year in total
  • Licensing summary lists duration as 90 days for a provisional license, then one year for a continuation license
  • On the state survey route, DQA will have up to 90 days to review application materials, including policies and procedures, before issuing a provisional license

What catches Wisconsin applicants

  • State licensure should be completed at the same time or before Medicare or Medicaid certification, and state licensure does not guarantee Medicare certification, per the DHS application page
  • Medicare certification and licensure are requirements for Medicaid certification; DHS states it is not possible to maintain Medicaid certification as an HHA without Medicare certification
  • Do not complete an HHA application if only providing personal care services such as help with activities of daily living, housekeeping or accompanying clients to medical appointments; Wisconsin has no state license for private pay non-medical home care (context supplied for this task and consistent with the DHS page)
  • The DHS HHA application page lists no certificate of need step

The Medicare layer, after the Wisconsin license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a home health agency in Wisconsin?

The figures Wisconsin publishes are these: $300 one-time application fee for a provisional license, payable to the Division of Quality Assurance Background check fee of $15 per individual, listed on the DHS application page For permanent HHA licenses, yearly fees are 0.25 percent of patient annual fees, ranging from $500 to $2,500 Beyond the published figures, the operating costs (clinical payroll before certification, insurance, and the reserve operating funds required by 42 CFR 489.28) are larger than every fee on the list.

How long does it take to get a home health license in Wisconsin?

Provisional licensure is good for three months and is extendable up to three times, providing one year in total Licensing summary lists duration as 90 days for a provisional license, then one year for a continuation license On the state survey route, DQA will have up to 90 days to review application materials, including policies and procedures, before issuing a provisional license The federal certification that follows runs on the survey calendar, and new Medicare enrollment is under a CMS moratorium to about mid-May 2027.

What do I need to start a home health agency in Wisconsin?

The Home Health Agency license - provisional license first, then permanent (continuation) license (Wisconsin Department of Health Services (DHS), Division of Quality Assurance (DQA)), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet Wisconsin rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in Wisconsin?

Apply to the Wisconsin Department of Health Services (DHS), Division of Quality Assurance (DQA) under Wis. Stat. section 50.49 and Wis. Admin. Code ch. DHS 133; Medicare certification under 42 CFR Part 484. The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
