How to Start a Group Home in Idaho

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Idaho. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Idaho licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Idaho licenses

Residential Assisted Living Facility (RALF)

Licensed by: Idaho Department of Health and Welfare, Division of Licensing and Certification, Residential Assisted Living Facilities Program. Serves: Adults in residential care or assisted living facilities surveyed and licensed by the RALF team, per the Division of Licensing and Certification report to the Legislature. State licensure is required and federal certification is not required for Residential Assisted Living Facilities, per the facility table in the Legislature report, which lists 258 such facilities. The DHW licensing page sets the multi step process reproduced in gh_steps, including policies and procedures review that can take up to 90 days and Fire Life Safety building evaluation before the license is issued.

Certified Family Home (CFH)

Licensed by: Idaho Department of Health and Welfare. Serves: Adults who need some assistance with activities of daily living but do not require a more restrictive institutional setting; resident reasons for vulnerability include advanced age, mental illness, developmental disability and physical disability, per the DHW Certified Family Homes page. A home certified by DHW to care for between one and four adult residents who cannot live on their own, per the DHW page. Idaho Code 39-3505 states the care provider shall admit or retain no more than two residents concurrently, and the department may grant a variance for up to four residents, and the care provider shall live in the home as a primary residence. Certification is required when a paid caregiver provides services to any vulnerable adult residing in the caregiver home. Residents may be eligible for Medicaid assistance to pay the CFH provider.

Residential Habilitation Agency

Licensed by: Idaho Department of Health and Welfare, Division of Licensing and Certification, Therapeutic Residential Programs. Serves: Participants with developmental disabilities receiving residential habilitation, including supported living in the participant home and residential habilitation in a Certified Family Home. Certification is required: IDAPA 16.04.17 states no agency may provide services within the state until the Department has approved the application for certification and issued a certificate, and no agency may provide services without a current certificate. Under IDAPA 16.03.10.705, Adult DD Waiver residential habilitation by an agency must be provided by an agency certified as a Residential Habilitation Agency under IDAPA 16.04.17, so the Adult DD Waiver funds this residential service.

Developmental Disabilities Agency (DDA)

Licensed by: Idaho Department of Health and Welfare, Division of Licensing and Certification, Therapeutic Residential Programs. Serves: Eligible participants with developmental disabilities receiving center based and home and community based services. A DDA is a certified entity that provides center and home and community based services to eligible participants, per DHW search description of the DDA page. Certification steps on the DHW DDA page are listed in gh_steps. Center based services require a site visit. Rules are in IDAPA 16.03.21, cited on the DHW DDA page for the policy and procedure manual.

Intermediate Care Facility for Individuals with Intellectual Disabilities (ICF/IID)

Licensed by: Idaho Department of Health and Welfare, Bureau of Facility Standards. Serves: Four or more individuals with intellectual disabilities or related conditions, not related to the owner, who require 24 hour active treatment, per IDAPA 16.03.11 definitions in the Idaho Administrative Code archive. License required: IDAPA 16.03.11.020 states an ICF/IID cannot be established, maintained or operated in Idaho without obtaining a license from the Department as required in Sections 39-1301 through 39-1314, Idaho Code. DHW provider page states applicants for Medicaid or Medicare certification and state licensure as an ICF/IID must complete and submit an application packet, and to contact BFS at 208-334-6626, option 2, to set up an initial contact.

Childrens Residential Care Facility

Licensed by: Idaho Department of Health and Welfare, Division of Licensing and Certification, Therapeutic Residential Programs. Serves: Children in residential care programs, distinct from childrens agencies that provide child placing services for adoption and foster care. Childrens residential programs are governed by IDAPA 16.04.18, per the DHW childrens residential programs description in search results. The Legislature report lists Childrens Residential Care as state licensed and not federally certified, with 29 providers.

How to start a group home in Idaho, step by step

  1. For a Residential Assisted Living Facility: request access to the online portal
  2. Once portal access is approved, complete an application and submit it to Licensing and Certification
  3. Submit a set of policies and procedures for approval using the Policy and Procedure Checklist
  4. Upon submission and approval of the application, Fire Life Safety will schedule an on site evaluation of the building; correct items identified, notify Fire Life Safety and request a final building evaluation
  5. Once the building has final approval, policies and procedures have been reviewed and all application materials are submitted, a final review is conducted and, after any final corrections, the license is issued
  6. For a Developmental Disabilities Agency, per the DHW DDA page: register the agency with the Idaho Secretary of State, register with the DHW Background Check Unit for criminal history clearance for employees, complete an application, include a policy and procedure manual meeting IDAPA 16.03.21, and complete a site visit when center based services will be provided; once certified, complete Medicaid provider enrollment separately if seeking Medicaid payment

How long does it take in Idaho?

  • Residential Assisted Living Facility policies and procedures selected for review can take up to 90 days, depending on how quickly changes are made and submitted, per the DHW RALF licensing page

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get a group home license in Idaho?

Residential Assisted Living Facility policies and procedures selected for review can take up to 90 days, depending on how quickly changes are made and submitted, per the DHW RALF licensing page

What are the requirements to open a group home in Idaho?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Idaho?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
