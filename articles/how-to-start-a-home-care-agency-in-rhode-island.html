How to Start a Home Care Agency in Rhode Island (Two Home Licenses Under One Rule, and a Scaled First Charge)

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 19, 2026

The Rhode Island story starts with a founder who read the rule book carefully and still budgeted wrong twice. The rule text told her she needed a certificate of need before she could file for a home care license, so she priced a need application and a quarter of waiting into her launch plan. Then the fee schedule told her the license cost $650 a year, full stop, so she budgeted $650. Both readings came straight off state pages, and both were wrong by the time she filed. The certificate of need precondition printed in the rule was removed for her door by the FY2027 budget signed June 12, 2026, and the $650 is only the annual half of the first charge: a new applicant also pays the scaled initial licensure charge in the same fee schedule, computed on projected first-year revenue. Rhode Island is a small state with a dense rule set, two home licenses under one rule, a hospice door that kept the need review the home doors lost, and a Medicaid enrollment pause running on its own clock beside the licenses. Here is the full picture, lane by lane, in the order a founder actually meets it.

The demand: about 233,000 seniors, more than one in five residents

Start with why owners keep looking at Rhode Island. The Census Bureau puts the state population at 1,114,521 as of July 1, 2025, and 20.9 percent of residents are age 65 or older. Run the math and you get about 233,000 seniors, more than one in five people in the state, packed into the smallest state in the country. The same Census QuickFacts page for Rhode Island puts median household income at $87,796 for 2020 to 2024, the strongest income base in this batch of states, which is the base a private pay agency sells into.

Density cuts both ways. Every client in the state sits inside a short drive, and every competitor does too, which is why the license record and the screening file carry more weight here than marketing reach. The founder who files the right license, at the right classification of services, with the fingerprint check and registry checks already running, is the founder referral sources can describe without hedging.

The license question, answered directly: two licenses under 216-RICR-40-10-17

Here is the searched question this article exists to answer. Do you need a license to start a home care agency in Rhode Island? Yes, and which license depends on the services. Rhode Island licenses home-based care through the Center for Health Facilities Regulation of the Rhode Island Department of Health, under R.I. Gen. Laws chapter 23-17 and one rule, 216-RICR-40-10-17. Non-medical home care, personal care and homemaker services in the client home, is a home care provider license. Skilled nursing and therapy in the home is a home nursing care provider license. No person may establish, conduct, or maintain a home care provider or use the protected titles without a license, under Part 17, section 17.4.1, and homemaker duties are not nursing assistant tasks: the rule keeps the two scopes separate.

Hospice sits on a third door, the hospice provider license under 216-RICR-40-10-11. Medicare-certified home health is the home nursing care provider license plus federal certification under 42 CFR Part 484. The residential doors are separate license families again: assisted living residences under chapter 23-17.4, community residences and shared living arrangements licensed through the Department of Behavioral Health, Developmental Disabilities and Hospitals, adult day care programs under 216-RICR-40-10-7, and nursing facilities under chapter 23-17. An assisted living residence license authorizes no in-home agency work, and no in-home agency needs it. No one license covers another license's services.

The fee question, answered directly: the scaled first charge plus $650 a year

Read the fee schedule at 216-RICR-10-05-2, section 2.10, the way its heading reads. The line is headed Application for Initial Licensure or Changes in Owner, Operator or Lessee, and it carries a scaled charge of 0.2 percent of projected first-year net patient revenue, with a floor of $1,500 and a ceiling of $50,000. That heading covers a new applicant: the initial-licensure charge for new applicants is that scaled charge, and the new applicant pays it plus the $650 annual licensure fee. Budget the first filing as the scaled charge, somewhere between $1,500 and $50,000 depending on the revenue projection filed, plus $650. A budget that carries only the $650 annual fee into year one understates the state's first charge by at least $1,500. Every license under Part 17 expires December 31 of the year it is issued, and the licensing agency decides a licensure application within 90 days of acceptance, after a 30-day public comment window, under section 17.4.2.

The certificate of need position changed in June 2026, and the stale text is the central trap of this state. The FY2027 budget signed June 12, 2026 amended chapter 23-15 so certificate of need review for new facilities is limited to hospitals, nursing facilities, hospice, inpatient rehabilitation, and freestanding emergency care. Home care providers and home nursing care providers are no longer in that review, even though the unamended text of Part 17, section 17.4.1(B), still prints a certificate of need precondition for both home licenses. The enacted budget controls. Do not file a need application for a home license door, and do not pay the $500 need filing fee that no longer applies to it.

The non-medical lane: the home care provider license, step by step

First, confirm the service menu is non-skilled: hands-on personal care, homemaker, and companion work only, with no nursing, no therapy, and no skilled tasks, and document the door determination in the startup file. Second, form the business with the Rhode Island Secretary of State, hold the EIN and tax registrations, and put the operating accounts and insurance in place; Part 17 requires evidence of the ability to comply with chapter 23-17 and the rule with the application. Third, confirm the certificate of need removal before any other filing, on the June 2026 budget, not on the stale rule text. Fourth, file the home care provider application with the Center for Health Facilities Regulation with the section 2.10 charges, the scaled initial licensure charge plus the $650 annual fee. Fifth, build the screening systems before the first visit, covered in the background check section below. Sixth, hold services until the license issues: no home care provider may provide services or use the protected titles until the department licenses it. The skilled lane runs the same shape on the home nursing care provider license, for ongoing registered and practical nursing and the therapeutic services the rule lists, delivered under practitioner orders, at the same annual fee and the same scaled first charge.

The hospice lane: the door that kept its certificate of need

Hospice runs the other way on need review. A certificate of need is still a precondition to hospice licensure, and a hospice plan sequenced like a home care plan, with no need review, stops at licensure. Where need review applies, the filing fee is $500 plus 0.25 percent of capital cost for regular or accelerated review, or $750 plus 0.25 percent for expedited review, under section 23-15-10. File the need application first, then the hospice provider application with the $3,000 annual licensure fee under section 2.10. A nonprofit hospice program holding a current home nursing care provider license is exempt from that annual fee under section 23-17-38. Medicare certification runs under 42 CFR Part 418 on Form CMS-855A through PECOS to National Government Services, Jurisdiction K, which is both the A/B MAC and the Home Health and Hospice MAC for Rhode Island, with the CY2026 institutional enrollment fee of $750, and the federal Medicare enrollment moratorium gates initial enrollment on the certified tracks only (the extension is a six-month period, running to about May 12, 2027). The same federal gate, CMS-6101-N2 for home health and CMS-6102-N2 for hospice, never blocks a state license.

Background checks: the fingerprint check, the registry check, and the exclusion checks

Build the screening systems before the first visit: the national fingerprint background check under section 23-17-34, the nursing assistant registry check, and the OIG and SAM exclusion checks. In the files I review, the pattern that draws survey attention is checks run at hire and never again, and plan of care reviews drifting past the cycle the rules print. Sequence the fingerprint check early enough that a slow return does not hold a start date, file every result in the personnel record, and calendar the repeat checks the same day the first ones clear.

The Medicaid lane: enrollment runs on a separate clock to January 1, 2027

Rhode Island Medicaid is administered by the Executive Office of Health and Human Services, and provider enrollment runs through the Rhode Island Medicaid Healthcare Portal, with Gainwell Technologies as fiscal agent. Home care and home health services run under 210-RICR-20-05-1, paid fee-for-service and through the RIte Care plans, Neighborhood Health Plan of Rhode Island, Tufts Health Public Plans (RITogether), and UnitedHealthcare Community Plan, and Rhody Health Partners. Electronic visit verification is mandatory for the services that rule covers, through the state EVV system offered through Sandata, or an alternate third-party system that meets state requirements, billed by the type of service delivered.

Two state moratoria gate the enrollment steps, and their scope is exact. The Executive Office has instituted a six-month moratorium on newly enrolling hospice and home health providers, and a six-month moratorium on newly enrolling home care and personal care providers in the subgroups Severely Disabled Nursing Homecare (Type 65) and Personal Care Aide or Assistant (Type 72), both effective July 1, 2026 to January 1, 2027. No applications for enrollment will be approved for new providers in those groups during the period. The moratoria gate Medicaid enrollment only: they do not pause department licensure, certificate of need review, or Medicare certification, and they do not affect providers already enrolled or applications submitted before July 1, 2026. Sequence the license first and time the enrollment filing against the January 1, 2027 end date the notices print.

What you still do today: form the business the Rhode Island way

Form the business with the Rhode Island Secretary of State, hold the EIN and tax registrations, and put the operating accounts and insurance in place. Part 17 asks for evidence of the ability to comply with the statute and the rule with the application, so the operating record is part of the filing, not an afterthought: liability insurance in place before the first client, the policy file built to the license held, the screening systems running, and a service agreement that states what the agency does and does not do, with skilled tasks on the does not do side of the line for a home care provider. No Rhode Island formation fee is printed in the sources checked for this article, so I print none: confirm the current filing fee on the day of filing.

The practical warning

Rhode Island punishes stale reading. File a certificate of need application for a home care door on the unamended Part 17 text and the cost is the filing fee plus a quarter spent waiting on a review the June 2026 budget abolished for that door. Build a hospice plan with no need review and the plan stops at licensure, because hospice kept its precondition in the same budget. Budget $650 as the whole first charge and the scaled initial licensure charge arrives as a surprise of at least $1,500. File a Medicaid enrollment inside the moratorium window for a subgroup the moratorium covers and the application comes back unapproved. And deliver skilled services on the home care provider door, the most expensive scope error in this state, and the license record follows the owner into every later filing. Read the enacted budget and the fee schedule heading, not the summary, and this state files clean.

Non-medical care uses the home care provider license, skilled nursing and therapy use the home nursing care provider license, and hospice uses its own provider license under 216-RICR-40-10-11 with a certificate of need precondition. How much does a home care license cost in Rhode Island?
