How to Start a Group Home in Georgia

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Georgia. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Georgia licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Georgia licenses

Personal Care Home (PCH)

Licensed by: DCH HFRD; Ga. Comp. R. 111-8-62, statute O.C.G.A. 31-7-12 (Rule authority is Title 31, Chapters 2 and 7). Serves: Two or more adults not related to the owner or administrator, needing housing, food service and one or more personal services. Georgia calls the authorization a permit, not a license; one permit, no separate small home license class, capacity stated on the permit; fee bands under 25 beds, 25 to 50 beds, 51 or more beds; 25 beds is the hinge for licensed administrator, CPA financial stability affidavit, Letter of Determination, food service permit and two-staff rule; sprinkler required for 7 or more beds; Memory Care Certificate inside the permit under Rule 111-8-62-.19 for $200 per year; Medicaid door is EDWP Alternative Living Services, Group Model 7 to 24 members and Family Model 2 to 6 beds, with no Medicare door for a PCH, a proven negative in the research

Assisted Living Community (ALC)

Licensed by: DCH HFRD; O.C.G.A. 31-7-12.2, Ga. Comp. R. 111-8-63. Serves: 25 or more residents in a personal care home licensed to provide assisted living care. Assisted living care means personal services, medication administration by a certified medication aide, assisted self-preservation and limited nursing services; a PCH permit alone does not authorize assisted living care; Memory Care Certificate sits inside this license as well

Community Living Arrangement (CLA)

Licensed by: DBHDD from January 1, 2026 under HB 584 (2025 session); Ga. Comp. R. 290-9-37, with DBHDD proposed Chapter 82-13-1 noticed July 22, 2026. Serves: Two or more adults whose services are supported in whole or in part by DBHDD funds. Filed in GAHLES using the DBHDD CLA application packet, Version October 2026; proposed text caps capacity at no more than four residents; a CLA cannot operate on the same premises as a PCH; CLA-only CRA agencies are limited to three CLA sites under the NOW/COMP manual section 608.2.3.1

Community Residential Alternative (CRA) group home

Licensed by: DCH COMP and NOW Provider Manual Part II, version July 1, 2026, on GAMMIS; sites licensed as CLA or PCH. Serves: NOW and COMP waiver participants in a group home setting. CRA Standard, Intensive and Specialized (COMP only for Specialized as listed); CRA has a 344-day annual unit limit and group home and host home rate structure in Part III Chapter 2300 section 2308

CRA host home / life-sharing

Licensed by: DBHDD provider system and Policy 02-704; enrolled through a CRA provider agency. Serves: 1 to 2 persons living in a private residence. Enrollment track, no own license; the provider number belongs to the agency, not the host home

Adult Residential Mental Health Program (ARMHP)

Licensed by: DBHDD from January 1, 2026 under HB 584; Ga. Comp. R. 111-8-2, O.C.G.A. 37-3-200 et seq.. Serves: Adults 18 or older in a subacute residential alternative, 4 or more beds, 24/7. Excludes CSUs, CLAs, accountability court programs and public beds in the rule definition cited

DBHDD behavioral health residential services, CRR I to IV and related services

Licensed by: DBHDD Provider Manual for Community Behavioral Health Providers, FY2027 Quarter 2, effective October 1 to December 31, 2026. Serves: Adults served in Community Residential Rehabilitation and related residential services. Service enrollment track, not a standalone site license: services listed are CRR I to IV, Independent, Semi-Independent and Intensive MH and AD residential, residential substance detoxification, Womens Treatment and Recovery residential, Crisis Respite Apartments and Structured Residential Supports; the site license is the ARMHP, CLA, PCH or DATEP license as the setting requires; CRR levels I and II detail in the research: 24/7 awake or on-site staff, licensed community-living setting, in no case exceeding 16 beds, minimum five hours weekly of skills training; PRTF is a register entry inside this track, with no standalone Georgia license chapter found

Crisis Stabilization Unit (CSU) / Behavioral Health Crisis Center

Licensed by: DBHDD; Ga. Comp. R. 82-3-1, O.C.G.A. 37-1-29. Serves: People needing short-term residential psychiatric stabilization and detoxification. A BHCC is a CSU with Crisis Service Center and Temporary Observation functions; licensure is not an entitlement to DBHDD funding; no fee figure is published in Chapter 82-3-1 as read, so no fee is quoted

DATEP residential programs, Residential Intensive Treatment and Residential Sub-Acute Detoxification

Licensed by: DBHDD Chapter 82-10-1 after HB 584 (formerly Ga. Comp. R. 111-8-19); O.C.G.A. 26-5-1 et seq.. Serves: People needing residential drug abuse treatment or sub-acute detoxification. Recovery residences and sober living homes are a register entry inside this track: O.C.G.A. 26-5-80 defines recovery residence, but non-clinical peer-supported recovery residences are not licensed, certified or registered by the state and GARR certification is voluntary, a proven negative; Narcotic Treatment Programs are a named register entry, an outpatient clinic door, not a home

Child-Caring Institution (CCI), including QRTP and Commercial Sexual Exploitation Recovery Center classes

Licensed by: DHS Residential Child Care Licensing (RCCL); Ga. Comp. R. 290-2-5, O.C.G.A. 49-5-1 et seq.. Serves: 6 or more children; more than 16 on premises needs RCCL approval. License, no charge, per the DHS CCI page; Child-Placing Agency and foster homes (capped at 6 children, approved through a CCI or CPA) are register entries inside this track, not facility licenses

Childrens Transition Care Center (CTCC)

Licensed by: DHS RCCL; Ga. Comp. R. 290-2-6. Serves: Children in transitional care toward home or family reunion, no more than 6 per residence and 16 per campus. Separate RCCL track; no fee figure is published in the rule subjects read, so no fee is quoted

Outdoor Child-Caring Program (OCCP)

Licensed by: DHS RCCL; Ga. Comp. R. 290-2-7. Serves: Children in an outdoor child-caring program. Application at least 90 days before opening; pre-application training by the Department required; no fee figure is published in the rule subjects read

Maternity Home

Licensed by: DHS RCCL; Ga. Comp. R. 290-2-29, O.C.G.A. 49-5-3, 49-5-8 and 49-5-12. Serves: Residents served by a maternity home as defined in the RCCL system. Separate RCCL track; maternity supportive housing residences registered under O.C.G.A. 49-5-25 are an exemption noted in the rule summary; no fee figure is published in the rule subjects read

Adult Day Center, social and medical models, including mobile adult day center

Licensed by: DCH HFRD; Ga. Comp. R. 111-8-1. Serves: Aging adults, 3 or more persons, for less than 24 hours a day. Non-residential: adult day care (social model) or adult day health services (medical model)

Nursing Home / Nursing Facility

Licensed by: DCH HFRD; Ga. Comp. R. 111-8-56 permit. Serves: Residents needing skilled nursing facility care. CON for new or expanded skilled nursing, intermediate care or intermingled facilities under Rule 111-2-2-.30; Medicare SNF under 42 CFR Part 483 Subpart B and Part 488 on CMS-855A

Intermediate Care Home, Georgias ICF/IID license class

Licensed by: DCH HFRD; Ga. Comp. R. 111-8-47. Serves: Individuals with intellectual or developmental disabilities needing intermediate care. Federal ICF/IID Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 483 Subpart I and Part 442; CON under Rule 111-2-2-.30; administration under a licensed nursing home administrator in the source summary

Traumatic Brain Injury Facility (TBIF)

Licensed by: DCH HFRD; Ga. Comp. R. 111-8-71 permit. Serves: People needing treatment and rehabilitative care for 24 hours or longer, with levels at Rule .11 including Transitional Living. Initial application through the DCH forms intake, with the $300 application processing fee noted on the DCH TBIF page in the research

How to start a group home in Georgia, step by step

  1. For the main residential track, Personal Care Home: register the entity with the Georgia Secretary of State if applicable and obtain city or county zoning approval documentation, because the applicant must address local zoning with responsible local officials under Rule 111-8-62-.05(7)
  2. Assemble the fourteen initial application items in the DCH checklist: Secretary of State evidence, zoning documentation, notarized Affidavit of Personal Identification under O.C.G.A. 50-36-1, photo ID copy, proof of ownership or legal control, fire safety inspection report with no violations dated within 12 months (sprinkler required for 7 or more beds), Electrical Inspection Compliance Form dated within 6 months, floor sketch with rooms labeled and bed placement, electronic Owner Form in GAHLES, GCHEXS satisfactory determination letters dated within 12 months, water and sewage approval, and, for 25 or more beds only, the DCH Office of Health Planning Letter of Determination and the CPA Affidavit of Financial Stability
  3. Establish all policies and procedures before filing and execute the notarized Affidavit of Compliance attesting they exist for Rule 111-8-62 and will be implemented on approval; policies are attested, not uploaded, and are produced at unannounced inspections
  4. Submit the complete packet online in GAHLES at gahles.dch.georgia.gov with the $300 application processing fee plus the initial fee, which equals the annual fee for the bed band; do not submit until every document is ready, because incomplete submission may cause delay, rejection and forfeiture of fees
  5. Respond to HFRD requests for additional documentation arriving from the HFRD do-not-reply address with a portal link and verification code; failure to upload requested documents results in denial
  6. Operate only with a current permit, at or below approved licensed capacity, with one permit per premises
  7. For the Medicaid ALS door after the PCH permit: hold a nonrestrictive permit, demonstrate direct provision of services and complete 12 consecutive months in business, attend the EDWP Enrollment Information Session within the 6 months before filing (sessions in February and August), file in the September 1 to 30 GAMMIS window for both CCSP (category 590) and SOURCE (category 930), complete CVO and DCH review and any site visit, and complete mandatory New Provider Training before a Medicaid provider number is assigned
  8. For a CLA group home instead of a PCH: file in GAHLES using the DBHDD CLA application packet, Version October 2026, with background checks through DBHDD, to the DBHDD licensure address stated in the packet, and confirm the current rule chapter on filing day because DBHDD Chapter 82-13-1 was proposed, not final, in the sources read

Georgia group home license fees and costs

  • Application processing fee, any HFRD program: $300 on submission (Rule 111-8-25-.03(8) and fee schedule); initial license fee is the same as the annual fee for the program
  • PCH annual fee: $350 for 2 to 24 beds (rule prints under 25 beds), $750 for 25 to 50 beds, $1,500 for 51 or more beds; worked filing totals for a new PCH are $650, $1,050 and $1,800 by band
  • Memory Care Certificate (ALC or PCH): $200 per year
  • Assisted Living Community: $750 per year for 25 to 50 beds; $1,500 per year for 51 or more beds
  • Community Living Arrangements: $350 per year on the DCH schedule and Rule 111-8-25, labeled in the research as the last published schedule figure after the DBHDD transfer, to be confirmed with DBHDD at filing
  • Adult Day Centers: $250 (social) / $350 (medical) per year
  • Adult Residential Mental Health Programs: $800 per year on the DCH schedule, labeled as the last published schedule figure after the DBHDD transfer
  • Drug Abuse Treatment Programs (DATEP): $500 per year on the DCH schedule, labeled as the last published schedule figure after the DBHDD transfer
  • Intermediate Care Facilities: $250 per year
  • Nursing Homes: $500 per year for 1 to 99 beds; $750 per year for 100 or more beds
  • Follow-up visit $250; late fee $150; permit replacement $50; involuntary application processing fee $550; list of facilities by license type $25
  • CCI license (RCCL): no charge, per the DHS CCI program page; CTCC, OCCP and Maternity Home: no fee figure is published in the rule subjects read, so no fee is quoted
  • CSU license (DBHDD): no fee figure is published in Chapter 82-3-1 as read, so no fee is quoted
  • PCH accreditation discount: Rule 111-8-25 marks PCH with a 25 percent discount asterisk for Department-approved accreditation at renewal, but the schedule list names no PCH-specific accreditor, so no accreditor is named for PCH in the research
  • Federal institutional enrollment fee: $750, calendar year 2026; GAMMIS, NOW/COMP and DBHDD provider enrollment: no state enrollment fee figure is published in the manuals and pages read, so no fee is quoted; EDWP ALS enrollment fee is unverified in the research and must not be quoted

How long does it take in Georgia?

  • PCH licensure processing time: HFRD publishes no processing timeline for PCH permits in the checklist, Rule 111-8-62 or Rule 111-8-25, a proven negative in the research; the 30 business day figure in other HFRD checklists must not be imported
  • CLA packet: initial review within 30 business days, per the DBHDD CLA application packet, Version October 2026, as cited in the expansion research
  • OCCP: application at least 90 days before opening, with pre-application training by the Department required
  • Annual HFRD fees due October 31, collected through December 31 without penalty, $150 late fee added January 1; a home licensed in 2026 owes the renewal in the October to December 2026 window for 2027; revocation exposure for failure to pay annually recurring fees within 60 days of a written invoice under Rule 111-8-25-.03(7)
  • PCH plan of correction due within 10 days of an inspection report; unannounced compliance inspections approximately every 11 to 15 months or as needed, per the DCH program page
  • Fire report within 12 months and electrical report within 6 months at PCH filing
  • EDWP ALS timing: information sessions in February and August, attendance within 6 months before filing, ALS filing window September 1 to 30, CVO review can take up to 51 business days, 12 months prior business experience required before applying; ICWP ALS requires at least one year as an approved CCSP ALS provider first and GAMMIS enrollment of each PCH within 30 calendar days of subcontract
  • HB 584 transfers of CLA, ARMHP and DATEP licensing to DBHDD took effect January 1, 2026; DBHDD CLA Chapter 82-13-1 was proposed July 22, 2026 and not final in the sources read

What are the requirements to open a group home in Georgia?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Georgia?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
