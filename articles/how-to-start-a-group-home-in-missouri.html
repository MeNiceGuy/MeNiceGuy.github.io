How to Start a Group Home in Missouri

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Missouri. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Missouri licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Missouri licenses

Residential Care Facility (formerly RCF I)

Licensed by: Missouri Department of Health and Senior Services, Section for Long-Term Care Regulation. Serves: Three or more residents who need or are provided with shelter, board and protective oversight. Provides 24 hour care, which may include storage, distribution or administration of medications and care during short-term illness or recuperation. Residents are required to make a path to safety unassisted. A licensed Nursing Home Administrator is not required. Licensed under the DHSS long-term care framework in chapter 198, RSMo.

Residential Care Facility (RCF, continues to comply with former RCF II requirements)

Licensed by: Missouri Department of Health and Senior Services, Section for Long-Term Care Regulation. Serves: Three or more residents who need or are provided with supervision of diets, assistance in personal care and protective oversight. Provides 24 hour accommodation, board and care, including storage, distribution or administration of medications and supervision of health care under the direction of a licensed physician, including care during short-term illness or recuperation. Residents are required to make a path to safety unassisted. A licensed Nursing Home Administrator is required.

Assisted Living Facility (ALF)

Licensed by: Missouri Department of Health and Senior Services, Section for Long-Term Care Regulation. Serves: Residents who are provided shelter and board and who may need assistance with activities of daily living including eating, dressing, bathing, toileting, transferring and walking. Provides 24 hour care, services and protective oversight, including oversight for storage, distribution or administration of medications and health care supervision under the direction of a licensed physician, consistent with a social model of care. A licensed Nursing Home Administrator is required. This type may accept or retain residents who require minimal assistance in their safe evacuation.

Assisted Living Facility (ALF, able to admit residents needing more than minimal assistance to safely evacuate)

Licensed by: Missouri Department of Health and Senior Services, Section for Long-Term Care Regulation. Serves: Residents needing assistance with activities of daily living who have an impairment that prevents safe evacuation with minimal assistance. Same ALF service description as the standard ALF, and a licensed Nursing Home Administrator is required. The facility may accept or retain a resident who cannot evacuate with minimal assistance only if it meets certain staffing requirements to assist in evacuations and includes an individualized evacuation plan in the resident service plan.

Intermediate Care Facility

Licensed by: Missouri Department of Health and Senior Services, Section for Long-Term Care Regulation. Serves: Three or more residents dependent for care and supervision. Provides 24 hour accommodation, board, personal care and basic health and nursing care services under the daily supervision of a licensed nurse and the direction of a licensed physician. A licensed Nursing Home Administrator is required.

Skilled Nursing Facility

Licensed by: Missouri Department of Health and Senior Services, Section for Long-Term Care Regulation. Serves: At least three residents requiring 24 hour care by licensed nursing personnel. Provides 24 hour accommodation, board and skilled nursing care and treatment services, commonly performed by or under the supervision of a registered professional nurse. A licensed Nursing Home Administrator is required.

Group Home, Individualized Supported Living and Shared Living for intellectual and developmental disabilities

Licensed by: Missouri Department of Mental Health, Division of Developmental Disabilities; home and community-based providers serving persons with intellectual and developmental disabilities are certified under 9 CSR 45-5.010 as cited by DMH. Serves: Individuals with intellectual and developmental disabilities who meet the Intermediate Care Facility for Individuals with Intellectual Disabilities level of care and are at risk of needing ICF/ID institutional services if waiver services are not provided. DMH states the Comprehensive Waiver is the only waiver that provides residential services: Group Home, Shared Living and Individualized Supported Living services. Residential habilitation settings include Group Home, Individualized Supported Living and Shared Living. The participant needs must also be determined not to be met in the Community Support Waiver for Comprehensive Waiver eligibility as DMH describes it.

Residential facilities for persons with a mental disorder, mental illness or developmental disability (DMH licensed)

Licensed by: Missouri Department of Mental Health. Serves: Persons described in RSMo section 630.705, including persons diagnosed with a mental disorder, mental illness or developmental disability. RSMo section 630.715 requires the Department of Mental Health to establish a procedure for licensing residential facilities and day programs for these persons. License fees in that section are $10 for establishments which accept more than three but less than ten persons and $50 for establishments which accept ten or more; no fee is charged for a governmental unit or a facility caring for less than four persons.

How to start a group home in Missouri, step by step

  1. For a long-term care facility, the DHSS Section for Long-Term Care Regulation receives applications for licensure and certification, issues licenses to facilities meeting licensure criteria, and certifies facilities for Medicare or Medicaid participation
  2. File the Application for License to Operate a Long Term Care Facility (Form MO 580-2631, DA-150) with the Section for Long-Term Care Regulation, with the licensure fee for each level of care
  3. Resolve certificate of need where it applies: the Missouri Health Facilities Review Committee reviews and approves or disapproves certificate of need applications under RSMo sections 197.300 to 197.366, and the DHSS Certificate of Need Law page sets the expenditure minimums that trigger review for beds in facilities licensed under chapter 198

Missouri group home license fees and costs

  • Long-term care facility licensure fee on the DHSS application form MO 580-2631: $100 for a facility with 3 to 24 beds, $300 for 25 to 100 beds, $600 for more than 100 beds; each level of care is licensed separately and the fee is nonrefundable unless the facility withdraws the application within ten days of receipt
  • Department of Mental Health residential facility license fee under RSMo section 630.715: $10 for establishments accepting more than three but less than ten persons, $50 for establishments accepting ten or more, and no fee for a governmental unit or a facility caring for less than four persons
  • Certificate of need application fee under RSMo section 197.315: $1,000 or one-tenth of one percent of the total cost of the proposed project, whichever is greater, where a certificate of need application is required

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to open a group home in Missouri?

The figures Missouri publishes are these: Long-term care facility licensure fee on the DHSS application form MO 580-2631: $100 for a facility with 3 to 24 beds, $300 for 25 to 100 beds, $600 for more than 100 beds; each level of care is licensed separately and the fee is nonrefundable unless the facility withdraws the application within ten days of receipt Department of Mental Health residential facility license fee under RSMo section 630.715: $10 for establishments accepting more than three but less than ten persons, $50 for establishments accepting ten or more, and no fee for a governmental unit or a facility caring for less than four persons Certificate of need application fee under RSMo section 197.315: $1,000 or one-tenth of one percent of the total cost of the proposed project, whichever is greater, where a certificate of need application is required The license fee is rarely the largest cost. The home itself, life safety and accessibility work, insurance, and staffing hired before the first placement carry the project.

What are the requirements to open a group home in Missouri?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Missouri?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
