How to Start a Home Health Agency in Maryland

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Maryland licenses home health agencies as a Home Health Agency license, through the Office of Health Care Quality (OHCQ), Maryland Department of Health, with Certificate of Need through the Maryland Health Care Commission (MHCC). The governing law is Health-General Title 19, Subtitle 4; COMAR 10.07.10; Certificate of Need under Health-General section 19-114(b) and COMAR 10.24.16. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the Maryland license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The Maryland license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Maryland, step by step

  1. Face the Certificate of Need first: obtain a certificate of need or an exemption from MHCC before applying for the license (Health-General section 19-114(b); COMAR 10.24.16); need is decided by jurisdiction under the State Health Plan chapter for home health agency services on MHCC's review schedule, and the Residential Service Agency license has no Certificate of Need and does not authorize operating as a home health agency
  2. Form the entity and prove good standing: register the business with SDAT and obtain the official letter of good standing through Maryland Business Express, and disclose ownership as the application directs
  3. Name the clinical leadership: services are provided under the supervision of a physician or registered professional nurse, and the governing structure, administrator, and supervising clinician are named before filing (COMAR 10.07.10)
  4. Adopt the home health policy set required by COMAR 10.07.10, including the procedures the application collects under COMAR 10.07.10.04
  5. Bind workers' compensation coverage and attach the declaration page, or the Certificate of Compliance or Letter of Exemption that fits the entity and staffing
  6. File the typed Application for a Home Health Agency License through OHCQ with the Certificate of Need documentation and required attachments; the current application states there is no fee to apply, and OHCQ holds an incomplete application for 180 days before it is administratively closed
  7. Pass the licensure survey: OHCQ schedules an on-site survey after the application and attachments are approved; with no deficiencies the agency receives a Notice of Compliance and the license, and with deficiencies it receives a Statement of Deficiencies and has 10 business days to submit a Plan of Correction, with a provisional license possible in certain circumstances (COMAR 10.07.10.06)
  8. For Medicare certification, complete federal enrollment through PECOS on the CMS-855A after the state license and demonstrate 42 CFR Part 484 at the certification survey conducted by OHCQ as the state survey agency; Maryland Medicaid home health enrollment, where planned, is its own MPRIME application after licensure

Maryland home health license fees and costs

  • There is no fee to apply for a license: the current OHCQ application forms for the Home Health Agency, Residential Service Agency, Nursing Referral Service Agency, general hospice, and assisted living licenses each state that line verbatim (forms checked 2026-11-11 and re-verified for the expansion forms on 2026-11-12)
  • Legacy fee text is not the current charge: COMAR 10.07.05.04 as published still prints a $1,000 fee for the three-year Residential Service Agency license and $1,000 at renewal, and an old copy of the Home Health Agency chapter printed a $350 figure; the research does not quote the $350 figure as current and instructs the owner not to mail a check based on printed rule text
  • Residential Service Agency amended license: $50 fee stated in COMAR 10.07.05.05 for an amended license on a change of name or address, with a fine of up to $500 per violation of the change-notice rule in the same section

How long does it take in Maryland?

  • Home Health Agency license is valid for 1 year from issuance, and renewal is filed at least 60 days before expiration (COMAR 10.07.10.04); the license may not be transferred or reassigned and is void if the agency ceases to operate
  • Incomplete OHCQ applications are held 180 days from initial receipt and then administratively closed, and a Plan of Correction is due within 10 business days of a Statement of Deficiencies (application forms checked 2026-11-11)
  • No Home Health Agency processing duration is published in the research; the only OHCQ-published processing figure found is for the Nursing Referral Service Agency, generally issued 1 to 2 months after a complete application

What catches Maryland applicants

  • Certificate of need: a Home Health Agency may not apply for licensure before obtaining a Certificate of Need or an exemption from MHCC; a general hospice also needs a Certificate of Need under COMAR 10.24.13, while a limited hospice is exempt from that Certificate of Need and may provide nonskilled palliative services only
  • Category split: the Residential Service Agency license under COMAR 10.07.05 is a different door and does not authorize operating as a home health agency; Maryland has a single RSA license with services listed on the application and no RSA levels and no companion-only license
  • Household and family support below the RSA definition (light housekeeping, meals, shopping, and errands only) is outside COMAR 10.07.05 under section 10.07.05.03B, and Maryland has no exemption filing for it; that boundary does not carry Medicaid personal assistance billing
  • Medicaid enrollment is separate from licensure and runs through MPRIME, which replaced ePREP on October 13, 2026 (ePREP access ended October 1, 2026 and drafts and denied applications did not transfer); newly enrolling providers with a complete, processable MPRIME application submitted on or before December 31, 2026 are eligible for a backdated enrollment effective date
  • Maryland Medicare Administrative Contractors in the research: Novitas Solutions, Jurisdiction L for institutional enrollment, and CGS, Jurisdiction 15, for home health and hospice claims
  • Medicaid personal assistance programs (Community First Choice at COMAR 10.09.84, Community Personal Assistance Services at 10.09.20, Community Options Waiver at 10.09.54, and Increased Community Services at 10.09.81) are payer enrollments delivered through the RSA and Assisted Living Program licenses, not standalone licenses, and since January 1, 2026 Medicaid reimbursement for personal assistance services is limited to employees under the Homecare Worker Rights Act of 2024

The Medicare layer, after the Maryland license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

What do I need to start a home health agency in Maryland?

The Home Health Agency license (Office of Health Care Quality (OHCQ), Maryland Department of Health, with Certificate of Need through the Maryland Health Care Commission (MHCC)), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet Maryland rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in Maryland?

Apply to the Office of Health Care Quality (OHCQ), Maryland Department of Health, with Certificate of Need through the Maryland Health Care Commission (MHCC) under Health-General Title 19, Subtitle 4; COMAR 10.07.10; Certificate of Need under Health-General section 19-114(b) and COMAR 10.24.16. The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
