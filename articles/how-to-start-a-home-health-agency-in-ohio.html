How to Start a Home Health Agency in Ohio

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Ohio licenses home health agencies as a Skilled home health services license, through the Ohio Department of Health (ODH). The governing law is ORC 3740.03(A) and OAC 3701-60-03(A), application on ODH form HEA 0621. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the Ohio license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The Ohio license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Ohio, step by step

  1. Confirm the skilled scope honestly: skilled nursing, physical therapy, speech-language pathology, occupational therapy, or medical social services (OAC 3701-60-01(Z)); a skilled license also covers nonmedical services, so the applicant does not file twice
  2. Form the entity with the Ohio Secretary of State and set the Ohio office; an agency operating in another state establishes an administrative office in Ohio before applying and keeps Ohio patient records at that office (OAC 3701-60-03(D))
  3. Pick one of the four standing routes and gather its proof (OAC 3701-60-03(A)(3)): Medicare certification, accreditation by a CMS-approved national accrediting organization recognized by the department, Ohio Department of Aging certification under ORC 173.391, or a notarized attestation that the agency otherwise meets the Medicare conditions of participation
  4. Complete ODH form HEA 0621 with the operating location, every owner and controlling interest, corporate officers and statutory agent, the list of skilled services, the geographic area, and the written criminal records check policy
  5. Provide the bond or grandfather proof: evidence of providing direct care on or immediately prior to September 30, 2021, or a $50,000 surety bond from an Ohio-licensed surety
  6. Pay the $250 fee by cashier's check or postal money order payable to Treasurer, State of Ohio, and submit with the application to ODH as printed on form HEA 0621
  7. Prepare for the ODH site visit if applying on the Medicare route, to verify the Medicare conditions of participation, unless a site visit occurred within the preceding five years (ORC 3740.04)
  8. For Medicare certification, build the clinical operation to 42 CFR Part 484, meet the federal aide floor of 75 hours of training and competency evaluation plus 12 hours of in-service per 12 months (42 CFR 484.80), pass the certification survey, and enroll on the CMS-855A through PECOS
  9. Enroll separately for Ohio Medicaid through the Provider Network Management (PNM) module, the single point of enrollment since October 1, 2022, using OH|ID sign-in, with no paper applications

Ohio home health license fees and costs

  • Skilled home health services license: $250 application fee, non-refundable, and a $250 renewal fee, on a three-year term
  • Nonmedical home health services license: $250 application fee, non-refundable, and a $250 renewal fee, on a three-year term
  • Surety bond: $50,000 on the skilled license and $20,000 on the nonmedical license, from a surety company licensed in Ohio, unless the applicant proves it provided direct care on or immediately prior to September 30, 2021
  • Home health license fees are paid by cashier's check or postal money order
  • Hospice care program license, a separate ODH license: $600 license fee for the three-year term, non-refundable, the same $600 at renewal, and a $200 change of ownership fee; licensure inspection fee $1,625, complaint inspection $850, follow-up inspection $350, desk audit or compliance review $250 (OAC 3701-19-03 and 3701-19-05)

How long does it take in Ohio?

  • Home health license term is three years; renewal is filed at least 90 days before expiration (OAC 3701-60-03(H) and ORC 3740.05)
  • Additional information the director requests after filing is due within 14 days of the mailing date of the request (OAC 3701-60-03(G)), and changes to application information are reported in writing within 10 days (OAC 3701-60-03(I))
  • ODH publishes no processing-time target for home health licenses; the research states none

What catches Ohio applicants

  • No certificate of need is stated in the research for an Ohio home health license
  • Category split: Ohio licenses nonmedical and skilled home health services separately under ORC 3740.03(B) and 3740.03(A), and a skilled license also covers nonmedical services
  • Medicaid enrollment is separate from licensure and runs through ODM PNM; the ODM moratorium covers the DODD waiver doors at PNM types 45 and 55
  • Medicare certification is a layer on the skilled license: certification survey first, then CMS-855A enrollment through PECOS, and services before the enrollment effective date are not payable
  • Ohio licensing rules set no fixed caregiver training hour count for the state license; the 75-hour and 12-hour figures are the federal Medicare aide floor at 42 CFR 484.80

The Medicare layer, after the Ohio license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a home health agency in Ohio?

The figures Ohio publishes are these: Skilled home health services license: $250 application fee, non-refundable, and a $250 renewal fee, on a three-year term Nonmedical home health services license: $250 application fee, non-refundable, and a $250 renewal fee, on a three-year term Surety bond: $50,000 on the skilled license and $20,000 on the nonmedical license, from a surety company licensed in Ohio, unless the applicant proves it provided direct care on or immediately prior to September 30, 2021 Home health license fees are paid by cashier's check or postal money order Hospice care program license, a separate ODH license: $600 license fee for the three-year term, non-refundable, the same $600 at renewal, and a $200 change of ownership fee; licensure inspection fee $1,625, complaint inspection $850, follow-up inspection $350, desk audit or compliance review $250 (OAC 3701-19-03 and 3701-19-05) Beyond the published figures, the operating costs (clinical payroll before certification, insurance, and the reserve operating funds required by 42 CFR 489.28) are larger than every fee on the list.

How long does it take to get a home health license in Ohio?

Home health license term is three years; renewal is filed at least 90 days before expiration (OAC 3701-60-03(H) and ORC 3740.05) Additional information the director requests after filing is due within 14 days of the mailing date of the request (OAC 3701-60-03(G)), and changes to application information are reported in writing within 10 days (OAC 3701-60-03(I)) ODH publishes no processing-time target for home health licenses; the research states none The federal certification that follows runs on the survey calendar, and new Medicare enrollment is under a CMS moratorium to about mid-May 2027.

What do I need to start a home health agency in Ohio?

The Skilled home health services license (Ohio Department of Health (ODH)), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet Ohio rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in Ohio?

Apply to the Ohio Department of Health (ODH) under ORC 3740.03(A) and OAC 3701-60-03(A), application on ODH form HEA 0621. The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
