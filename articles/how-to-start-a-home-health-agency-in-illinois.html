How to Start a Home Health Agency in Illinois

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Illinois licenses home health agencies as a Home Health Agency license, through the Illinois Department of Public Health (IDPH). The governing law is Home Health, Home Services, and Home Nursing Agency Licensing Act, 210 ILCS 55; 77 Ill. Adm. Code Part 245 (Home Health, Home Services, and Home Nursing Agency Code). That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the Illinois license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The Illinois license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Illinois, step by step

  1. File the licensure application with IDPH on IDPH forms for the home health agency license under 245.90, with a Certificate of Insurance documenting minimum liability coverage of $1 million per occurrence and $3 million in the aggregate (245.90(a)(2))
  2. Pay the $1,500 fee for the two-year home health agency license (245.95(d)); if applying for multiple Part 245 licenses, pay the fee for each and operate any dual agency and placement operation as separate entities (245.95(e))
  3. Receive the provisional license for a new agency: IDPH will issue it within 90 days after receipt of a complying application, and it is valid for 240 days (245.100(a))
  4. Complete the provisional period and survey and receive the full home health agency license, which expires two years from the end of the month in which the provisional license was first issued
  5. For Medicare, complete Form CMS-855A through PECOS to the Medicare Administrative Contractor and complete the other Medicare forms, including CMS-1572 and CMS-1561 and Office for Civil Rights clearance as listed on the IDPH Medicare information sheet
  6. Complete the initial Medicare certification survey by an accreditation organization (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission); if the accrediting organization has not completed the survey within 150 days from the date of the approved 855A, IDPH is mandated to perform the initial certification survey
  7. For Medicaid, enroll separately through IMPACT (the single Illinois Medicaid enrollment system) as a Facility/Agency/Organization provider; Medicare-certified HHAs enroll for Medicaid through IMPACT as a separate enrollment from Medicare certification
  8. File renewal not less than 60 days and not more than 90 days before expiration of the current license (245.90(b)(1))

Illinois home health license fees and costs

  • Home health agency license fee: $1,500 for a two-year license (77 Ill. Adm. Code 245.95(d), amended effective September 2, 2025); fee is not refundable
  • Home services agency and home nursing agency license fee: $1,500 per year (245.95(b); rule cap is not to exceed $1,500 annually and IDPH charges the full amount)
  • Home services placement agency and home nursing placement agency license fee: $500 per year (245.95(c))
  • Applicants for multiple licenses pay the fee for each license (245.95(a))
  • Federal institutional provider enrollment fee: $750 for calendar year 2026 (CMS; FR 2025-21877); no separate Illinois IMPACT enrollment fee is published by HFS
  • Stale-form warning: an older IDPH combined form printing a $25 home health fee is superseded by 245.95(d) and must not be used

How long does it take in Illinois?

  • Provisional license for a new agency: issued within 90 days after receipt of the application and valid for 240 days (245.100(a)); a separate change-of-ownership provisional provision caps that term at 120 days
  • Home health agency full license term: 2 years; home services, home nursing, home services placement, and home nursing placement licenses: 1 year
  • Renewal filed 60 to 90 days before expiration (245.90(b)(1))
  • Medicare backstop: IDPH performs the initial certification survey if the accrediting organization has not surveyed within 150 days of the approved 855A
  • IMPACT revalidation at least every 5 years under 42 CFR 455.414, with HFS noting April 2026 CMS guidance moving certain providers to an upcoming 2-year cycle (perishable, as checked November 12, 2026)
  • EVV dates per HFS: personal care services January 1, 2020 and home health care services January 1, 2023 under the Cures Act; IDOA CCP and DRS Home Services Program EVV integration live March 2, 2026; current standard is 75 percent visit compliance per program per state fiscal year quarter

What catches Illinois applicants

  • No certificate of need is stated for a home health agency in the research; a Health Facilities Planning Board permit and certificate of need apply to other facility types in the research (a new nursing facility requires a CON permit, and a new ICF/DD requires a Planning Board permit), not to the Part 245 home health license
  • State licensure and Medicare certification are separate doors; IDPH licensure alone is not Medicare billing authority, and Medicare certification is available only to the home health agency license type
  • Medicaid enrollment is separate: IMPACT is the single system, and Illinois Medicaid payment for in-home services to older adults runs through IDOA Community Care Program certification (see the CCP track); IDPH home services licensure documentation may be accepted toward CCP administrative requirements under 89 Ill. Adm. Code 240.1505(c), but it is never a substitute for CCP certification
  • Priority track: IDOA Community Care Program (CCP) provider certification under 89 Ill. Adm. Code Part 240, certifying authority the Illinois Department on Aging under the Illinois Act on the Aging, 20 ILCS 105; the three-part online application is Step 1 Legal Entity Application, Step 2 In-Home Service Application and/or Adult Day Service Application, and Step 3 Application Complete Submission Form; IDOA published 7-step process is application, review (precertification on-site review), certification, management training, IMPACT enrollment and GATA registration, contracting, and renewal; no CCP fee is published (proven negative); certification is up to 3 years and Provider Agreements are generally 3 years, entered no more frequently than semiannually; experience under 240.1505(a)(6) is in-home 3 years in business operations providing in-home service with 1 year in Illinois, adult day 2 years direct social services programming, EHRS 5 years, and AMD 5 years, with provisional certification at IDOA discretion not to exceed 2 years; in-home CCP providers must be enrolled in IMPACT as an atypical agency, email the IMPACT application ID to Aging.IMPACTApplications@illinois.gov, and have the HHAeXchange EVV portal before serving participants (EVV rule at 240.1531, in-home only)
  • CMS HHA and hospice moratorium: extended and modified effective November 12, 2026 for an additional six months (Federal Register 2026-23241, CMS-6101-N2 for HHA and 2026-23242, CMS-6102-N2 for hospice); initial enrollment of new HHAs and hospices remains blocked, the modification ends the freeze on HHA branches and hospice practice locations for currently enrolled providers as of November 12, 2026, and neither notice prints a calendar end date; no Illinois Medicaid HHA or hospice moratorium was found, and Illinois is not on the CMS state list
  • Illinois has no license category called private duty; the market term maps to the home nursing agency, home services agency, or placement licenses by employer model and by medical versus non-medical services
  • A home services agency must not provide medical services that would be performed by a home health or home nursing agency (245.210(d)(1)); a placement agency does not employ the worker, the client is the employer, and a home services placement agency must also be licensed by the Illinois Department of Labor as a Private Employment Agency
  • Hospice is a separate license family (210 ILCS 60 and 77 Ill. Adm. Code Part 280): comprehensive initial $500, volunteer initial $250, 1-year term, renewal $500 per current rule text, and a hospice residence add-on of $500 with a statewide cap of 48 residences
  • Medicare MACs: Illinois home health and hospice run through the HH+H MAC Palmetto GBA, Jurisdiction M; the A/B MAC Jurisdiction 6 is Wellpoint Federal (formerly National Government Services, name change effective April 1, 2026); SNF Part A stays with Jurisdiction 6

The Medicare layer, after the Illinois license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a home health agency in Illinois?

The figures Illinois publishes are these: Home health agency license fee: $1,500 for a two-year license (77 Ill. Adm. Code 245.95(d), amended effective September 2, 2025); fee is not refundable Home services agency and home nursing agency license fee: $1,500 per year (245.95(b); rule cap is not to exceed $1,500 annually and IDPH charges the full amount) Home services placement agency and home nursing placement agency license fee: $500 per year (245.95(c)) Applicants for multiple licenses pay the fee for each license (245.95(a)) Federal institutional provider enrollment fee: $750 for calendar year 2026 (CMS; FR 2025-21877); no separate Illinois IMPACT enrollment fee is published by HFS Stale-form warning: an older IDPH combined form printing a $25 home health fee is superseded by 245.95(d) and must not be used Beyond the published figures, the operating costs (clinical payroll before certification, insurance, and the reserve operating funds required by 42 CFR 489.28) are larger than every fee on the list.

How long does it take to get a home health license in Illinois?

Provisional license for a new agency: issued within 90 days after receipt of the application and valid for 240 days (245.100(a)); a separate change-of-ownership provisional provision caps that term at 120 days Home health agency full license term: 2 years; home services, home nursing, home services placement, and home nursing placement licenses: 1 year Renewal filed 60 to 90 days before expiration (245.90(b)(1)) Medicare backstop: IDPH performs the initial certification survey if the accrediting organization has not surveyed within 150 days of the approved 855A IMPACT revalidation at least every 5 years under 42 CFR 455.414, with HFS noting April 2026 CMS guidance moving certain providers to an upcoming 2-year cycle (perishable, as checked November 12, 2026) EVV dates per HFS: personal care services January 1, 2020 and home health care services January 1, 2023 under the Cures Act; IDOA CCP and DRS Home Services Program EVV integration live March 2, 2026; current standard is 75 percent visit compliance per program per state fiscal year quarter The federal certification that follows runs on the survey calendar, and new Medicare enrollment is under a CMS moratorium to about mid-May 2027.

What do I need to start a home health agency in Illinois?

The Home Health Agency license (Illinois Department of Public Health (IDPH)), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet Illinois rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in Illinois?

Apply to the Illinois Department of Public Health (IDPH) under Home Health, Home Services, and Home Nursing Agency Licensing Act, 210 ILCS 55; 77 Ill. Adm. Code Part 245 (Home Health, Home Services, and Home Nursing Agency Code). The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
