How to Start a Home Health Agency in Mississippi

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Mississippi licenses home health agencies as a Home Health Agency license, through the Mississippi State Department of Health (MSDH), Division of Health Facilities Licensure and Certification. The governing law is Miss. Code Ann. section 41-71-1 et seq.; MSDH Minimum Standards for Home Health Agencies, Title 15, Part 16, Subpart 1, chapter 46. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the Mississippi license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The Mississippi license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Mississippi, step by step

  1. File a Notice of Intent to apply for a Certificate of Need for establishment of a home health agency, as tracked in MSDH Certificate of Need reporting
  2. Apply for licensure with MSDH Division of Health Facilities Licensure and Certification, the division MSDH describes as responsible for licensing health facilities and for certifying facilities for Medicare and Medicaid participation

What catches Mississippi applicants

  • Verification is thin for this state: the MSDH minimum standards document could not be fully fetched in this pass, so no home health license fee or processing timeline is listed, because no official page fetched or official search snippet in this pass printed one
  • MSDH lists home health agencies among the licensed facilities in its Health Facilities Directory and describes its licensure division as also certifying facilities for Medicare and Medicaid participation; the ordered licensure steps beyond the Certificate of Need notice are not presented as a step list on the official pages fetched
  • Certificate of Need: MSDH Certificate of Need weekly reporting lists establishment of a home health agency as a CON project with a Notice of Intent stage

The Medicare layer, after the Mississippi license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

What do I need to start a home health agency in Mississippi?

The Home Health Agency license (Mississippi State Department of Health (MSDH), Division of Health Facilities Licensure and Certification), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet Mississippi rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in Mississippi?

Apply to the Mississippi State Department of Health (MSDH), Division of Health Facilities Licensure and Certification under Miss. Code Ann. section 41-71-1 et seq.; MSDH Minimum Standards for Home Health Agencies, Title 15, Part 16, Subpart 1, chapter 46. The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
