How to Start a Group Home in New Jersey

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in New Jersey. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type New Jersey licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types New Jersey licenses

Community Residence for People with Developmental Disabilities, group home and supervised apartment

Licensed by: New Jersey Department of Human Services, Office of Licensing (Office of Program Integrity and Accountability), for the Division of Developmental Disabilities (DDD). Serves: Adults with developmental disabilities, age 21 and older for DDD Community Care Program services, living in provider-managed licensed settings. Licensure under N.J.A.C. 10:44A is required if the setting is provider-managed, the individual receives services through the Community Care Program (CCP), and the individual requires personal guidance as documented in the Individual Service Plan. The CCP is a Medicaid waiver program within the Comprehensive Medicaid Waiver, a 1115 HCBS waiver in the current CCP manual, that funds services for eligible adults in licensed settings such as a group home or supervised apartment and prevents institutional placement. DHS licensing data cited on the licensing page: as of 2024, 2,297 licensed programs and 9,628 licensed beds for people with developmental disabilities.

Community Residence for People with Traumatic Brain Injury

Licensed by: New Jersey Department of Human Services, Office of Licensing. Serves: People with traumatic brain injury. Listed by the DHS Office of Licensing as a separate licensed provider type under N.J.A.C. 10:44C.

Assisted Living Residence

Licensed by: New Jersey Department of Health, Office of Certificate of Need and Healthcare Facility Licensure. Serves: Four or more adult persons unrelated to the proprietor. Standards at N.J.A.C. 8:36. Definition in the 8:36 readoption: a facility licensed by the Department of Health to provide apartment-style housing and congregate dining and to assure assisted living services are available when needed, for four or more adult persons unrelated to the proprietor. Department facility type page: apartment units offer at a minimum one unfurnished room, a private bathroom, a kitchenette and a lockable door on the unit entrance. Following receipt of a certificate of need, an applicant applies to the Commissioner for a license on Department forms, per N.J.A.C. 8:36-2.2.

Comprehensive Personal Care Home

Licensed by: New Jersey Department of Health, Office of Certificate of Need and Healthcare Facility Licensure. Serves: Four or more adults unrelated to the proprietor. Standards at N.J.A.C. 8:36. Definition in the 8:36 readoption: a facility licensed by the Department of Health to provide room and board and to assure assisted living services are available when needed, to four or more adults unrelated to the proprietor. Residential units house no more than two residents and have a lockable door on the unit entrance.

Assisted Living Program

Licensed by: New Jersey Department of Health, Office of Certificate of Need and Healthcare Facility Licensure. Serves: Residents of publicly subsidized housing that cannot become licensed as an assisted living residence because of regulations or local housing laws. Standards at N.J.A.C. 8:36. Provides meals and assisted living services when needed to residents of publicly subsidized housing, and may also provide staff resources and other services to a licensed assisted living residence or a licensed comprehensive personal care home, per the Department facility types page.

Residential Health Care Facility

Licensed by: New Jersey Department of Health, Office of Certificate of Need and Healthcare Facility Licensure. Serves: Residents needing food, shelter, supervised health care and related services in a homelike setting. Standards at N.J.A.C. 8:43. Department facility types page: a facility attached to another licensed long-term care facility that provides food, shelter, supervised health care and related services in a homelike setting. Counted as a long-term care facility type at N.J.S.A. 26:2H-12.87 with nursing homes, assisted living residences, comprehensive personal care homes and dementia care homes.

Dementia Care Home

Licensed by: New Jersey Department of Health, Division of Certificate of Need and Licensing. Serves: Persons with dementia needing residential care. Licensing standards for Dementia Care Homes are at N.J.A.C. 8:37, listed on the Department statutes and rules page, and dementia care homes are counted as long-term care facilities licensed under P.L. 1971, c. 136, N.J.S.A. 26:2H-1 et seq.

Adult Family Care, caregiver and sponsor agency

Licensed by: New Jersey Department of Health, Division of Certificate of Need and Licensing. Serves: Adults placed with an adult family care caregiver, arranged through a licensed sponsor agency. Standards for Licensure of Adult Family Care Caregivers and Sponsor Agencies are at N.J.A.C. 8:43B, listed in Department rule proposal material fetched. This is New Jersey adult foster care equivalent in the Department of Health licensing list.

Behavioral health residential, mental health and addiction services

Licensed by: New Jersey Department of Health, Office of Certificate of Need and Licensing, Behavioral Health. Serves: Persons needing licensed mental health or addiction residential services. The DHS Office of Licensing page directs mental health and addiction services licensing inquiries to the Department of Health Office of Certificate of Need and Licensing, Behavioral Health. No specific residential program definitions, fees or timelines for this lane were printed on the official pages fetched, so none are stated here.

How to start a group home in New Jersey, step by step

  1. Apply to become an approved DDD provider, for a community residence under N.J.A.C. 10:44A
  2. Review the New Provider Development Process, referencing the New Provider Development Process Chart
  3. Complete the licensing application, following the instructions in N.J.A.C. 10:44A-1.4
  4. Engage with the New Provider Development Team: staff from DDD and the Office of Licensing contact the applicant to begin the new provider development process
  5. Work with the Office of Licensing Technical Assistance Unit to develop a compliant Policy and Procedure Manual; do not acquire housing before consulting with the Divisions Office of Housing and Resource Development
  6. After licensing, expect annual inspections and compliance monitoring, with some inspections unannounced; the Office of Licensing states it is increasing unannounced inspections beginning in 2026, and two consecutive Provisional Licenses may result in non-renewal

Frequently asked questions

What are the requirements to open a group home in New Jersey?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in New Jersey?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
