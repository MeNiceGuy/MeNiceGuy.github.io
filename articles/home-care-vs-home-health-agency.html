Home Care vs Home Health Agency: Which License Do You Actually Need

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

This is the most expensive confusion in the industry. A founder picks the wrong track, spends months and real money building an agency that cannot sell the services or bill the payer they planned on, and finds out at the survey, or worse, after opening. The line between home care and home health is not a branding choice. It is written into state license categories and federal payment rules, and it is drawn by one thing: the service list.

Home care: the non-medical track

Home care agencies provide non-medical services in the home: companionship, homemaking, meal preparation, transportation, and hands-on personal care such as bathing, dressing, grooming, and mobility help. Caregivers are aides, not licensed clinicians, and there is no physician plan of care. States license this track under their own names, an HCSSA Personal Assistance Services category in Texas, a Private Home Care Provider license in Georgia, a Home Services Agency in Illinois, and in a few states no license at all for private pay work. Payment is private pay, long term care insurance, veterans programs, and Medicaid personal care programs where the state enrolls home care providers. The national walk-through for this track is How to Start a Home Care Agency in 2026, and every state version sits in the state guides.

Home health: the skilled track

Home health agencies provide skilled services: nursing, physical, occupational, and speech therapy, medical social services, and home health aide services under a plan of care. The state licenses the agency as a home health agency, and Medicare payment requires federal certification against the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, earned through the CMS-855A enrollment and an initial survey. Payment is Medicare, Medicare Advantage, Medicaid, and commercial insurance. The full path is How to Start a Home Health Agency, and the federal half is broken out on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies.

The test that decides it

Write your service list down, in plain language, and test every line. Bathing, meals, rides, supervision, reminders: home care. Wound care, injections, catheter care, therapy, medication administration, anything a nurse or therapist must do or supervise under a plan of care: home health. If one line crosses, the agency needs the skilled track for that work, and in most states the two tracks are separate licenses with separate surveys. Agencies can hold both, and many established owners do, but each is earned on its own application, in its own order.

What I tell owners who are deciding

Decide by payer and by hiring reality, not by which name sounds bigger. Home care starts faster, costs less to license, and hires from a wider labor pool, and its ceiling is the private pay and personal care market. Home health takes longer, costs more, requires clinical leadership from day one, and is currently behind a federal enrollment moratorium for new agencies that runs to about mid-May 2027, and its ceiling is the Medicare market. Both are real businesses. The wrong one for your capital and your market is the expensive one.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a home care agency and a home health agency?

Home care is non-medical: companionship, homemaking, and hands-on help with daily living, licensed by the state under home care rules. Home health is skilled: nursing and therapy services under a plan of care, licensed by the state as a home health agency and, for Medicare payment, certified against the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484. The services, the staff, the license, and the payers are all different.

Which license do I actually need?

Follow the service list. If every service you will sell is non-medical help with daily living, you need the home care track in your state. If any service is skilled, nursing, therapy, wound care, injections, or care under a physician plan of care, you need the home health track, and Medicare work requires certification on top of the license. Writing the service list down first, in plain language, is the step that decides everything else.

Can a home care agency bill Medicare?

No. Medicare pays for skilled home health services from certified home health agencies. A non-medical home care license cannot bill Medicare, and a personal care category cannot become certified. Home care agencies get paid by private pay, long term care insurance, veterans programs, and in many states Medicaid waiver and managed care programs for personal care services, each with its own enrollment.
