40 Referrals, 2 to 3 Months, and a Census Dying on the Vine: The Georgia EDWP Escalation Ladder

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · October 25, 2026

This morning a Georgia home care provider put a number on the table in an owners group I am part of, and the number was 40. Forty referrals sent to the Area Agency on Aging for the Elderly Disabled Waiver Program, EDWP, and not one of those clients assessed after 2 to 3 months of waiting. The question she asked was plain: how are you sustaining your business waiting 2 to 3 months for the AAA to assess clients? She added the part nobody puts in the brochure. It is a challenge to keep employees employed without clients, and some clients pass away in the process, get hospitalized, or move to a nursing facility because help never came.

That is not a marketing problem. That is a census dying on the vine while the paperwork sits in somebody else's queue.

What the thread confirmed

She was not alone, and her numbers were not the worst ones in the thread. Other Georgia providers confirmed waits of up to 6 months for an assessment. Providers pointed to a system changeover as part of the stall, a period where the care management companies were not pulling referrals monthly the way the process assumes they do. And owners named a quieter factor that does not show up in any state report: clients who stop answering the assessment calls, or never answer them, so even a referral that gets pulled can die of no contact.

Hold those three facts together, because they decide what the fix has to be. The queue is long. The pull from the queue has been irregular since the changeover. And some of the loss is happening at the telephone, on the client's end, while the referral ages. A provider cannot shorten the state's calendar, but a provider can do three things: prove exactly what is waiting and how long it has waited, push the file up the ladder in the right order with that proof attached, and stop letting unassessed referrals carry the business while the push runs.

Why referrals stall at the AAA assessment queue

In Georgia's EDWP, the referral does not become a client when you send it. It becomes a client after the assessment happens and services are authorized. Everything between your fax and that authorization sits in the AAA assessment queue, and the queue moves at the speed of the assessors and the care management pull, not at the speed of your staffing plan. When the pull is monthly and the month gets skipped, your referral did not move backward. It just sat, aging, while your caregivers waited for hours that do not exist yet.

That is why my first answer in that thread was not about the wait. It was about the math and the paper.

Do not staff off referrals. Staff off approved authorizations with a start date. Referrals are hope, authorizations are revenue. Keep a weekly aging sheet on every referral: name, date sent, days waiting, and who you last spoke to at the AAA, so when one hits 60 days you have a paper trail, not a memory. And keep a private pay lane open, even a few hours a week per client, because waiver work pays slow even after approval.

Then I posted the second answer, the one this story exists to record: the ladder for the wait itself.

The escalation ladder, step by step

The published comment opened with the whole idea in one line: the fix for a phone that does not get answered is to stop relying on the phone. Do this in order, and keep your aging list current before every step, because the list is what every office below will ask you for.

Step 1: Email the AAA your aging list first. Before you call anyone above the AAA, send the agency your full aging list by email: every referral waiting, the date each one was sent, and the days each one has been waiting, oldest first. This does two things. It gives the AAA the chance to work the list, and it creates the dated record that every step after this one stands on. An escalation without that email is a complaint. An escalation with it is a file.

Step 2: Call the Division of Aging Services at 1-866-552-4464. The Division of Aging Services sits over the AAA network inside the Department of Human Services. When the aging list has been sent and the wait is measured in months, this is the next call. Bring the list, the date you emailed it to the AAA, and the oldest case on it. You are not reporting a rude office. You are reporting a queue with dates on it.

Step 3: Open the constituent and provider channels at DHS and DCH. Call DHS Constituent Services at (877) 423-4746. On the Department of Community Health side, which runs the Medicaid waiver itself, call DCH Provider Services at 1-800-766-4456 and DCH Constituent Services at (404) 656-4496, and use the CCSP mailbox at ccsp.messages@dch.ga.gov. These offices exist for exactly this: a provider with a documented stall that the normal chain has not cleared. By the time you reach this step you should be able to say, in one breath, how many referrals are waiting, how old the oldest one is, when the AAA got the list, and when you called the Division of Aging Services.

One caution, and it is not optional. Keep client names and Medicaid numbers out of emails to general mailboxes. An aging list you send up the ladder should carry counts, dates sent, and days waiting. The identifying detail belongs in the secure channels those offices give you after contact, not in a general inbox. Protecting the client's information is part of the provider's job even when the provider is the one being failed.

The operating rules underneath the ladder

The ladder is the emergency move. These are the rules that keep the emergency from running your agency while it plays out.

Aging report discipline. The weekly aging sheet is not busywork. Update it every week, work it oldest first, and note every contact: who you spoke to, when, and what they said. When a referral crosses 60 days, it stops being pipeline and becomes an escalation item with a date on it. Memory does not escalate. Paper does.

Never staff against unapproved cases. A referral is not a client, an assessment is not an authorization, and an authorization without a start date is not a schedule. Caregivers hired against referrals that never assess are payroll you carry until the money runs out, and then the caregivers leave, and then the approved cases that finally land cannot be staffed either. Staff from authorizations. Hire ahead of signed, dated work only.

Run a parallel private pay pipeline. Waiver referrals will stall again. That is the nature of the queue, not a prediction about any one office. A private pay lane, even a few hours a week per client, keeps revenue and caregiver hours alive while the waiver census matures, and waiver work pays slow even after approval. The agencies that survive a 3 month assessment queue are the ones whose whole month was never riding on it.

What this is, and what it is not

I did not fix this provider's queue this morning, and I am not claiming an outcome. This was public advice on another provider's live problem, posted in the open in an owners group, and the ladder above is the play for this stall as I gave it there. Queues like this one move when a documented file reaches the office that owns the next step, in the right order, with the dates attached. That is the whole method: establish the record, age the list, climb in steps, and verify.

If you run EDWP referrals in Georgia, build the aging sheet this week, before you need it. The day a referral hits day 60 is the wrong day to start counting.
