How to Start a Home Health Agency in Tennessee

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Tennessee licenses home health agencies as a Home Health Services license (home health agency), through the Tennessee Health Facilities Commission (HFC). The governing law is Tennessee Code Annotated (T.C.A.) Section 68-11-201 et seq. (rules cited on the application) and Section 68-11-206 (Commission ratification); Certificate of Need provisions at T.C.A. Section 68-11-1607(r)(1) and (s)(3) for the pediatric and EEOICPA accreditation exception. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the Tennessee license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The Tennessee license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Tennessee, step by step

  1. 1. Apply for a Certificate of Need (CON) from the Health Facilities Commission before applying for licensure (exception for pediatric-only and/or EEOICPA-only agencies, which must instead obtain accreditation within two years)
  2. 2. Once the CON is obtained, submit a notarized application with the appropriate licensure fee to the address at the bottom of the application
  3. 3. About 30 to 45 days before being ready to open, send a letter to the Regional Office in your area to request a survey of the facility; the Regional Office will notify you to schedule the survey
  4. 4. After the survey, the surveyor forwards the forms to the Regional Office for the Regional Director's signature, then to the Central Office Licensure Unit in Nashville
  5. 5. Licensure staff process the forms and send an initial approval letter; the application is then presented to the Commission at the next regularly scheduled Commission meeting for ratification; if ratified, the license is ordered and should be received in 7 to 10 business days

Tennessee home health license fees and costs

  • Initial licensure fee: $1,404, non-refundable (HFC Home Health Services Application for Initial Licensure, HF-3506 Rev 6/2024)

How long does it take in Tennessee?

  • Request the survey about 30 to 45 days before being ready to open; if not ready on the survey date, it will most likely be 30 days or more before the survey can be rescheduled
  • License should be received in 7 to 10 business days after Commission ratification
  • Pediatric-only and/or EEOICPA-only agencies must obtain accreditation within 2 years of initiation of services

What catches Tennessee applicants

  • State licensure comes before Medicare and Medicaid certification contact: the HFC initial approval letters state it is the agency's responsibility to contact its HFC regional office for participation in Medicare/Medicaid
  • Certificate of Need is required first for a general home health agency; only an agency providing only pediatric services and/or EEOICPA federal program services is exempt from the CON before licensure, and that agency must obtain accreditation within two years of initiation of services
  • Separate non-medical track, not home health: non-medical personal support in Tennessee is licensed as a Personal Support Services Agency (PSSA) through the Department of Mental Health and Substance Abuse Services, with a $810 per site fee (context verified on the client's site; TDMHSAS is confirmed on tn.gov as the PSSA licensing authority and PSSA initial inspections are electronic desk audits). DDA also lists Personal Support Services among the services it licenses, so the service category appears under both authorities on state pages fetched

The Medicare layer, after the Tennessee license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a home health agency in Tennessee?

The figures Tennessee publishes are these: Initial licensure fee: $1,404, non-refundable (HFC Home Health Services Application for Initial Licensure, HF-3506 Rev 6/2024) Beyond the published figures, the operating costs (clinical payroll before certification, insurance, and the reserve operating funds required by 42 CFR 489.28) are larger than every fee on the list.

How long does it take to get a home health license in Tennessee?

Request the survey about 30 to 45 days before being ready to open; if not ready on the survey date, it will most likely be 30 days or more before the survey can be rescheduled License should be received in 7 to 10 business days after Commission ratification Pediatric-only and/or EEOICPA-only agencies must obtain accreditation within 2 years of initiation of services The federal certification that follows runs on the survey calendar, and new Medicare enrollment is under a CMS moratorium to about mid-May 2027.

What do I need to start a home health agency in Tennessee?

The Home Health Services license (home health agency) (Tennessee Health Facilities Commission (HFC)), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet Tennessee rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in Tennessee?

Apply to the Tennessee Health Facilities Commission (HFC) under Tennessee Code Annotated (T.C.A.) Section 68-11-201 et seq. (rules cited on the application) and Section 68-11-206 (Commission ratification); Certificate of Need provisions at T.C.A. Section 68-11-1607(r)(1) and (s)(3) for the pediatric and EEOICPA accreditation exception. The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
