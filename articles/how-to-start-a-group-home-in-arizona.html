How to Start a Group Home in Arizona

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Arizona. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Arizona licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Arizona licenses

Assisted living center

Licensed by: ADHS, Bureau of Residential Facilities Licensing. Serves: Residents needing assisted living services; 11 or more residents. Licensed under A.R.S. 36-401(A)(8) and AAC R9-10-102(A)(12), Article 8; applicant states the levels requested (supervisory care, personal care, or directed care services); memory care is an authorization inside this track under R9-10-816

Assisted living home

Licensed by: ADHS, Bureau of Residential Facilities Licensing. Serves: Residents needing assisted living services; 10 or fewer residents. Licensed under A.R.S. 36-401(A)(10) and AAC R9-10-102(A)(13), Article 8, in the same Article 8 license family as assisted living centers; any other individual residing in the home must comply with A.R.S. 36-411 fingerprinting or be supervised by a fingerprint clearance card holder

Adult foster care home

Licensed by: ADHS, Bureau of Residential Facilities Licensing. Serves: 1 to 4 adults; ALTCS participants or VA contract; sponsor or manager lives in the home. Licensed under A.R.S. 36-401(A)(6)-(7) and AAC R9-10-102(A)(14), Article 8; no Table 1.3 fee, and the certified assisted living manager requirement does not apply (A.R.S. 36-446.04(I))

Behavioral health residential facility

Licensed by: ADHS, Bureau of Behavioral Health Facilities Licensing. Serves: Persons needing behavioral health residential services. Article 7 class in the expansion track map with the same fee row as behavioral health inpatient; the adult residential care institution subclass (R9-10-102(A)(11), R9-10-717.01) is a register inside this track, and the enhanced residential treatment facility is a named watch entry only, defined at A.R.S. 36-401(A)(20) but absent from R9-10-102(A) with no article or fee row, so not buildable

Secure behavioral health residential facility

Licensed by: ADHS, Bureau of Behavioral Health Facilities Licensing. Serves: Persons with serious mental illness who are chronically resistant, placed by court order only. Articles 23 and 24; A.R.S. 36-425.06 requires court-order placement only, a 16-bed cap, and a separate license per dwelling unit; Article 23 covers Title 36 court orders and Article 24 covers Title 13

Adult behavioral health therapeutic home

Licensed by: ADHS, Bureau of Behavioral Health Facilities Licensing. Serves: Persons served in a therapeutic home setting for behavioral health. Article 18 class in the expansion track map

Behavioral health respite home

Licensed by: ADHS, Bureau of Behavioral Health Facilities Licensing. Serves: Persons served in a behavioral health respite home setting. Article 16 class in the expansion track map

Nursing-supported group home

Licensed by: ADHS (Article 22 license). Serves: Persons in a nursing-supported group home setting. Article 22 (R9-10-2201 to 2226) with a live Table 1.3 fee row; the research notes an enumeration anomaly: nursing-supported group home is not enumerated in R9-10-102(A) in Supp. 26-3, but Article 22, the fee row, and the A.R.S. 36-401 definitions remain live, so the track is built and the anomaly is stated plainly

DDD group home

Licensed by: ADHS, Bureau of Special Licensing (license under AAC Title 9, Chapter 33), with DES Division of Developmental Disabilities (DDD) Qualified Vendor system and Office of Licensing, Certification and Regulation (OLCR). Serves: Members with developmental disabilities served through DES/DDD. License under R9-33-101 to 109 and 201 to 207 (authority A.R.S. 36-132(A)(21) and 36-591) plus RFQVA Exhibit N, an OLCR per-home certificate, and APEP provider type PT 25; Chapter 33 has no fee section; application requires the DDD contract or pending-contract documentation (R9-33-104); group home definition is at A.R.S. 36-551(25)

DDD behavioral-supported group home

Licensed by: ADHS, Bureau of Special Licensing (Chapter 33 license), with DES/DDD. Serves: Members with developmental disabilities needing behavioral supports. Same Chapter 33 license plus the distinct clinical staffing law at A.R.S. 36-591.01 (transfer effective July 1, 2024, HB 2166), RFQVA Exhibit O, and APEP PT 25; no fee; behavioral-supported definition is at A.R.S. 36-551(8)

DDD child developmental home

Licensed by: DES/OLCR (license to the individual). Serves: Children with developmental disabilities; sibling group or up to 3 children. Title 6 Article 10, A.R.S. 36-551(13); no fee, 1-year term, about 45 training hours, entry through Quick Connect via contracted licensing agencies

DDD adult developmental home

Licensed by: DES/OLCR (license to the individual). Serves: Adults with developmental disabilities in a developmental home setting. Title 6 Article 11, A.R.S. 36-551(2); about 20 training hours, in the same frame as the child developmental home track

Child developmental certified home

Licensed by: Department of Child Safety (DCS) foster license plus DES certification. Serves: Foster children, with a cap of 5 foster children total and not more than 3 with developmental disabilities. DCS foster license under A.R.S. 8-509 plus DES certification under A.R.S. 36-593.01; certification renews on the foster license anniversary; no certification fee is stated in 36-593.01 and the foster license fee was unverified, so no figure is printed

Sober living home

Licensed by: ADHS, Bureau of Behavioral Health Facilities Licensing. Serves: Residents in a sober living home setting. Licensed under A.R.S. 36-2061 and 36-2062 and 9 A.A.C. 12 (Title 36 Chapter 18 Article 4), not Chapter 10; no clinical payer door, resident payment only

DCS residential group care

Licensed by: DCS Office of Licensing and Regulation. Serves: Children in residential group care under the child welfare system. Child Welfare Agency license plus a per-facility operating certificate under AAC Title 21 Chapter 7 (Article 1 R21-7-101 to 136; Article 2 R21-7-201 to 240); no statewide numeric capacity cap exists, capacity, ages, and gender are set on the license and certificate (R21-7-110); shelter care facility and transitional/independent living program are registers inside this track

DCS group foster home

Licensed by: DCS (foster licensing). Serves: More than 5 and not more than 10 children. A.R.S. 8-501(A)(9), Title 21 Chapter 6; license valid 2 years under A.R.S. 8-509(A); foster fee unverified, no figure printed

How to start a group home in Arizona, step by step

  1. Use the main residential track (assisted living, Article 8) as the model: file the ADHS application through the Licensing Management System at least 60 but not more than 120 days before the anticipated date of operation, with the $50 application fee
  2. Complete the R9-10-105 application contents, including the disclosure for the owner and any 10 percent or greater interest holder, statutory agent, governing authority, and chief administrative officer
  3. File the Article 8 supplemental application (R9-10-802) stating which levels of assisted living services are requested (supervisory care, personal care, or directed care) and whether adult day health care services or behavioral health services authorization is requested
  4. Put a certified assisted living manager in place for an assisted living center or home: board-approved course, board examination, 2,080 hours of paid work experience in a health-related field within the preceding 5 years, and fingerprinting (A.R.S. 36-446.04(C)); this certification requirement does not apply to adult foster care home managers
  5. Meet Article 8 personnel requirements (R9-10-806): caregiver 18 or older with an approved caregiver training program, verified skills before services, tuberculosis evidence on or before the first day, current first aid and adult CPR before services, and a documented back-up plan
  6. Notify ADHS the home is ready for licensing inspection, complete the inspection during substantive review, and receive the license after inspection and fee payment
  7. For a DDD group home instead, obtain the DDD contract or pending-contract documentation first, then the Chapter 33 license, the RFQVA exhibit, the OLCR per-home certificate, and APEP enrollment as PT 25

Arizona group home license fees and costs

  • Every ADHS Chapter 10 track also pays the $50 application fee (R9-10-106(A)) and the $250 late payment fee applies (R9-10-106(D))
  • Assisted living center annual fee: $370 (no capacity or 1 to 59), $739 (60 to 99), $1,109 (100 to 149), $1,848 (150 or more), plus licensed capacity times $98 (Table 1.3)
  • Assisted living home annual fee: $370 plus capacity times $98 (Table 1.3)
  • Adult foster care home: no fee (Table 1.3 shows none, and R9-10-106(C) exempts adult foster care homes from the Table fees)
  • Behavioral health residential: $495 / $495 / $990 / $1,485 / $2,475 plus capacity times $132; the adult residential care institution subclass fee row is $495 / $495 plus capacity times $132 (Table 1.3)
  • Secure behavioral health residential: $495 / $495 / $990 / $1,485 / $2,475 plus capacity times $132 (Table 1.3)
  • Adult behavioral health therapeutic home: $495 / $495 plus capacity times $132 (Table 1.3)
  • Behavioral health respite home: $495 / $495 plus capacity times $132 (Table 1.3)
  • Nursing-supported group home: $383 / $383 plus capacity times $102 (Table 1.3)
  • DDD group home and DDD behavioral-supported group home: no fee; Chapter 33 has no fee section and DES charges no fee for the Qualified Vendor Agreement
  • Sober living home: $500 plus $100 per maximum resident, manager not counted (R9-12-103)
  • DCS residential group care: $600 per bed, initial and renewal (R21-7-136(D))
  • Adult day health care facility: $370 flat, no per-bed addition (Table 1.3)

How long does it take in Arizona?

  • Chapter 10 licensing time-frame: 120 calendar days overall (30 days administrative completeness plus 90 days substantive review) under R9-10-108 and Table 1.1; file 60 to 120 days before anticipated operation
  • Chapter 10 license does not expire; annual fees apply, the initial fee is due within 60 days after the approval notice (90 days with the late fee), and a provisional license is not more than 1 year with no consecutive provisional licenses
  • Assisted living manager certification renews biennially with board-rule continuing education (A.R.S. 36-446.07(F))
  • DDD group home (Chapter 33): Table 1.1 time-frames are 120/60/60 for initial and 60/30/30 for renewal, and renewal is at least 60 days before expiration
  • DDD child developmental home: 1-year term
  • DCS group foster home: license valid 2 years (A.R.S. 8-509(A))
  • Perishable status dated November 12, 2026: every DDD residential exhibit (group home, behavioral-supported, nursing supported, vendor supported developmental home, and both room and board exhibits) is listed as Closed effective March 31, 2023 on the DES Contract Information page, and Supported Living shows Closed dated February 1, 2026 as published; affected packets open with that dated status and a re-check instruction

What are the requirements to open a group home in Arizona?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Arizona?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
