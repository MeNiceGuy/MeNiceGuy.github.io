How to Start a Group Home in Oregon

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Oregon. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Oregon licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Oregon licenses

Adult Foster Home, Aging and People with Disabilities (APD)

Licensed by: Oregon Department of Human Services, Office of Aging and People with Disabilities, through local licensing offices. Serves: Older adults and adults with physical disabilities, up to five adult residents. Licensed single-family residences offering care and support for up to five adult residents, providing 24-hour care in a home-like setting. Rules at OAR Chapter 411, Divisions 049 to 052, with facility standards in Division 50 as referenced on the ODHS application page. Minimum requirements include being at least 21, passing a background check, a home meeting structural and safety requirements, the provider or a qualified resident manager living in the home, and completing the adult foster home basic training course and passing an exam. Senate Bill 739 (2025) allows a two-year license for homes licensed and in substantial compliance for at least three years, with annual inspection still required.

Residential Care Facility and Assisted Living Facility (Community-Based Care)

Licensed by: Oregon Department of Human Services. Serves: Adults who need residential care or assisted living services, in facilities licensed for two years under OAR 411-054. Licensed by ODHS under ORS 443.400 to 443.455 and OAR Chapter 411, Division 54. No one may establish or operate a residential care or assisted living facility without a license (OAR 411-054-0010 summary). Each license expires two years after issuance. Initial licensing fees in OAR 411-054-0013 by beds: 1 to 15 beds application fee $2,000 and biennial renewal $1,000; 16 to 49 beds $3,000 and $1,500; 50 to 99 beds $4,000 and $2,000; 100 to 150 beds $5,000 and $2,500; 151 or more beds $6,000 and $3,000. Applicants must provide a complete licensing application at least 60 days before expected licensure. Memory Care Communities are an endorsement on a residential care, assisted living, or nursing facility license under OAR 411-057 for specialized services in a secured environment for individuals with dementia.

24-Hour Residential Setting and Host Home, Intellectual and Developmental Disabilities

Licensed by: Oregon Department of Human Services, Office of Developmental Disabilities Services (ODDS). Serves: Individuals with intellectual and developmental disabilities. Operated by a Medicaid agency certified under OAR 411-323 with at least one service endorsement, plus a separate site license for each 24-Hour Residential or Host Home site under OAR 411-325. Agency certification fee $500, endorsement fees $100 for supported living, employment, and community living supports endorsements per OAR 411-323-0024 and the ODDS fee schedule. 24-Hour Residential license fees: initial $75 per capacity in Clackamas, Multnomah, and Washington counties and $50 per capacity in all other counties, renewal $50 per capacity, capacity increase $50 per added capacity; Host Home renewal $50 per home. Certification and endorsements renew every two years. An initial site application must be submitted at least 30 days prior to anticipated licensure per OAR 411-325-0040 as amended November 25, 2025.

Adult Foster Home, Intellectual and Developmental Disabilities

Licensed by: Oregon Department of Human Services, Office of Developmental Disabilities Services (ODDS). Serves: People with intellectual and developmental disabilities age 18 and older, in a home environment. Licensed under OAR 411-360. Steps on the ODDS prospective provider page include reviewing OAR 411-360, completing the ODDS initial adult foster home basic training course and provider orientation and passing it, paying application fees through the adult foster home payment portal, submitting the online application with documents including floor plan, financial statement, physician statement, and plan of daily operations, and completing background checks. The foster home license must be renewed every year.

Adult Foster Home, Mental Health

Licensed by: Oregon Health Authority, Behavioral Health Division. Serves: Adults diagnosed with a qualifying mental health condition, up to five adult residents. Licensed single-family residences providing housing, services, and assistance with activities of daily living in a home-like setting, with the provider or a resident manager living on site. Licensed annually under OAR Chapter 309, Division 40. Provider, resident manager, and substitute caregiver must complete at least 12 hours of training each year directly related to care for persons with mental illness.

Residential Treatment Home, Residential Treatment Facility, and Secure Residential Treatment Facility, Mental Health

Licensed by: Oregon Health Authority, Behavioral Health Division, Licensing and Certification. Serves: Adults diagnosed with a qualifying mental illness, staffed 24 hours a day. Licensed under OAR 309-035. Residential Treatment Homes serve up to five residents; Residential Treatment Facilities and Secure Residential Treatment Facilities serve six to 16 residents, with few contracted facilities serving 16 or more. Application fees in the OHA step-by-step guide: $30 for a Residential Treatment Home and $60 for a Residential Treatment Facility or Secure Residential Treatment Facility, and the application must be submitted at least 60 days before the planned opening date. Other residential programs are licensed every two years per the OHA Licensing and Certification page. OHA also licenses residential programs for substance use and problem gambling disorders serving up to 16 individuals under OAR Chapter 309, Division 18.

How to start a group home in Oregon, step by step

  1. Adult foster home (APD): check the home meets Division 50 facility standards, contact the local licensing office and ask for an Adult Foster Home Orientation, complete required training including CPR and First Aid, Ensuring Quality Care, Home and Community-Based Services, Pre-Service Dementia Care, and Providing Inclusive Care, then turn in the completed application and all required documents to the local licensing office (ODHS page)
  2. Residential care and assisted living: for new construction, comply with OAR 411-054-0012 before applying for a building permit; provide ODHS a complete licensing application (SE 0570) at least 60 days before expected licensure, with the licensing fee in ORS 443.415 and OAR 411-054-0013 (ODHS Community-Based Care licensing page)
  3. I/DD Medicaid agency: meet OAR 411-323 qualifications, pass the Medicaid Agency Orientation, pay certification and endorsement fees and save the receipt, submit the online agency and endorsement application with executive director and business documents, then submit a separate license application for each 24-Hour Residential or Host Home site and complete the site walkthrough and any Plan of Correction (ODHS prospective provider page)
  4. Mental health residential: submit the application at least 60 days before the planned opening date, with the $30 or $60 fee in the OHA step-by-step guide to applying for a mental health residential license under OAR 309-035

Oregon group home license fees and costs

  • Residential care and assisted living initial application fees by beds, OAR 411-054-0013: 1 to 15 beds $2,000, 16 to 49 beds $3,000, 50 to 99 beds $4,000, 100 to 150 beds $5,000, 151 or more beds $6,000; biennial renewal fees are half those amounts as listed in the rule summary
  • I/DD Medicaid agency certification $500, endorsements $100 each for supported living, employment, and community living supports, 24-Hour Residential initial license $75 per capacity in Clackamas, Multnomah, and Washington counties and $50 per capacity elsewhere, renewal $50 per capacity - ODDS fee schedule, August 2026, and OAR 411-323-0024
  • Mental health residential application fees: Residential Treatment Home $30, Residential Treatment Facility or Secure Residential Treatment Facility $60 - OHA step-by-step guide, September 2026

How long does it take in Oregon?

  • Residential care and assisted living licenses expire two years after issuance (OAR 411-054-0010 summary and ODHS consumer statements stating the facility license is valid for two years)
  • Residential care and assisted living applications are due at least 60 days before expected licensure (ODHS page)
  • I/DD agency certification and endorsements renew every two years; I/DD adult foster home licenses renew every year (ODHS prospective provider page)
  • Mental health residential applications must be submitted at least 60 days before the planned opening date (OHA guide); other behavioral health residential programs are licensed every two years and adult foster homes annually (OHA page)

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to open a group home in Oregon?

The figures Oregon publishes are these: Residential care and assisted living initial application fees by beds, OAR 411-054-0013: 1 to 15 beds $2,000, 16 to 49 beds $3,000, 50 to 99 beds $4,000, 100 to 150 beds $5,000, 151 or more beds $6,000; biennial renewal fees are half those amounts as listed in the rule summary I/DD Medicaid agency certification $500, endorsements $100 each for supported living, employment, and community living supports, 24-Hour Residential initial license $75 per capacity in Clackamas, Multnomah, and Washington counties and $50 per capacity elsewhere, renewal $50 per capacity - ODDS fee schedule, August 2026, and OAR 411-323-0024 Mental health residential application fees: Residential Treatment Home $30, Residential Treatment Facility or Secure Residential Treatment Facility $60 - OHA step-by-step guide, September 2026 The license fee is rarely the largest cost. The home itself, life safety and accessibility work, insurance, and staffing hired before the first placement carry the project.

How long does it take to get a group home license in Oregon?

Residential care and assisted living licenses expire two years after issuance (OAR 411-054-0010 summary and ODHS consumer statements stating the facility license is valid for two years) Residential care and assisted living applications are due at least 60 days before expected licensure (ODHS page) I/DD agency certification and endorsements renew every two years; I/DD adult foster home licenses renew every year (ODHS prospective provider page) Mental health residential applications must be submitted at least 60 days before the planned opening date (OHA guide); other behavioral health residential programs are licensed every two years and adult foster homes annually (OHA page)

What are the requirements to open a group home in Oregon?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Oregon?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
