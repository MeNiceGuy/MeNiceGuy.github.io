How to Start a Group Home in Tennessee

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Tennessee. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Tennessee licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Tennessee licenses

Assisted Care Living Facility (ACLF)

Licensed by: Tennessee Health Facilities Commission (HFC). Serves: Residents of an assisted care living facility; the HFC application packet is the assisted living category in Tennessee. Initial licensure fee is by bed capacity, from $1,040 for less than 25 beds (see gh_fees). The application states ACLFs seeking Medicaid reimbursement must be licensed by HFC and must comply with the federal Home and Community Based Services (HCBS) Settings Rule to be credentialed as a TennCare provider.

Home for the Aged / Residential Home for the Aged

Licensed by: Tennessee Health Facilities Commission (HFC); a Tier 1 Home for the Aged category is also listed among services licensed through the Department of Disability and Aging (DDA). Serves: Aged residents in a residential home setting. The HFC change of ownership form shows 1 thru 3 beds as Not Licensed and a fee schedule starting at $390 for 4 thru 5 beds. Rules are promulgated under T.C.A. Section 68-11-201 as cited on HFC applications.

Adult Care Home - Level II

Licensed by: Tennessee Health Facilities Commission (HFC). Serves: Residents of a small adult care home (the application procedure addresses homes including five or fewer beds). Initial fee $1,404. Architectural plans are required, except that an existing single-family home to be licensed for five or fewer beds is not required to submit plans signed and sealed by an architect or Tennessee licensed engineer. Residents cannot be admitted until the initial approval letter is received.

ID and DD Residential Habilitation (and related DDA residential services)

Licensed by: Tennessee Department of Disability and Aging (DDA), Office of Licensure. Serves: Persons with intellectual and developmental disabilities; DDA licensure also covers persons in need of personal support services. DDA licenses ID and DD Residential Habilitation, ID and DD Institutional Habilitation, ID and DD Boarding Home, ID and DD Placement Services, ID and DD Respite Care Services, ID and DD Supportive Living, ID and/or DD Semi-Independent Living, and Personal Support Services, among other services. Providers licensed for Supportive Living, Semi-Independent Living and Placement Services (Family Model) must submit a list of all active residential sites with the initial application and annually at renewal, and must submit Inspection Request Forms for new homes intended for 1915(c) and/or 1115 (ECF CHOICES/CHOICES) services.

Mental Health Supportive Living, Mental Health Supportive Residential Facility, and other TDMHSAS residential categories

Licensed by: Tennessee Department of Mental Health and Substance Abuse Services (TDMHSAS), Office of Licensure. Serves: Individuals diagnosed with mental illness or a co-occurring substance use disorder, and persons with alcohol and drug abuse or dependency disorders. TDMHSAS category definitions include: Mental Health Supportive Living - housing in the community for individuals diagnosed with a mental illness or co-occurring substance use disorder; Mental Health Supportive Residential Facility - 24-hour residential care with a treatment and rehabilitation component less intensive than a Residential Treatment Facility; Mental Health Residential Treatment Program for Adults - 24-hour intensive, coordinated and structured services; Crisis Stabilization Unit - short-term stabilization, up to 96 hours, for adults 18 and older with a serious mental illness who agree voluntarily; plus alcohol and drug residential detoxification, residential rehabilitation for adults, and residential treatment for children and youth.

How to start a group home in Tennessee, step by step

  1. ACLF (HFC): 1. Submit a notarized application with the licensure fee, a CPA financial statement, local business license copy and business organization documents; 2. Obtain signed and sealed architectural plans, submit to HFC Plans Review, and build or renovate after approval; 3. About 30 to 45 days before completion, write the Regional Office to request a survey; 4. After the survey, forms go to the Regional Director and then the Central Office Licensure Unit in Nashville, which sends an initial approval letter - residents cannot be admitted until that letter is received; 5. The application is presented to the Commission at the next regularly scheduled meeting for ratification, and the license should be received in 7 to 10 business days after ratification
  2. DDA licensed residential and support services: submit the complete Initial Application Packet at the same time in one submission - DDA Licensure Initial Application, Financial Statement Form, Board of Directors/Advisory Board Form, Fact Sheets, policies and procedures required by the Licensure Rules, and an active Secretary of State filing; surveyors are not assigned until all initial documents are received; a national criminal background check through TBI or a licensed provider via IdentoGO with the VECHS waiver is required
  3. TDMHSAS licensed residential services: familiarize yourself with the services licensed and the Licensure Administrative Rules, submit the required forms to the Regional Office of Licensure nearest the location, submit the licensing fee (invoice sent after forms are received), pass a fire and life safety inspection for a residential facility, and correct deficiencies - an Initial License is then issued for up to 1 year

Tennessee group home license fees and costs

  • ACLF initial licensure, non-refundable, by bed capacity: less than 25 beds $1,040; 25 thru 49 $1,300; 50 thru 74 $1,560; 75 thru 99 $1,820; 100 thru 124 $2,080; 125 thru 149 $2,340; 150 thru 174 $2,600; 175 thru 199 $2,860; 200 or more $2,860 plus $200 for each additional 25 beds or fraction
  • Adult Care Homes Level II initial licensure: $1,404 non-refundable
  • Homes for the Aged change of ownership fee schedule shown on the HFC form: 1 thru 3 beds Not Licensed; 4 thru 5 $390; 6 thru 24 $1,040; then the same bed bands as ACLF up to $2,860 plus $200 per additional 25 beds at 200 or more
  • DDA licensure: no fixed amount is printed on the DDA pages fetched; DDA states the cost breakdown is in Licensure Rules Chapter 2 (0465-02-02-.05 FEES) and an invoice is provided, calculated on the services applied for, payable on receipt of the invoice

How long does it take in Tennessee?

  • DDA initial licensure: review and determination of an initial license may take at least six months due to application volume; applications are reviewed in the order they become complete; the standard duration of a DDA license is one year
  • HFC facilities: request the survey about 30 to 45 days before being ready to open; if not ready on the survey date it will most likely be 30 days or more before the survey can be rescheduled; license arrives 7 to 10 business days after Commission ratification
  • TDMHSAS: an Initial License is issued for up to 1 year after inspection and correction of deficiencies

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to open a group home in Tennessee?

The figures Tennessee publishes are these: ACLF initial licensure, non-refundable, by bed capacity: less than 25 beds $1,040; 25 thru 49 $1,300; 50 thru 74 $1,560; 75 thru 99 $1,820; 100 thru 124 $2,080; 125 thru 149 $2,340; 150 thru 174 $2,600; 175 thru 199 $2,860; 200 or more $2,860 plus $200 for each additional 25 beds or fraction Adult Care Homes Level II initial licensure: $1,404 non-refundable Homes for the Aged change of ownership fee schedule shown on the HFC form: 1 thru 3 beds Not Licensed; 4 thru 5 $390; 6 thru 24 $1,040; then the same bed bands as ACLF up to $2,860 plus $200 per additional 25 beds at 200 or more DDA licensure: no fixed amount is printed on the DDA pages fetched; DDA states the cost breakdown is in Licensure Rules Chapter 2 (0465-02-02-.05 FEES) and an invoice is provided, calculated on the services applied for, payable on receipt of the invoice The license fee is rarely the largest cost. The home itself, life safety and accessibility work, insurance, and staffing hired before the first placement carry the project.

How long does it take to get a group home license in Tennessee?

DDA initial licensure: review and determination of an initial license may take at least six months due to application volume; applications are reviewed in the order they become complete; the standard duration of a DDA license is one year HFC facilities: request the survey about 30 to 45 days before being ready to open; if not ready on the survey date it will most likely be 30 days or more before the survey can be rescheduled; license arrives 7 to 10 business days after Commission ratification TDMHSAS: an Initial License is issued for up to 1 year after inspection and correction of deficiencies

What are the requirements to open a group home in Tennessee?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Tennessee?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
