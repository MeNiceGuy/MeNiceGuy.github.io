How to Start a Home Health Agency in Colorado

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Colorado licenses home health agencies as a Home Care Agency license, Class A (skilled, may also provide personal care), through the Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment (CDPHE). The governing law is Sections 25-27.5-101 et seq., C.R.S.; 6 CCR 1011-1 Chapter 26, Part 4.1(A)(1). That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the Colorado license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The Colorado license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Colorado, step by step

  1. Confirm the skilled scope: list every skilled service against Chapter 26, Part 2.29 (nursing, nurse aide services, therapies, medical social services, medication administration, infusion, wound care); personal care rides on the same Class A license and no second license is needed
  2. Seat the governing body and the administrator under Chapter 26, Parts 6.1 and 6.4, with an RN or another appropriate professional covering after hours under Part 6.2
  3. Send the letter of intent to CDPHE (Chapter 2, Part 2.3.1); CDPHE responds with COHFI access for the application
  4. File the complete application and the Class A initial fee in COHFI at least 90 calendar days before the anticipated startup date (Chapter 2, Part 2.3.2), with insurance or surety bond evidence at the Class A minimums of $500,000 per occurrence and $3,000,000 aggregate and the list of contiguous counties to be served (Chapter 26, Part 4.2)
  5. Clear fingerprint-based CBI checks for owners, managers, and administrators, submitted with the application or within 10 days of a change (Part 4.2)
  6. Build the Part 6 clinical system before the survey, including skilled nursing under RN direction, nurse aide services, therapy and medical social services, agency evaluation at least annually, and the Part 5 system for rights, personnel, emergency preparedness, quality management, infection control, and records
  7. Pass the initial survey with zero deficiencies; the license issues on a zero-deficiency survey and deficiencies go to a plan of correction under Chapter 2, Part 2.10.4(B)
  8. For Medicare certification, complete the Class A license first, choose the certification route under 42 CFR Part 484 (direct survey or an approved accreditation organization with deeming authority, home health is deeming eligible per the CDPHE index), check the CMS moratorium status, and enroll through PECOS on the CMS-855A
  9. Enroll separately with Health First Colorado through HCPF for Medicaid payment; IHSS agency participation is specialty 656 layered on a Class A or Class B license under 10 CCR 2505-10, Section 8.552 and Section 25.5-6-1203, C.R.S.

Colorado home health license fees and costs

  • Class A initial license fee on the CDPHE FY2026-27 schedule (July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027): $3,709.49; Class B initial: $2,720.30; all licensure fees are non-refundable (Chapter 26, Part 4.4 and the CDPHE facility fee schedule for home care agencies)
  • Rule text figures in Chapter 26, Part 4.4 (effective July 1, 2024): initial Class A $3,240.30 and Class B $2,376.22, renewal base Class A $1,674.15 and Class B $1,431.13; the current schedule governs payment and the research states both sets of figures
  • Class B renewal base on the current schedule: $1,638.36, plus a volume fee of $123.65 for agencies with 50 to 99 annual admissions and $247.30 at 100 or more admissions; Medicare or Medicaid certified agencies deduct $100 from the base renewal fee and accredited agencies take a 10 percent deeming discount
  • Branch offices $247.30 each and workstations $61.83 each on the current schedule; change of name or change of address $92.74 each; change of ownership is charged at the initial license fee for the class; revisit fee is 100 percent of the initial or renewal fee
  • Home care placement agency registration: $939.69 annual registration fee as stated in Chapter 26, Part 3.8, with a direction to confirm against the current CDPHE schedule
  • Hospice license fees are not printed in Chapter 21 and no figure was captured from the CDPHE hospice schedule, so the research states no hospice fee figure
  • CDPHE fee schedules are republished every March 1 and take effect July 1; every home care agency fee figure in the FY2026-27 schedule expires June 30, 2027

How long does it take in Colorado?

  • Complete application and fee are due at least 90 calendar days before the anticipated startup date (Chapter 2, Part 2.3.2)
  • CDPHE initial licensing guidance states 3 to 12 months is typical for initial licensure, with home care applications taking additional time, and issuance on a zero-deficiency survey
  • Home Care Agency license runs one year; renewal carries the base fee plus the volume fee at 50 or more annual admissions and separate branch and workstation fees
  • HCPF enrollment pause under Informational Memo 26-009: enrollment of NEW Class B and IHSS provider agencies is paused from November 1, 2026 to May 1, 2027; licensure itself is not paused and existing agencies are unaffected and keep serving members
  • PACE enrollment was also paused November 1, 2026, with no end date verified in the research

What catches Colorado applicants

  • Category split: Class A is skilled and may also provide personal care; Class B is personal care only under Chapter 26, Part 4.1(A)(2); private duty nursing and long-term home health have no separate Colorado license and sit inside Class A
  • No certificate of need is stated in the research for a Colorado home care agency license
  • Medicaid enrollment is separate from CDPHE licensure and runs through HCPF; the November 1, 2026 to May 1, 2027 pause hits new Class B and IHSS Medicaid enrollment only, not CDPHE licensure
  • Colorado has no nurse pool or nurse registry license; the closest type is the home care placement agency registration at Chapter 26, Part 3, which is a registration, not a care license
  • Homemaker-only organizations are excluded from the home care agency definition at Chapter 26, Part 2.10(B)(1), and consumer-directed attendant programs administered by HCPF are expressly excluded at Part 2.10(B)(5)
  • Medicare certification does not substitute for the state Class A license, and Medicare billing opens only after certification and the enrollment effective date

The Medicare layer, after the Colorado license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

What do I need to start a home health agency in Colorado?

The Home Care Agency license, Class A (skilled, may also provide personal care) (Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment (CDPHE)), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet Colorado rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in Colorado?

Apply to the Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment (CDPHE) under Sections 25-27.5-101 et seq., C.R.S.; 6 CCR 1011-1 Chapter 26, Part 4.1(A)(1). The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
