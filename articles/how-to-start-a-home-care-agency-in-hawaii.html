How to Start a Home Care Agency in Hawaii (Home Care Is Licensed at $1,500, Home Health at $2,750, Hospice Not at All)

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 19, 2026

The Hawaii story starts with a founder on Oahu who assumed the islands ran loose. She had worked as a caregiver for years, she had families asking when she would open her own agency, and she had read a national guide that treated non-medical home care as unlicensed almost everywhere. Hawaii is not almost everywhere. The first thing I tell owners looking at this state is that Hawaii licenses the non-medical door itself, by name, and a new agency must hold that license before the first client visit. The second thing I tell them is that the state runs three different doors that founders keep merging into one: a home care agency license for non-medical care, a home health agency license for skilled care with a certificate of need step in front of it, and a hospice door with no state license at all. Here is the full picture, lane by lane, in the order a founder actually meets it.

None of this makes Hawaii a bad market. It makes Hawaii a sequenced market. The sections below follow the Hawaii packet, which is built from Hawaii primary sources checked on November 17, 2026.

The demand: about 324,000 seniors, more than one in five residents

Start with why owners keep looking at Hawaii. The Census Bureau puts the state population at 1,432,820 as of July 1, 2025, and 22.6 percent of residents are age 65 or older. Run the math and you get about 324,000 seniors, more than one in five people in the state, the oldest age profile in this series so far. The same Census QuickFacts page for Hawaii puts median household income at $100,389 for 2020 to 2024, the income base a private pay agency sells into.

The license question, answered directly: both home-based lanes are licensed, hospice is not

Here is the searched question this article exists to answer. Do you need a license to start a non-medical home care agency in Hawaii? Yes. Under HRS section 321-14.8, a home care agency must be licensed by the Department of Health, and HAR chapter 11-700 is the licensing rule, administered by the Office of Health Care Assurance, called OHCA. Home care services are personal care, meaning assistance with dressing, feeding, and personal hygiene to facilitate self-care, plus homemaker assistance such as housekeeping, shopping, and meal planning and preparation, plus respite care and support provided to the family, all delivered to clients in their residence. The license authorizes no nursing and no therapy: nursing services and other clinical professional services sit outside the definition, and managing a client money or balancing a client checkbook is not a home care service either. An individual caregiver, including an incorporated individual or a volunteer, sits outside the agency definition.

The skilled lane is a second license, not an upgrade. Skilled care in the home is a home health agency license under HAR chapter 11-97.1, and a certificate of need step through the State Health Planning and Development Agency, called SHPDA, runs in front of it. Hospice is the odd door: HRS section 321-15.63 says the department may license hospice service agencies, and OHCA testified on April 17, 2026 that it is not currently exercising that authority, so there is no Hawaii hospice license to apply for and the hospice door is federal Medicare certification only. No one door covers another door services.

The non-medical lane: the home care agency license under HAR chapter 11-700

The sequence for the non-medical door runs in a plain order. First, confirm the service menu is non-skilled: hands-on personal care, homemaker, and companion work only, with no nursing, no therapy, and no skilled tasks, and document that door determination in the agency startup file. Second, form the business with the Hawaii Department of Commerce and Consumer Affairs, hold the EIN, the Hawaii general excise tax license, and the tax registrations, and put the operating accounts and insurance in place; the general excise tax license is a business tax registration, not a care license, and the two are never merged in the filing record. Third, file the home care agency application with OHCA under HAR chapter 11-700, with the policy file the chapter requires; the application names the administrator, the services, and the service area.

The fee facts for this door come from HAR chapter 11-103.1, Exhibits A and B, effective September 16, 2022: the initial licensure fee is $1,500 and the renewal fee is $1,000. The $1,500 is paid when OHCA notifies the agency after the licensure survey, fees are due before issuance, are paid to the State of Hawaii OHCA Special Fund, and are non-refundable. The renewal fee is paid at least 30 days before the license renewal date, and failure to pay it forfeits the license on the renewal date. Hold services until the license issues, because new agencies must be licensed before providing services to clients, and licensed agencies operate under unannounced inspection readiness. One trap figure circulates from the superseded chapter 11-103 schedule, which priced this license lower before it was repealed; the chapter 11-103.1 schedule controls.

The Medicaid door on this track is separate from the license. QUEST Integration home and community based services are paid through the member health plan, AlohaCare, HMSA, Kaiser Permanente, Ohana Health Plan, and UnitedHealthcare Community Plan on the Med-QUEST page checked for this build, after Med-QUEST provider enrollment through HOKU. A home care agency license does not itself enroll the agency in Med-QUEST. Electronic visit verification through the Sandata state system, or a certified alternate feeding the Sandata Aggregator, attaches to Med-QUEST personal care visits, and a claim must match an EVV visit in the aggregator before it is paid. No more than 15 percent of a provider visits in a month may be manually edited or manually entered on the current Med-QUEST frame, under memorandum QI-2605 of February 6, 2026.

The skilled lane: home health under HAR chapter 11-97.1, behind the SHPDA certificate of need step

The moment your plan includes skilled nursing or therapy, you have left the non-medical lane. The home health agency license under HAR chapter 11-97.1, effective September 16, 2022, covers direct or indirect skilled nursing services and other therapeutic services under a physician direction to homebound patients. The licensure duty turns on meeting the home health definition, not on the payer source, the frequency, or the volume: an entity serving one or two clients is still a home health agency. One citation trap matters here: the OHCA opening page for this door still cites the repealed chapter 11-97; the current rule is chapter 11-97.1, and a manual built on the repealed chapter surveys against a rule that no longer exists.

The certificate of need gate runs first. Under the OHCA procedure, the applicant first obtains an approved certificate of need from SHPDA, or a SHPDA letter indicating that a certificate of need is not necessary. An applicant that will participate in Medicare or Medicaid needs the approved certificate of need through the public hearing process under HRS chapter 323D; an applicant that will not pursue federal certification receives the no-need letter. Either way, a copy of the SHPDA response goes to OHCA with the licensure request, and OHCA then schedules the initial onsite survey. The expenditure minimum at HRS section 323D-2 is $4,000,000 for capital expenditures. Home care agencies carry no certificate of need step on the OHCA page for that door, a checked negative worth holding onto, because founders routinely stall the wrong license waiting on a step the other door does not have.

The fee facts for this door, again from HAR chapter 11-103.1: the initial licensure fee is $2,750 and the renewal fee is $2,000, due before issuance and non-refundable, with renewal paid at least 30 days before the renewal date. The trap figure is the $1,500 home care agency fee, which belongs to the non-medical door; a home health budget built on it underfunds this filing by $1,250. OHCA prints no application processing timeframe for either license in the sources checked for this build, and OHCA states that, because of existing workload, it may not be able to accommodate requests for initial home health licensure or initial Medicare certification surveys for several months. That is a workload statement, not a timeline and not a moratorium, and the packet prints it labeled that way.

The certified and hospice lanes: Medicare certification, and no state hospice license

Medicare certification layers on the state license; it never replaces it. The certified home health sequence completes the home health license first, including the SHPDA certificate of need for a Medicare participant, then runs enrollment on Form CMS-855A through PECOS to Wellpoint Federal, Jurisdiction 6, the Home Health and Hospice MAC for Hawaii, with the CY2026 federal institutional enrollment fee of $750. The A/B MAC for Hawaii is Noridian Healthcare Solutions, Jurisdiction E, and Noridian Jurisdiction D is the DME MAC, a different door under the same contractor name. The agency builds to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, completes the certification survey through OHCA as the state survey agency or an approved accrediting organization, and holds CMS certification before billing Medicare. A licensed agency may admit patients before certification, and there is no Medicare or Medicaid reimbursement until the certification process completes.

Hospice runs the federal sequence only. There is no Hawaii hospice license application and no hospice fee line in HAR chapter 11-103.1, so the state fee on this track is NO FEE PUBLISHED, not zero by design but absent because no license is issued. The program forms, runs the Medicare component on Form CMS-855A to Wellpoint Federal, Jurisdiction 6, with the CY2026 fee of $750, builds the file the hospice Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 418 require, including the interdisciplinary group record, the core services evidence, and the volunteer program record, and completes the certification survey through OHCA as the state survey agency. Initial Medicare enrollment on both certified tracks is gated by the federal enrollment moratoria, CMS-6101-N2 for home health and CMS-6102-N2 for hospice, extended and modified effective November 12, 2026 (the extension is a six-month period, running to about May 12, 2027). Those federal notices gate the Medicare components only. No Hawaii Medicaid enrollment moratorium or pause was located for any track in the sources checked for this build.

The residential doors: group homes and facilities run through OHCA and DHS

Founders who want a home, not an agency, meet a different Hawaii map, and the packet builds those doors too. The group home default is the developmental disabilities domiciliary home under HAR chapter 11-89, at $325 initial. Community care foster family homes are certified under HAR chapter 11-800 at $200 initial. Adult residential care homes, including the expanded form, are licensed under HAR chapter 11-100.1 at $350 initial and $225 renewal, assisted living facilities under HAR chapter 11-90 at $1,000 initial, and adult day health centers under HAR chapter 11-96 at $1,400 initial, with adult day care centers licensed by the Department of Human Services under HAR chapter 17-1424 at $1,000 initial. Every fee in that list comes from the same HAR chapter 11-103.1 schedules, and none is a home care fee.

Background checks and the personnel file: the Hawaii frame

Background checks run on the Hawaii frame at HRS section 321-15.2 and HAR chapter 11-106 for staff with direct client contact, and every worker or volunteer who gives care or supervises care carries the background check record the home care rule names at HAR section 11-700-9 and HAR chapter 11-106. The personnel file the packet builds for each worker holds the application, references, and the signed job description, license or credential verification for every licensed role, the background check record before the first assignment with direct client contact, OIG and SAM exclusion checks before hire and monthly, orientation, competency evaluation, and the annual performance record, plus the health screening and tuberculosis clearance record the applicable standards require. The deficiency pattern I see owners repeat is checks run at hire and never again; the file has to show a rhythm, not a single date.

What you still do today: form the business the Hawaii way

The formation step is ordinary, with two Hawaii specifics. You form the entity with the Department of Commerce and Consumer Affairs, then do the employer load in the right order: an EIN from the IRS, the Hawaii general excise tax license and tax registrations held as tax records and never merged with the care license in the filing record, liability insurance in place before the first client, written policies and procedures covering the services you will actually sell, caregiver training and background screening set up as a system rather than a scramble, and a service agreement that says clearly what your agency does and does not do, with nursing and clinical tasks on the does not do side of the line for a home care agency. No state formation fee figure is printed in the sources checked for this build, so none is quoted here; the packet prices the care doors, and the formation quote belongs to the filing office at filing time.

The practical warning

Three Hawaii traps cost new owners money. The first is the hospice license that does not exist: a plan built around a Hawaii state hospice license application stalls on a door OHCA does not currently open, because the authority at HRS section 321-15.63 is not being exercised. The second is the certificate of need step run in the wrong order or on the wrong door: a Medicare or Medicaid home health filing sent to OHCA before the SHPDA response exists stalls the license, and a private pay only home health applicant waiting on a full certificate of need instead of the SHPDA no-need letter stalls itself. The third is scope drift, the most expensive scope error in Hawaii home-based care: skilled services marketed or delivered on the home care agency door, when the home care definition excludes nursing and other clinical services. Write the service boundary into the care plan and the caregiver instructions, and train to it, because in Hawaii the boundary is the license.

Frequently asked questions

How do I start a home care agency in Hawaii?

You start with the door your services require, and in Hawaii that door is a license for both home-based lanes. Non-medical care files for the home care agency license with the Office of Health Care Assurance under HRS section 321-14.8 and HAR chapter 11-700, at a $1,500 initial fee, and holds services until the license issues. Skilled care runs the SHPDA certificate of need step first, then the home health agency license under HAR chapter 11-97.1 at $2,750. What are the Hawaii home care license requirements?

Hawaii home-based care runs on separate doors. Non-medical home care is licensed as a home care agency by the Department of Health, Office of Health Care Assurance, under HRS section 321-14.8 and HAR chapter 11-700. Skilled home health is licensed under HAR chapter 11-97.1, behind the SHPDA certificate of need step under HRS chapter 323D. Hospice has no Hawaii state license at this time, because OHCA does not currently exercise its authority under HRS section 321-15.63, so hospice runs on federal Medicare certification. No one door covers another door services.

How much does a home care license cost in Hawaii?

The home care agency license costs $1,500 initial and $1,000 on renewal, and the home health agency license costs $2,750 initial and $2,000 on renewal, both under the HAR chapter 11-103.1 fee schedules, non-refundable and due before issuance. Hospice carries no state fee, because no state hospice license is issued, so the packet prints NO FEE PUBLISHED for that door; the federal Medicare enrollment fee on the certified tracks is $750 for CY2026. Yes. Hawaii licenses non-medical home care as a home care agency under HRS section 321-14.8 and HAR chapter 11-700, and a new agency must be licensed before providing services to clients. Your next step

Work in lane order. Decide first which lane your first clients are in, non-medical home care, skilled home health, or hospice, because that one decision determines whether you file the home care agency license at $1,500 under HAR chapter 11-700, run the SHPDA certificate of need step and then the home health license at $2,750 under HAR chapter 11-97.1, or build hospice on Medicare certification alone with no state license step.
