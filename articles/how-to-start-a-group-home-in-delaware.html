How to Start a Group Home in Delaware

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Delaware. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Delaware licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Delaware licenses

Assisted Living Facility

Licensed by: Delaware DHSS, Division of Health Care Quality (DHCQ). Serves: Medically stable adult individuals who need help with activities of daily living or instrumental activities of daily living and do not require the skilled nursing services of a nursing facility. Defined in 16 Del. C. sec. 1102 as a special combination of housing, supportive services, supervision, personalized assistance, and health care. Listed by DHCQ among its long-term care facility license categories. Regulation is 16 DE Admin. Code 3225, Assisted Living Facilities, per the DHCQ regulations list.

Neighborhood Home

Licensed by: Delaware DHSS, Division of Health Care Quality (DHCQ) for the long-term care license, with DDDS Lifespan Waiver funding for eligible residents. Serves: Individuals with intellectual or developmental disabilities. Defined in 16 Del. C. sec. 1102 as a residence for no more than 5 individuals that is fully integrated in the community, not on the grounds of an institution, has shared common living areas, is where the individual chooses to live, and offers 24-hour supports. DDDS lists Neighborhood Group Home as a living setting option under the DDDS Waiver, not under Diamond State Health Plan Plus.

Group Home for Persons with Mental Illness

Licensed by: Delaware DHSS, Division of Health Care Quality (DHCQ). Serves: Between 3 and 10 adults with a primary diagnosis of psychiatric disabilities. Defined in 16 Del. C. sec. 1102 as a residence providing 24-hour supports, mental health treatment, rehabilitation, and housing, fully integrated in the community, not on the grounds of an institution, with shared common living areas, and where the individual chooses to live. Does not include supervised apartments. Regulation is 16 DE Admin. Code 3305, which states the Department is authorized by 16 Del. C. Ch. 11 to license and regulate group homes for adults, the license is not transferable, and application is made on forms provided by the Department.

Group Home for Persons with AIDS

Licensed by: Delaware DHSS, Division of Health Care Quality (DHCQ). Serves: 16 or fewer individuals with AIDS with an established diagnosis and disease progression requiring a routine and frequent combination of physician, professional nursing, and supportive services. Defined in 16 Del. C. sec. 1102 and listed by DHCQ among its long-term care facility license categories.

Family Care Home

Licensed by: Delaware DHSS, Division of Health Care Quality (DHCQ). Serves: 2 or 3 residents who cannot live independently and who need or could benefit from a family living situation. Defined in 16 Del. C. sec. 1102 as a home that provides beds and personal care services, and provides shelter, housekeeping services, food, meals, and personal care for residents.

Rest (Residential) Facility - Rest Residential Facility

Licensed by: Delaware DHSS, Division of Health Care Quality (DHCQ). Serves: Adult individuals who are normally able to manage their own activities of daily living. Defined in 16 Del. C. sec. 1102 as a facility that provides resident beds and personal care services in a homelike environment. Listed by DHCQ as Rest (Residential) Homes among its licensed facility categories.

Intensive Behavioral Support and Educational Residence

Licensed by: Delaware DHSS, Division of Health Care Quality (DHCQ). Serves: Adult individuals with autism, developmental disabilities, or severe mental or emotional disturbances who have specialized behavioral needs. Defined in 16 Del. C. sec. 1102 and listed by DHCQ among its long-term care facility license categories.

Neighborhood Group Home and Community Living Arrangement under DDDS residential habilitation

Licensed by: Delaware Division of Developmental Disabilities Services (DDDS) for waiver services, administered under DHSS. Serves: People with intellectual and developmental disabilities, including autism spectrum disorder and Prader-Willi Syndrome, eligible for the DDDS Lifespan Waiver. The DDDS Lifespan Waiver is Delaware 1915(c) Home and Community Based Services waiver for individuals with intellectual and developmental disabilities, operated continuously since 1987 as stated on the DHSS waiver programs page. Residential habilitation may be provided in a neighborhood group home or apartment, by a Shared Living provider, or in the client private home. Habilitation services in the DDDS description include specialized training and supervision in residential settings such as group homes, adult family living homes, foster training homes, neighborhood homes, supervised apartments, and staffed apartments.

How to start a group home in Delaware, step by step

  1. For a Group Home for Persons with Mental Illness: application for a license shall be made on forms provided by the Department, per 16 DE Admin. Code 3305 sec. 4.3, and the license shall be posted in a conspicuous place in the group home and is not transferable
  2. For long-term care facilities generally under 16 Del. C. Ch. 11: a person may not establish, conduct, or maintain a long-term care facility without first obtaining a license from the Department and thereafter renewing the license on an annual basis, per the definition and licensure text in Chapter 11

How long does it take in Delaware?

  • Long-term care facility licenses under 16 Del. C. Ch. 11 are renewed on an annual basis

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get a group home license in Delaware?

Long-term care facility licenses under 16 Del. C. Ch. 11 are renewed on an annual basis

What are the requirements to open a group home in Delaware?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Delaware?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
