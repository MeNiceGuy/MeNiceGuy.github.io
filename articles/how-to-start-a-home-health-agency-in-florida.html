How to Start a Home Health Agency in Florida

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Florida licenses home health agencies as a Home Health Agency license for hands-on personal care and home health services; companion and homemaker only services are a registration, not a license, through the Florida Agency for Health Care Administration (AHCA). The governing law is Chapter 400, Part III, Florida Statutes (sections 400.461 to 400.512) and Rule Chapter 59A-8, Florida Administrative Code for the home health agency license; section 400.509, Florida Statutes and Rule 59A-8.025 for the companion and homemaker registration; FLMMIS provider type 65 Home Health Services, specialty 965 in the research payer list. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the Florida license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The Florida license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Florida, step by step

  1. Decide the level of regulation by service: companion and homemaker services with no home health service are exempt from licensure under section 400.509 but must register with AHCA before operating; hands-on personal care such as bathing, feeding and changing a client is outside the registration and belongs under a home health agency license or a licensed nurse registry
  2. Form the business in the form AHCA expects: a corporation or LLC registers with the Florida Department of State, Division of Corporations (fee separate), or a sole proprietor may register under their legal name; check the business name because a homemaker and companion name cannot include words like home health, personal care, nursing, medical or health care
  3. Complete Level 2 background screening for the managing employee and the financial officer before registration, and for every new homemaker and companion before hiring
  4. For the home health agency license, file the Health Care Licensing Application for Home Health Agencies, AHCA Form 3110-1011, with the license fee and biennial assessment, proof of financial ability to operate, and malpractice and general liability insurance of at least $250,000 per claim under section 400.471, Florida Statutes, then pass the AHCA initial licensure survey before the license issues
  5. For the companion and homemaker track, file AHCA Form 3110-1003 through the AHCA online licensing system, naming the specific geographic area built from counties inside the health service planning district, and put the registration number in advertising to the public, on the website and in printed materials, as section 400.509 requires
  6. For Medicaid payment, enroll separately through the Florida Medicaid Web Portal (FLMMIS) Online Provider Enrollment Wizard under Rule 59G-1.060; most payment flows through Statewide Medicaid Managed Care plans after enrollment; Medicare A/B MAC for Florida is First Coast Service Options, Inc., Jurisdiction N

Florida home health license fees and costs

  • Home health agency license fee on AHCA Form 3110-1011: $1,705 license fee plus a $300 biennial assessment, $2,005 for the two year license
  • Companion and homemaker registration fee, a separate non-medical track: $50.75 for a two year registration on AHCA Form 3110-1003 (the statute sets $50 per biennium under section 400.509); this is a registration fee, not a home health agency license fee
  • Federal institutional provider application fee: $750 for calendar year 2026 at initial enrollment, revalidation and new practice location

How long does it take in Florida?

  • AHCA reviews a registration application and, within 30 days after receipt, notifies the applicant in writing of any apparent errors or omissions
  • On an omissions letter the applicant has 21 days from receipt to send the missing information; if it does not arrive the application is withdrawn and the fee is not refunded
  • Within 60 days after AHCA receives a complete application, it must approve or deny it
  • Registration is valid for no more than two years; renewal applications are due at least 60 days before expiration; an expired registration cannot be renewed and the provider must stop operating until a new registration issues

What catches Florida applicants

  • No license is required for non-medical companion and homemaker care in Florida, but registration with AHCA is required before operating, including for a single individual working alone; the only registration exemption named in the article is an organization under contract with the Agency for Persons with Disabilities providing companion services only to people with developmental disabilities
  • Certificate of Need gates in the research: new hospices, skilled nursing facilities and ICF/DD require CON review before licensure projects (AHCA Certificate of Need unit)
  • Medicaid enrollment is a separate door from AHCA licensure or registration: FLMMIS enrollment types are Fully Enrolled, Limited Enrolled and Ordering/Referring (ROPA), with Level 2 background screening under section 408.809 and Provider Enrollment Policy Rule 59G-1.060 effective April 8, 2026
  • A registered homemaker and companion provider may operate from home if the home is in Florida and inside the service area, and current Florida law does not require liability insurance for this registration, both as stated by AHCA in the article
  • The new all-homes tracks in the expansion research are not HHA or hospice agency tracks and are not themselves under the Medicare moratorium; the moratorium status matters to the existing Florida certified HHA and hospice tracks

The Medicare layer, after the Florida license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a home health agency in Florida?

The figures Florida publishes are these: Home health agency license fee on AHCA Form 3110-1011: $1,705 license fee plus a $300 biennial assessment, $2,005 for the two year license Companion and homemaker registration fee, a separate non-medical track: $50.75 for a two year registration on AHCA Form 3110-1003 (the statute sets $50 per biennium under section 400.509); this is a registration fee, not a home health agency license fee Federal institutional provider application fee: $750 for calendar year 2026 at initial enrollment, revalidation and new practice location Beyond the published figures, the operating costs (clinical payroll before certification, insurance, and the reserve operating funds required by 42 CFR 489.28) are larger than every fee on the list.

How long does it take to get a home health license in Florida?

AHCA reviews a registration application and, within 30 days after receipt, notifies the applicant in writing of any apparent errors or omissions On an omissions letter the applicant has 21 days from receipt to send the missing information; if it does not arrive the application is withdrawn and the fee is not refunded Within 60 days after AHCA receives a complete application, it must approve or deny it Registration is valid for no more than two years; renewal applications are due at least 60 days before expiration; an expired registration cannot be renewed and the provider must stop operating until a new registration issues The federal certification that follows runs on the survey calendar, and new Medicare enrollment is under a CMS moratorium to about mid-May 2027.

What do I need to start a home health agency in Florida?

The Home Health Agency license for hands-on personal care and home health services; companion and homemaker only services are a registration, not a license (Florida Agency for Health Care Administration (AHCA)), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet Florida rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in Florida?

Apply to the Florida Agency for Health Care Administration (AHCA) under Chapter 400, Part III, Florida Statutes (sections 400.461 to 400.512) and Rule Chapter 59A-8, Florida Administrative Code for the home health agency license; section 400.509, Florida Statutes and Rule 59A-8.025 for the companion and homemaker registration; FLMMIS provider type 65 Home Health Services, specialty 965 in the research payer list. The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
