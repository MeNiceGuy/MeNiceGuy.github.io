How to Start a Home Care Agency in West Virginia (Registration Only for Non-Medical Care, and a Certificate of Need in Front of Home Health, Hospice, and Personal Care)

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 19, 2026

The West Virginia story starts with a founder who got two opposite answers in the same week. One consultant told her she needed a home health agency license before she could send a caregiver into a home in Charleston. Another told her no license existed and she could start tomorrow. Both answers were half right, and that is exactly the problem in this state, because West Virginia runs a registration door and a license door under similar names, with a certificate of need statute standing in front of more of the map than most founders expect. When owners bring me a West Virginia plan, the first thing I do is separate the doors by service, not by what the agency wants to call itself. Personal care and homemaker services run on registration only, and the statute bars the state from charging a fee for it. Skilled home health runs on a license. Hospice runs on its own license. And establishing a home health agency, providing hospice services, and providing personal care services each require a certificate of need. Here is the full picture, lane by lane, in the order a founder actually meets it.

The demand: about 397,000 seniors, more than one in five residents

Start with why owners keep looking at West Virginia. The Census Bureau puts the state population at 1,766,147 as of July 1, 2025, and 22.5 percent of residents are age 65 or older. Run the math and you get about 397,000 seniors, more than one in five people in the state. The same Census QuickFacts page for West Virginia puts median household income at $59,608 for 2020 to 2024, the lowest income base in this article series, which is why the payer mix matters more here than in most states: Medicaid waiver and state plan personal care work carries a large share of the real demand, and the agencies I work with plan for that mix from day one.

The license question, answered directly: registration for non-medical care, licenses for home health and hospice, and a certificate of need gate

Here is the searched question this article exists to answer. Do you need a license to start a non-medical home care agency in West Virginia? No license exists for that door. Under W. Va. Code section 15-2C-7, an agency providing personal care and homemaker services registers through the Office of Health Facility Licensure and Certification, OHFLAC, and that registration is the whole state credential. The same section bars any fee for registration, so the packet prints NO FEE PUBLISHED on this door for a stronger reason than usual: charging a fee is not just unpublished, it is barred by statute. Budgeting a license fee for this door, or skipping the registration because someone called it optional, are both errors, in opposite directions.

The licensed doors are specific. Skilled home health uses the home health agency license through OHFLAC, and no agency may provide home health services before that license issues. Hospice uses the state hospice license at W. Va. Code section 16B-8-3 and 64 CSR 54, with Medicare certification under 42 CFR Part 418 layered on it for the Medicare door. Standing in front of all three service doors is the certificate of need: under W. Va. Code section 16-2D-8(b), a certificate of need is required, regardless of expenditure, for establishing a home health agency, for providing hospice services, and for providing personal care services, issued by the West Virginia Health Care Authority. That CON duty is the fact older West Virginia summaries most often get wrong, in both directions, and it is why the packet tests the CON posture before any other filing on the licensed tracks. An assisted living residence license is a residential facility credential issued by OHFLAC; it authorizes no in-home agency work, and no in-home agency needs it.

The non-medical lane: registration under section 15-2C-7, then the Medicaid doors

The private pay sequence on this door runs in the order the packet prints. Confirm the service menu is non-skilled first: hands-on personal care, homemaker, and companion work only, with no nursing, no therapy, and no skilled tasks, and document that finding in the startup file so the absence of a license is deliberate, not an oversight. Form the business with the West Virginia Secretary of State, hold the EIN and the tax registrations, and put the operating accounts and insurance in place. Register the agency under section 15-2C-7 through OHFLAC; no facility license application is filed for this door, and no fee may be charged for the registration. Then build the screening and file systems before the first visit, covered in the background check section below, and run private pay clients under agency service agreements with a plan of services and supports documented for each client.

The Medicaid side of this lane runs through the Bureau for Medical Services, BMS, in the Department of Human Services. For waiver work, the agency enrolls with West Virginia Medicaid as a provider for the specific waiver and service, meeting the BMS provider standards for that waiver before the first billed visit, and West Virginia has exactly four waivers to plan against: the Aged and Disabled Waiver, the Intellectual and Developmental Disabilities Waiver, the Traumatic Brain Injury Waiver, and the Children with Serious Emotional Disorder Waiver. Any longer waiver list attached to a West Virginia plan came from another state and should be discarded on sight. State plan personal care is its own door beside the waivers. Electronic visit verification is mandatory for the personal care and home health services the West Virginia Medicaid EVV requirements cover, through the HHAeXchange state portal or an approved alternate solution feeding the state aggregator, and the state funded HHAeXchange portal is offered at no cost to providers. Managed care runs through Mountain Health Trust, and the current organizations are Aetna Better Health of West Virginia, Highmark Health Options, The Health Plan, and Wellpoint; plan lists naming UniCare are stale, because that plan rebranded as Wellpoint. No West Virginia Medicaid enrollment moratorium was located in the primary sources checked, and the packet prints no Medicaid pause for this state.

The skilled lane: certificate of need first, then the OHFLAC home health license

The moment your plan includes skilled nursing or therapy, you have left the registration lane. The home health sequence starts with the certificate of need, not with the license application: establishing a home health agency requires a certificate of need regardless of expenditure under section 16-2D-8(b), from the West Virginia Health Care Authority, and a filing sequenced before the CON posture is tested is a filing built backwards. With the CON settled, the agency builds the home health file, the administrator and clinical leadership records, the policy register, the personnel file system, and the plan of treatment process, then files the OHFLAC application for licensure with the policy file and holds services until the license issues. The licensure survey follows, with deficiencies corrected through the plan of correction process, and the agency holds the regular license before operating as a home health agency. On fees and clocks, the packet is deliberately plain: no home health agency license fee figure and no license processing timeline were located in the sources checked for this build, so the packet prints NO FEE PUBLISHED for the state fee and supplies no invented clock. After licensure, the agency enrolls with West Virginia Medicaid, configures EVV, and contracts with the Mountain Health Trust organizations as its populations require.

Medicare certification layers on the licensed agency, never under it. The certified sequence reads the federal gate first: initial home health enrollment is blocked under CMS-6101-N2, the federal moratorium extended and modified effective November 12, 2026 with no end date printed (the extension is a six-month period, running to about May 12, 2027). The enrollment itself runs on Form CMS-855A through PECOS to CGS Administrators, Jurisdiction 15, with the CY2026 federal enrollment fee of $750, the manual is built to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, and the certification survey runs through the state survey agency or an approved accrediting organization before Medicare billing starts. The A/B desk for West Virginia is Palmetto GBA, Jurisdiction M; CGS Jurisdiction C is the durable medical equipment desk and enrolls nobody on these tracks. The federal moratorium gates the Medicare door only. It does not create, extend, or lift any West Virginia Medicaid rule, and no West Virginia Medicaid pause is printed in this packet because none was located.

The hospice lane: a state license at 64 CSR 54, with a published fee

Hospice is its own door in West Virginia, not an add-on to the home health license. The program forms, settles its classification and service area, and tests the certificate of need duty first, because providing hospice services requires a certificate of need under section 16-2D-8(b). The state license sits at W. Va. Code section 16B-8-3 and 64 CSR 54, and this is the one West Virginia home-based door with a clean published state fee: $100 for the initial license at 64 CSR 54, section 3.2.a, and renewal at $100 for a hospice whose yearly caseload exceeds ten patients and $50 for fewer than ten, at section 3.3.a. The agency files the OHFLAC hospice application with the certificate of need or exemption evidence the rule requires, completes the licensure inspection, and holds the license before operating as a hospice. The Medicare layer runs in parallel on Form CMS-855A to CGS Administrators, Jurisdiction 15, with the $750 CY2026 federal fee, under 42 CFR Part 418, gated for initial enrollment by CMS-6102-N2 on the same federal terms as the home health moratorium (the extension is a six-month period, running to about May 12, 2027), and the Part 418 file carries the interdisciplinary group record, the core services evidence, the inpatient care arrangements where they apply, and the volunteer program record. Neither door replaces the other: a Medicare certified hospice still needs the state license, and a state licensed hospice that skips certification has no Medicare door.

Background checks and the personnel file

The packet's personnel file for West Virginia carries the application, references, and the signed job description for the role held; license or credential verification for every licensed role, from the primary source where the board provides it; the West Virginia Nurse Aide Registry check through OHFLAC before hire and on the cycle the applicable standards set; OIG and SAM exclusion checks before hire and monthly; the criminal history and fingerprint record where the door requires it; the orientation, competency evaluation, and annual performance record; and the health screening and immunization record the agency policy and the applicable standards require. For Medicaid waiver work, the criminal background check process the waiver standards require is part of that same file, built before the first billed visit rather than assembled after an audit letter arrives. The deficiencies the packet flags on these lanes are the expensive ones: skilled services marketed or delivered on the registration door, a home health filing sequenced before the certificate of need posture is tested, plan of treatment reviews drifting past the review cycle the skilled standards print, EVV exceptions left unworked in the aggregator, and registry and exclusion checks run at hire and never again.

What you still do today: form the business the West Virginia way

Formation is ordinary and the packet prices it the same honest way it prices the license doors: no West Virginia formation fee figure is printed in the sources this packet is built from, so none is printed here. File the business with the West Virginia Secretary of State, get the EIN and the state tax registrations, and put the operating accounts and insurance in place. Then do the employer load in the right order: liability insurance before the first client, policies and procedures matched to the door you actually hold, registration for the non-medical door or licensure for the skilled doors, caregiver screening and training set up as a system, and a service agreement that says clearly what your agency does and does not do, with skilled tasks on the does not do side of the line for any agency holding only the registration.

The practical warning

West Virginia carries the sharpest warning in this series because its published summaries contradict each other, and I have read versions of this state's packet that contradicted themselves on the same page. Three corrections decide whether a West Virginia plan is real. First, the certificate of need is required: for establishing a home health agency, for providing hospice services, and for providing personal care services, under section 16-2D-8(b). Any source that tells you West Virginia has no certificate of need requirement for these doors is wrong, and a plan built on it collapses at the Health Care Authority. Second, the non-medical door is registration only, and the fee is barred: section 15-2C-7 registration is not a license, grants no license, and may not be charged for, so a budget line pricing a non-medical license fee is pricing a credential the state does not sell. Third, the hospice fee is published: $100 initial, $100 or $50 on renewal by caseload, at 64 CSR 54. A packet that suppresses that fee as unpublished is working from a stale build. Check every West Virginia claim against the door it belongs to, and discard any waiver list longer than the four waivers BMS actually runs.

Your next step

Work in door order. If your first clients are private pay personal care and homemaker work, form the entity this week, register under section 15-2C-7 at no fee, and stand up the screening, training, and service agreement file before you market. If Medicaid work is in the plan, test the certificate of need posture for personal care services first, then enroll with BMS for the specific waiver or state plan service and configure EVV before the first billed visit.
