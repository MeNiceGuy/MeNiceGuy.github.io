How to Start a Group Home in Maine

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Maine. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Maine licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Maine licenses

Assisted Living Facility

Licensed by: Maine DHHS, Division of Licensing and Certification, Assisted Housing Program. Serves: Residents in an assisted housing facility needing housing, assistance with activities of daily living and instrumental activities of daily living, personal supervision, protection, meals, diet care, care management, and diversional or motivational activities. Authorized by 22 MRS Chapters 1663 and 1664. An assisted living program is a facility that offers services to residents in private apartments, per DLC. Rule is 10-144 CMR Ch. 113 Part A, effective September 18, 2025. The rule states assisted housing facilities may also provide medication administration and nursing services if licensed to do so under the rule, and a license must be issued prior to commencement of operation.

Residential Care Facility, Levels I to IV, including Private Non-Medical Institution (PNMI) Levels I to IV

Licensed by: Maine DHHS, Division of Licensing and Certification, Assisted Housing Program. Serves: Residents needing residential care in private or semi-private bedrooms. A Residential Care Facility is a facility that offers services to residents in private or semi-private bedrooms, per DLC. Rule is 10-144 CMR Ch. 113 Part B, effective September 18, 2025. PNMIs funded under 10-144 CMR Ch. 101, MaineCare Benefits Manual Section 97, are stated in Ch. 113 to be a type of residential care facility, and PNMIs offering behavioral health services may be subject to licensure under 10-144 CMR Ch. 123, Behavioral Health Organizations Licensing Rule.

Home and Community Support Service Agency residential programs - waiver homes, including agency group home and shared living settings

Licensed by: Maine DHHS, Division of Licensing and Certification, jointly with the Office of Aging and Disability Services (OADS). Serves: Adults with an intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, a related condition, or an acquired brain injury, where the service is funded in whole or in part by the Department. Licensing rule is 10-144 CMR Ch. 108, Home and Community Support Service Agencies Licensing Rule, implementing 34-B MRS sec. 1203-B, effective as an emergency rule September 3, 2025 in the materials reviewed. Residential services listed in Ch. 108 include adult Private Non-Medical Institutions, home support, personal care, self care and home management reintegration, respite, crisis intervention, and residential habilitation. DLC and OADS state this licensing covers providers of MaineCare waiver services under Sections 13, 18, 20, 21, 29, and 97 Appendix F. Section 21 is the Comprehensive Waiver and Section 29 is the Support Waiver for adults with intellectual disabilities or autism, both offering Home Supports. Residential programs for individuals with intellectual disabilities formerly licensed under Ch. 113 were directed to transition to Ch. 108 licensure, with no fee for that transition application in the September 3, 2025 DLC memo.

Intermediate Care Facility for Individuals with Intellectual Disabilities (ICF/IID)

Licensed by: Maine DHHS, Division of Licensing and Certification, Medical Facilities Unit. Serves: Individuals with intellectual disabilities, with a focus on active treatment services. DLC states ICF/IID facilities must all be licensed by the State of Maine and federally certified. State regulation cited by DLC is 10-144 CMR Ch. 118. Federal regulation cited is 42 CFR Part 483 for ICF/IID.

How to start a group home in Maine, step by step

  1. For a Residential Care Facility, Assisted Living Facility, or Adult Day Program: select the Residential Care Facility, Assisted Living Facility, Adult Day Program Online Application linked from the DLC Assisted Housing page, and complete the appropriate application and submit it with the required documentation and fees as detailed in the application form. For a renewal or change, have the license number and access code available
  2. For facilities or sites with private wells: complete the Basic Safety Test of well water prior to filing an initial or renewal application and submit a copy of test results to DLC with the application, per DLC Assisted Housing instructions
  3. For Home and Community Support Service Agencies: visit the DLC licensing page for application submission as directed on the OADS Home and Community Support Services Licensing page

Maine group home license fees and costs

  • Residential Care Facility Levels I to IV: fee $10 per bed per year, as printed on the official Assisted Housing Program Residential Care Application, Form 020104 Rev 01/2024, which lists initial application for 1 year and renewal application for 2 years
  • PNMI Levels I to IV: fee $10 per bed per year, on the same official application form
  • Assisted Living Facility: annual license fee $200, as stated in 10-144 CMR Ch. 113 Part A and in 22 MRS sec. 7854 as summarized in the official rule text
  • Background check fee $31 for owner or applicant on the Residential Care Application form, as printed on that form

How long does it take in Maine?

  • Residential Care Application form lists initial application for 1 year and renewal application for 2 years

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to open a group home in Maine?

The figures Maine publishes are these: Residential Care Facility Levels I to IV: fee $10 per bed per year, as printed on the official Assisted Housing Program Residential Care Application, Form 020104 Rev 01/2024, which lists initial application for 1 year and renewal application for 2 years PNMI Levels I to IV: fee $10 per bed per year, on the same official application form Assisted Living Facility: annual license fee $200, as stated in 10-144 CMR Ch. 113 Part A and in 22 MRS sec. 7854 as summarized in the official rule text Background check fee $31 for owner or applicant on the Residential Care Application form, as printed on that form The license fee is rarely the largest cost. The home itself, life safety and accessibility work, insurance, and staffing hired before the first placement carry the project.

How long does it take to get a group home license in Maine?

Residential Care Application form lists initial application for 1 year and renewal application for 2 years

What are the requirements to open a group home in Maine?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Maine?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
