How to Start a Home Health Agency in Kansas

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Kansas licenses home health agencies as a Home Health Agency License - Skilled Services; this license level also covers Home and Community Based Services (HCBS) and Non-Medical Supportive Care Services, through the Kansas Department of Health and Environment (KDHE), Division of Public Health, Bureau of Facilities and Licensing. The governing law is Kansas Home Health Agency Act, K.S.A. 65-5101 to 65-5117, including 65-5102 (license required); Kansas Administrative Regulations 28-51-100 to 28-51-118, including 28-51-101 (licensing procedure). That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the Kansas license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The Kansas license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Kansas, step by step

  1. Email the Kansas Home Health Agency application and supporting documents to kdhe.bfla@ks.gov (KDHE application instructions)
  2. Submit the Skilled Services Attestation form, the Agency Release of Information form, the Personal Release of Information form and ownership attachments, plus evidence of licensure and a resume for the administrator and alternate administrator
  3. Pay the nonrefundable initial fee for the skilled tier, online through PayIt from July 1, 2025
  4. Submit the policies and procedures required by K.A.R. 28-51-101, as required by K.A.R. 28-51-103, 28-51-104, 28-51-117 and 28-51-118
  5. KDHE reviews the documents; when the requirements are met, KDHE issues the license. No person shall provide home health services, HCBS or supportive care services before a license is issued (K.A.R. 28-51-101)

Kansas home health license fees and costs

  • Initial Skilled Services license: $500.00 for an applicant intending to provide home health services or HCBS with a total unique patient and client count of under 100; $750.00 for 100 or more (KDHE Skilled Services HHA Application, K.A.R. 28-51-101)
  • Fees are nonrefundable; from July 1, 2025, KDHE home health agency fees are paid online through PayIt (KDHE licensure pages)

How long does it take in Kansas?

  • Each home health agency license remains in effect for one year unless suspended or revoked (K.A.R. 28-51-101, terms of license)

What catches Kansas applicants

  • License required before Medicare or Medicaid work: K.S.A. 65-5102 states any agency, including Medicare and Medicaid providers, that provides home health services, supportive care services or attendant care services specified in K.S.A. 65-5101 shall be licensed
  • Skilled services are medical services that must include nursing services and an order from a patient medical provider (KDHE Skilled Services page)
  • HCBS waiver program services require a skilled service license, as they may include medical services driven by a plan of care (KDHE Skilled Services page)
  • No certificate of need step appears in the KDHE skilled services application or licensure pages reviewed

The Medicare layer, after the Kansas license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a home health agency in Kansas?

The figures Kansas publishes are these: Initial Skilled Services license: $500.00 for an applicant intending to provide home health services or HCBS with a total unique patient and client count of under 100; $750.00 for 100 or more (KDHE Skilled Services HHA Application, K.A.R. 28-51-101) Fees are nonrefundable; from July 1, 2025, KDHE home health agency fees are paid online through PayIt (KDHE licensure pages) Beyond the published figures, the operating costs (clinical payroll before certification, insurance, and the reserve operating funds required by 42 CFR 489.28) are larger than every fee on the list.

How long does it take to get a home health license in Kansas?

Each home health agency license remains in effect for one year unless suspended or revoked (K.A.R. 28-51-101, terms of license) The federal certification that follows runs on the survey calendar, and new Medicare enrollment is under a CMS moratorium to about mid-May 2027.

What do I need to start a home health agency in Kansas?

The Home Health Agency License - Skilled Services; this license level also covers Home and Community Based Services (HCBS) and Non-Medical Supportive Care Services (Kansas Department of Health and Environment (KDHE), Division of Public Health, Bureau of Facilities and Licensing), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet Kansas rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in Kansas?

Apply to the Kansas Department of Health and Environment (KDHE), Division of Public Health, Bureau of Facilities and Licensing under Kansas Home Health Agency Act, K.S.A. 65-5101 to 65-5117, including 65-5102 (license required); Kansas Administrative Regulations 28-51-100 to 28-51-118, including 28-51-101 (licensing procedure). The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
