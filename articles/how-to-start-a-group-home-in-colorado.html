How to Start a Group Home in Colorado

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Colorado. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Colorado licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Colorado licenses

Group Home for persons with IDD (Community Residential Home)

Licensed by: CDPHE license plus HCPF and CDPHE PASA certification. Serves: Persons with intellectual and developmental disabilities. Licensed under 6 CCR 1011-1 Chapter 8, Part 2.5: a group living situation for at least 4 but no more than 8 persons, the same as a community residential home under Section 25.5-10-214, C.R.S.; Medicaid door is Residential Habilitation Group Home specialty 673 on the DD waiver, which requires the CDPHE Group Home license plus a PASA application

ICF/IID

Licensed by: CDPHE license plus federal Medicaid certification. Serves: Adults with intellectual and developmental disabilities needing active treatment. Chapter 8, Part 2.7: a residential facility certified by CMS to provide active treatment and habilitative, therapeutic, and specialized support; a facility is licensed as either ICF/IID or a Group Home depending on size and services (Part 3.1); federal layer at 42 CFR Part 483 Subpart I and Part 442; Medicaid only, no Medicare path

PASA residential habilitation, IRSS and Host Home

Licensed by: CDPHE PASA certification and HCPF enrollment, no facility license. Serves: People on the DD waiver receiving Residential Habilitation Services and Supports, including in a host home. Specialty 674 requires a PASA application and no CDPHE license, with the same survey and Certification and Transmittal route as other PASA specialties; RHSS at 10 CCR 2505-10, Section 8.7541 is 24-hour support in a residential setting; PASA is the CDPHE certification vehicle for DD, SLS, CES, and CHRP provider agencies

Assisted Living Residence

Licensed by: CDPHE. Serves: Adults needing assisted living residence care. Licensed under Sections 25-27-101 et seq., C.R.S. and 6 CCR 1011-1 Chapter 7; Medicaid layers on this license are Alternative Care Facility specialty 602, Supported Living Program specialty 680, and Transitional Living Program specialty 682 on the Brain Injury waiver, each an HCPF certification, not a separate license

Residential Child Care Facility (RCCF)

Licensed by: Colorado Department of Human Services (CDHS). Serves: Children and youth in residential child care. Licensed under Sections 26-6-901 to 26-6-923, C.R.S. and 12 CCR 2509-8, Section 7.701; overlays on the RCCF license, each with its own add-on fee at Section 7.701.4.E, are Qualified Residential Treatment Program, Psychiatric Residential Treatment Facility under 42 CFR 441.151 to 441.182, and Runaway and Homeless Youth and Shelter Care

Specialized Group Facility

Licensed by: Colorado Department of Human Services (CDHS). Serves: Children ages 3 to 16, older by court order, needing 24-hour care. Section 7.709: sponsored and supervised by a county department or a licensed child placement agency, 24-hour care for 3 or more but fewer than 12 children; two classes, specialized group home, where the group home parents live in a home they own or control, and specialized group center, supervised by a paid agency staff member with an MSW or behavioral science master's plus two years of experience (Section 7.709.22)

Secure Residential Treatment Center

Licensed by: Colorado Department of Human Services (CDHS). Serves: Children and youth needing secure residential treatment. A distinct CDHS licensed class with its own fee line in Section 26-6-907(1), C.R.S. and Section 7.701.4.E

Foster Care Home

Licensed by: CDHS certification through a county department or a licensed child placement agency. Serves: Children in foster care, including CHRP waiver children. Foster homes are certified, not licensed, under Section 7.708; CDHS rule at 12 CCR 2509-6, Section 7.500.3 sets a combined maximum of 3 individuals receiving an HCBS waiver in a foster care home for CHRP children; Therapeutic Foster Care and Treatment Foster Care are CPA license add-ons

Child Placement Agency

Licensed by: Colorado Department of Human Services (CDHS). Serves: Agencies placing children for foster care and adoption. Licensed under Section 7.710 with separate fee tables for CPA-Foster Care and CPA-Adoption at Section 7.701.4.E; an agency licensed for both pays one fee, the greater (Section 7.701.4.F)

Behavioral Health Entity residential and overnight services

Licensed by: Behavioral Health Administration (BHA). Serves: People receiving behavioral health residential and overnight care, including crisis, SUD, and services for children and youth under 18. One BHE license under Title 27, Article 50, C.R.S. and 2 CCR 502-1, residential and overnight standards at Section 2.26 and endorsements in Chapters 3 to 10; BHA may not issue or renew until it receives a certificate of compliance from the Division of Fire Prevention and Control (Section 27-50-501, C.R.S.); safety-net approval at Chapter 12 is a separate approval, not the license

Mental Health Transitional Living Home

Licensed by: Behavioral Health Administration (BHA) endorsement on a BHE license, HCPF waiver door. Serves: Adults in mental health transitional living, Level 1 on the CMHS waiver and Level 2 Supported Therapeutic Transitional Living. Rule revision MSB 25-09-03-B to 10 CCR 2505-10, Section 8.7532 moves MHTL licensing and oversight from CDPHE to BHA, with existing providers transitioning to the BHA license when their ALR license expires; Level 1 is Provider Type 36, specialty 682 as published in HCPF IM 25-007, and Level 2 is Provider Type 96, specialty 561; the older CDPHE route printed ALR license plus CDHS contract at specialty 755, with CDHS not entering new Level 1 contracts

Recovery residence

Licensed by: BHA-approved certifying body today, BHA license from July 1, 2027. Serves: People in recovery living in a recovery residence or sober living home. Current law at Section 27-80-129(3), C.R.S.: a home may not operate or advertise as a recovery residence, and licensed providers may not refer to it, unless it is certified by a BHA-approved certifying body (Ohio Recovery Housing, Colorado, under contract with BHA), chartered by Oxford House, or a 30-year home as of May 23, 2019; SB 26-113, effective August 12, 2026, requires a BHA license on and after July 1, 2027 under new Sections 27-50-1001 to 27-50-1008, C.R.S.

Nursing facility

Licensed by: CDPHE. Serves: Residents needing nursing facility care. Licensed under 6 CCR 1011-1 Chapter 5; administrator holds an active Colorado nursing home administrator license under 3 CCR 717-1; federal layer at 42 CFR Part 483 Subpart B with CMS-855A enrollment through PECOS and Novitas Jurisdiction H, plus CDPHE survey

Adult Day Services

Licensed by: HCPF certification with CDPHE certification survey, no CDPHE license. Serves: Adults receiving facility-based adult day services. HCPF certification at 10 CCR 2505-10, Section 8.491, specialty 601 in the expansion track list, through the letter of intent, CDPHE survey, and Certification and Transmittal route; CDPHE charges no fee for Medicaid Certification applications

How to start a group home in Colorado, step by step

  1. Comply with local building and zoning codes before initial licensure and renewal and comply with Chapter 2 (Chapter 8, Part 3.2); a facility is licensed as either a Group Home or an ICF/IID depending on size and services offered (Part 3.1)
  2. Obtain the CDPHE Chapter 8 Group Home license for the home (at least 4 and no more than 8 persons)
  3. File the Program Approved Service Agency (PASA) application, which is required for DD waiver Residential Habilitation Group Home specialty 673
  4. Pass the CDPHE initial survey; a zero-deficiency survey produces a Certification and Transmittal (C&T) uploaded to COHFI
  5. Attach the C&T and a copy of the Group Home license to the HCPF Medicaid Provider Enrollment Application for specialty 673; HCPF enrollment is per physical location for PASA specialties
  6. Operate under the Chapter 8 standards, including governing body, administrator, personnel and staffing, training, admissions, resident rights, resident funds and records, dietary, medications including QMAP, and emergency management (Chapter 8, Parts 4 to 21)

Colorado group home license fees and costs

  • Chapter 8 Group Home, current rule figures in the mirror through June 10, 2025 (Part 3.3): initial $2,821.89, renewal $423.29 effective July 1, 2024; ICF/IID: initial $6,772.56, renewal $1,806.01 effective July 1, 2024; the 2022 rule text printed initial Group Home $2,612.62 and ICF/IID $6,270.31 with renewals of $391.90 and $1,672.08; confirm against the CDPHE facility fee schedule in effect on the filing date
  • CDPHE charges no fee for Medicaid Certification applications on the PASA, Adult Day, and group home Certification and Transmittal routes; HCPF may charge a fee for Provider Enrollment
  • Nursing facility (Chapter 5, Section 32, all fees non-refundable and submitted with the application): initial license $6,772.56 per facility; annual renewal for a Medicare and/or Medicaid certified facility $1,806.01 base plus $9.03 per bed, and for a non-certified facility $3,928.09 base plus $9.03 per bed; change of ownership $6,772.56 per facility; opening a secure unit $1,806.01 in addition to other fees
  • Assisted Living Residence initial license by licensed beds (Chapter 7, Part 3.6, rule figures, all non-refundable): 3 to 8 beds $6,804.63, 9 to 19 beds $7,884.73, 20 to 49 beds $9,450.88, 50 to 99 beds $12,475.16, 100 or more beds $15,931.48, and a qualifying disproportionate share facility $3,240.30; renewal $388.84 per facility plus $111.25 per bed; provisional license $1,080.10 (Part 3.8)
  • CDHS fees at 12 CCR 2509-8, Section 7.701.4.E, beginning July 1, 2025 and beyond: Residential Child Care Facility original $1,803.00, with QRTP add-on original $500.00, PRTF add-on original $500.00, and Runaway and Homeless Youth and Shelter Care add-on original $250.00, each plus the RCCF fee; Specialized Group Facility original $365.00 and continuation $275.00; Secure Residential Treatment Center original $1,854.00; Child Placement Agency Foster Care original $1,428.00 and Adoption original $1,091.00; changes to all license types include increase to licensed capacity $100.00 and duplicate license $44.00
  • BHA Behavioral Health Entity license fees were not captured from a primary source in the research, so no BHA fee figure is stated; the packets direct the applicant to confirm the current fee with BHA before filing
  • Recovery residence: no BHA fee exists yet; BHA must adopt minimum standards and a fee schedule by May 1, 2027; the Ohio Recovery Housing, Colorado certification fee is the certifier's own schedule and is not printed as a state fee figure in the research
  • Federal institutional provider enrollment fee on the nursing facility CMS-855A door: $750 for calendar year 2026

How long does it take in Colorado?

  • CDHS licensing clocks (Section 7.701.4): application fee due with the application at least 90 calendar days before the anticipated opening date, and annual continuation fee plus declaration at least 60 calendar days before the license anniversary; a move requires a new original application and original fee; the research also logs a conflicting 60-day completed-application line at Section 7.701.2 and prints the fee section's 90-day rule
  • Unlicensed operation penalties for CDHS classes: up to $250 per day on day one, $500 on day two, $1,000 per day on day three and after, capped at $10,000 (Section 26-6-921, C.R.S.)
  • BHA must act on a complete BHE application within 30 days (Section 27-50-501, C.R.S.); BHE licenses run one year (Section 27-50-503); provisional licenses are 90 days, with a second for a like term and fee and no third in the same year
  • Recovery residence transition: BHA license required on and after July 1, 2027; a residence certified under Section 27-80-129 as of June 30, 2027 must notify BHA before July 1, 2027, its certification is in lieu of licensure until it is licensed, and on and after July 1, 2028 a previously certified residence may not operate without an approved license; Section 27-80-129 is repealed effective July 1, 2027
  • CDPHE fee schedules for group homes, IDD providers, and nursing homes republish March 1, 2027 and take effect July 1, 2027

What are the requirements to open a group home in Colorado?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Colorado?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
