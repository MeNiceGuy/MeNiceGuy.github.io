How to Start a Group Home in South Dakota

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in South Dakota. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type South Dakota licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types South Dakota licenses

Assisted Living Center

Licensed by: South Dakota Department of Health, Office of Health Care Facilities Licensure and Certification. Serves: Residents who do not require more than intermittent nursing care by a licensed nurse. Licensed by the state to provide personal care and services beyond basic food, shelter, and laundry. Licensure rules are at ARSD 44:70, under SDCL chapter 34-12. Licensure may include special approvals to offer medication administration, care of the cognitively or physically impaired, oxygen administration, and therapeutic diets; the posted license indicates which approvals are granted. An assisted living center may also provide home health agency services for short-term skilled services for a specific medical reason. Assisted living is a covered service under the HOPE Waiver per the Medicaid.gov waiver factsheet.

Adult Foster Care Home

Licensed by: South Dakota Department of Health, Office of Health Care Facilities Licensure and Certification. Serves: Aged, blind, physically disabled, developmentally disabled, or socially or emotionally disabled adults who obtain personal care, health supervision services, and household services in a family atmosphere. Family residences licensed by the state. Licensure rules are listed by DOH as ARSD 44:77, Adult Foster Care Facilities.

Community Living Home

Licensed by: South Dakota Department of Health, Health Facility Licensure. Serves: At least one but no more than four adult residents unrelated to the owner or operator. A family-style residence whose owner or operator is engaged in the business of providing individualized and independent residential community living support for compensation. The home serves at least one but no more than four adult residents unrelated to the owner or operator, provides one or more regularly scheduled health-related services directly or in collaboration with an outside health care provider, and is subject to inspections to ensure resident health and safety needs are met. Licensed under SDCL Title 34-12 and ARSD 44:82. The term does not include any setting certified or accredited through chapter 34-20A, Title 27A, or Title 27B. Community living home is a covered service under the HOPE Waiver per the Medicaid.gov waiver factsheet.

Residential Living Center

Licensed by: South Dakota Department of Health (registration). Serves: Two or more elderly or disabled persons not related to the owner, receiving services for compensation. Residential Living Centers are not licensed but are required to register with the state, and to register annually, to provide services for compensation for two or more elderly or disabled persons not related to the owner. DOH cites chapter 44:23:01, Registration of Residential Living Centers, on its Get Licensed page.

Residential Habilitation under the CHOICES Waiver

Licensed by: South Dakota Department of Human Services, Division of Developmental Disabilities (program); Medicaid coverage through the Department of Social Services. Serves: Children and adults with intellectual and developmental disabilities who meet an ICF/IID level of care. The CHOICES Waiver provides residential habilitation and shared living residential habilitation, along with case management, day services, and supported employment, to individuals with intellectual disabilities or developmental disabilities who meet an ICF/IID level of care (Medicaid.gov waiver factsheet). The state CHOICES description states residential services help individuals live as independently as possible and range from around-the-clock supervision to daily check-ins.

How to start a group home in South Dakota, step by step

  1. Submit a written request for information to license and/or certify a specific facility type to the Office of Health Facilities Licensure and Certification (DOH Get Licensed as a Healthcare Facility page)
  2. Application forms will be sent when eligibility for licensure or certification is evident
  3. Buildings must meet state and federal construction, electrical, and plumbing requirements specific for the intended use
  4. Community living homes: obtain licensure through the South Dakota Department of Health and follow the Administrative Rules of South Dakota for Community Living Homes, Article 44:82

How long does it take in South Dakota?

  • Residential Living Centers are registered, not licensed, and are required to register annually with the South Dakota Department of Health (ARSD chapter 44:23, cited on the DOH Get Licensed page)

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get a group home license in South Dakota?

Residential Living Centers are registered, not licensed, and are required to register annually with the South Dakota Department of Health (ARSD chapter 44:23, cited on the DOH Get Licensed page)

What are the requirements to open a group home in South Dakota?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in South Dakota?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
