How to Start a Group Home in Oklahoma

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Oklahoma. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Oklahoma licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Oklahoma licenses

DDS Group Home

Licensed by: Oklahoma Department of Human Services (OKDHS), Developmental Disabilities Services (DDS). Serves: Residents 18 years of age or older with intellectual disability; DDS serves individuals age 3 and older with intellectual disabilities and certain persons with related conditions who would otherwise require ICF/IID placement. Group Homes offer living arrangements for 6 to 12 people who share a home and receive up to 24 hours per day of supervision, support, and training in daily living skills. Group Homes are single-family homes in the community, owned or leased by a private agency, and the agency receives reimbursement from DDS for supervising and supporting residents - OKDHS DDS Service Information: Group Homes. Group home services are provided per OAC 317:40-5-152, and group homes must be licensed by OKDHS per Title 10, section 1430.1 et seq. of the Oklahoma Statutes, as stated in that rule. Residential waiver funding named in OAC 317:40-1-1 includes the Community Waiver and the Homeward Bound Waiver.

Assisted Living Center

Licensed by: Oklahoma State Department of Health, Health Facility Systems, Long-Term Care Service. Serves: Two or more unrelated persons domiciled in the center who need personal care, nursing supervision, intermittent nursing, medication assistance, or transfer and ambulation assistance. An assisted living center is any home or establishment offering, coordinating or providing services to two or more persons who are domiciled therein, are unrelated to the operator, need assistance with personal care or nursing supervision by choice or functional impairments, may need intermittent or unscheduled nursing care, may need medication assistance, and may need assistance with transfer or ambulation - OAC 310:663-1-2, citing 63 O.S. 1-890.2(1). An assisted living center cannot provide 24-hour skilled nursing care as provided in a nursing facility - OSDH Long Term Care Programs page. State license required; Medicare and Medicaid certification: none - same page.

Residential Care Home

Licensed by: Oklahoma State Department of Health, Health Facility Systems. Serves: Persons who need supportive assistance in a residential setting, including housekeeping and help with eating, bathing and dressing, and who do not routinely require nursing care - OSDH long-term care ombudsman guide hosted at oklahoma.gov. The residential care home offers or provides residential accommodations, food service, and supportive assistance. It may provide assistance with meals, dressing, bathing and other personal needs, and may assist in administration of medications. It cannot provide medical care. State license required; Medicare and Medicaid certification: none - OSDH Long Term Care Programs page. License application is ODH Form 728 - OSDH Health Facility Systems page.

Intermediate Care Facility for Individuals with Intellectual Disabilities (ICF/IID)

Licensed by: Oklahoma State Department of Health for state licensure of long-term care facilities; Oklahoma Health Care Authority for SoonerCare level of care at OAC 317:30-5-122. Serves: Persons with intellectual disabilities or related conditions needing health or habilitative services in a protected residential setting. ICF/IID care is for persons with intellectual disabilities or related conditions to provide health or habilitative services in a protected residential setting. To qualify for ICF/IID level of care, persons must have substantial functional limitations in three or more areas of major life activity - OAC 317:30-5-122 as reproduced in Oklahoma Health Care Authority regulatory text at oklahoma.gov.

DDS residential supports - Specialized Foster Care, Agency Companion, Daily Living Supports and Extensive Residential Supports

Licensed by: Oklahoma Department of Human Services, Developmental Disabilities Services, under contracts with the Oklahoma Health Care Authority. Serves: Persons with intellectual disability or certain persons with related conditions eligible for DDS Home and Community-Based Services waivers. Residential supports in OAC 317:30-5-422 include agency companion services, specialized foster care, daily living supports under the Community Waiver and the Homeward Bound Waiver, group home services, extensive residential supports, and community transition services. Providers must enter contractual agreements with the Oklahoma Health Care Authority for these residential support services.

How to start a group home in Oklahoma, step by step

  1. Assisted Living Center: file the application for an initial license at least 30 days before beginning operation - ODH Form 624, citing OAC 310:663-21-1, 21-2 and 27-1 and 2
  2. Group Home: group home services require a completed and approved application to provide DDS group home services and a current contract with the Oklahoma Health Care Authority to provide DDS Home and Community-Based Services, as stated in OAC 317:40-5-152 as reproduced in state policy text

Oklahoma group home license fees and costs

  • Assisted Living Center and Continuum of Care Facility license fee: $10 for each bed per year included in maximum bed capacity; licenses renewed every three years effective November 1, 2021, so total fee equals number of beds times $10 times 3 years - ODH Form 624
  • Assisted Living Center establishment application fee: a nonrefundable application fee on a sliding scale developed by the State Board of Health, not to exceed $1,000 for each application - 63 O.S. 1-890.4, in the OSDH Continuum of Care and Assisted Living Act text

How long does it take in Oklahoma?

  • Assisted Living Center initial license application must be filed at least 30 days before beginning operation - ODH Form 624

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to open a group home in Oklahoma?

The figures Oklahoma publishes are these: Assisted Living Center and Continuum of Care Facility license fee: $10 for each bed per year included in maximum bed capacity; licenses renewed every three years effective November 1, 2021, so total fee equals number of beds times $10 times 3 years - ODH Form 624 Assisted Living Center establishment application fee: a nonrefundable application fee on a sliding scale developed by the State Board of Health, not to exceed $1,000 for each application - 63 O.S. 1-890.4, in the OSDH Continuum of Care and Assisted Living Act text The license fee is rarely the largest cost. The home itself, life safety and accessibility work, insurance, and staffing hired before the first placement carry the project.

How long does it take to get a group home license in Oklahoma?

Assisted Living Center initial license application must be filed at least 30 days before beginning operation - ODH Form 624

What are the requirements to open a group home in Oklahoma?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Oklahoma?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
