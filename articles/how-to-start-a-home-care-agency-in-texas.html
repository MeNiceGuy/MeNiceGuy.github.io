How to Start a Home Care Agency in Texas (The HCSSA License, Step by Step)

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · October 25, 2026

The call usually starts the same way. Someone has decided to start a non-medical home care agency in Texas, they have families asking for help with bathing, meals, and getting through the day safely at home, and they assume non-medical means no license. In some states that assumption holds. In Texas it does not, and finding that out late is expensive.

Texas licenses this business. The license is called a Home and Community Support Services Agency license, HCSSA for short, and a non-medical startup applies in one category only: Personal Assistance Services, or PAS. Providing personal assistance services without that license, or even using the words personal assistance services to advertise work you are not licensed to do, is a violation of Texas law. I walk the full path in the video below, and this article walks it in the same order, with every fee and deadline checked against the Texas Health and Human Services Commission, HHSC, and Title 26, Chapter 558 of the Texas Administrative Code.

The demand is real, and it is not the bottleneck

Start with why Texas draws so many new owners. The Census Bureau puts Texas population at 31,709,821 as of July 1, 2025, and 14.9 percent of residents are age 65 or older. Run the math and you get about 4.72 million seniors. The same Census QuickFacts page for Texas puts median household income at $78,476, which matters because private pay home care is bought out of household income. The clients are there. Getting licensed in the right category, in the right order, is the part that stops new owners.

One category, drawn before you apply

HHSC defines Personal Assistance Services as routine, ongoing care or services required by a person in a residence or independent living environment that enables that person to engage in activities of daily living, including respite. That is the non-medical lane: companionship, homemaking, hands-on personal care, and respite, delivered at home.

Draw the line before you file, not after. Anything on the skilled list pushes an agency into a different category, Licensed Home Health Services, with different staffing: nursing, including blood pressure monitoring and diabetes treatment, physical, occupational, speech, or respiratory therapy, medical social services, intravenous therapy, dialysis, services by unlicensed personnel under a licensed health professional, furnishing medical equipment and supplies, or nutritional counseling. A PAS only agency is not required to have a supervising nurse, and PAS cannot become Medicare certified. Only the Licensed Home Health, Licensed and Certified Home Health, and hospice categories can. If your service list, your website, or your brochure promises skilled work, you have described a different agency than the one a PAS application builds. The full rulebook is 26 TAC Chapter 558, Licensing Standards for Home and Community Support Services Agencies, under Texas Health and Safety Code Chapter 142.

The seven step path HHSC actually uses

The sequence matters more than any single form, because two training steps have to happen before HHSC will accept the packet at all.

First, complete the application. For a parent agency that is Form 2021. Second, the administrator and the alternate administrator both complete the HHSC presurvey computer based training, and the signed participation certificates go in with the application. Packets are not accepted without them, so treat the training as step one in practice, not as paperwork you catch up on later. Third, submit everything through TULIP, the Texas Unified Licensure Information Portal, with all documents and the license fee, and confirm the portal shows payment received. HHSC's How to Become a Licensed HCSSA Provider page lays out that same sequence.

Fourth, once the initial license issues, begin services. Fifth, enroll your first client, then file Form 2020, Notification of Readiness for Initial Survey, with the regional office no later than six months after the license effective date. Sixth and seventh, prepare for the initial survey and complete it. That second clock is the one owners miss. The license is not the finish line. It starts a six month deadline that only a filed Form 2020 stops.

Fees and the state clock, in the state's own numbers

The fee schedule is published by HHSC in its presurvey training. On the HHSC license fees page, the initial licensing fee for a three year license is $2,625 for a parent agency or branch and $1,500 for an alternate delivery site. Renewal for a three year license is $2,625 for a parent agency or branch and $900 for an alternate delivery site. Reporting most changes carries a $30 standard fee. Every license fee is non-refundable, which is why a complete first submission matters more in Texas than in most states.

The processing clock is written into 26 TAC 558.31. HHSC has up to 45 days to process a thoroughly complete application. If the application is incomplete, HHSC notifies you and you have 30 days to respond with complete and correct information or the application is denied. After that response, the license is issued or denied within 45 days. HHSC states the same point plainly on its Step 3, Submit the Application page: submit the missing information no later than 30 days after the notice date, or HHSC considers the packet incomplete and will deny the application, and HHSC does not refund the license fee. The license itself is good for three years from the date of issuance. Renewal has to be filed at least 45 days before expiration to avoid a late fee. For a three year parent agency license, that late fee is $1,312.50.

The administrator rule for a PAS only agency

Every agency designates an administrator and an alternate administrator, and both have to meet the qualification before designation and complete the presurvey training. For a PAS only agency, HHSC states the bar this way on its administrator qualifications page: a high school diploma or GED with at least one year of experience or training in caring for individuals with functional disabilities, or two years of full time study at an accredited college or university in a health related field. A first time administrator also completes 8 clock hours of training in agency administration before designation and 16 more within the first 12 months, under 26 TAC 558.259, with continuing education after that. Name people who actually meet the rule and can prove it with documents. A designation HHSC cannot verify is a deficiency waiting to happen.

Background checks and policies are not back office details

Before anyone is hired, the screening work is set by rule. Criminal history checks run under Texas Health and Safety Code Chapter 250. Unlicensed staff, volunteers, and contractors with face to face client contact are checked against the Employee Misconduct Registry and the Nurse Aide Registry before hire and annually, and an agency may not employ a person listed as unemployable. Build those checks into your hiring file from the first caregiver, because surveyors read personnel files, not intentions.

The same is true of policies. Chapter 558 requires written policies in specific areas, including client care, client rights, infection control, staffing and employability verification, personnel records, financial solvency and business records, emergency preparedness, and the standards specific to PAS at 26 TAC 558.404. Write the policies to the rule sections, keep them in the order a surveyor will ask for them, and train to them. At the initial survey, the review reaches client records, policies, personnel files, and your complaint and quality processes. An agency that looks ready on paper and runs that way in the field passes that day very differently from one that bought a generic binder.

The practical warning

Here is the warning I give every Texas owner. Do not operate, advertise, or hold yourself out as providing personal assistance services before the license issues, and do not let the survey clock run while you wait for the perfect first client. The two failures I see are mirror images: starting too early without a license, and starting too late on Form 2020 after the license arrives. Both are avoidable with a calendar built on the day you file, not the day you open.

One more boundary to keep straight. The HCSSA license authorizes operation. Texas Medicaid enrollment is a separate application through the Provider Enrollment and Management System, and electronic visit verification, EVV, applies to Medicaid personal care services, not to private pay work. Getting licensed does not enroll you, and enrolling does not license you.

Your next step

I keep the Texas fees, the license types, and the timeline collected in one place on the Texas state guide, with the walkthrough video embedded on the same page.

Write your service list down first, in plain language, and check every item against the PAS definition and the skilled trigger list above. If the list is clean, designate an administrator and alternate who meet 26 TAC 558.244, finish the presurvey training, and only then file Form 2021 in TULIP. That order is the whole game in Texas. Owners who follow it spend their 45 days waiting on a decision. Owners who reverse it spend theirs answering deficiencies.
