How to Start a Home Health Agency in California

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

California licenses home health agencies as a Home Health Agency (HHA) license, through the California Department of Public Health (CDPH), Center for Health Care Quality, Licensing and Certification Program, Centralized Applications Branch (CAB). The governing law is Health and Safety Code sections 1725 to 1742 and Health and Safety Code section 1728 and 1728.1 as cited in the CDPH packet and checklist; Title 22 California Code of Regulations section 74600(a) definition and section 74661 application requirements; Senate Bill 164, Chapter 27, Statutes of 2026, for the licensure moratorium. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the California license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The California license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in California, step by step

  1. After June 29, 2026, begin by submitting an exception request to CAB: a written justification and supporting documentation to demonstrate need based on geographic concentration
  2. If CAB determines there is a need based on geographic concentration, CAB notifies the applicant that they may submit an application
  3. Submit a completed application packet to CAB, not to the local CDPH district office, using the provider instructions and provider checklist
  4. Include the packet forms listed by CDPH, including HS 200, HS 215A, HS 309, CDPH 322, CDPH 325 and BCIA 8016, and, for certification, HS 328, DHCS 9098, CMS 1572, CMS 1561, CMS 855A and HHS 690
  5. Complete CAB review: on the CDPH Apply for Licensure page, application fees are not accepted until a CAB analyst validates that all required forms and supporting documents are received

California home health license fees and costs

  • Home Health Agencies: statewide license fee $2,946 per facility for fiscal year 2026-27, effective July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027, on the CDPH licensing fee page
  • Home Health Agencies in Los Angeles County: supplemental license fee $2,103 shown in the same CDPH fiscal year 2026-27 fee table

How long does it take in California?

  • Moratorium on new HHA licensure under SB 164 applies as of June 29, 2026, covering initial licensure, addition of a branch office, change of geographical service area, and change of location outside an approved geographical service area

What catches California applicants

  • A state license is required to operate as a Home Health Agency in California, per the CDPH HHA Initial Application Packet
  • CDPH defines an HHA at Title 22 CCR section 74600(a) as an organization which provides, or arranges for the provision of, skilled nursing services to persons in their temporary or permanent place of residence
  • There is no general certificate of need program named for HHAs in the CDPH packet reviewed; after June 29, 2026 the operative need test is the SB 164 moratorium exception based on geographic concentration
  • For a licensed-only HHA with no Medi-Cal or Medicare, the CDPH checklist cites Title 22 CCR section 74661(a)(6): evidence of sufficient financial resources to operate for the first 3 months is required
  • Whether state licensure must precede Medicare certification was not stated in a single sentence on the CDPH packet page fetched; CDPH handles licensure and certification in the same packet, with separate Medicare forms listed in the packet
  • SB 164 also requires fingerprints to the California Department of Justice for any person with a 5 percent or greater interest in a corporation, partnership or association that owns a private HHA, per the CDPH packet

The Medicare layer, after the California license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a home health agency in California?

The figures California publishes are these: Home Health Agencies: statewide license fee $2,946 per facility for fiscal year 2026-27, effective July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027, on the CDPH licensing fee page Home Health Agencies in Los Angeles County: supplemental license fee $2,103 shown in the same CDPH fiscal year 2026-27 fee table Beyond the published figures, the operating costs (clinical payroll before certification, insurance, and the reserve operating funds required by 42 CFR 489.28) are larger than every fee on the list.

How long does it take to get a home health license in California?

Moratorium on new HHA licensure under SB 164 applies as of June 29, 2026, covering initial licensure, addition of a branch office, change of geographical service area, and change of location outside an approved geographical service area The federal certification that follows runs on the survey calendar, and new Medicare enrollment is under a CMS moratorium to about mid-May 2027.

What do I need to start a home health agency in California?

The Home Health Agency (HHA) license (California Department of Public Health (CDPH), Center for Health Care Quality, Licensing and Certification Program, Centralized Applications Branch (CAB)), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet California rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in California?

Apply to the California Department of Public Health (CDPH), Center for Health Care Quality, Licensing and Certification Program, Centralized Applications Branch (CAB) under Health and Safety Code sections 1725 to 1742 and Health and Safety Code section 1728 and 1728.1 as cited in the CDPH packet and checklist; Title 22 California Code of Regulations section 74600(a) definition and section 74661 application requirements; Senate Bill 164, Chapter 27, Statutes of 2026, for the licensure moratorium. The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
