How to Start a Group Home in Alaska

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Alaska. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Alaska licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Alaska licenses

Assisted Living Home (ALH)

Licensed by: Alaska Department of Health, Residential Licensing Unit, Division of Health Care Services. Serves: Adults needing assisted living care. The SDS waiver guidance lists ALH license categories including Developmental Disability and Mental Health (DDMH), serving populations tied to APDD, CCMC for ages 18 to 22, and IDD, and Senior Services (SS), with APDD and IDD only if the provider has an approved licensing variance. Residential Licensing licenses assisted living homes, provides orientation on state rules, licensing and fees, inspects all licensed facilities annually, and maintains a current list of licensed homes. An onsite inspection is part of the application process.

Residential Habilitation - Group Home (waiver group home)

Licensed by: Alaska Department of Health, Senior and Disabilities Services (SDS) for waiver provider certification, with the home itself holding a State of Alaska assisted living home license. Serves: Adults age 18 or older receiving residential habilitation under the APDD, CCMC, or IDD waiver programs, per the SDS waiver guidance and rate chart summaries in official SDS materials. All group homes must be operated by the agency seeking SDS certification. The applicant submits a Group Home Habilitation Site Information form (Cert-12), reviews the Residential Habilitation Services Conditions of Participation, and submits a copy of the current ALH license for the home. Funded through Alaska Home and Community-Based Waiver Services.

Residential Habilitation - Family Home (adult and child) and Residential Supported Living

Licensed by: Alaska Department of Health, Senior and Disabilities Services (SDS) for waiver certification, with Residential Licensing for the underlying home license. Serves: Waiver recipients under ALI, APDD, CCMC, and IDD programs. Residential Supported Living service declarations in official SDS materials select ALI and APDD, and state that an ALH license indicating the provider can serve both populations, or an approved variance, is required to serve both. Family Home Habilitation for adults requires a Family Home Habilitation Site Information form (Cert-13) and a copy of the current ALH license. Family Home Habilitation for children requires Cert-13 and a copy of the current Community Care Foster Home License. Residential Supported Living is described in 7 AAC 130.255 in the official SDS service declaration. ISW is stated in SDS guidance as not eligible for licensed residential services.

How to start a group home in Alaska, step by step

  1. For an assisted living home: download and complete the New Home Application Packet and submit it to Residential Licensing
  2. For an assisted living home: a supervisor reviews the application and assigns a specialist to help the applicant meet licensing requirements, and an onsite inspection is part of the process
  3. For waiver residential services: submit the SDS Waiver Services Provider Certification Application (Cert-01), the service declarations for each service requested, and the Provider Certification Application Policy Assurances form (Cert-37), with a copy of the current State of Alaska business license and the current ALH license for the home
  4. For a waiver group home specifically: submit the Group Home Habilitation Site Information form (Cert-12)

How long does it take in Alaska?

  • Licensed assisted living homes are inspected annually, per the Residential Licensing page

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get a group home license in Alaska?

Licensed assisted living homes are inspected annually, per the Residential Licensing page

What are the requirements to open a group home in Alaska?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Alaska?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
