How to Start a Group Home in Florida

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Florida. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Florida licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Florida licenses

Assisted living facility, Standard, ECC, LNS and LMH licenses

Licensed by: AHCA; Chapter 429 Part I, Rule 59A-36, Form 3110-1008. Serves: Residents needing assisted living, including Extended Congregate Care, Limited Nursing Services and Limited Mental Health specialty licenses. ALF with Limited Mental Health specialty is mental health group living in the completeness map; LMH adds no fee in the verified schedule

Adult family care home

Licensed by: AHCA; Chapter 429 Part II, Rule 59A-37, Form 3180-1022. Serves: Adults in a small family care home setting. Separate full track from the ALF track

Adult day care center, including Specialized Alzheimer designation

Licensed by: AHCA; Chapter 429 Part III, Rule 59A-16, Form 3180-1004. Serves: Adults in a day care center program. County and municipal centers are exempt from the fee under section 429.907(4)

Nursing home / nursing facility

Licensed by: AHCA; Chapter 400 Part II, Rule 59A-4, Form 3110-6001. Serves: Residents needing skilled nursing facility care. CON required for new skilled nursing facilities before licensure projects; homes for special services under section 400.801 is a named register entry inside this track because AHCA publishes no HSS application form in the sources checked

ICF/DD (ICF/IID)

Licensed by: AHCA; Chapter 400 Part VIII, section 400.962, Rule 59A-26, Form 3110-5003. Serves: Individuals with developmental disabilities needing intermediate care. CON required for ICF/DD before licensure projects

Prescribed pediatric extended care center (PPEC)

Licensed by: AHCA; Chapter 400 Part VI, Rule 59A-13, Form 3110-8002. Serves: Medically complex children in a day program. License expires two years from issuance under Rule 59A-13.004 as cited in the research

Transitional living facility

Licensed by: AHCA; Chapter 400 Part XI, section 400.9972, Rule 59A-17, Form 3110-9001. Serves: Residents in transitional living, including spinal cord and brain injury populations served by the class. Full track in the expansion research

APD residential, foster care facility, 3 or fewer residents

Licensed by: Agency for Persons with Disabilities; Chapter 393, Rule 65G-2. Serves: People with developmental disabilities in a small APD licensed home. Class inside the APD residential track; no APD license fee figure appears in Rule 65G-2.002 or on the APD group home pages checked, so no fee is quoted

APD residential, group home facility, 4 to 15 residents

Licensed by: Agency for Persons with Disabilities; Chapter 393, Rule 65G-2. Serves: People with developmental disabilities in a group home. Class inside the APD residential track; license valid one year at most and renewal due at least 45 days before expiration

APD residential, residential habilitation center, 9 or more residents

Licensed by: Agency for Persons with Disabilities; Chapter 393, Rule 65G-2. Serves: People with developmental disabilities in a larger residential habilitation setting. No new RHC licenses after October 1, 1989 and capacity may not increase, per section 393.063 register note in the research

APD supported living

Licensed by: Agency for Persons with Disabilities; Chapter 393, APD enrollment, iBudget and CDC+ programs. Serves: People with developmental disabilities living in their own home with supported living coaching. Supported living in the person own home, including iBudget Supported Living Coaching and CDC+ self-direction; APD iBudget provider enrollment is a free process per APD provider pages and is separate from a facility license, and a Medicaid provider number from AHCA is also required

Mental health residential treatment facility (RTF)

Licensed by: AHCA and DCF system as cited; section 394.875, Rule 65E-4, Form 3180-5005. Serves: People needing mental health residential treatment. Crisis Stabilization Unit and Short-term Residential Treatment are a register inside this track, on Form 3180-5003

Residential treatment center for children and adolescents, including therapeutic group home and QRTP

Licensed by: Section 394.875, Rule 65E-9, Form 3180-5004. Serves: Children and adolescents needing residential treatment. Therapeutic group home is 12 or fewer children under Rule 65E-9.006; community residential home is 7 to 14 beds and is also a Chapter 419 siting overlay, not a separate license; QRTP is named inside this track

Substance use residential, Levels 1 to 4

Licensed by: Department of Children and Families; Chapter 397, Rule 65D-30, DCF LEADS, Form CF-MH 4024. Serves: People needing residential substance use treatment at Levels 1 to 4 and day or night treatment with community housing. All Chapter 397 components are in this track; recovery residences under section 397.487 have no license, voluntary FARR certification only, as a register entry inside this track

Child-caring agency homes, traditional group home, residential group home, maternity home, runaway shelter, emergency shelter and wilderness camp

Licensed by: Department of Children and Families; section 409.175, Rule 65C-46, Form CF-FSP 5412. Serves: Children in child-caring agency operated homes. Traditional group home capacity is 14 or fewer in the completeness map; QRTP credential is named in this track; no license fee figure was found in Rule 65C-46 or section 409.175 in the sources checked, so no fee is quoted; family foster homes (Rule 65C-45) and child-placing agencies (Rule 65C-15) are register entries, not facility tracks

How to start a group home in Florida, step by step

  1. Identify the exact license class and form for the home type on the AHCA Applications for Licensure page or the APD or DCF door that owns the class, for example ALF Form 3110-1008, AFCH Form 3180-1022, nursing facility Form 3110-6001, ICF/DD Form 3110-5003, PPEC Form 3110-8002 or TLF Form 3110-9001
  2. Complete Certificate of Need review first where the research requires it: new hospices, skilled nursing facilities and ICF/DD require CON review before licensure projects
  3. Complete Level 2 background screening under section 408.809 as part of Florida Medicaid and provider enrollment, and meet the screening the license class requires
  4. File the current AHCA, APD or DCF application form for the class; the research warns AHCA forms checked November 12, 2026 are the August 2023 editions and AHCA revises forms without notice, so pull the current form on the day of filing
  5. Pay the verified fee for the class (see gh_fees), or, for APD residential and child-caring classes where the research found no fee figure, quote no fee
  6. Enroll separately for Medicaid through FLMMIS where payment is sought; for APD homes complete APD iBudget provider enrollment and obtain a Medicaid provider number from AHCA, full enrollment type

Florida group home license fees and costs

  • ALF, Form 3110-1008: standard license $387.73 plus $64.96 per private-pay bed, cap $14,253.64; ECC adds $546.07 plus $10.15 per bed; LNS adds $322.77 plus $10.15 per bed; LMH no added fee; biennial assessment $2.00 per bed, maximum $300; change fee $25
  • Adult family care home, Form 3180-1022: $226.34 initial and renewal; change $25; statute section 429.67(3) prints $200 per biennium, conflict noted in the research with the form figure quoted
  • Adult day care, Form 3180-1004: $172.55; change $25; statute section 429.907(3) prints a $150 cap, conflict noted with the form figure quoted
  • Nursing facility: $100 per bed biennial plus $0.50 per bed resident protection plus $12 per bed data collection assessment, total $112.50 per bed (Rule 59A-4.103, section 400.062(3) and section 408.20(1)(b) as cited)
  • ICF/DD, Form 3110-5003: $262.88 per bed plus $300 biennial assessment; change $25; statute section 400.962(3) prints $234 per bed unless modified by rule, conflict noted with the form figure quoted
  • PPEC, Rule 59A-13.004: biennial licensure fee $1,512.35
  • Transitional living facility, Form 3110-9001: $4,588 plus $90 per bed per biennium; bed increase $90 per bed; change $25
  • CSU/SRT, Form 3180-5003: $197.92 per bed; change $25
  • RTF, Form 3180-5005: $191.83 per bed; change $25
  • RTC, Form 3180-5004: $240.00 per bed; change $25
  • APD residential license: no fee figure appears in Rule 65G-2.002 or on the APD group home pages checked; packets quote no APD license fee
  • APD iBudget provider enrollment: free process per APD provider pages
  • DCF substance use, Rule 65D-30.0035: publicly funded, addictions receiving facility $325, detoxification $325, intensive inpatient $325, residential treatment $300, day or night treatment with community housing $250, day or night treatment $250, intensive outpatient $250, outpatient $250, methadone MAT $350, aftercare $200, intervention $200, prevention $200; privately funded, $375, $375, $350, $350, $300, $300, $300, $300, $400, $250, $250, $250 in the same order; discounts by number of facilities operated, public 10 percent (2 to 5) up to 30 percent (20 or more), private 5 percent up to 25 percent
  • Child-caring: no license fee figure found in Rule 65C-46 or section 409.175 in the sources checked; packets quote none
  • Federal institutional provider application fee: $750 for calendar year 2026

How long does it take in Florida?

  • APD residential: renewal due at least 45 days before expiration; license valid one year at most; Rule 65G-2 updated effective March 26, 2026
  • PPEC: license expires two years from issuance
  • ALF, AFCH and adult day care fees in the research are biennial figures on the August 2023 AHCA forms checked November 12, 2026

What are the requirements to open a group home in Florida?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Florida?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
