How to Start a Group Home in Vermont

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Vermont. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Vermont licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Vermont licenses

Residential Care Home, Level III

Licensed by: Vermont Department of Disabilities, Aging and Independent Living (DAIL), Division of Licensing and Protection. Serves: Residents who need room, board, personal care, and nursing overview; in the developmental disabilities system, small group homes of three to six participants are licensed in this category. Licensed as Level III or Level IV. Both levels provide room and board, assistance with personal care, general supervision, and/or medication management; Level III adds nursing overview. Licensing is required at the threshold of three or more residents under 33 V.S.A. section 7102, and the Residential Care Home and Assisted Living Residence Licensing Rules took effect April 1, 2025. In the Developmental Disabilities Services system, homes housing three to six participants must be licensed by the Division of Licensing and Protection, and homes over six need Developmental Disabilities Services Division permission as well. Medicaid: only Level III homes and assisted living residences can serve residents through Assistive Community Care Services (ACCS) and Enhanced Residential Care (ERC) under Choices for Care.

Residential Care Home, Level IV

Licensed by: Vermont Department of Disabilities, Aging and Independent Living (DAIL), Division of Licensing and Protection. Serves: Residents who need room, board, personal care, general supervision, and/or medication management without nursing overview. Licensed as Level III or Level IV. Both levels provide room and board, assistance with personal care, general supervision, and/or medication management; Level IV does not provide the nursing overview that Level III requires. The difference between Level III and Level IV is the degree to which on-site nursing oversight is required. Rules took effect April 1, 2025.

Assisted Living Residence

Licensed by: Vermont Department of Disabilities, Aging and Independent Living (DAIL), Division of Licensing and Protection. Serves: Residents who need housing, health, and supportive services in an apartment-style setting. An assisted living residence must meet all Level III residential care home licensing requirements plus the assisted living requirements, including a private apartment for each resident: a private bedroom, private bath, living space, kitchen capacity, and a lockable door, in a homelike setting. Enhanced Residential Care provides 24-hour care and supervision in approved licensed Level III Residential Care Homes or Assisted Living Residences, including personal care, housekeeping, meals, activities, nursing oversight, and medication management, and the home may also bill Medicaid for Assistive Community Care Services; the individual pays for room and board (DAIL Adult Services Division, Choices for Care Options At-A-Glance).

Therapeutic Community Residence

Licensed by: Vermont Department of Disabilities, Aging and Independent Living (DAIL), Division of Licensing and Protection. Serves: Primarily participants in the Developmental Disabilities Services system in small group living arrangements. The DLP license application lists Therapeutic Community Residence as a license type with a bed count. Historically, the three licensure categories for small group living in the Developmental Disabilities Services system, usually operated by Designated Agencies and Specialized Service Agencies, have been the Therapeutic Community Residence and Residential Care Homes Level III and Level IV; in June 2025 there were 4 designated-agency operated Therapeutic Community Residences serving 20 individuals (Act 69 report, legislature.vermont.gov).

Adult Family Care home (Choices for Care setting)

Licensed by: Vermont Department of Disabilities, Aging and Independent Living (DAIL), Adult Services Division (Choices for Care). Serves: Adults who need nursing home level of care and choose a family care home setting under Choices for Care. Choices for Care, Vermont long-term care Medicaid, serves people age 18 and over who need a nursing home level of care; eligible people choose where to receive services: in their home, in their family's home, an Adult Family Care home, Enhanced Residential Care, or a nursing facility (DAIL Adult Services Division). No separate license facts for Adult Family Care homes were printed on the official pages reviewed, so no fee or license term is listed.

How to start a group home in Vermont, step by step

  1. File the DAIL Division of Licensing and Protection License Application / Reapplication, selecting the license type: Assisted Living Residence, Residential Care Home Level III or Level IV, or Therapeutic Community Residence (DLP application form)
  2. Complete criminal record and abuse registry checks as required on the DAIL application
  3. For an initial application or a request for increased licensed capacity, submit building plans and blueprints to the Department of Public Safety, Division of Fire Safety in the district (DLP application form)
  4. Give written notice to DAIL Division of Licensing and Protection at least 90 days in advance of a sale or change of ownership where residents will be required to move (DLP application form)

How long does it take in Vermont?

  • Residential Care Home and Assisted Living Residence Licensing Rules, implementation date April 1, 2025 (DLP regulations presentation)

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get a group home license in Vermont?

Residential Care Home and Assisted Living Residence Licensing Rules, implementation date April 1, 2025 (DLP regulations presentation)

What are the requirements to open a group home in Vermont?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Vermont?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
