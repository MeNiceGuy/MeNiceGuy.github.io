How to Start a Group Home in New Hampshire

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in New Hampshire. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type New Hampshire licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types New Hampshire licenses

Assisted Living Residence - Residential Care Facility

Licensed by: New Hampshire Department of Health and Human Services, Health Facilities Administration. Serves: Residents of assisted living residences receiving residential care. Listed by the Health Facilities Administration as a licensed facility type, assisted living residence - residential care facilities. The HFA fee list charges Residential and Supported Residential Care Homes $15.00 per licensed bed.

Assisted Living Residence - Supported Residential Health Care Facility (He-P 805)

Licensed by: New Hampshire Department of Health and Human Services, Health Facilities Administration. Serves: Residents who need supported residential health care below the level of 24-hour nursing supervision. He-P 805 sets forth the classification of and licensing requirements for supported residential health care facilities pursuant to RSA 151:2, I(e)(2) and as described in RSA 151:9, VII(a)(2) (rulemaking notice 2025-90, gc.nh.gov). Listed by the Health Facilities Administration as assisted living residence - supported residential care facilities. The HFA fee list charges Residential and Supported Residential Care Homes $15.00 per licensed bed.

Adult Family Care Home

Licensed by: New Hampshire Department of Health and Human Services, Health Facilities Administration. Serves: Residents receiving care in an adult family care home setting. Listed by the Health Facilities Administration as a licensed facility type, adult family care homes. The HFA fee list charges Adult Family Care Homes $25.00 per licensed bed.

Community Residence for Developmental Services (certified, He-M 1001)

Licensed by: New Hampshire Department of Health and Human Services, Bureau of Developmental Services; certification by the Office of Legal and Regulatory Services. Serves: Persons with a developmental disability or acquired brain disorder whose services are funded by the department. This is a certification, not an RSA 151 facility license. He-M 1001 defines the standards and procedures for the certification of community residences in New Hampshire for persons with a developmental disability or acquired brain disorder, under RSA 126-A:19-20 and RSA 171-A. A community residence is either an agency residence or a family residence that provides residential services for at least one individual with a developmental disability under He-M 503 or an acquired brain disorder under He-M 522, provides services and supervision on a daily and ongoing basis in the home and in the community, serves individuals whose services are funded by the department, and is certified pursuant to He-M 1001. Residential services are funded through the Developmental Disabilities Waiver and the Acquired Brain Disorder Waiver described in He-M 517.

Residential Treatment and Rehabilitation Facility

Licensed by: New Hampshire Department of Health and Human Services, Health Facilities Administration. Serves: Residents receiving residential treatment and rehabilitation services. Listed by the Health Facilities Administration as a licensed facility type, residential treatment and rehabilitation facilities. The HFA fee list charges Residential Treatment and Rehabilitation Facilities $25.00 per licensed bed.

How to start a group home in New Hampshire, step by step

  1. Apply to the New Hampshire Department of Health and Human Services, Health Facilities Administration, 129 Pleasant Street, Concord, NH 03301, using the application for the facility type (facility types and fees are listed on the HFA page)
  2. For a Home Care Service Provider Agency under He-P 822, mail or hand-deliver the application documents to the Department of Health and Human Services, Health Facilities Administration, 129 Pleasant Street, Concord, NH 03301 (He-P 822.04)
  3. An He-P 822 application is complete when the department determines that all items required by He-P 822.04(a) have been received (He-P 822.05)

New Hampshire group home license fees and costs

  • Residential and Supported Residential Care Homes: $15.00 per licensed bed (DHHS Health Facilities Administration fee list)
  • Adult Family Care Homes: $25.00 per licensed bed (DHHS Health Facilities Administration fee list)
  • Personal Care Providers (He-P 822): less than 10 clients $25.00; 10 or more clients $100.00 (DHHS Health Facilities Administration fee list)

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to open a group home in New Hampshire?

The figures New Hampshire publishes are these: Residential and Supported Residential Care Homes: $15.00 per licensed bed (DHHS Health Facilities Administration fee list) Adult Family Care Homes: $25.00 per licensed bed (DHHS Health Facilities Administration fee list) Personal Care Providers (He-P 822): less than 10 clients $25.00; 10 or more clients $100.00 (DHHS Health Facilities Administration fee list) The license fee is rarely the largest cost. The home itself, life safety and accessibility work, insurance, and staffing hired before the first placement carry the project.

What are the requirements to open a group home in New Hampshire?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in New Hampshire?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
