How to Start a Home Health Agency in Connecticut

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Connecticut licenses home health agencies as a Home Health Care Agency license, through the Connecticut Department of Public Health (DPH), Facility Licensing and Investigations Section, Home Health Unit. The governing law is Regulations of Connecticut State Agencies for Home Health Care Agencies at sections 19-13-D66 to 19-13-D79, including D66 Definitions, D67 Personnel, D68 General requirements, D69 Services, as listed on the DPH Regulations and Statutes page; Home Health Aide Agency regulations are listed separately on that page. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the Connecticut license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The Connecticut license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Connecticut, step by step

  1. Contact the DPH Home Health Unit to receive application forms and copies of pertinent Connecticut regulations: call 860-509-7444 or email Colleen.Judge@ct.gov - DPH Home Health Unit page
  2. Achieve licensure as a Home Health Care Agency from the Department of Public Health - DPH Home Health Unit page
  3. After DPH licensure, apply with an Accrediting Organization such as the Joint Commission, CHAP or ACHC to seek Medicare certification for reimbursement - DPH Home Health Unit page
  4. For hospice in the home: an applicant must first achieve licensure as a Home Health Care Agency before seeking the addition of hospice services to the same license - DPH Home Health Unit page

What catches Connecticut applicants

  • State license before Medicare: DPH states the sequence directly. Upon achieving licensure from the Department of Public Health, licensed home health agencies in Connecticut may apply with Accrediting Organizations such as the Joint Commission, CHAP or ACHC to seek certification from Medicare for reimbursement purposes.
  • Two levels of licensure: DPH Home Health Unit regulates home health with two levels - Home Health Care agencies, and Home Health Aide agencies licensed to provide solely home health aide services with Registered Nurse oversight.
  • DPH does not license homemaker-companion agencies; persons seeking to operate one are directed to the Department of Consumer Protection - DPH Home Health Unit page.
  • Certificate of need and fees and timelines: the DPH Home Health Unit page and Facility Licensing page fetched did not print a home health application fee, a processing timeline, or a certificate of need step for a home health care agency. Those items are omitted here rather than estimated. Verification on CON for Connecticut home health is thin.
  • DPH also conducts Medicare recertification surveys for existing home health and hospice agencies in Connecticut - DPH Home Health Unit page.

The Medicare layer, after the Connecticut license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

What do I need to start a home health agency in Connecticut?

The Home Health Care Agency license (Connecticut Department of Public Health (DPH), Facility Licensing and Investigations Section, Home Health Unit), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet Connecticut rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in Connecticut?

Apply to the Connecticut Department of Public Health (DPH), Facility Licensing and Investigations Section, Home Health Unit under Regulations of Connecticut State Agencies for Home Health Care Agencies at sections 19-13-D66 to 19-13-D79, including D66 Definitions, D67 Personnel, D68 General requirements, D69 Services, as listed on the DPH Regulations and Statutes page; Home Health Aide Agency regulations are listed separately on that page. The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
