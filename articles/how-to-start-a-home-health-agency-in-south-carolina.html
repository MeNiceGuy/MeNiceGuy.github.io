How to Start a Home Health Agency in South Carolina

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

South Carolina licenses home health agencies as a Home Health Agency license, through the South Carolina Department of Public Health (DPH), Bureau of Healthcare Quality. The governing law is Licensure of Home Health Agencies Act, S.C. Code Section 44-69-10 et seq.; Regulation 60-77, Standards for Licensing Home Health Agencies (statutory authority S.C. Code Sections 44-69-10 et seq.). That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the South Carolina license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The South Carolina license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in South Carolina, step by step

  1. Obtain a Certificate of Need (CON) from the Department before licensure - a home health agency shall obtain a CON before licensure under S.C. Code Section 44-69-75(A), as stated in the 2026 South Carolina Health Plan
  2. Submit the DPH Application for Licensure for a home health agency, with the license fee submitted with the application
  3. For Medicare certification, DPH's Health Insurance Benefit Program processes initial applications for Medicare certification for home health agencies and conducts recertification surveys

South Carolina home health license fees and costs

  • Initial and renewal license fee: $100 plus $50 for each county served (Regulation 60-77, Section 102.H); governmental home health agencies are exempt
  • Certificate of Need initial filing fee: $500 non-refundable, paid at application submission through the SC.GOV payment portal (DPH Certificate of Need application page)

What catches South Carolina applicants

  • DPH defines a home health agency as a public, nonprofit or proprietary organization, whether owned or operated by one or more persons or legal entities, which furnishes or offers to furnish home health services
  • A state license is required before operation; the CON step comes before licensure, not after it
  • No startup timeline in months was printed on the DPH home health agency page or in Regulation 60-77 excerpts fetched, so no timeline is listed

The Medicare layer, after the South Carolina license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a home health agency in South Carolina?

The figures South Carolina publishes are these: Initial and renewal license fee: $100 plus $50 for each county served (Regulation 60-77, Section 102.H); governmental home health agencies are exempt Certificate of Need initial filing fee: $500 non-refundable, paid at application submission through the SC.GOV payment portal (DPH Certificate of Need application page) Beyond the published figures, the operating costs (clinical payroll before certification, insurance, and the reserve operating funds required by 42 CFR 489.28) are larger than every fee on the list.

What do I need to start a home health agency in South Carolina?

The Home Health Agency license (South Carolina Department of Public Health (DPH), Bureau of Healthcare Quality), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet South Carolina rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in South Carolina?

Apply to the South Carolina Department of Public Health (DPH), Bureau of Healthcare Quality under Licensure of Home Health Agencies Act, S.C. Code Section 44-69-10 et seq.; Regulation 60-77, Standards for Licensing Home Health Agencies (statutory authority S.C. Code Sections 44-69-10 et seq.). The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
