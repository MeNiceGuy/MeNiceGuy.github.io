How Much Does It Cost to Start a Home Health Agency?

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Ask what a home health agency costs to start and most answers give you a single invented number. Here is the honest version: the fees the government publishes are small and exact, and everything that actually decides whether you survive the first year is operating capital. This page separates the two, prints every figure a primary source prints, and leaves the rest as categories to budget, because any total I gave you without your state, your market, and your staffing plan would be a guess wearing a suit.

The figures that are published, exactly as published

  • Medicare enrollment application fee: $750 for calendar year 2026. Home health agencies enroll as institutional providers on Form CMS-855A and pay this fee to CMS. It is adjusted annually.
  • State home health license fees: set by each state. Two printed examples from the state pages in this guide: Wisconsin charges a $300 nonrefundable application fee for the skilled home health license under DHS 133, and New York charges a $2,000 application fee for a certified home health agency under Public Health Law section 3606. Your state page prints your state fee where the state publishes one.
  • Accreditation fees: set by the accrediting organization. ACHC, CHAP, and The Joint Commission each quote their own application and survey fees. No government source prints them, so this page does not invent them. The state survey route charges no survey fee.

The costs that are not fees, and decide the outcome

  • The reserve. Under 42 CFR 489.28, a new home health agency must show reserve operating funds for its first months of operation. This is real money, documented, and it cannot be the Medicare payments you are expecting.
  • Clinical payroll before revenue. You must be operating, with at least 10 skilled patients served and 7 active, before the initial survey, and Medicare pays nothing until certification. Nurses, therapists, and the administrator are on payroll through that whole runway.
  • The moratorium reality. With new Medicare enrollment paused to about mid-May 2027, a new agency's runway is longer than the old playbook assumed. Budget the state license and readiness phase as its own funded stage.
  • Insurance, systems, and compliance. Professional and general liability coverage, the clinical record and OASIS capable software, background checks, and the policy and quality infrastructure the survey tests.
  • The state layer. Entity filing, any certificate of need application costs in need states, and the state license fee above.

How to run your own number

Start with the two published figures for your case, the $750 federal fee and your state license fee from your state page. Then lay out the runway honestly: months of clinical and administrative payroll until certification, the reserve the federal rule requires, and three months of operating costs after that. The free Home Care Agency Startup Cost Worksheet gives you the worksheet structure owners use for this math, and How to Start a Home Health Agency puts each cost in its step of the sequence.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a home health agency?

The published figures are the small part: the Medicare enrollment application fee is $750 for calendar year 2026, and state home health license fees are set by each state (Wisconsin prints a $300 application fee for its skilled home health license under DHS 133, and New York prints a $2,000 application fee for a certified home health agency). The large part is operating capital: clinical payroll before the first payment, accreditation if you choose it, insurance, and the reserve operating funds federal rules require a new agency to show under 42 CFR 489.28.

What is the CMS-855A application fee in 2026?

$750. It is the Medicare enrollment application fee for institutional providers, set by CMS for calendar year 2026 and adjusted annually. Home health agencies pay it when they file Form CMS-855A.

Do I have to show reserve funds to start a home health agency?

Yes. Federal rules at 42 CFR 489.28 require a new home health agency to demonstrate reserve operating funds sufficient for its first months of operation, calculated from the agency's projected costs and kept separate from expected Medicare receivables. It is a capitalization test, not a fee, and it is one of the most common reasons a new agency's timeline slips.
