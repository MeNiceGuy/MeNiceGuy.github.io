How to Start a Home Health Agency in North Carolina

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

North Carolina licenses home health agencies as a Home Care Agency license, with separate skilled home health and Medicare-certified home health tracks in the research, through the NC Division of Health Service Regulation (DHSR), NC Department of Health and Human Services. The governing law is G.S. 131E-138, 10A NCAC 13J; mandate at G.S. 131E-138(a). That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the North Carolina license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The North Carolina license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in North Carolina, step by step

  1. Decide the track by touch: hands-on in-home aide services require the Home Care Agency license under 10A NCAC 13J; no-touch companion, sitter and respite only is the Home Assistance Services Provider track
  2. If a first-time owner with no prior ownership or operation of a home care agency, complete DHSR approved training on licensure requirements, the licensure process and operating rules before a license can issue under 10A NCAC 13J .0903(a), and keep proof for the packet
  3. Download and complete the DHSR licensure application, include the $510 fee by check, money order or certified check payable to the N.C. Division of Health Service Regulation, and include proof of prior ownership or operation or proof of training completion, then mail the packet to DHSR in Raleigh
  4. DHSR reviews the packet for accuracy and completeness and emails confirmation of receipt, then emails a request for policies, procedures and personnel documents
  5. Complete the initial survey, which is a review of the submitted policies, procedures and personnel documents against 10A NCAC 13J, including the eleven policy areas at 10A NCAC 13J .1001(a)
  6. On approval, access and print the license through the Enterprise system; each premises is separately licensed, licensure runs one year, and the license is not transferable
  7. For Medicare-certified home health, complete the separate Certificate of Need gate, which belongs to the certified home health side and is not part of the non-medical home care path
  8. Enroll separately in NCTracks for NC Medicaid and, for Medicare certification, enroll as an institutional provider through the federal door; North Carolina A/B and home health and hospice MAC is Palmetto GBA, Jurisdiction M

North Carolina home health license fees and costs

  • Home care agency license fee $510, nonrefundable, annual, under G.S. 131E-138(c); same amount at renewal every year
  • NCTracks (NC Medicaid) enrollment: $100 North Carolina application fee (G.S. 108C-2.1) plus the federal fee where the Provider Permission Matrix requires it: $750 for calendar year 2026
  • Medicare enrollment, institutional providers: $750 for calendar year 2026 (CMS MLN9658742)

How long does it take in North Carolina?

  • DHSR publishes no promised processing window for the home care license; no 90 to 180 day timeline is published by the state in the sources read
  • An applicant must be able to complete all necessary requirements within one year, twelve months, from the date DHSR receives the initial application and fee
  • Home care licensure runs for one year and expires at midnight on the expiration date printed on the license

What catches North Carolina applicants

  • Certificate of Need: no CON step in the home care path; CON belongs to the separate certified home health side, and the Medicare-certified home health track is listed in the research as with CON
  • Medicaid enrollment through NCTracks is a separate door from licensure, with national accreditation and then a Tailored Plan or LME-MCO contract for NC Innovations residential doors
  • Two license tracks since Session Law 2023-150: Home Care Agency for hands-on care and Home Assistance Services Provider for no-touch services; a home assistance provider may not submit claims to any private or government funded health insurer, managed care organization or health plan
  • Extensive versus limited assistance rule at 10A NCAC 13J .1107(b): if the plan of care requires extensive assistance the aide shall be listed on the Nurse Aide Registry under G.S. 131E-255; limited assistance only does not require Registry listing
  • Medicare moratorium covers initial applications, new practice locations and branches, and non-exempt changes in majority ownership under 42 CFR 424.550(b); applications received before May 13, 2026 are not affected and there are no individual exceptions

The Medicare layer, after the North Carolina license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a home health agency in North Carolina?

The figures North Carolina publishes are these: Home care agency license fee $510, nonrefundable, annual, under G.S. 131E-138(c); same amount at renewal every year NCTracks (NC Medicaid) enrollment: $100 North Carolina application fee (G.S. 108C-2.1) plus the federal fee where the Provider Permission Matrix requires it: $750 for calendar year 2026 Medicare enrollment, institutional providers: $750 for calendar year 2026 (CMS MLN9658742) Beyond the published figures, the operating costs (clinical payroll before certification, insurance, and the reserve operating funds required by 42 CFR 489.28) are larger than every fee on the list.

How long does it take to get a home health license in North Carolina?

DHSR publishes no promised processing window for the home care license; no 90 to 180 day timeline is published by the state in the sources read An applicant must be able to complete all necessary requirements within one year, twelve months, from the date DHSR receives the initial application and fee Home care licensure runs for one year and expires at midnight on the expiration date printed on the license The federal certification that follows runs on the survey calendar, and new Medicare enrollment is under a CMS moratorium to about mid-May 2027.

What do I need to start a home health agency in North Carolina?

The Home Care Agency license, with separate skilled home health and Medicare-certified home health tracks in the research (NC Division of Health Service Regulation (DHSR), NC Department of Health and Human Services), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet North Carolina rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in North Carolina?

Apply to the NC Division of Health Service Regulation (DHSR), NC Department of Health and Human Services under G.S. 131E-138, 10A NCAC 13J; mandate at G.S. 131E-138(a). The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
