Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies: The Application Process, Step by Step

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Medicare certification is the federal half of starting a home health agency, and it runs on federal rules that do not bend by state. This page walks the certification sequence on its own, step by step. For the full startup path including the state license, read How to Start a Home Health Agency. For your state license, fee, and clock, pick your state page from that guide.

The current gate: the enrollment moratorium

CMS imposed a nationwide moratorium on new Medicare enrollment for home health agencies and hospices on May 13, 2026, and extended it on November 12, 2026. It currently runs to about mid-May 2027 and can be extended again in six month steps. Existing agencies keep operating. New agencies cannot finish enrollment while it runs. Everything below is the process as it stands, so you can be ready the day the gate reopens, and so nobody sells you a shortcut around a closed federal door.

Medicare certification for home health agencies, step by step

  1. Hold the state license. Where your state licenses home health agencies, the license comes first. Certification surveys an operating licensed agency, not a plan.
  2. Build to the Conditions of Participation. 42 CFR Part 484 covers patient rights, assessment and care planning, care delivery, the quality assessment and performance improvement program, infection prevention and control, skilled professional services, home health aide services, and the clinical record. Your policies and your actual practice both have to match it.
  3. Set up OASIS transmission. CMS expects a new agency to demonstrate it can transmit OASIS data before the initial certification survey.
  4. Reach the census threshold. Survey guidance requires the agency to have provided skilled care to a minimum of 10 patients, with at least 7 receiving skilled care at the time of the survey. The patients do not have to be Medicare beneficiaries. In medically underserved areas, CMS can reduce the floor to 5 patients served and 2 active.
  5. File Form CMS-855A through PECOS. The enrollment application for institutional providers, with full ownership and managing control disclosures, and the enrollment application fee, $750 for calendar year 2026. Your Medicare Administrative Contractor reviews it, and the survey cannot be scheduled until the application is approved.
  6. Choose the surveyor. The state survey agency, at no survey fee but on the state calendar, or an accrediting organization with CMS deeming authority for home health: ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission, at the organization fees, usually on a faster calendar. The standard tested is identical.
  7. Pass the initial certification survey. Surveyors review all Conditions of Participation against live operation: patient records, personnel files, visit documentation, and interviews. Deficiencies get a plan of correction and, at condition level, a revisit.
  8. Certification, agreement, and billing privileges. Under 42 CFR 489.13, the participation effective date follows the survey outcome: when every requirement is met on the survey completion date, that date controls. The reserve operating funds requirement at 42 CFR 489.28 also applies to new home health agencies, so the capitalization has to be real, documented, and separate from expected Medicare receivables.
  9. Then the state payers. Medicaid enrollment and managed care credentialing are separate applications that follow certification, each with its own review.

Accreditation or the state survey: which route?

What I tell owners: the choice is calendar and cash, not standard. Both routes test the same Conditions of Participation, and deemed status from an accrediting organization is accepted by CMS in place of the state survey. The state route costs no survey fee and waits in a queue you do not control. The accreditation route charges application and survey fees set by the organization and generally schedules sooner. Neither route skips the census threshold, the 855A approval, or the moratorium.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Medicare certification process for a home health agency?

State license first, then Form CMS-855A filed through PECOS and approved by your Medicare Administrative Contractor, then an initial certification survey against the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, conducted by your state survey agency or by an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). CMS then issues the certification and the provider agreement. Before the survey, your agency must already have served at least 10 skilled patients, with at least 7 receiving care at survey time.

How much is the Medicare enrollment application fee for a home health agency?

Home health agencies enroll as institutional providers on Form CMS-855A and pay the Medicare enrollment application fee: $750 for calendar year 2026, set by CMS and adjusted each year.

Can I get Medicare certified without a state license?

In a state that licenses home health agencies, no. The state license comes first, and the federal survey tests an operating, licensed agency. A small number of states do not license home health agencies at the state level; in those states the certification survey is the only gate, and your state page will say so plainly.

Is Medicare enrollment for home health agencies open right now?

No. CMS imposed a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency and hospice Medicare enrollment on May 13, 2026, and extended it on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. New agencies can complete the state license and survey readiness work, but the federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs.
