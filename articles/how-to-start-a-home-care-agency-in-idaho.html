How to Start a Home Care Agency in Idaho (No State License Exists, and Home Health Licensure Closed in 2025)

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 19, 2026

The Idaho story starts with a founder in the Boise area who did everything a national startup guide told her to do. She set aside money for the state license fee, she looked for the licensure survey checklist, and she waited to market until the license arrived. The license never arrived, because it does not exist. The first thing I tell owners looking at Idaho is that this state licenses no home care agency at all, from primary sources rather than by absence alone: no provision of Title 39 or IDAPA creates a home care license. The second thing I tell them is the newer fact, the one older guides get wrong in the other direction. Since July 1, 2025, Idaho issues no new home health licenses either. Here is the full picture, lane by lane, in the order a founder actually meets it.

Idaho is therefore a build-the-systems state. With no licensure gate, the proof of seriousness sits in the enrollment record, the screening file, the training record, and the policies, and the founders who win here are the ones who build those before they need them. The sections below follow the Idaho packet, which is built from Idaho primary sources checked on November 17, 2026.

The demand: about 380,000 seniors in one of the fastest growing states

Start with why owners keep looking at Idaho. The Census Bureau puts the state population at 2,029,733 as of July 1, 2025, up 10.4 percent from the April 1, 2020 base, and 18.7 percent of residents are age 65 or older. Run the math and you get about 380,000 seniors. The same Census QuickFacts page for Idaho puts median household income at $77,800 for 2020 to 2024, which is the income base a private pay agency sells into. Growth is the demand story here: a state adding residents at that pace adds senior households, adult children looking for help for a parent, and referral sources looking for agencies that answer the phone.

The license question, answered directly: no Idaho license exists for home-based care

Here is the searched question this article exists to answer. Do you need a license to start a non-medical home care agency in Idaho? No state license exists to get. Title 39 licenses facilities and, before July 2025, home health agencies, and a personal care agency that wants Medicaid work enrolls instead: it enrolls as a Personal Assistance Agency, called a PAA, through Idaho Medicaid provider enrollment, run by the Bureau of Long Term Care inside the Idaho Department of Health and Welfare, with the fiscal agent Gainwell at idmedicaid.com. Private pay work runs on the agency service agreements with no state license behind it.

The skilled door changed by statute. Before July 1, 2025, Title 39, Chapter 24 required home health licensure under Idaho Code section 39-2403 and rules at IDAPA 16.03.07. The Streamlined Facilities Licensing and Inspection Act then provided at Idaho Code section 39-1303(8) that neither Medicare certified hospice agencies nor home health agencies are required to be licensed or certified under Title 39, Chapter 13, and the Department of Health and Welfare home health page states the result directly: Medicare certified home health agencies are no longer required to be licensed in Idaho, and no new licenses will be issued after July 1, 2025. That pre-2025 license is a closed door for a new entrant, and any guide still printing its fee or its application sequence is describing a door that shut. Hospice has never carried an Idaho license: Idaho Code section 39-1301C provides that neither a Medicare certified hospice agency nor its hospice home is required to be licensed or certified by the state, and the hospice house and its operator must hold deemed status through a CMS recognized accrediting organization. No certificate of need requirement exists for home health agencies or hospices in Idaho, a checked negative.

The non-medical lane: no license, and the Medicaid door is Personal Assistance Agency enrollment

The non-medical sequence runs on business and enrollment steps, not a licensure filing. Form the business, hold the EIN, put insurance and the operating accounts in place, and write the service agreements that carry the private pay work. For Medicaid clients, enroll as a Personal Assistance Agency and deliver State Plan Personal Care Services and Aged and Disabled Waiver services under Bureau of Long Term Care oversight. State Plan Personal Care Services are grooming, bathing, toileting, dressing, food and nutrition, and incidental housekeeping. The Aged and Disabled Waiver adds attendant care, chore services, companion services, homemaker services, respite, and non-medical transportation, among the services the Bureau page lists. The service vocabulary on this door is the Medicaid vocabulary, and using it loosely in private pay marketing is how agencies promise services no payer record supports.

The credential set on this door is specific. It is the PAA enrollment record, the completed Prospective Home and Community Based Services Provider Toolkit, the training delivered by a Department of Health and Welfare Quality Assurance Specialist before application approval and before any waiver or personal care service is provided, and the Department background check clearances under IDAPA 16.05.06. No fee figure is printed for this door because no license exists to carry one: the packet prints NO FEE PUBLISHED for the state door. The one state figure nearby is a screening cost, covered in the background check section below, and budgeting it as a license fee misstates the filing.

The skilled lane: Medicare certification is the door after the 2025 closure

The moment your plan includes skilled nursing or therapy, the build sequence is the federal and payer sequence. There is no certificate of need gate in front of this door, and no state license application behind it. Medicare certification runs where Medicare billing is planned, on Form CMS-855A through PECOS, and Idaho Medicaid provider enrollment through Gainwell at idmedicaid.com runs for Medicaid services. The Department states plainly that Medicaid provider enrollment is a separate process from federal certification and state licensure, so a certified agency that never enrolled with Gainwell has no Medicaid door, and an enrolled agency holds no certification it did not earn. The Home Health and Hospice MAC for Idaho is Noridian Healthcare Solutions, and the A/B MAC is Noridian Healthcare Solutions, Jurisdiction F. Noridian Jurisdiction D is the DME MAC, a different door under the same contractor name, and DME correspondence is never home health enrollment correspondence.

An agency that does not seek Medicare certification still holds no Idaho license on this door. Its operating credentials are its Medicaid enrollment where Medicaid pays, its practitioner order and plan of care systems run under 42 CFR Part 484 discipline, and its personnel files, and the packet builds those systems rather than a license application. No state license fee is printed for this door, NO FEE PUBLISHED, because Idaho issues no new license to carry one. The federal figure that does exist is the CY2026 institutional provider enrollment fee of $750, filed with Form CMS-855A, on the certified home health and hospice tracks only. Initial Medicare enrollment on those tracks is gated by the federal enrollment moratoria, CMS-6101-N2 for home health and CMS-6102-N2 for hospice, extended and modified effective November 12, 2026 (the extension is a six-month period, running to about May 12, 2027). No Idaho Medicaid provider enrollment moratorium or pause was located in the primary sources checked for this build.

The hospice lane: never state licensed, deemed status is the credential

Hospice in Idaho is a federal door from the first step. Under Idaho Code section 39-1301C, no state license or certification is required for a Medicare certified hospice agency, and the hospice house and its operator hold deemed status through a CMS recognized accrediting organization. The build is the Medicare hospice sequence: the program structure and service area, enrollment on Form CMS-855A with the CY2026 fee of $750, the file the hospice Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 418 require, and the certification survey. No state hospice license fee figure is published, because no Idaho hospice license exists, and the packet prints NO FEE PUBLISHED for the state fee. A founder shopping for an Idaho hospice license application is shopping for a document the state has never printed.

The residential doors: group homes and facilities are licensed or certified by DHW

The no-license headline covers home-based care only, and founders who want a home meet a licensed map. Residential assisted living facilities are licensed by the Department under IDAPA 16.03.22, adult residential care facilities are certified under IDAPA 16.03.21, and certified family homes for two or fewer adults are certified under IDAPA 16.03.19, each printed in the packet as no fee published where the sources print none. Supported living group homes for adults with developmental disabilities run through a certified Residential Habilitation Agency on the Adult DD Waiver, skilled nursing facilities are licensed under IDAPA 16.03.02, ICF/IID facilities under IDAPA 16.03.11, and childrens residential care facilities under IDAPA 16.04.18. Adult day health reaches clients as a waiver service in certified settings under IDAPA 16.03.10. The residential figures, where any exist, belong to those tracks and are never borrowed for a home-based door.

Background checks: the DHW clearance under IDAPA 16.05.06, at up to $70 per check

Background checks are the one state gate every Idaho home-based founder actually pays, and its price is the figure most often misread. The Department of Health and Welfare Background Check Unit charges up to $70 per check under IDAPA 16.05.06, and the ledger for this build verified that figure against IDAPA 16.05.06.050. That is a per-person screening cost for listed provider types, not an agency license fee, and no packet page prints it as one. The personnel file the packet builds for each worker holds the application, references, and the signed job description for the role held, license or credential verification for every licensed role from the primary source where the board provides it, the Department background check clearance under IDAPA 16.05.06 before hire and on the cycle the rule sets, OIG and SAM exclusion checks before hire and monthly, orientation, competency evaluation, and the annual performance record, plus the health screening and immunization record the applicable standards require. In a state with no licensure survey, that file is the survey a family or a referral partner conducts, and it should be kept like one.

What you still do today: form the business the Idaho way

Formation is the ordinary employer load, done in the right order: form the entity, get an EIN from the IRS, put liability insurance in place before the first client, adopt written policies and procedures covering the services you will actually sell, set up caregiver training and the Department background check rhythm as a system rather than a scramble, and use a service agreement that says clearly what your agency does and does not do, with skilled tasks on the does not do side of the line for a non-medical agency. No state formation fee figure is printed in the sources checked for this build, so none is quoted here. The Idaho difference is what comes after formation: instead of a license file, the founder builds the enrollment record, the screening file, the EVV and policy systems, and that build money is where the old license budget belongs.

The practical warning

Two Idaho traps cost new owners money, and both come from shopping for a license that does not exist. The first is the stale guide trap: owners budget for an Idaho home care license, a home health license fee, or a licensure survey, working from pre-2025 materials that describe the closed door at IDAPA 16.03.07. Every week spent hunting that application is a week the enrollment, screening, and policy systems sit unbuilt. The second trap is the reverse error, reading no license as no standards. Idaho Medicaid enrollment is not a license and a license is not enrollment, and on the private pay side there is no state gate at all, which means your screening file, your training records, your insurance certificate, and your policies are the whole proof of seriousness. Low friction cuts both ways: it lets you start fast, and it lets every unprepared competitor start fast too. Your preparation is the moat.
