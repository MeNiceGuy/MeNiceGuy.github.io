How to Start a Home Care Agency in Maryland (Personal Care Runs Inside the Residential Service Agency License)

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 19, 2026

The Maryland story starts with a founder hunting for a non-medical license that does not exist under that name. She had a companion care plan for families around Baltimore, a caregiver list, and a budget built from a national guide, and she kept asking the state for the personal care application. Maryland does not split its home care license by medical and non-medical. Personal care in Maryland is delivered inside one license, the Residential Service Agency license, as home health aide services under registered nurse supervision. When owners come to me with a Maryland plan, the first thing I do is point them at the right license name, because the application, the nurse supervision calendar, and the survey all hang off it.

The regulator is the Maryland Department of Health, Office of Health Care Quality, called OHCQ. OHCQ licenses Residential Service Agencies under COMAR 10.07.05, Home Health Agencies under COMAR 10.07.10, Nursing Referral Service Agencies under COMAR 10.07.07, hospice care programs under COMAR 10.07.21, and Assisted Living Programs under COMAR 10.07.14, and it is also Maryland's state survey agency for federal Medicare certification. Certificate of Need review for home health agencies and general hospice programs sits with the Maryland Health Care Commission. Here is the full picture, lane by lane.

The demand: about 1.15 million seniors, in the highest income market in this series

Start with why owners keep looking at Maryland. The Census Bureau puts the state population at 6,265,347 as of July 1, 2025, and 18.4 percent of residents are age 65 or older. Run the math and you get about 1.15 million seniors. The same Census QuickFacts page for Maryland puts median household income at $103,678 for 2020 to 2024, the income base a private pay agency sells into, and a big part of why Maryland private pay rates support agencies that thinner markets cannot.

Density cuts the other way. Maryland packs those seniors into a compact state between two major metros, which means referral sources, hospitals, and competitors are all close together. The license is the entry ticket, and the nurse-supervised operating standard inside it is what separates an agency that keeps referral partners from one that loses them at the first survey.

The license question, answered directly: one license, the Residential Service Agency

Here is the searched question this article exists to answer. Do you need a license to start a non-medical home care agency in Maryland? If your business employs or contracts with individuals to provide at least one home health care service for compensation to an unrelated sick or disabled individual in that individual's residence, yes: a person may not operate a Residential Service Agency without a license under Health-General Subtitle 4A and COMAR 10.07.05.03 and .04. The home health care service list at Health-General Section 19-4A-01 drives the license: nursing services, home health aide services, physical, occupational, speech, and respiratory therapy, medical social services, dietary and nutritional services, drug services, laboratory services, and home medical equipment services. Personal care sits inside that list as home health aide services. There is no separate non-medical license and no level split in the current chapter; the services on your license decide which operating rules attach. No Certificate of Need is required for this license under Health-General Section 19-4A-05.

The one no-license route is narrow: COMAR 10.07.05.03 excludes providers of only household and family support services, light housekeeping, meal preparation, shopping, and errands, with no home health care service. Maryland has no exemption filing for that route in the sources checked for the packet: no form to submit and no OHCQ letter to obtain. The boundary is drawn by tasks. The first hands-on task, bathing, dressing, toileting, transferring, or feeding a sick or disabled client, is a home health care service, and providing even one of them for compensation makes the business a Residential Service Agency that needs the license before that visit. The other doors are separate licenses: the Home Health Agency license for the centrally administered combination, the Nursing Referral Service Agency license for a referral model that screens and refers but does not employ the caregivers, general and limited hospice licenses, and the Assisted Living Program license for the residential model, which no home care license authorizes.

The main lane: the Residential Service Agency license, nurse-supervised from day one

Two requirements shape this license before the first client. The license is nurse-supervised for hands-on care: for clients who require skilled services or assistance with the activities of daily living, a registered nurse assesses each new client, participates in the plan of care and in assigning personnel, and provides oversight for implementation of the care plan, delegation, supervision, and training, with periodic on-site supervision at least every 45 days if staff administer medications, at least every 3 months if staff assist with self-administration, and at least every 4 months if staff do neither, under COMAR 10.07.05.12. And worker classification is a licensure condition: a person with authority over the agency's pay or employment practices must read OHCQ's worker-classification guidance and complete the RSA Certification online, as a condition of initial licensure and of staying licensed, under Health-General Section 19-4A-11. COMAR 10.07.05 carries no attorney requirement for this license; any guide that tells a Maryland founder an attorney must file or hold a piece of this application is adding a requirement the chapter does not print.

The application itself is built like a survey in advance. Applications are typed, handwritten applications are not accepted and are returned, and the application must demonstrate financial and administrative ability with a business plan containing a 1-year operating budget, a marketing plan that identifies the populations to be served, and a detailed description of agency services. Policies and procedures under COMAR 10.07.05.08B are a required attachment, indexed by document name and page numbers in Section B of the application, not a post-license project. The entity registers with the Maryland Department of Assessments and Taxation and attaches an official letter of good standing obtained through Maryland Business Express, plus the workers compensation declaration page or the Certificate of Compliance or Letter of Exemption that fits the entity. OHCQ holds an incomplete application for 180 days from initial receipt, after which it is deemed inactive and administratively closed. The license is not transferable, and a sale, a change of ownership, or a transfer of stock over 25 percent or that changes control must be flagged to OHCQ and to clients at least 45 days in advance under COMAR 10.07.05.05, with a $50 rule fee for an amended license on a name or address change with no change of ownership.

The fee question, answered honestly: the rule and the current application disagree

Read this section before you budget, because Maryland carries a live conflict between its rule text and its current form, and the packet carries both sides instead of picking one. COMAR 10.07.05.04 prints a fee of $1,000 for a 3-year Residential Service Agency term. The current OHCQ application, revised April 22, 2026, collects no fee and states there is no fee to apply for a license. On the term itself, Chapter 661 of 2018, Senate Bill 108, made the RSA authorization non-expiring, and OHCQ publishes no RSA renewal process, which sits against the older 3-year framing in the rule text. The working instruction is the one the packet prints: file on the current form, follow its fee statement on the day you file, do not mail a check on the strength of the printed rule text, and confirm the fee and term posture with OHCQ at filing. Third-party guides that quote the $1,000 figure as the settled cost are quoting the rule, not the current application.

The other licensed lanes: home health, referral, and hospice

The Home Health Agency license under COMAR 10.07.10 covers the centrally administered combination of skilled nursing, home health aide services, and at least one other home health care service, under the supervision of a physician or registered professional nurse. An RSA license does not authorize operating as a home health agency. The gate in front of this license is the Certificate of Need: before a person may apply for the license, the person shall obtain a certificate of need or an exemption from the Maryland Health Care Commission under Health-General Section 19-114(b), with need reviewed by jurisdiction under COMAR 10.24.16. The license itself is valid for 1 year from issuance, and renewal is filed at least 60 days before expiration under COMAR 10.07.10.04. Medicare certification layers on this license under 42 CFR Part 484 through PECOS on the CMS-855A, with OHCQ as the state survey agency, and initial Medicare enrollment is currently blocked under the federal moratorium extended and modified by CMS-6101-N2, effective November 12, 2026, with no calendar end date printed in the notice (the extension is a six-month period, running to about May 12, 2027). That federal gate governs Medicare enrollment only; it does not change the Maryland licensure or Certificate of Need steps.

A Nursing Referral Service Agency under COMAR 10.07.07 screens and refers licensed health professionals or care providers to clients at the client's request; it does not employ the caregivers it refers, deliver the care, or supervise the work in the home, and a business that crosses into employing caregivers is an RSA instead. General hospice under COMAR 10.07.21 also needs a Certificate of Need or exemption first under COMAR 10.24.13, files the federal certification set with the state application, and receives a license with conditions until federal certification is complete, with CMS-6102-N2 gating new hospice Medicare enrollment on the same federal terms. A limited hospice provides palliative and supportive nonskilled services only, needs no Certificate of Need, and may not provide any medical, nursing, or other health services directly or through a contractual arrangement under Health-General Section 19-909.

Background checks: criminal history records checks through CJIS

Criminal history records checks for Maryland health care workers run under Health-General Sections 19-1901 et seq. through the Criminal Justice Information System Central Repository, or a private agency check where the program's rules allow one. The screening, training, and record systems the chapter requires are working before the first hire is assigned, and training delivered by an outside trainer requires OHCQ approval of the trainer's materials before the training under COMAR 10.07.05.11. Nursing tasks are delegated only inside Maryland's delegation framework: delegation runs under the Maryland Nurse Practice Act and COMAR 10.27.11, through a registered nurse who delegates, supervises, and remains responsible for the delegation decision. Provider enrollment for Medicaid moved from ePREP to MPRIME, the Maryland Provider Registration and Information Management Enterprise, on October 13, 2026, and agency-delivered personal assistance under Community First Choice and Community Personal Assistance Services enrolls through the licensed RSA door.

What you still do today: form the business the Maryland way

Register the business with the Maryland Department of Assessments and Taxation and obtain the official letter of good standing through Maryland Business Express, hold the EIN, and bind workers compensation coverage before the application goes in. Then do the employer load in the order the license tests it: the registered nurse lined up with the supervision calendar built, 45 days, 3 months, or 4 months by medication role, the policy set adopted and indexed to the application, the RSA Certification on worker classification completed, the screening file system running, and 24-hour on-call coverage with the procedures and phone number given to clients under COMAR 10.07.05.12. Keep the office open for announced and unannounced inspection during the business hours stated in the application. None of that waits for the license to issue, because OHCQ may inspect on site before deciding.

The practical warning

Two Maryland traps cost new owners money. The first is the fee mirage, budgeting or paying the $1,000 COMAR figure while the current application collects no fee, or the reverse error, assuming the no-fee form settles the term question when the rule text and the 2018 law point in different directions. Confirm both with OHCQ at filing and keep the confirmation with the application file. The second trap is scope drift across license lines: a household-support business whose aide helps a client into the shower as a favor has become an unlicensed Residential Service Agency, an RSA that assembles the centrally administered skilled combination has become an unlicensed home health agency that needed a Certificate of Need first, and an RSA license never authorizes an assisted living program no matter how the marketing describes the setting. In Maryland the task list is the license, and the license has to be in hand before the visit, not after the referral source asks for a copy.
