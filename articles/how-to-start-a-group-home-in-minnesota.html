How to Start a Group Home in Minnesota

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Minnesota. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Minnesota licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Minnesota licenses

Community Residential Setting (CRS)

Licensed by: Minnesota Department of Human Services (DHS), Licensing Division, under Minn. Stat. chapters 245A and 245D. Serves: People with disabilities and people age 65 and older receiving home and community based services, including developmental disabilities waiver participants. Residential supports and services under Minn. Stat. 245D.03, including community residential services and supported living services in a community residential setting. Adult foster care and CRS licenses have a maximum licensed capacity of four beds, with a capacity of five only in the limited cases in Minn. Stat. 245A.11 subdivision 2a. Funded through the Developmental Disabilities, Community Access for Disability Inclusion, Brain Injury and Community Alternative Care waivers named in section 245D.03.

Adult Foster Care (family and corporate)

Licensed by: Minnesota Department of Human Services (DHS), with county licensing involvement, under Minn. Stat. chapters 245A and 245D. Serves: Adults with disabilities and older adults receiving foster care and residential supports. Family residential services and foster care services under section 245D.03 are provided in a family adult foster care residence. Same four bed maximum capacity rule as CRS in section 245A.11 subdivision 2a, with five beds only in limited cases.

Assisted Living Facility, and Assisted Living Facility with Dementia Care

Licensed by: Minnesota Department of Health (MDH), under Minn. Stat. chapter 144G. Serves: Adults, mainly older adults, who need assisted living services such as help with dressing, bathing, toileting, grooming, standby assistance and medication reminders. New providers start on a provisional license, valid for one year, during which MDH surveys the provider; on substantial compliance MDH grants the assisted living facility license. Separate provisional applications exist for six or fewer residents and for seven or more residents. A dementia care designation is a separate license level under chapter 144G.

Supervised Living Facility, including ICF/DD

Licensed by: Minnesota Department of Health for supervised living facility and ICF/DD certification, with DHS chapter 245D standards for residential services. Serves: People with developmental disabilities, including more than four persons in a supervised living facility. Section 245D.03 covers residential services provided to more than four persons with developmental disabilities in a supervised living facility, including intermediate care facilities for individuals with developmental disabilities (ICF/DD), and residential services in a supervised living facility certified by the Department of Health as an ICF/DD.

How to start a group home in Minnesota, step by step

  1. For 245D home and community based services: meet the pre-qualifications in the DHS how to apply guide, including designated coordinator and designated manager, staffing, protection and maltreatment reporting policies
  2. Register the business with the Minnesota Secretary of State and complete the HCBS provider training steps in the DHS guide
  3. Apply through the DHS Licensing Division Electronic Licensing Management System (ELMS) as described in the DHS 245D application guide
  4. For assisted living: submit the provisional license application and checklist through the MDH Facility and Provider Licensing System, notify MDH within two days of first providing services to a resident, and complete the initial survey within the provisional year

Minnesota group home license fees and costs

  • Assisted living facility provisional license: $2,000 base rate plus $125 per licensed resident capacity (MDH provisional application for seven or more residents)
  • Assisted living facility with dementia care provisional license: $3,000 base rate plus $150 per licensed resident capacity (MDH provisional application)
  • 245D license application fee: $500, refundable to applicants who will not receive a license due to the temporary moratorium (DHS 245D licensing page)

How long does it take in Minnesota?

  • Assisted living provisional license is valid for 1 year; MDH must conduct an initial survey of the provisional licensee within one year (MDH assisted living application materials)
  • Temporary 245D licensing moratorium: DHS stopped accepting new 245D license applications on January 1, 2026, anticipated duration 24 months, ending December 31, 2027, with an exception process based on lead agency requests (DHS 245D licensing page)

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to open a group home in Minnesota?

The figures Minnesota publishes are these: Assisted living facility provisional license: $2,000 base rate plus $125 per licensed resident capacity (MDH provisional application for seven or more residents) Assisted living facility with dementia care provisional license: $3,000 base rate plus $150 per licensed resident capacity (MDH provisional application) 245D license application fee: $500, refundable to applicants who will not receive a license due to the temporary moratorium (DHS 245D licensing page) The license fee is rarely the largest cost. The home itself, life safety and accessibility work, insurance, and staffing hired before the first placement carry the project.

How long does it take to get a group home license in Minnesota?

Assisted living provisional license is valid for 1 year; MDH must conduct an initial survey of the provisional licensee within one year (MDH assisted living application materials) Temporary 245D licensing moratorium: DHS stopped accepting new 245D license applications on January 1, 2026, anticipated duration 24 months, ending December 31, 2027, with an exception process based on lead agency requests (DHS 245D licensing page)

What are the requirements to open a group home in Minnesota?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Minnesota?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
