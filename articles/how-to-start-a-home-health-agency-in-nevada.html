How to Start a Home Health Agency in Nevada

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Nevada licenses home health agencies as a Agency to provide nursing in the home - home office or subunit agency, and branch office (NRS 449.0015), through the Nevada Health Authority, Health Care Purchasing and Compliance Division, Bureau of Health Care Quality and Compliance (HCQC), formerly Division of Public and Behavioral Health HCQC. The governing law is NRS Chapter 449 (license required under NRS 449.030, definition at NRS 449.0015) and NAC Chapter 449, including fee rule NAC 449.016. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the Nevada license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The Nevada license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Nevada, step by step

  1. Review NAC Chapter 449, Medical and Other Related Facilities, as Step 1 in the Nevada State Fire Marshal health care agencies guidance that points applicants to HCQC
  2. Go through HCQC first to apply for a new license, as Step 2 in the same guidance
  3. Enter a new application in the HCQC online licensing system (CLICS/ALIS): select the credential (facility type) and any endorsements, complete days and hours, and add ownership information
  4. Submit all documents on the facility type checklist with the application packet, as instructed in the HCQC CLICS steps document
  5. Complete fingerprint background investigation for owners or designees of an agency to provide nursing in the home, per HCQC background investigation instructions citing NRS 449.122
  6. For Nevada Medicaid Provider Type 29 enrollment, hold licensure as an agency to provide nursing in the home and maintain active enrollment in Medicare, per the Nevada Medicaid enrollment checklist for Provider Type 29

Nevada home health license fees and costs

  • Agency to provide nursing in the home, home office or subunit agency: $5,168 initial application fee and $2,584 renewal fee in the Division of Public and Behavioral Health Health Medical Facilities Fee Schedule under NAC 449.016
  • Agency to provide nursing in the home, branch office: $5,358 initial and $2,679 renewal in the same fee schedule

What catches Nevada applicants

  • A state license is required before operating: the skilled category is a separate license from the agency to provide personal care services in the home, and Nevada Medicaid Provider Type 29 requires both the state nursing in the home license and active Medicare enrollment, so state licensure comes before Medicare and Medicaid enrollment in the sources reviewed
  • No certificate of need for home health was identified in the official Nevada sources reviewed
  • No startup timeline for home health was printed in the official sources fetched, so no timeline is listed

The Medicare layer, after the Nevada license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a home health agency in Nevada?

The figures Nevada publishes are these: Agency to provide nursing in the home, home office or subunit agency: $5,168 initial application fee and $2,584 renewal fee in the Division of Public and Behavioral Health Health Medical Facilities Fee Schedule under NAC 449.016 Agency to provide nursing in the home, branch office: $5,358 initial and $2,679 renewal in the same fee schedule Beyond the published figures, the operating costs (clinical payroll before certification, insurance, and the reserve operating funds required by 42 CFR 489.28) are larger than every fee on the list.

What do I need to start a home health agency in Nevada?

The Agency to provide nursing in the home - home office or subunit agency, and branch office (NRS 449.0015) (Nevada Health Authority, Health Care Purchasing and Compliance Division, Bureau of Health Care Quality and Compliance (HCQC), formerly Division of Public and Behavioral Health HCQC), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet Nevada rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in Nevada?

Apply to the Nevada Health Authority, Health Care Purchasing and Compliance Division, Bureau of Health Care Quality and Compliance (HCQC), formerly Division of Public and Behavioral Health HCQC under NRS Chapter 449 (license required under NRS 449.030, definition at NRS 449.0015) and NAC Chapter 449, including fee rule NAC 449.016. The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
