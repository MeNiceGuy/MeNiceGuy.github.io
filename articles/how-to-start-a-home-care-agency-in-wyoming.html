How to Start a Home Care Agency in Wyoming (No License for Non-Medical Care; Home Health and Hospice Licensed by the State)

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 19, 2026

The Wyoming story starts with a founder outside Cheyenne who kept two browser tabs open for a month. One tab held a seller page pricing a Wyoming home care license she could buy help with. The other held the state licensure list, where no such license appeared. She asked me which tab was lying. The seller page was. Wyoming issues no home care license for non-medical agencies, and the fee on that door is not hidden or unpublished in the usual sense; there is no license to price. What Wyoming does license, it licenses cleanly and cheaply by the standards of this series: home health agencies and hospice programs, through Healthcare Licensing and Surveys, on a statute fee schedule a founder can read in one sitting. The confusion in Wyoming is not the license map. It is the payer map, where Medicaid enrollment and waiver service certification are two separate doors run through two separate systems, and founders routinely complete one and assume they hold the other. Here is the full picture, lane by lane, in the order a founder actually meets it.

The demand: about 122,000 seniors, more than one in five residents

Start with why owners keep looking at Wyoming. The Census Bureau puts the state population at 588,753 as of July 1, 2025, and 20.7 percent of residents are age 65 or older. Run the math and you get about 122,000 seniors, more than one in five people in the least populous state in the country. The same Census QuickFacts page for Wyoming puts median household income at $76,176 for 2020 to 2024, which is the income base a private pay agency sells into. The Wyoming market is a distance market: small senior counts spread over very large service areas, so the agencies I work with plan the drive time and the caregiver map before they plan the marketing, because in this state the service area decision is the business model.

The license question, answered directly: no license for non-medical care, state licenses for home health and hospice

Here is the searched question this article exists to answer. Do you need a license to start a non-medical home care agency in Wyoming? No, because Wyoming does not issue one. No Wyoming statute or rule prints a license, a licensing agency, or a license fee for a private pay non-medical agency, and the packet prints NO FEE PUBLISHED on this door because there is no license to price. The definition line is what matters: the home health agency definition at W.S. 35-2-901(a)(xi) turns on nursing or other health care services, so a menu of hands-on personal care, homemaker, and companion work sits outside it, and crossing into skilled services without the license is a licensure violation, not a gray area.

The licensed doors are two, and they are separate. Home health agencies are licensed by Healthcare Licensing and Surveys, HLS, in the Wyoming Department of Health, under W.S. 35-2-901(a)(xi), with licensure standards at Chapter 10 and program administration at Chapter 9. Hospice programs hold their own license under W.S. 35-2-901(a)(xii); a hospice plan built on the home health license is built on the wrong credential. Wyoming has no certificate of need program, checked and confirmed for this build, so no CON step belongs in any Wyoming plan, and W.S. 35-2-906 plan approval for construction of a licensed facility is not a substitute CON gate for an agency. Residential facility licenses, assisted living among them, authorize no in-home agency work. The payer doors are separate from both licenses: Wyoming Medicaid enrollment runs through one system and Community Choices Waiver service certification through another, and the next sections keep them apart on purpose.

The open lane: non-medical home care, and the two-step Medicaid waiver door

The private pay sequence is formation and preparation, in the order the packet prints. Confirm the service menu is non-skilled: hands-on personal care, homemaker, and companion work only, with no nursing, no therapy, and no other health care service, and document the door determination in the startup file. Form the business with the Wyoming Secretary of State, hold the EIN and the tax registrations, and put the operating accounts, insurance, and the workers compensation account through the state fund in place. Wyoming has no corporate income tax and no state income tax, which simplifies the tax side of formation and changes nothing about the employer duties. Build the screening and training systems before the first visit, covered in the background check section below, and serve private pay clients under your service agreements. No state application sits on this door.

The Medicaid door on this lane has two steps, and the order is fixed. Step one is enrollment: the agency enrolls through the Wyoming Medicaid Provider Portal, where the enrollment contractor is HHS Technologies, using the Community Choices Waiver taxonomy 251B00000X for waiver services. A provider cannot be certified for waiver services until Medicaid enrollment is approved, so enrollment is never the second step. Step two is certification: the agency completes HCBS Section certification for each Community Choices Waiver service it will bill, companion, homemaker, personal support, and respite on the non-medical side, through the WHP portal, with the required trainings and background screenings for each service. Enrollment and certification are different doors in different systems run for different purposes, and holding one says nothing about the other. Claims process through the Benefit Management System administered by Acentra, under the Division of Healthcare Financing. Wyoming Medicaid is fee-for-service, so there are no Wyoming Medicaid managed care organizations to contract with for these programs, and any plan list naming managed care plans for Wyoming was imported from another state. Electronic visit verification is mandatory for the covered personal care and home health services through CareBridge, the state aggregator, or an interoperable compliant alternative the Division directs, configured before the first billed visit.

The licensed lanes: home health at $150 a year, hospice at $250 a year

The home health sequence runs in six steps. Form the business and settle the service area and the governing body structure the Chapter 9 and Chapter 10 standards require. Confirm the certificate of need negative before any other filing: Wyoming has no CON program, and no Wyoming Medicaid enrollment moratorium for this type was located in the sources checked. Obtain the fidelity bond of no less than $2,500, augmented in relation to the number of employees, and assemble the licensure file the HLS checklist, revised March 16, 2026, names. File the HLS home health agency application with the $150 fee at W.S. 35-2-904(g)(xi) and the policy file, and hold services until the provisional license issues. Complete the unannounced initial survey, correct any deficiencies through the plan of correction process, and hold the permanent license before operating past the provisional term. Enroll with Wyoming Medicaid after licensure and configure EVV through CareBridge for the waiver home health aide and skilled nursing visits. The license year runs July 1 to June 30, the fee is annual, and the full fee is due for a partial year. No application processing timeframe for this license is printed in the chapters read for this build, and the packet supplies none.

Hospice runs on its own track. The program forms, settles its service area and governing body, and files the HLS hospice program application with the $250 yearly fee at W.S. 35-2-904(g)(xii), naming the geographic service area the license record carries. The statute fee line labels this door a hospice facility fee; HLS licenses the hospice program on that line, and the label does not limit the license to inpatient programs. The agency holds the license before operating as a hospice and calendars the annual renewal inside the July 1 to June 30 license year, then enrolls with Wyoming Medicaid for the hospice benefit in parallel. Medicare certification layers on either licensed track the same way: the state license sequence completes first, the federal gate is read before timing, because initial enrollment is blocked under CMS-6101-N2 for home health and CMS-6102-N2 for hospice, extended and modified effective November 12, 2026 with no end date printed (the extension is a six-month period, running to about May 12, 2027), and the enrollment runs on Form CMS-855A through PECOS to CGS Administrators, Jurisdiction 15, with the CY2026 federal enrollment fee of $750, the manual built to 42 CFR Part 484 for home health or Part 418 for hospice, and the certification survey completed before Medicare billing. Noridian holds two other Wyoming desks, Jurisdiction F for A/B claims and Jurisdiction D for durable medical equipment, and neither enrolls a home health agency or a hospice.

Background checks and the personnel file

The packet's personnel file for Wyoming carries the application, references, and the signed job description for the role held; license or credential verification for every licensed role, from the primary source where the board provides it; the Department of Family Services central registry check where the role or the payer door requires it, before hire and on the cycle the applicable standards set, which is the check that matters where the work touches children or vulnerable adults; OIG and SAM exclusion checks before hire and monthly; the orientation, competency evaluation, and annual performance record; and the health screening and immunization record the agency policy and the applicable standards require. For Community Choices Waiver services, the required trainings and background screenings attach service by service at certification, so the file is built per service, not per agency. The deficiencies the packet flags are the Wyoming regulars: skilled services marketed or delivered on the no-license door, a residential facility license treated as an in-home credential, a home health agency operating past its provisional frame, EVV exceptions left unworked in the CareBridge aggregator, and central registry, background, and exclusion checks run at hire and never again.

What you still do today: form the business the Wyoming way

Formation is the whole state filing on the non-medical door, and the packet prices it honestly: no Wyoming formation fee figure is printed in the sources this packet is built from, so none is printed here. File with the Wyoming Secretary of State, get the EIN and the tax registrations, open the operating accounts, and set up the workers compensation account through the state fund. Then do the employer load in the right order: liability insurance before the first client, policies and procedures matched to the services you will actually sell, caregiver screening and training set up as a system, and a service agreement that says clearly what your agency does and does not do, with skilled and medical tasks on the does not do side of the line. Founders following generic national guides should note the two Wyoming facts those guides usually miss: there is no state income tax filing rhythm to build around, and there is no license application to wait on, so the timeline on this door is set by preparation and distance, not by a department queue.

The practical warning

Two Wyoming traps cost new owners money. The first is the old fee schedule. The 1998 Chapter 1 Licensure Fees rule and older HLS application forms price fees that the 2021 statute schedule at W.S. 35-2-904(g) replaced, including bed tiered boarding home fees on an old form that no longer controls anything. Budget from the statute schedule: $150 for home health, $250 for hospice, and nothing for the non-medical door because no license exists there. The second trap is the enrollment and certification mix-up on the waiver door. An agency that enrolls through HHS Technologies and never completes HCBS Section certification through the WHP portal cannot bill Community Choices Waiver services, and an agency that pursues certification before its Medicaid enrollment is approved has run the sequence backwards, because certification cannot attach to an unenrolled provider. One more timing fact belongs in every Wyoming plan that touches existing providers: the 2026 revalidation and NPI mandate gates continued enrollment for providers already enrolled, with a valid NPI required on file on or before October 31, 2026, payment suspension on November 1, 2026, and disenrollment on February 1, 2027 for providers who miss it. It is not an initial enrollment gate for a new agency, and no Wyoming Medicaid enrollment moratorium on new applicants was located in the sources checked.

What are the Wyoming home care license requirements?

Wyoming home-based care runs on separate doors. Non-medical home care has no state license and no license fee. Skilled home health uses the home health agency license from Healthcare Licensing and Surveys under W.S. 35-2-901(a)(xi), with licensure at Chapter 10 and program administration at Chapter 9, at $150 per year. Hospice uses its own program license under W.S. 35-2-901(a)(xii) at $250 per year. Wyoming has no certificate of need program. Medicare certification layers on the state license for the certified tracks, and Community Choices Waiver services require Medicaid enrollment plus service certification. How much does a home care license cost in Wyoming?

The federal Medicare enrollment fee is $750 for CY2026 on the certified tracks. Budget from the statute schedule at W.S. 35-2-904(g), not from older fee schedules. Your next step

Work in door order. If your first clients are private pay non-medical care, form the entity this week, stand up the EIN, the tax registrations, the workers compensation account, the insurance, the policies, and the screening and training file, and market across a service area you have honestly mapped for drive time. If Medicaid waiver revenue is in the plan, run the two-step door in its order: enrollment through the Wyoming Medicaid Provider Portal first, with taxonomy 251B00000X, then service certification through the WHP portal for each Community Choices Waiver service, with EVV configured before the first billed visit. If your plan is skilled home health or hospice, file the HLS license at the statute fee, $150 or $250, complete the survey sequence, and time any Medicare enrollment against the federal moratorium. An owner who runs that order starts at the speed of her own preparation in a state with about 122,000 seniors, instead of waiting on a license that does not exist or billing a waiver door she only half opened.
