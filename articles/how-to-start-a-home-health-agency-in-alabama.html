How to Start a Home Health Agency in Alabama

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Alabama does not license home health agencies at the state level. The Alabama Department of Public Health states that home health agencies are not required to be licensed by the State Board of Health, and may apply for certification in the Medicare and Medicaid programs. The state gate that does apply is the Certificate of Need, run by the State Health Planning and Development Agency (SHPDA) under Ala. Admin. Code 410-1-7: a Letter of Intent first, then the Certificate of Need application. This page gives you the Alabama entry sequence in its own terms, then the federal certification layer in the order it runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The state-side entry work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Alabama, step by step

  1. Submit a Letter of Intent to SHPDA at least 30 days prior to submission of a formal Certificate of Need application, with the $250 processing fee; the letter must be in writing to the Executive Director and a letter of intent remains effective for six months
  2. File the formal Certificate of Need application with SHPDA electronically in text searchable PDF format as required by Ala. Admin. Code r. 410-1-3-.09; SHPDA directs all official filings, including form submissions, to its electronic filing address on its Certificate of Need forms page
  3. Pay the required Certificate of Need filing fee with the application; all required filing fees must be submitted as the rule directs
  4. Obtain Certificate of Need approval for the proposed home health service area; SHPDA maintains home health specific Certificate of Need materials, including a Home Health Contiguous County Letter form, and the State Health Plan home health section is at Ala. Admin. Code r. 410-2-4-.07 as cited in SHPDA filings
  5. After the Certificate of Need gate, apply for Medicare and Medicaid certification through ADPH Bureau of Health Provider Standards; ADPH states a home health agency may apply for certification in the Medicare and Medicaid programs and its Provider Services Unit processes certification applications

Alabama home health license fees and costs

  • Certificate of Need Letter of Intent processing fee: $250 (Ala. Admin. Code r. 410-1-7-.05 as published by SHPDA)
  • Certificate of Need application filing fee: one percent of the estimated cost of the proposed new institutional health service, with a minimum of $3,500 and a maximum of $12,000 indexed, under Ala. Admin. Code r. 410-1-7-.06 as amended in 2025; reduced percentages and caps in that rule apply to certain high Medicaid census applicants and rural hospital applicants
  • SHPDA notice effective October 1, 2025 states the maximum Certificate of Need filing fee was increased for inflation from $25,706 to $26,143; applicants should confirm the current indexed maximum with SHPDA before filing
  • No state home health license fee is listed, because ADPH does not license home health agencies

How long does it take in Alabama?

  • Letter of Intent: at least 30 days before the formal Certificate of Need application, and effective for six months from its date

What catches Alabama applicants

  • ADPH defines a home health agency as a program which provides skilled nursing services and at least one of physical therapy, speech language pathology, occupational therapy, medical social services or home health aide services in a place of residence used as the patient home, with at least one listed service provided directly and in its entirety by employees of the HHA
  • Because there is no state license, the state license before Medicare certification question does not apply in Alabama in the same form as in licensed states; the verified state gates are SHPDA Certificate of Need review and ADPH handled Medicare and Medicaid certification
  • No overall Certificate of Need review timeline or Medicare certification timeline was verified from an official page fetched for this research, so none is listed

The Medicare layer

With the state-side entry handled and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a home health agency in Alabama?

The figures Alabama publishes are these: Certificate of Need Letter of Intent processing fee: $250 (Ala. Admin. Code r. 410-1-7-.05 as published by SHPDA) Certificate of Need application filing fee: one percent of the estimated cost of the proposed new institutional health service, with a minimum of $3,500 and a maximum of $12,000 indexed, under Ala. Admin. Code r. 410-1-7-.06 as amended in 2025; reduced percentages and caps in that rule apply to certain high Medicaid census applicants and rural hospital applicants SHPDA notice effective October 1, 2025 states the maximum Certificate of Need filing fee was increased for inflation from $25,706 to $26,143; applicants should confirm the current indexed maximum with SHPDA before filing No state home health license fee is listed, because ADPH does not license home health agencies Beyond the published figures, the operating costs (clinical payroll before certification, insurance, and the reserve operating funds required by 42 CFR 489.28) are larger than every fee on the list.

How long does it take to start a home health agency in Alabama?

Letter of Intent: at least 30 days before the formal Certificate of Need application, and effective for six months from its date The federal certification that follows runs on the survey calendar, and new Medicare enrollment is under a CMS moratorium to about mid-May 2027.

What do I need to start a home health agency in Alabama?

Medicare certification as a home health agency, because Alabama has no state home health license. Beyond that: an administrator and clinical leadership, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Any state gate named on this page, such as a certificate of need where the state requires one, comes first, and Medicaid enrollment is a separate application.

Do I need a state license to start a home health agency in Alabama?

No. No state home health agency license. ADPH states home health agencies are not required to be licensed by the Alabama State Board of Health but may apply for certification in the Medicare and Medicaid programs. The operative gate is Medicare certification, which follows the federal sequence laid out on this page.
