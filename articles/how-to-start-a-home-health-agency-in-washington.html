How to Start a Home Health Agency in Washington

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Washington licenses home health agencies as a In-Home Services Agency license with the home health service category, through the Washington State Department of Health (DOH). The governing law is RCW 70.127 (In-Home Services Agencies); WAC 246-335, including WAC 246-335-320 (application) and WAC 246-335-990 (fees). That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the Washington license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The Washington license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Washington, step by step

  1. State licensure first: submit a copy of the certificate of completion from the In-Home Services Orientation Class (applications will not be processed without it), the completed in-home services application with the initial licensing fee, proof of commercial general liability insurance under WAC 246-335-320(2)(b), disclosure statements and Washington State Patrol criminal history background checks for the on-site administrator and director of clinical services dated within three months, copies of government-issued business licenses for each office, the full-time equivalent worksheet, and descriptions of organizational structure, management and supervision, and services provided directly and under contract
  2. After review and acceptance, the DOH Office of Investigation and Inspection contacts the applicant and schedules an initial survey; the applicant must pass the survey for the license to be issued
  3. Medicare certification, Step 1: review the federal Medicare home health regulations (42 CFR Part 484 and the State Operations Manual sections listed by DOH)
  4. Medicare certification, Step 2: contact the DOH Certificate of Need program by email before applying for Medicare certification
  5. Medicare certification, Step 3: once Certificate of Need approval is granted, submit Medicare application form 855A to the CMS Fiscal Intermediary
  6. Medicare certification, Steps 4 to 7: submit the completed application materials to the Medicare Program in the Office of Investigation and Inspection; the Fiscal Intermediary reviews the 855A and sends approval to that program; the program contacts the applicant for specific Medicare forms; submit the completed forms
  7. Medicare certification, Step 8: after review and acceptance, the Office of Investigation and Inspection survey team conducts an on-site survey before recommending the agency for Medicare certification

Washington home health license fees and costs

  • Initial license: $5,000 for each service category (home care, home health, hospice) for a 12-month license, for persons not currently licensed to provide in-home services in Washington (WAC 246-335-990, effective June 1, 2026)
  • Adding a new service category to an existing license: $5,000 for each new service category
  • Change of ownership: $500 for each licensed service category (WAC 246-335-990)

How long does it take in Washington?

  • Under WAC 246-335-320(4), DOH may close out an application, with no refund of licensing fees, if an applicant has not completed all steps required for licensure within nine months

What catches Washington applicants

  • State licensure as a home health agency is a requirement for federal Medicare certification, stated on the DOH home health license requirements page
  • Certificate of Need: the applicant must contact the DOH Certificate of Need program before applying for Medicare certification, and may submit the Medicare 855A only once Certificate of Need approval is granted
  • The same RCW 70.127 license also covers the home care (non-medical) service category, at the same $5,000 initial fee per service category effective June 1, 2026 (context verified on the client's site and confirmed in WAC 246-335-990)
  • Medicaid residential funding named on DSHS pages: the Community Options Program Entry System (COPES) is a 1915(c) Medicaid waiver operated by the Home and Community Services division that provides services to clients who live in their own homes, adult family homes or assisted living facilities

The Medicare layer, after the Washington license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a home health agency in Washington?

The figures Washington publishes are these: Initial license: $5,000 for each service category (home care, home health, hospice) for a 12-month license, for persons not currently licensed to provide in-home services in Washington (WAC 246-335-990, effective June 1, 2026) Adding a new service category to an existing license: $5,000 for each new service category Change of ownership: $500 for each licensed service category (WAC 246-335-990) Beyond the published figures, the operating costs (clinical payroll before certification, insurance, and the reserve operating funds required by 42 CFR 489.28) are larger than every fee on the list.

How long does it take to get a home health license in Washington?

Under WAC 246-335-320(4), DOH may close out an application, with no refund of licensing fees, if an applicant has not completed all steps required for licensure within nine months The federal certification that follows runs on the survey calendar, and new Medicare enrollment is under a CMS moratorium to about mid-May 2027.

What do I need to start a home health agency in Washington?

The In-Home Services Agency license with the home health service category (Washington State Department of Health (DOH)), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet Washington rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in Washington?

Apply to the Washington State Department of Health (DOH) under RCW 70.127 (In-Home Services Agencies); WAC 246-335, including WAC 246-335-320 (application) and WAC 246-335-990 (fees). The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
