How to Start a Group Home in Maryland

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Maryland. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Maryland licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Maryland licenses

DDA group home

Licensed by: Maryland Developmental Disabilities Administration (DDA), Maryland Department of Health. Serves: Individuals with developmental disabilities, at least 4 but not more than 8 per home. Community residential service defined at COMAR 10.22.01.03 and Health-General section 7-101, with 10 or more hours of supervision per home per week; a license from the Administration is required before providing community residential services (COMAR 10.22.02.01-1A(2)); the waiver door is the Community Pathways Waiver at COMAR 10.09.26, after the Family Supports and Community Supports waivers were combined into Community Pathways effective October 6, 2025

DDA alternative living unit (ALU)

Licensed by: Maryland Developmental Disabilities Administration (DDA). Serves: Individuals with developmental disabilities, not more than 3 per residence. Defined at COMAR 10.22.01.03 and Health-General section 7-101, with 10 or more hours of supervision per unit per week; Maryland separates ALU and group home by capacity, so the research builds them as separate tracks under COMAR 10.22.02

DDA individual family care home (IFC)

Licensed by: Maryland Developmental Disabilities Administration (DDA). Serves: 1 to 3 individuals with developmental disabilities not related to the caregiver, in a private single family residence. Licensed under COMAR 10.22.08 and served through the same DDA provider application; registered inside the ALU track in the research

ICF/IID

Licensed by: OHCQ, Maryland Department of Health. Serves: Individuals with intellectual disabilities in a related institution. Licensed by OHCQ under COMAR 10.07.20 as a related institution under Health-General section 19-301, and certified against the federal Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 483 Subpart I (sections 483.400 to 483.480) and Part 442; OHCQ runs initial licensure, annual certification surveys, and complaint investigations; Medicaid only, no Medicare benefit for the class

Assisted Living Program

Licensed by: OHCQ, Maryland Department of Health. Serves: Residents needing assisted living care at levels of care 1, 2, or 3 as shown on the license. Licensed under Health-General Title 19, Subtitle 18 and COMAR 10.07.14, comprehensively revised effective April 28, 2025; an Alzheimer's special care unit is an approval inside this license (disclosure form plus OHCQ written approval before operating), not a separate license; the assisted living manager must hold a Board of Long-Term Care Administrators license under COMAR 10.33.03.01A, effective July 1, 2026

Behavioral Health Administration Residential Rehabilitation Program (RRP)

Licensed by: Maryland Behavioral Health Administration (BHA). Serves: People receiving residential rehabilitation for behavioral health conditions. Accreditation-based license at COMAR 10.63.02.02A(16) with additional requirements at COMAR 10.63.04; the program must hold accreditation from an MDH-approved accreditation organization before BHA licenses it; legacy therapeutic group home rules at COMAR 10.21.07, the legacy group home license at COMAR 10.21.04, and the RRP residence approval history at COMAR 10.21.22 are registered as history inside this track

Behavioral Health Administration Residential Crisis Services (RCS)

Licensed by: Maryland Behavioral Health Administration (BHA). Serves: People receiving residential crisis services. Accreditation-based license at COMAR 10.63.02.02A(11); the Behavioral Health Crisis Stabilization Center at COMAR 10.63.02.02A(20) is registered inside this track

Behavioral Health Administration SUD residential, ASAM Levels 3.1, 3.3, 3.5, and 3.7

Licensed by: Maryland Behavioral Health Administration (BHA), with a Maryland Health Care Commission Certificate of Need door on Level 3.7 only. Serves: People receiving substance use disorder residential treatment at low, medium, high, and intensive levels. Accreditation-based licenses at COMAR 10.63.02.02A(12) to (15) and COMAR 10.63.03.11 to .14, kept as separate level lines in one track; the Level 3.7 Certificate of Need door is at COMAR 10.24.14 and applies to that level only

Residential child care program, including group homes and child care institutions

Licensed by: Licensing agency designated under COMAR 14.31.02 through the Governor's Office for Children, across the Department of Human Services, Maryland Department of Health, and Department of Juvenile Services. Serves: Children in residential child care, including in group homes, therapeutic group homes, shelter care, secure care, mother-infant programs, programs for medically fragile children, programs for pregnant adolescents, psychiatric respite, residential crisis, respite, wilderness, diagnostic programs, and alternative living units for children. Licensed under COMAR 14.31.05, 14.31.06, and 14.31.07; a person must be licensed before operating a private residential child care program (14.31.05.02B); group home is defined at 14.31.06.03B(20); group homes and child care institutions run through one license family in Maryland; foster family homes are not licensed under this chapter (14.31.06.02C)

Residential Treatment Center

Licensed by: OHCQ, Maryland Department of Health, plus CMS Psychiatric Residential Treatment Facility certification. Serves: Children and adolescents with severe and chronic emotional disturbances needing campus-based intensive evaluation and treatment. Defined at COMAR 10.07.04.02B(14), license required at COMAR 10.07.04.03A, and a Certificate of Need or exemption from MHCC must come first under Health-General section 19-319; all Maryland RTCs are also certified by CMS as PRTFs under 42 CFR 441.151 to 441.182, requiring a provider agreement with the State Medicaid Agency and accreditation by The Joint Commission

Recovery residence

Licensed by: Behavioral Health Administration, Maryland Certification of Recovery Residences (MCORR). Serves: Individuals with substance-related or addictive disorders living in alcohol-free and illicit drug-free housing. Housing, not a licensed treatment program, and exempt from COMAR 10.63 program licensure at COMAR 10.63.01.04A(4); the credential is an MCORR certificate of compliance under Health-General sections 19-2501 to 19-2504, applied for per service site; advertising a residence as certified without a certificate carries a civil penalty of up to $1,000 for each offense (section 19-2504)

Forensic residential center (Secure Evaluation and Therapeutic Treatment unit)

Licensed by: OHCQ license with DDA standards. Serves: Individuals in forensic residential care tied to developmental disabilities services. Must hold an OHCQ license before operating, with attachments through COMAR 10.22.02.02, including a business plan, demonstrated experience in developmental disabilities, a DDA-approved written quality assurance plan under COMAR 10.22.02.02 and 10.22.14, and a letter of good standing; OHCQ schedules an on-site licensure survey after approving the application and attachments

Comprehensive care facility / nursing home

Licensed by: OHCQ, Maryland Department of Health, with Certificate of Need through MHCC for new capacity. Serves: Residents needing comprehensive or extended nursing care. Licensed under COMAR 10.07.02, procedure at COMAR 10.07.02.04; federal layer at 42 CFR Part 483 Subpart B with CMS-855A enrollment through PECOS and Novitas Jurisdiction L and the $750 federal institutional enrollment fee for calendar year 2026; Medicaid door is MPRIME enrollment under COMAR 10.09.36; extended care facilities sit in the same track family

Adult Medical Day Care

Licensed by: OHCQ, Maryland Department of Health. Serves: Elderly adults and adults with a medical disability receiving day care in a center. Licensed under COMAR 10.12.04, procedure at section 10.12.04.04; two-step sequence with a letter of interest approved by OHCQ before the license application is accepted, and the license issues for a two-year period; social-only adult day programs sit outside COMAR 10.12.04; medical day care is a Maryland Medicaid service delivered by licensed centers with MPRIME enrollment

How to start a group home in Maryland, step by step

  1. Confirm the model and capacity: a DDA group home admits at least 4 but not more than 8 individuals, while an alternative living unit admits not more than 3, and each model takes its own track under COMAR 10.22.02
  2. File the DDA Qualified Services Provider Application for an initial agency, a renewal, or adding services (form revised 9.19.25); a license from the Administration is required before providing community residential services (COMAR 10.22.02.01-1A(2))
  3. Build the licensee policy system prescribed at COMAR 10.22.02.10, including the emergency plan an alternative living unit or group home licensee must adopt
  4. Submit the agency's COMAR 10.22.02.10 policies to OHCQ within 30 days of DDA approval of the application, as the provider application and agency questionnaire direct
  5. Enroll with Maryland Medicaid through MPRIME and deliver services under the Community Pathways Waiver at COMAR 10.09.26, with services planned and paid through LTSSMaryland with a Coordinator of Community Services
  6. Meet the added child rules where the home serves a child with a developmental disability: residential services for a child must also meet COMAR 14.31.05, 14.31.06, and 14.31.07 (COMAR 10.22.02.01-1D)

Maryland group home license fees and costs

  • DDA group home and ALU: no application fee figure appears in the DDA Qualified Services Provider Application (revised 9.19.25) or in COMAR 10.22.02 as checked on 2026-11-12, and no fee figure is quoted in the research
  • OHCQ license applications: the current forms state there is no fee to apply for a license, re-verified on 2026-11-12 for the ICF/IID application (revised 03.20.2026), forensic residential center application (revised 03.20.2026), residential treatment center application (revised 03.13.2026), adult medical day care application (revised 03.13.2026), health care staff agency application (revised 02.20.2026), and the nursing home change of ownership application (revised 3/11/2026); legacy COMAR and checklist fee figures are named as superseded where they exist
  • Adult medical day care legacy schedule, named in the research as superseded and not quoted as current: an older October 2014 OHCQ checklist printed a two-year license fee computed at $200 plus $12 times licensed capacity and a $100 fee per additional pre-licensure site visit
  • Children's residential programs: no program license fee figure appears in COMAR 14.31.05 or 14.31.06 as checked; the personnel credential fees that do exist are State Board for the Certification of Residential Child Care Program Professionals fees: Certified Residential Child Care Program Administrator initial application $200, standards examination $125, and biennial certificate renewal $200, and Residential Child and Youth Care Practitioner application $50
  • Assisted living manager license, a Board of Long-Term Care Administrators fee and not an OHCQ facility fee: $100 application fee and $200 license fee on approval, both nonrefundable; the same Board schedule for nursing home administrators is $100 initial application, $200 original license, and $200 biennial renewal
  • BHA residential programs: Health-General section 7.5-402(c) authorizes reasonable license fees by regulation, but no BHA license fee schedule was captured from a primary source in the research, so no BHA fee figure is stated
  • Recovery residence MCORR certificate: no fee figure appears in the MCORR application (MDH #4799, revised 6/8/2026) as checked, and none is quoted
  • Limited private inpatient facilities, a register entry inside the nursing facility track only: COMAR 10.07.16.05I prints a $500 nonrefundable provisional license fee for a facility that has filed for accreditation, plus $1,500 on accreditation, and a $1,500 nonrefundable license fee per three-year accreditation period
  • Health care staff agency: no fee to apply on the current OHCQ form, and OHCQ may impose a $100 fine if a required change notification does not occur within 30 calendar days (COMAR 10.07.03)
  • Federal institutional provider enrollment fee on the nursing facility CMS-855A door: $750 for calendar year 2026

How long does it take in Maryland?

  • DDA track: the agency's COMAR 10.22.02.10 policies are submitted to OHCQ within 30 days of DDA approval of the provider application; the research publishes no DDA license term length and states none
  • ICF/IID and other OHCQ applications are held 180 days from initial receipt and then administratively closed if incomplete
  • Recovery residence MCORR certificate is valid for one year, issuing after approval and a passed site inspection (MCORR application and Health-General section 19-2502(c))
  • Adult medical day care license issues for a two-year period (COMAR 10.12.04.04D(1)), and the license application is not accepted until OHCQ reviews and approves the letter of interest
  • Comprehensive care facility licensing procedure at COMAR 10.07.02.04 includes filing at least 60 days before the anticipated issuance of the license
  • Children's residential staff clearances: criminal background checks and child protective services clearances under COMAR 14.31.06.05, renewed every two years
  • MPRIME perishable: newly enrolling providers with a complete, processable application submitted on or before December 31, 2026 are eligible for a backdated enrollment effective date; COMAR 10.63 was proposed for repeal and replacement in the August 21, 2026 Maryland Register, with comments accepted through September 21, 2026, and the BHA tracks are built on the COMAR 10.63 in force with that rewrite flagged

What are the requirements to open a group home in Maryland?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Maryland?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
