How to Start a Group Home in Montana

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Montana. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Montana licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Montana licenses

Community Home for Persons with Developmental Disabilities (developmental disabilities group home)

Licensed by: DPHHS, Office of Inspector General, Licensure Bureau for the facility license. DPHHS Developmental Disabilities Program, in the Behavioral Health and Developmental Disabilities Division, administers the 0208 Medicaid Home and Community Based Services waiver referenced in ARM 37.34.912 and related rules.. Serves: Persons with developmental disabilities, as defined in MCA 53-20-202(3) and referenced in ARM 37.100.302.. Family oriented residence under MCA 53-20-302 and ARM 37.100.301 through 37.100.340. Licensed for 2 to 8 residents, and 9 to 12 residents only with special written permission from the department under conditions in ARM 37.100.307, including fire marshal verification. MCA 53-20-305 provides for department licensing of community homes, and ARM 37.100.307 provides that the department issues the license annually, with renewal application at least 30 days before expiration. ARM 37.100.302 defines an applicant as a non-profit or state-operated entity. Residential habilitation group home services under the 0208 waiver are listed in DPHHS Developmental Disabilities Program materials.

Community Home for Persons with Physical Disabilities (community home for persons with severe disabilities)

Licensed by: DPHHS, Office of Inspector General, Licensure Bureau, using the same group home application portal as community homes for developmental disabilities in the DPHHS prospective provider letter for Community Homes for Persons with Developmental or Physical Disabilities.. Serves: Persons with developmental or physical disabilities, as addressed jointly in the DPHHS prospective provider letter for this license type.. Governed by ARM Title 37, chapter 100, subchapter 4, Community Homes for Persons with Physical Disabilities. ARM 37.100.407 provides that a community home may be licensed for 2 through 8 residents. MCA 50-5-101 and MCA 50-5-301 exclude community homes for persons with severe disabilities licensed under MCA 52-4-203 from the intermediate care facility and long-term care facility definitions used for certificate of need. The DPHHS letter for this combined type requires a floor plan, program description, policies and procedures at least 45 days before opening, fire marshal and sanitarian certification, and criminal background checks for direct care staff, followed by a six (6) month provisional license.

Assisted Living Facility, Category A, B, C and D

Licensed by: DPHHS, Office of Inspector General, Licensure Bureau, under MCA 50-5-225 through 50-5-227 and ARM Title 37, chapter 106, subchapter 28.. Serves: Residents at least 18 years of age who need personal care, per MCA 50-5-226(1) as summarized in DPHHS materials. ARM 37.106.2802 describes assisted living as a setting for frail, elderly, or disabled persons providing supportive health and service coordination.. MCA 50-5-227(3) requires four licensing categories. Category A serves residents requiring the level of care in MCA 50-5-226(2). Category B provides skilled nursing care or other skilled services to 5 or fewer residents who meet MCA 50-5-226(3). Category C provides services to residents with cognitive impairments requiring the level of care in MCA 50-5-226(4). Category D provides services to residents with mental disorders who may be a temporary harm to themselves or others and who require the level of care in MCA 50-5-226(5). ARM 37.106.2803 sets which rules apply to each category, with Category C also meeting ARM 37.106.2891 through 37.106.2898 and Category D also meeting ARM 37.106.2899 through 37.106.2899H. The DPHHS assisted living letter states no certificate of need is required, requires a separate additional application for a Category B or C endorsement, and requires the facility resident agreement to meet ARM 37.106.2823.

Adult Foster Care Home (AFCH)

Licensed by: DPHHS, Office of Inspector General, Licensure Bureau, under MCA 50-5-101(4), MCA 50-5-215 and ARM Title 37, chapter 100, subchapter 1.. Serves: Aged persons or disabled adults who are not related to the owner or manager by blood, marriage, or adoption and are not under the full guardianship of the owner or manager.. Limited to 4 or fewer residents under MCA 50-5-101(4) and ARM 37.100.102, offering only light personal care, custodial care, and supervision, and not skilled nursing care except as provided in ARM 37.100.136. ARM 37.100.120 provides every AFCH must be licensed by the department. ARM 37.100.121 provides a license period of 1 to 3 years and renewal application at least 30 days before expiration. The DPHHS 2025 AFCH letter requires a personal statement of health for each employee and household member other than residents, a release of information for background checks for employees and family members over 18 in the home, a safety compliance checklist, a floor plan, policies and procedures at least 45 days before opening, and CPR and first aid certification documentation under ARM 37.100.165.

Mental Health Group Home (endorsement of a licensed Mental Health Center)

Licensed by: DPHHS, Office of Inspector General, Licensure Bureau, for the mental health center license and group home endorsement, under ARM Title 37, chapter 106, subchapter 19. DPHHS Behavioral Health and Developmental Disabilities Division administers the Severe and Disabling Mental Illness (SDMI) waiver residential habilitation policies that use this license.. Serves: Adults with a mental illness, for residential treatment. ARM 37.106.1938 states the purpose of a mental health group home is to provide residential treatment for adults with a mental illness.. A mental health group home is not a standalone license in the rules cited: ARM 37.88.901 and ARM 37.90.453 provide that mental health group home services are provided under a group home endorsed mental health center license, and a mental health group home must be a licensed mental health center with a group home endorsement. The DPHHS mental health center letter states State Fire Marshal or designee inspection and approval are required for license endorsement of mental health group homes. DPHHS SDMI policy for Intensive Mental Health Group Home (SDMI 378) requires a licensed mental health center with a group home endorsement in accordance with ARM Title 37, chapter 106, subchapter 19, plus approval by the Behavioral Health and Developmental Disabilities Division, 24 hour on-site response capability, and referral only from Montana State Hospital, Montana Mental Health Nursing Care Center, or the Behavioral Health and Developmental Disabilities Division.

Substance Use Disorder Facility, Residential

Licensed by: DPHHS, Office of Inspector General, Licensure Bureau, for the facility license under ARM Title 37, chapter 106, subchapter 14. DPHHS Behavioral Health and Developmental Disabilities Division handles separate state approval, and its provider resources state substance use disorder facilities must begin the licensing process first before applying for state approval.. Serves: Persons receiving substance use disorder treatment at the ASAM level of care the facility is licensed for, adult or adolescent as the specific rule for that level provides.. The DPHHS 2025 prospective provider letter lists four licensed substance use disorder facility types: outpatient, inpatient, residential, and partial hospitalization, and states these facilities do not need a certificate of need. ARM 37.106.1411 states the subchapter establishes licensing requirements for substance use disorder facilities as outlined in the ASAM Criteria, including ASAM 3.7, 3.7-WM, 3.5, 3.3, 3.2-WM, 3.1, 2.5 partial hospitalization and outpatient levels, with level specific rules at ARM 37.106.1471 (3.1), 37.106.1472 (3.3, adult only), 37.106.1473 (3.5) and 37.106.1475 (3.7). MCA 50-5-101 (current temporary version) lists substance use disorder facilities among health care facilities.

Youth Group Home (Youth Care Facility)

Licensed by: DPHHS. Youth care facility licensing authority is in MCA Title 52, chapter 2, part 6, including MCA 52-2-603, and ARM Title 37, chapter 97. DPHHS Child and Family Services Division contracts with youth care facilities for placements.. Serves: Youth in substitute care. DPHHS Child and Family Services policy describes group home care for youth whose adequate adjustment to family life is unlikely but for whom intensive residential treatment in a child care agency is unnecessary.. ARM 37.97.102 defines a youth group home as a youth care facility in which substitute care is provided to 7 to 12 youth, and defines a youth care facility to include youth foster homes, youth group homes, youth shelter care facilities, therapeutic group homes, and child care agencies. DPHHS Child and Family Services policy 401-2 states the same 7 to 12 youth range for youth group homes. The DPHHS CFSD Facility Profile Guidelines state a youth group home facility must maintain a youth group home license prior to contracting with CFSD for services. ARM 37.97.106 provides the department shall issue a one year youth care facility license to an applicant meeting all licensing requirements after a licensing survey, and may issue a provisional license for up to 6 months in the circumstances stated in that rule.

Therapeutic Group Home (Therapeutic Youth Group Home, Youth Care Facility)

Licensed by: DPHHS, as a youth care facility licensed and under contract with the department, under MCA Title 52, chapter 2, part 6 and ARM Title 37, chapter 97, including ARM 37.97.903 through 37.97.907 cited in DPHHS CFSD guidance.. Serves: Youth with severe emotional disturbance and or behavioral disorders, as determined by the department, and youth needing therapeutic services in a therapeutic environment under an individualized written treatment plan.. ARM 37.97.102 defines a therapeutic group home as a treatment facility providing therapeutic services, licensed and under contract with the department as a youth care facility, with the supervision and intensity of treatment required to manage and treat up to 8 youth who present severe emotional disturbance and or behavioral disorders. DPHHS CFSD Facility Profile Guidelines state that, in addition to the youth care facility rules, each therapeutic group home unit or facility must meet ARM 37.97.903 through 37.97.907, that therapeutic services funding is provided through Medicaid, and that room and board funding is provided by the placing agency. ARM 37.97.106 provides a therapeutic group home must be accredited by CARF, the Joint Commission, the Council on Accreditation, or another independent non-profit accrediting organization approved by the Secretary of the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services.

Intermediate Care Facility for the Developmentally Disabled (ICF/IID)

Licensed by: DPHHS, Office of Inspector General, Licensure Bureau, as a health care facility under MCA Title 50, chapter 5.. Serves: Persons with a developmental disability, or persons with related problems, needing intermediate developmental disability care, as defined in MCA 50-5-101.. Montana does license this type: DPHHS publishes an Intermediate Care Facility for Developmental Disabilities prospective provider letter and application track. MCA 50-5-101 defines an intermediate care facility for the developmentally disabled as a facility or part of a facility that provides intermediate developmental disability care for 2 or more persons, and expressly excludes community homes for persons with developmental disabilities licensed under MCA 53-20-305 and community homes for persons with severe disabilities licensed under MCA 52-4-203. The DPHHS letter requires an application through the group home portal, a floor plan, articles of incorporation, bylaws or sponsoring board letter, organizational chart, board list, list of licensed professionals and license numbers, job descriptions, policies and procedures at least 45 days before opening, and fire marshal certification, followed by a six (6) month provisional license and a survey within that period.

How to start a group home in Montana, step by step

  1. For health care facility residential types (assisted living, adult foster care, community homes for persons with disabilities, mental health center group homes, residential substance use disorder facilities, and intermediate care facilities for the developmentally disabled), start with the DPHHS Licensure Bureau application for that type, through the online licensure portal or the application linked on the DPHHS page for that facility type, with the required fee where the letter for that type requires one. Do not submit an application earlier than 6 months prior to the desired licensure date, in the letters that state that limit.
  2. Submit a floor plan of the facility documenting the size of all rooms and spaces used by residents, hand drawn only if dimensions are included, as required in the DPHHS letters for assisted living, community homes, mental health centers, substance use disorder facilities, adult foster care homes, and intermediate care facilities for the developmentally disabled.
  3. Where the letter for the type requires it, submit a BFS Plan Submittal Form to the Licensure Bureau Construction Consultant (mental health center and substance use disorder facility letters), and submit local building code approval or, for new construction, a certificate of occupancy, plus State Fire Marshal or designee certification or inspection where required for that type.
  4. Submit site documentation that applies to the property, as listed in the DPHHS letters: a certified laboratory report of well water for potability dated within the past year if the facility uses well water, a county health department septic system inspection reflecting the number of bedrooms if the facility is not on a city sewer system, and sanitarian certification for community homes for persons with disabilities.
  5. For community homes and intermediate care facilities for the developmentally disabled, submit organizational documents listed in the DPHHS letters: articles of incorporation, bylaws or letter from sponsoring board, organizational chart, list of board of directors with terms and addresses as applicable, job descriptions for each staff position, and, for community homes, a program description and a list of licensed professionals where required.
  6. Submit policies and procedures for review and approval at least 45 days prior to the expected facility opening date. Assisted living Category B and C endorsements require additional policies and procedures, and an additional application specific to the category type.
  7. Submit type specific documents listed in the DPHHS letter for that type, for example the assisted living facility resident agreement meeting ARM 37.106.2823, community home grievance procedures and orientation and training plan, adult foster care personal statements of health, releases of information for background checks, safety compliance checklist and CPR and first aid documentation, and criminal background checks for direct care staff in community homes.
  8. Submit an attestation statement from the prospective administrator stating that he or she has reviewed the rules pertaining to that facility type, as required in the DPHHS letters for assisted living, adult foster care, substance use disorder facilities, mental health centers, and home health agencies, and as required generally by MCA 50-5-203(3).
  9. Schedule an on-site physical compliance inspection with the Bureau Construction Consultant where the letter for the type requires it. Review and approval of all required documentation and approval by the Construction Consultant are required before a license is issued in those letters. A facility may not admit residents until it is licensed.
  10. After submission and approval of all information and documentation and final Construction Consultant approval where required, the Licensure Bureau issues a six (6) month provisional license for the health care facility residential types covered by those letters, and a Licensure Bureau surveyor conducts an on-site survey within the provisional license period to assess compliance with that type of regulation.

Montana group home license fees and costs

  • MCA 50-5-202: $20 for facilities with 20 beds or less, which is the health care facility license fee schedule that applies to assisted living facilities, mental health centers, substance use disorder facilities, and intermediate care facilities for the developmentally disabled licensed under MCA Title 50, chapter 5
  • MCA 50-5-202: $1 per bed for facilities with 21 beds or more
  • No separate application fee amount is printed in the DPHHS prospective provider letters fetched for assisted living, adult foster care, community homes for persons with disabilities, mental health centers, residential substance use disorder facilities, or intermediate care facilities for the developmentally disabled. Those letters state that a completed application and fee, or an application through the portal, must be submitted, without stating an amount, so no other residential fee is listed here

How long does it take in Montana?

  • Do not submit an application earlier than 6 months prior to the desired licensure date (DPHHS letters for adult foster care, community homes for persons with disabilities, mental health centers, and substance use disorder facilities)
  • Application at least 45 days prior to the opening of a facility (MCA 50-5-203, for health care facilities)
  • Policies and procedures at least 45 days prior to the expected facility opening date (DPHHS letters for assisted living, adult foster care, community homes for persons with disabilities, mental health centers, substance use disorder facilities, and intermediate care facilities for the developmentally disabled)
  • Six (6) month provisional license after approval of all submitted documentation, with an on-site survey within the provisional license period (DPHHS letters for the health care facility residential types listed in gh_steps)
  • Assisted living administrators who do not hold a valid nursing home administrator license must, within 6 months of opening, complete the Senior Living University Administrator Level 1 certification described in the DPHHS assisted living letter, consistent with ARM 37.106.2814, which allows completion of the referenced self study course within 6 months from hire
  • Community homes for persons with developmental disabilities: license issued annually, renewal application at least 30 days prior to the expiration date of the current license (ARM 37.100.307)
  • Adult foster care homes: license period of 1 to 3 years, renewal application at least 30 days prior to expiration (ARM 37.100.121)
  • Youth care facilities, including youth group homes and therapeutic group homes: one year license, with a provisional license of up to 6 months available in the circumstances stated in ARM 37.97.106

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to open a group home in Montana?

The figures Montana publishes are these: MCA 50-5-202: $20 for facilities with 20 beds or less, which is the health care facility license fee schedule that applies to assisted living facilities, mental health centers, substance use disorder facilities, and intermediate care facilities for the developmentally disabled licensed under MCA Title 50, chapter 5 MCA 50-5-202: $1 per bed for facilities with 21 beds or more No separate application fee amount is printed in the DPHHS prospective provider letters fetched for assisted living, adult foster care, community homes for persons with disabilities, mental health centers, residential substance use disorder facilities, or intermediate care facilities for the developmentally disabled. Those letters state that a completed application and fee, or an application through the portal, must be submitted, without stating an amount, so no other residential fee is listed here The license fee is rarely the largest cost. The home itself, life safety and accessibility work, insurance, and staffing hired before the first placement carry the project.

What are the requirements to open a group home in Montana?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Montana?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
