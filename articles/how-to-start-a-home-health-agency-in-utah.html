How to Start a Home Health Agency in Utah

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Utah licenses home health agencies as a Home Health Agency license (health facility license), through the Utah Department of Health and Human Services, Division of Licensing and Background Checks (DLBC), Office of Licensing. The governing law is Utah Code Title 26B, Chapter 2, Health Care Facility Licensing and Inspection Act (definitions at section 26B-2-201, license required at section 26B-2-206 in the statutory text reviewed); Utah Administrative Code R432-700, Home Health Agency Rule; general health facility licensing provisions at R432-1 and background check rule R432-35, both listed by DLBC as applying to all health facility programs. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the Utah license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The Utah license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Utah, step by step

  1. Contact DLBC before completing any forms: the DLBC Health Facilities Applications page says to reach out to the licensing contact before completing forms because different facilities have different requirements (DLBC page fetched)
  2. File the Request for Agency Action / License Application on the form furnished by the department, with the appropriate licensing fee, required documentation, and applicable local and state clearances: the DLBC New Health Facility Application states the request is considered complete only on submission of the form, the appropriate licensing fee, required documentation, and the applicable clearances, and warns of sanction for operating before a complete packet is submitted
  3. Complete background check clearance through the DLBC Direct Access Clearance System (DACS) and upload the facility-specific documents the application lists for the category, such as fire clearance and administrator qualifications where the application requires them (DLBC application packet summary in search results from dlbc.utah.gov)
  4. Comply with the program-specific Home Health Agency Rule R432-700 and the general rules DLBC lists for all health facilities, R380-80, R380-600, R432-1, and R432-35

What catches Utah applicants

  • Utah licenses home health agencies at the state level as health facilities. DLBC lists Home Health Agency R432-700 as a program-specific health facility license type alongside Personal Care Agency R432-725, and Utah Code section 26B-2-201 lists home health agencies in the health care facility definitions in the statutory text reviewed.
  • No fee amount or processing timeline for a home health agency license was printed on the DLBC pages fetched for this sheet, so no fee or timeline is listed. DLBC fee amounts are set in the annual department fee schedule and were not verified from an official page in this pass.
  • No certificate of need statement for home health agencies was found on the DLBC pages fetched; CON was not verified for Utah in this pass and should not be assumed either way from this sheet.
  • Whether a Utah state license must issue before Medicare certification was not stated in the DLBC text fetched; the application packet does state a complete packet must be submitted prior to the start of operation.

The Medicare layer, after the Utah license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

What do I need to start a home health agency in Utah?

The Home Health Agency license (health facility license) (Utah Department of Health and Human Services, Division of Licensing and Background Checks (DLBC), Office of Licensing), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet Utah rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in Utah?

Apply to the Utah Department of Health and Human Services, Division of Licensing and Background Checks (DLBC), Office of Licensing under Utah Code Title 26B, Chapter 2, Health Care Facility Licensing and Inspection Act (definitions at section 26B-2-201, license required at section 26B-2-206 in the statutory text reviewed); Utah Administrative Code R432-700, Home Health Agency Rule; general health facility licensing provisions at R432-1 and background check rule R432-35, both listed by DLBC as applying to all health facility programs. The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
