How to Start a Home Health Agency in Michigan

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Michigan does not license home health agencies at the state level. There is no Michigan home health agency license: Article 17 of the Public Health Code does not list home health agencies among its licensed health facilities, and the skilled home health track in Michigan is Medicare certification only. The federal sequence is the whole gate: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey through an accrediting organization with deeming authority or the state survey agency, then CHAMPS Medicaid enrollment for state payment. This page lays out that sequence with the Michigan specifics inside it.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The state-side entry work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Michigan, step by step

  1. Form the business entity and obtain a federal employer identification number
  2. Enroll as a Medicare provider on Form CMS-855A through the Medicare Administrative Contractor; initial federal certification, re-certification, change of ownership, change of information, and change of administrator or leadership are the federal actions handled through BSC
  3. Choose the survey route: accreditation route through a CMS-approved accrediting organization (MDHHS ASM 125 names Accreditation Commission for Health Care (ACHC) and Community Health Accreditation Partner (CHAP) as the Michigan-sourced examples), or the state agency route with an initial certification survey by LARA BSC as State Survey Agency
  4. Meet the federal Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484 and pass the initial survey (accreditor or BSC); BSC issues the federal survey report on Form CMS-2567, sent to the HHA approximately 10 business days after exit
  5. For Medicaid payment, enroll in CHAMPS as a Facility/Agency/Organization (FAO) provider with a Type 2 NPI, after Medicare certification; for dates of service on and after April 1, 2024, HHAs that complete CHAMPS enrollment must complete the HHAeXchange provider onboarding form for electronic visit verification, even if they keep an existing EVV vendor
  6. Follow the Medicaid coverage rule: home health services must be ordered by a physician and provided by a Medicare certified home health agency, for individuals requiring services on an intermittent basis (MDHHS ASM 125)
  7. Route ongoing federal actions (change of ownership, address or name change, administrator or leadership change) through BSC

Michigan home health license fees and costs

  • State license fee: none, no license exists; any source quoting a Michigan home health agency license fee is inventing it
  • State certification survey fee: no fee published in the Michigan primary sources checked

How long does it take in Michigan?

  • No state license, so no state license term
  • Federal recertification surveys occur at least every 3 years (deemed agencies are surveyed by their accrediting organization, non-deemed agencies by the State agency)
  • BSC sends the federal survey report on Form CMS-2567 approximately 10 business days after exit
  • Medicaid enrollment is subject to CHAMPS revalidation at least once every five years, or more often if MDHHS requires it (stated for Medicaid enrollment generally in the research)

What catches Michigan applicants

  • Certificate of need: no home health agency certificate of need step is stated in the research; the hospice steps require checking certificate of need under Part 222 before building or expanding, with the CON fee schedule at MCL 333.20161(3) if it applies, but no hospice-specific CON step is stated generally
  • Separate Medicaid enrollment: Medicaid home health coverage requires Medicare certification, and CHAMPS enrollment follows Medicare certification; a state license alone does not exist to substitute for it
  • Michigan also does not license non-medical home care agencies: there is no state license, registration, or license fee for a private-pay non-medical home care agency; the Medicaid door for non-medical care is MDHHS Home Help agency provider approval, and the default home-care track is home-help-agency
  • By contrast, hospice IS state-licensed in Michigan under Public Health Code Part 214 (MCL 333.21401 to 333.21421) through LARA BCHS, with fees of $500 per agency license plus a $2,000 initial licensure application fee, and $500 per facility license plus $5 per licensed bed plus the $2,000 initial fee for a hospice residence, a term of no longer than 1 year, and separate federal hospice certification under 42 CFR Part 418
  • Verification method for the no-license finding is a three-part primary-source proof (the MCL 333.20106 closed list, the MCL 333.20161 fee schedule, and the LARA BSC April 15, 2025 presentation stating State Licensure - Not required for HHAs); no single we-do-not-license sentence was found on a live LARA web page in the research pass, and the live BSC HHA page could not be text-fetched, so that live check remains open in the research gaps

The Medicare layer

With the state-side entry handled and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a home health agency in Michigan?

The figures Michigan publishes are these: State license fee: none, no license exists; any source quoting a Michigan home health agency license fee is inventing it State certification survey fee: no fee published in the Michigan primary sources checked Beyond the published figures, the operating costs (clinical payroll before certification, insurance, and the reserve operating funds required by 42 CFR 489.28) are larger than every fee on the list.

How long does it take to start a home health agency in Michigan?

No state license, so no state license term Federal recertification surveys occur at least every 3 years (deemed agencies are surveyed by their accrediting organization, non-deemed agencies by the State agency) BSC sends the federal survey report on Form CMS-2567 approximately 10 business days after exit Medicaid enrollment is subject to CHAMPS revalidation at least once every five years, or more often if MDHHS requires it (stated for Medicaid enrollment generally in the research) The federal certification that follows runs on the survey calendar, and new Medicare enrollment is under a CMS moratorium to about mid-May 2027.

What do I need to start a home health agency in Michigan?

Medicare certification as a home health agency, because Michigan has no state home health license. Beyond that: an administrator and clinical leadership, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Any state gate named on this page, such as a certificate of need where the state requires one, comes first, and Medicaid enrollment is a separate application.

Do I need a state license to start a home health agency in Michigan?

No. No Michigan home health agency license exists (Medicare-certified home health agency is a federal certification only, not a state license) The operative gate is Medicare certification, which follows the federal sequence laid out on this page.
