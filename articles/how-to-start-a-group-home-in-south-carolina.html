How to Start a Group Home in South Carolina

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in South Carolina. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type South Carolina licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types South Carolina licenses

Community Residential Care Facility (CRCF)

Licensed by: South Carolina Department of Public Health (DPH). Serves: Persons 18 years old or older, not related to the licensee within the third degree of consanguinity; this is the category South Carolina uses for assisted living. Offers room and board and, unlike a boarding house, provides or coordinates a degree of personal care for a period in excess of 24 consecutive hours for two or more persons. Includes a facility that offers a beneficial or protected environment specifically for individuals who have mental illness or disabilities. Regulation 60-84, statutory authority S.C. Code Section 44-7-260. Annual license fee is $10 per licensed bed or $75, whichever is greater. DPH also lists CRCFs enrolled with the S.C. Department of Health and Human Services in the Optional State Supplement (OSS) program in its CRCF comparison material.

Community Training Home I (CTH-I) - Foster Care model

Licensed by: South Carolina Department of Behavioral Health and Developmental Disabilities, Office of Intellectual and Developmental Disabilities (BHDD-OIDD, formerly DDSN); BHDD-OIDD issues the license, a contracted Quality Improvement Organization conducts licensing inspections, and DPH inspects CRCF and ICF/IID locations. Serves: People with intellectual and related disabilities receiving Residential Habilitation. Personalized care, supervision and individualized training in a support provider's own home, to a maximum of two people, where they essentially become one of the family. Support providers are qualified and trained private citizens. CTH-I homes meet Office of State Fire Marshal Foster Home Regulations.

Community Training Home II (CTH-II)

Licensed by: BHDD-OIDD (formerly DDSN), license issued by BHDD-OIDD after QIO licensing inspection. Serves: People with intellectual and related disabilities receiving Residential Habilitation. Homelike community residence under the supervision of qualified and trained staff; care, skills training and supervision per the service plan. No more than four people live in each residence.

Supervised Living Program II (SLP-II) and Supported Living Program I (SLP-I)

Licensed by: BHDD-OIDD (formerly DDSN). Serves: People with intellectual and related disabilities who need intermittent (SLP-II) or occasional (SLP-I) supervision. SLP-II is typically an apartment setting with staff available on site or able to reach the site within 15 minutes of being called, 24 hours daily. SLP-I is an apartment or house setting with staff available 24 hours a day by phone. Both are Residential Habilitation settings licensed annually.

Community Integrated Residential Services (CIRS)

Licensed by: BHDD-OIDD (formerly DDSN). Serves: People with disabilities transitioning from 24-hour supervised living to semi-independent living. Participants select support providers, housemates and housing; a lease support agreement connects participants with landlords. CIRS settings are among the BHDD-OIDD settings licensed annually and inspected by the QIO.

Intermediate Care Facility for Individuals with Intellectual Disabilities (ICF/IID)

Licensed by: South Carolina Department of Public Health (DPH). Serves: Persons with intellectual disabilities or related conditions whose conditions require health or rehabilitative services on a regular basis. Serves four or more persons and provides room, board and active treatment for their intellectual disability or related conditions. Regulation 60-13, Standards for Licensing Intermediate Care Facilities for Individuals with Intellectual Disabilities.

Residential Treatment Facility for Children and Adolescents

Licensed by: South Carolina Department of Public Health (DPH). Serves: Children and adolescents in need of mental health treatment. Operated for the assessment, diagnosis, treatment and care of two or more children and/or adolescents in need of mental health treatment. Regulation 60-103.

Facility for Chemically Dependent or Addicted Persons - Residential Facility

Licensed by: South Carolina Department of Public Health (DPH). Serves: Persons receiving care, treatment or services for psychoactive substance abuse or dependence. Residential license types on the DPH application include Medical Withdrawal Management, Social Withdrawal Management Program and Residential Treatment Program beds. Regulation 60-93. Application states licensing fee of $10 per bed or $75, whichever is greater.

How to start a group home in South Carolina, step by step

  1. CRCF and ICF/IID: submit the DPH Application for Licensure listed on the DPH facility page for that type
  2. BHDD-OIDD residential programs: submit the Application to Operate to BHDD-OIDD; the contracted QIO completes the licensing inspection; on approval BHDD-OIDD issues the license to operate the facility

South Carolina group home license fees and costs

  • CRCF annual license fee: $10 per licensed bed or $75, whichever is greater (Regulation 60-84, Section 103.J as found in the regulation text)
  • Facility for Chemically Dependent or Addicted Persons, residential: licensing fee $10 per bed or $75, whichever is greater (DPH licensure application, Regulation 60-93)

How long does it take in South Carolina?

  • BHDD-OIDD: SLP-II, CTH-II, CTH-I and CIRS settings are licensed annually, and the facility review process must include an annual on-site review; DPH determines the inspection schedule for ICF/IID and CRCF locations

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to open a group home in South Carolina?

The figures South Carolina publishes are these: CRCF annual license fee: $10 per licensed bed or $75, whichever is greater (Regulation 60-84, Section 103.J as found in the regulation text) Facility for Chemically Dependent or Addicted Persons, residential: licensing fee $10 per bed or $75, whichever is greater (DPH licensure application, Regulation 60-93) The license fee is rarely the largest cost. The home itself, life safety and accessibility work, insurance, and staffing hired before the first placement carry the project.

How long does it take to get a group home license in South Carolina?

BHDD-OIDD: SLP-II, CTH-II, CTH-I and CIRS settings are licensed annually, and the facility review process must include an annual on-site review; DPH determines the inspection schedule for ICF/IID and CRCF locations

What are the requirements to open a group home in South Carolina?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in South Carolina?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
