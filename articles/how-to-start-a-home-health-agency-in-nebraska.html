How to Start a Home Health Agency in Nebraska

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Nebraska licenses home health agencies as a Home Health Agency license (health care service license under the Health Care Facility Licensure Act), through the Nebraska Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS), Division of Public Health, Licensure Unit, Office of Outpatient and In-Home Care Services. The governing law is Nebraska Revised Statutes sections 71-401 to 71-479 (Health Care Facility Licensure Act), home health agency defined at section 71-417; Title 175 Nebraska Administrative Code Chapter 14 (175 NAC 14), licensing requirements at 175 NAC 14-003, fees referenced at 175 NAC 14-004.10 on the DHHS page; federal certification under 42 CFR Part 484. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the Nebraska license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The Nebraska license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Nebraska, step by step

  1. Stage 1: Submit to the Department a complete application (DHHS Home Health Agencies page, Initial Licensure Requirements, 175 NAC 14-003.01)
  2. Stage 2: Pass an onsite inspection. The Department has 30 working days to conduct the initial onsite inspection after a complete application is received, unless the applicant requests a later date, and the applicant must notify the Department in writing of the date the applicant will be ready
  3. Be ready to operate, but not operating, before the inspection: the owner, Administrator, and back-up Administrator must demonstrate compliance with 175 NAC 14-006 through agency-specific organizational charts, job descriptions, policies, procedures, and forms. The agency may not operate without an active license issued by the Department
  4. After state licensure, pursue Medicare and Medicaid certification: the DHHS page states the service must have a Home Health Agency state license issued by the Department prior to being certified in the Medicare and Medicaid program, and a new agency must demonstrate it can transmit OASIS data prior to the initial certification survey

Nebraska home health license fees and costs

  • License fees include a base fee of fifty dollars plus an additional fee of no more than one thousand dollars for home health agencies - Neb. Rev. Stat. section 71-434 (official Nebraska Legislature text). The DHHS home health page states the licensee must pay fees as set forth at 175 NAC 14-004.10 but does not print the dollar amount on the page fetched, so the specific initial fee in the current rule was not verified from a .gov page in this pass.

How long does it take in Nebraska?

  • Initial onsite inspection: Department has 30 working days after receipt of a complete initial licensure application (DHHS Home Health Agencies page)
  • Change of ownership, lease, or discontinuance: licensee must notify the Department in writing 10 days before a home health agency is sold, leased, or discontinued (DHHS Home Health Agencies page)

What catches Nebraska applicants

  • A state license is required before Medicare certification in Nebraska. DHHS states verbatim that the service must have a Home Health Agency (HHA) state license issued by the Department prior to being certified in the Medicare/Medicaid program, and that certification means compliance with 42 CFR Part 484.
  • Definition: a home health agency provides skilled nursing care or a minimum of one other therapeutic service (physical therapy, speech pathology, occupational therapy, respiratory care, home health aide, social work, intravenous therapy, or dialysis) to a person in their permanent or temporary residence, other than a hospital or nursing home (DHHS page).
  • Certificate of need: DHHS Facilities, Services and Establishment Licensing page states Certificate of Need in Nebraska only applies to rehabilitation and nursing home beds and no other facility, service, or equipment is subject to CON. Home health agencies are therefore not a CON category on that official statement.

The Medicare layer, after the Nebraska license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a home health agency in Nebraska?

The figures Nebraska publishes are these: License fees include a base fee of fifty dollars plus an additional fee of no more than one thousand dollars for home health agencies - Neb. Rev. Stat. section 71-434 (official Nebraska Legislature text). The DHHS home health page states the licensee must pay fees as set forth at 175 NAC 14-004.10 but does not print the dollar amount on the page fetched, so the specific initial fee in the current rule was not verified from a .gov page in this pass. Beyond the published figures, the operating costs (clinical payroll before certification, insurance, and the reserve operating funds required by 42 CFR 489.28) are larger than every fee on the list.

How long does it take to get a home health license in Nebraska?

Initial onsite inspection: Department has 30 working days after receipt of a complete initial licensure application (DHHS Home Health Agencies page) Change of ownership, lease, or discontinuance: licensee must notify the Department in writing 10 days before a home health agency is sold, leased, or discontinued (DHHS Home Health Agencies page) The federal certification that follows runs on the survey calendar, and new Medicare enrollment is under a CMS moratorium to about mid-May 2027.

What do I need to start a home health agency in Nebraska?

The Home Health Agency license (health care service license under the Health Care Facility Licensure Act) (Nebraska Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS), Division of Public Health, Licensure Unit, Office of Outpatient and In-Home Care Services), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet Nebraska rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in Nebraska?

Apply to the Nebraska Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS), Division of Public Health, Licensure Unit, Office of Outpatient and In-Home Care Services under Nebraska Revised Statutes sections 71-401 to 71-479 (Health Care Facility Licensure Act), home health agency defined at section 71-417; Title 175 Nebraska Administrative Code Chapter 14 (175 NAC 14), licensing requirements at 175 NAC 14-003, fees referenced at 175 NAC 14-004.10 on the DHHS page; federal certification under 42 CFR Part 484. The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
