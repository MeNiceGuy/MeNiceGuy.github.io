How to Start a Group Home in Hawaii

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Hawaii. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Hawaii licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Hawaii licenses

Adult Residential Care Home (ARCH) Type I

Licensed by: Hawaii Department of Health, Office of Health Care Assurance (OHCA), State Licensing Section. Serves: Adults who require at least minimal assistance with activities of daily living. Type I is for residents requiring minimal assistance with ADLs such as bathing, changing, walking, eating, and getting out of bed. Total capacity 1 to 5 residents, per the OHCA ARCH Vacancy List Legend. An ARCH is defined in OHCA enforcement releases as a facility providing 24-hour living accommodations for a fee to adults unrelated to the operating family, who require at least minimal assistance in ADLs, personal care, protection, and health care services, but do not need professional health services of an intermediate, skilled nursing, or acute care facility. Hawaii law requires all ARCHs to be licensed. Regulation cited in OHCA materials is HAR 11-100.1.

Adult Residential Care Home (ARCH) Type II

Licensed by: Hawaii Department of Health, Office of Health Care Assurance (OHCA), State Licensing Section. Serves: Adults who require at least minimal assistance with activities of daily living, same ADL description as Type I. Total capacity 6 or more residents, per the OHCA ARCH Vacancy List Legend.

Expanded Adult Residential Care Home (Expanded ARCH, E-ARCH)

Licensed by: Hawaii Department of Health, Office of Health Care Assurance (OHCA), State Licensing Section. Serves: Residents who require 24-hour assistance with normal ADLs and may require skilled nursing services. Expanded ARCH capacity 1 to 5 residents, and ARCH II-Exp is the same expanded level with capacity greater than 6 residents, per the OHCA ARCH Vacancy List Legend. Listed by OHCA as a State Licensing Section facility type separate from regular ARCH.

Assisted Living Facility (ALF)

Licensed by: Hawaii Department of Health, Office of Health Care Assurance (OHCA), State Licensing Section. Serves: Residents receiving assisted living services in a licensed facility setting. Listed by OHCA as a community based facility that must be licensed by the State and is not eligible for Medicare participation as a facility type, under the State Licensing Section. OHCA survey materials cite HAR Chapter 11-90 for assisted living facilities, including sec. 11-90-8 on range of services.

Community Care Foster Family Home (CCFFH)

Licensed by: Hawaii Department of Health, Office of Health Care Assurance (OHCA), State Licensing Section, which has designated Community Ties of America (CTA) to perform the licensing and certification activities. Serves: Adults at an intermediate care facility or nursing facility level of care, in a program OHCA describes as primarily for Medicaid persons. OHCA guidance on private pay requests states the CCFFH program aim is to provide persons on Medicaid equal access to nursing level of care services in CCFFHs. Certified and regulated by OHCA State Licensing Section through CTA, per OHCA. Regulation is HAR Chapter 11-800, cited in OHCA enforcement releases for CCFFHs. OHCA guidance for allowing two private pay individuals states criteria including that the desired CCFFH has been certified and in operation for at least one year and is certified for three beds.

Developmentally Disabled Domiciliary Home (DDDH)

Licensed by: Hawaii Department of Health, Office of Health Care Assurance (OHCA), State Licensing Section. Serves: Persons with developmental disabilities, in a domiciliary home setting. Listed by OHCA as a State Licensing Section facility type. In Hawaii Developmental Disabilities Division materials, DD Domiciliary Homes are named among licensed or certified settings where individuals in the Medicaid 1915(c) I/DD Waiver needing residential habilitation services may receive them, along with Adult Foster Homes for individuals with I/DD and Adult Residential Care Homes.

Therapeutic Living Program (TLP) and Special Treatment Facility (STF)

Licensed by: Hawaii Department of Health, Office of Health Care Assurance (OHCA), State Licensing Section, with accreditation listing by the Department of Health Alcohol and Drug Abuse Division for substance abuse facilities. Serves: Persons in therapeutic living and special treatment settings, including adult and youth residential substance abuse programs in the Alcohol and Drug Abuse Division accredited list. Both TLP and STF are listed by OHCA as State Licensing Section facility types that must be licensed by the State and are not eligible for Medicare participation as facility types. The Alcohol and Drug Abuse Division list shows STF and TLP entries with OHCA license numbers, bed counts, and adult or youth residential designations.

How to start a group home in Hawaii, step by step

  1. Choose the population and the home type from the list above. The license follows the population, so this decision comes before property, staffing, or funding.
  2. Read the rule chapter for that home type and confirm the requirements with Hawaii Department of Health, Office of Health Care Assurance (OHCA), State Licensing Section before you sign a lease.
  3. Check local zoning, occupancy, and fire and life safety requirements for the specific house.
  4. Form the business, secure insurance, and prepare the home to the rule chapter's standards for bedrooms, accessibility, alarms, and life safety systems.
  5. Write the policies the license requires: admission, care planning, medication handling, incident reporting, emergencies, staffing and training, and resident rights.
  6. Apply to Hawaii Department of Health, Office of Health Care Assurance (OHCA), State Licensing Section with the application, fees, and background checks for owners and staff, and complete the home inspection and life safety survey.
  7. After the license issues, enroll as a Medicaid waiver or state program provider for the population you serve, because the license alone does not fund placements.
  8. Admit only residents whose assessed needs match your licensed level, and staff the home to those needs around the clock.

Frequently asked questions

What are the requirements to open a group home in Hawaii?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Hawaii?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
