How to Start a Group Home in California

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in California. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type California licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types California licenses

Adult Residential Facility (ARF)

Licensed by: California Department of Social Services (CDSS), Community Care Licensing Division (CCLD), Adult and Senior Care Program. Serves: Clients ages 18 to 59, or any person 60 years of age or older under specified requirements; clients may have a mental, physical or developmental disability. Provides 24-hour non-medical care and supervision. This is the main California group home type for adults with developmental disabilities when vendored through a regional center. DDS states the Home and Community-Based Services Waiver for Californians with Developmental Disabilities, a 1915(c) waiver, secures federal funding for regional center services.

Residential Care Facility for the Elderly (RCFE)

Licensed by: CDSS, CCLD, Adult and Senior Care Program (Senior Care). Serves: Persons 60 years of age and over. Housing arrangement where 24-hour non-medical care and supervision is provided. RCFEs are often referred to as assisted living facilities or board and care homes in California.

Residential Care Facility for the Elderly - Continuing Care Retirement Community (RCFE-CCRC)

Licensed by: CDSS, CCLD, Adult and Senior Care Program. Serves: Persons 60 years of age and over under a continuing care contract. Listed by CDSS Centralized Applications Bureau as a separate RCFE type. An RCFE that offers a long-term continuing care contract and provides housing, residential services and nursing care, per CDSS Title XX reporting.

Adult Residential Facility for Persons with Special Health Care Needs (ARFPSHN)

Licensed by: CDSS, CCLD, Adult and Senior Care Program. Serves: Adults with developmental disabilities who have special health care needs and intensive support needs. Provides 24-hour services for up to five adults.

Enhanced Behavioral Support Home - ARF (EBSH)

Licensed by: CDSS, CCLD, Adult and Senior Care Program. Serves: Individuals with developmental disabilities who require enhanced behavioral supports. An Adult Residential Facility that provides 24-hour nonmedical care to individuals with developmental disabilities under specified requirements.

Community Crisis Home - ARF (CCH)

Licensed by: CDSS, CCLD, Adult and Senior Care Program. Serves: Individuals with developmental disabilities receiving regional center services and in need of crisis intervention services. An Adult Residential Facility that provides 24-hour nonmedical care under specified requirements.

Social Rehabilitation Facility (SRF)

Licensed by: CDSS, CCLD, Adult and Senior Care Program. Serves: Adults recovering from a mental illness who temporarily need assistance, guidance or counseling. Provides 24-hour non-medical care and supervision in a group setting. This is a behavioral health residential type licensed by CDSS, distinct from a psychiatric hospital.

Residential Care Facility for the Chronically Ill (RCFCI)

Licensed by: CDSS, CCLD, Adult and Senior Care Program. Serves: Adults who have HIV disease or AIDS, emancipated minors with HIV disease or AIDS, family units with HIV disease or AIDS, or persons with a terminal illness. Provides care and supervision to the population listed by CDSS. CDSS overview material lists a maximum capacity of 25 for this type.

Medical Foster Home for Veterans (MFHV)

Licensed by: CDSS, CCLD, Adult and Senior Care Program, in collaboration with the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs. Serves: Veteran residents age 18 or older with chronic or medically complex health conditions. Provides 24-hour care and supervision in a private, home-like setting for up to three veteran residents, including assistance with daily living. Established under Health and Safety Code Chapter 3.15, sections 1568.21 to 1568.40, per CDSS PIN 26-04-ASC.

Group Home (children)

Licensed by: CDSS, CCLD, Childrens Residential Program. Serves: Children who are no longer able to live with their parents or guardians, for short-term or long-term care. Listed by CDSS among Childrens Residential categories with Crisis Nurseries, Small Family Homes, Foster Family Homes and Transitional Housing Placement. CDSS application instructions LIC 281 cover Group Homes under Title 22 Division 6, and require proof of registration in a 40-hour Group Home certification program with a department-issued certificate before licensure.

Small Family Home

Licensed by: CDSS, CCLD, Childrens Residential Program. Serves: Children needing residential care in a family home setting. Listed by CDSS in the LIC 281 facility license instructions and in Childrens Residential categories. Distinct from adult Small Family or adult foster models; California does not list a general adult foster care license in the CDSS adult facility types fetched.

Intermediate Care Facility for the Developmentally Disabled (ICF/DD, ICF/DD-Habilitative, ICF/DD-Nursing)

Licensed by: California Department of Public Health, Center for Health Care Quality. Serves: Persons with developmental disabilities requiring intermediate care. Licensed health facility types, not CDSS community care facilities. CDPH fee table lists ICF/DD, ICF/DD-H and ICF/DD-N at $2,066 per bed statewide for fiscal year 2026-27, plus a $900 Los Angeles County supplemental fee.

How to start a group home in California, step by step

  1. Obtain information online from the CCLD Adult and Senior Care Program Centralized Applications Bureau (CAB) page
  2. Register for and attend orientation: the application process includes a three-component orientation, Component I Application Orientation online, Component II Application Interview, and Component III Operations and Record Keeping Orientation in person after a pre-licensing inspection
  3. Download, complete and print the application package and gather all required documents, per LIC 281 Application Instructions for a Facility License
  4. Submit the application and pay the application fee
  5. Complete the criminal background check process
  6. Schedule the Component II operational readiness interview with the applicant
  7. Schedule and conduct a pre-licensing inspection and Component III session: pre-licensing visits are on site before a license is issued to ensure full compliance
  8. Final review and license issuance

California group home license fees and costs

  • Adult and Senior Care orientation fee: $50 plus a $4.85 processing fee, $54.85 total, non-refundable and non-transferable, on the CDSS Online Orientations page
  • Medical Foster Home for Veterans application fee: $88, not refundable, per CDSS PIN 26-04-ASC
  • No single initial facility license fee amount for ARF or RCFE was printed on the CDSS pages fetched in this run, so no ARF or RCFE license fee amount is stated here

How long does it take in California?

  • Online orientation access: 30 days from registration to view the course or retrieve the completed certificate, per the CDSS Online Orientations page

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to open a group home in California?

The figures California publishes are these: Adult and Senior Care orientation fee: $50 plus a $4.85 processing fee, $54.85 total, non-refundable and non-transferable, on the CDSS Online Orientations page Medical Foster Home for Veterans application fee: $88, not refundable, per CDSS PIN 26-04-ASC No single initial facility license fee amount for ARF or RCFE was printed on the CDSS pages fetched in this run, so no ARF or RCFE license fee amount is stated here The license fee is rarely the largest cost. The home itself, life safety and accessibility work, insurance, and staffing hired before the first placement carry the project.

How long does it take to get a group home license in California?

Online orientation access: 30 days from registration to view the course or retrieve the completed certificate, per the CDSS Online Orientations page

What are the requirements to open a group home in California?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in California?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
