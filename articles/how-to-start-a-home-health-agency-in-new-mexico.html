How to Start a Home Health Agency in New Mexico

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

New Mexico licenses home health agencies as a Home Health Agency license - annual license, with temporary license in the initial process, through the New Mexico Health Care Authority (HCA), Division of Health Improvement (DHI), Health Facility Licensing and Certification Bureau. The governing law is 8.370.22 NMAC, Requirements for Home Health Agencies, effective July 1, 2024, replacing repealed 7.28.2 NMAC; statutory authority Subsection E of Section 9-8-6 NMSA 1978 and Sections 24-1-2(D), 24-1-3(I) and 24-1-5 of the Public Health Act NMSA 1978; fees at 8.370.3 NMAC. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the New Mexico license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The New Mexico license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in New Mexico, step by step

  1. Submit a letter of intent to facility.license@hca.nm.gov with a valid email and designated contact person, per the HCA How to License a Health Care Facility page and 8.370.22.10 NMAC, which lists letter of intent as the first document for initial licensure
  2. Write a program narrative including characteristics of the population to be served, services and care to be provided, and anticipated contract services
  3. Once the letter of intent is approved, an application packet is emailed to the contact specified; the packet and process vary by facility type
  4. Submit the application for initial license with the required nonrefundable fee and a functional program outline, per 8.370.22.10 NMAC
  5. Submit annotated policy and procedure manuals with a crosswalk to the rules to the licensing department
  6. Obtain Board of Pharmacy approval before licensure if the facility will handle drugs, per the HCA licensing page
  7. Request the initial health survey in writing when in full operation; upon successful completion of the initial health survey, an annual license is issued, per the HCA licensing page general steps

New Mexico home health license fees and costs

  • Home health agency: $300 per license under 8.370.3.10 NMAC
  • Amended license: $300 under 8.370.3.11 NMAC

How long does it take in New Mexico?

  • Application, policy and procedure review and plan review are good for one year, beginning from the date of receipt of the application fee; at the end of that year, if a license has not been issued, the provider may be required to submit a new application and begin from the start, per the HCA licensing page
  • Temporary license period in the Public Health Act text reproduced in legislative material: a temporary license shall not be issued for a period exceeding 120 days, nor more than two consecutive temporary licenses
  • Annual license is a one year license, per the definitions in 8.370.3 NMAC

What catches New Mexico applicants

  • State licensure is required for home health agencies: 8.370.22.2 NMAC applies to public, profit or nonprofit home health agencies and to any facility that by federal regulation must be licensed by the state of New Mexico to obtain or maintain federal funding
  • Life safety codes apply to all facility types except for home health agencies and home health hospice agencies, per the note on the HCA licensing page, so the general building and life safety steps on that page do not apply to home health
  • No certificate of need for home health was identified in the official New Mexico sources reviewed
  • Regulation transferred from the Department of Health to the Health Care Authority effective July 1, 2024, when 7.28.2 NMAC was repealed and replaced by 8.370.22 NMAC

The Medicare layer, after the New Mexico license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a home health agency in New Mexico?

The figures New Mexico publishes are these: Home health agency: $300 per license under 8.370.3.10 NMAC Amended license: $300 under 8.370.3.11 NMAC Beyond the published figures, the operating costs (clinical payroll before certification, insurance, and the reserve operating funds required by 42 CFR 489.28) are larger than every fee on the list.

How long does it take to get a home health license in New Mexico?

Application, policy and procedure review and plan review are good for one year, beginning from the date of receipt of the application fee; at the end of that year, if a license has not been issued, the provider may be required to submit a new application and begin from the start, per the HCA licensing page Temporary license period in the Public Health Act text reproduced in legislative material: a temporary license shall not be issued for a period exceeding 120 days, nor more than two consecutive temporary licenses Annual license is a one year license, per the definitions in 8.370.3 NMAC The federal certification that follows runs on the survey calendar, and new Medicare enrollment is under a CMS moratorium to about mid-May 2027.

What do I need to start a home health agency in New Mexico?

The Home Health Agency license - annual license, with temporary license in the initial process (New Mexico Health Care Authority (HCA), Division of Health Improvement (DHI), Health Facility Licensing and Certification Bureau), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet New Mexico rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in New Mexico?

Apply to the New Mexico Health Care Authority (HCA), Division of Health Improvement (DHI), Health Facility Licensing and Certification Bureau under 8.370.22 NMAC, Requirements for Home Health Agencies, effective July 1, 2024, replacing repealed 7.28.2 NMAC; statutory authority Subsection E of Section 9-8-6 NMSA 1978 and Sections 24-1-2(D), 24-1-3(I) and 24-1-5 of the Public Health Act NMSA 1978; fees at 8.370.3 NMAC. The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
