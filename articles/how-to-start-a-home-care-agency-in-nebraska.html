How to Start a Home Care Agency in Nebraska (No License for Non-Medical Care, a $650 License for Skilled Home Health)

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 19, 2026

The Nebraska story starts with a founder who did the opposite of waiting. She had her service menu set, companionship, homemaker help, and hands-on personal care for families around Omaha, and she went looking for the license application so she could file it and start. The search itself was the lesson. Nebraska has no license for the agency she described. There is no application to find, no fee to pay, and no department queue between a prepared non-medical founder and a first private pay client. What Nebraska does have is a hard line one step over: the moment the menu turns skilled, a real license with a real fee and real inspections controls the work, and a second licensed door covers hospice. In the agency records I review, Nebraska mistakes are almost never about effort. They are about doors, an owner budgeting the skilled license fee for a business that needs no license, or selling skilled help on a door that carries none. Here is the full picture, lane by lane.

The demand: about 369,000 seniors, close to one in five residents

Start with why owners keep looking at Nebraska. The Census Bureau puts the state population at 2,018,006 as of July 1, 2025, and 18.3 percent of residents are age 65 or older. Run the math and you get about 369,000 seniors. The same Census QuickFacts page for Nebraska puts median household income at $76,475 for 2020 to 2024, which is the income base a private pay agency sells into. The demand is spread across a large, mostly rural state, so the practical question for a Nebraska founder is less whether seniors exist and more which payer door, private pay, State Plan personal assistance, or a waiver program, the first clients will actually arrive through.

The license question, answered directly: non-medical care has no Nebraska license, skilled care and hospice do

Do you need a license to start a non-medical home care agency in Nebraska? No state license exists for that work. The Nebraska Department of Health and Human Services, Division of Public Health, Licensure Unit, licenses health care services under the Health Care Facility Licensure Act, Neb. Rev. Stat. 71-401 to 71-479.002, and an agency that provides only attendant, companion, and homemaker services sits outside that license list. The In-Home Personal Services Act, Neb. Rev. Stat. 71-6501 to 71-6504, covers that door with worker duties and creates no license. Two licensed doors sit beside it. A home health agency, defined at section 71-415 as an agency primarily engaged in skilled nursing care or a minimum of one other therapeutic service, holds the license at 175 NAC 14. Hospice is its own licensed health care service under 175 NAC 16. No one door covers another door's services, and naming them correctly is how a Nebraska founder stays out of trouble.

One state gate sits on the payer side, and it is current. DHHS Provider Bulletin 26-17, dated October 1, 2026, imposed a temporary moratorium on enrollment of new Home and Community-Based Services 1915(c) waiver agency providers for six months under 42 CFR 455.470, covering the waivers the Division of Disability and Aging manages: the Family Support Waiver, the Developmental Disabilities Adult Day and Comprehensive Developmental Disabilities waivers, the Aged and Disabled Waiver, and the Traumatic Brain Injury Waiver. It pauses new agency providers and new locations of current agency providers. It does not apply to individual providers, and it does not pause licensure. A founder can hold a license and still be sequenced around that enrollment window, which is exactly why the door and the payer calendar have to be planned separately.

The non-medical lane: no license, a worker record, and a Medicaid enrollment door

The non-medical build is short on filings and long on records. No DHHS facility application is filed for this door. What the In-Home Personal Services Act requires is the screening file: under sections 71-6502 and 71-6503 the agency runs a criminal history record check, a driving record check, and Adult Protective Services and Child Protective Services registry checks on every in-home personal services worker, and holds the worker qualification record the act requires. Build that file before the first visit, because it is the proof of seriousness a family or a referral source can be shown in a state that issues no license for the work.

The Medicaid door on this lane is enrollment, not licensure. Personal Assistance Services under the State Plan at 471 NAC 15 and waiver services through the Division of Disability and Aging run through Maximus, the state screening and enrollment contractor, in the Provider Data Management System, and paper applications ended June 1, 2025. Sequence any new 1915(c) waiver agency plan against the Bulletin 26-17 window described above; State Plan Personal Assistance Services is not a 1915(c) waiver door. Electronic visit verification attaches to Personal Assistance Services and waiver in-home services through Netsmart Mobile Caregiver+, formerly Tellus, the state provided system and aggregator, or an approved alternate vendor feeding the aggregator, and developmental disabilities waiver visits are recorded in Therap or an approved alternate. There is no Medicare door on this lane at all.

The skilled lane: the home health agency license under 175 NAC 14 at $650

The moment your plan includes skilled nursing or therapy, you are on the licensed door. Under 175 NAC 14, rewritten effective May 5, 2024, the agency must be primarily engaged in providing skilled nursing care or a minimum of one other therapeutic service, physical therapy, speech pathology, occupational therapy, respiratory care, home health aide services, social work, intravenous therapy, or dialysis, in approved counties only, and the agency may not operate without an active license. A state license must issue before Medicare or Medicaid certification.

The sequence is the license itself. There is no certificate of need gate in front of it: Nebraska certificate of need law at Neb. Rev. Stat. 71-5801 and following reaches long-term care beds and rehabilitation beds only. You form the business and settle the service area and governing body structure, build the 175 NAC 14 file, the administrator record, the clinical leadership and registered nurse supervision record, the policy register, the personnel file system, and the plan of treatment process, then file the DHHS application with the initial fee of $650 at 175 NAC 14-003.01 and complete the licensure inspections at 175 NAC 14-005. Renewal fees are tiered by unduplicated consumer admissions in the past year: $650 for 1 to 50, $850 for 51 to 200, and $950 for 201 or more under 175 NAC 14-003.02. The $1,000 figure at Neb. Rev. Stat. 71-434 is the statutory cap on the variable part of the fee, not a fee an applicant pays. After licensure, Medicaid runs through Maximus PDMS enrollment, Heritage Health contracting, and EVV configuration for home health visits, with capture required from April 1, 2024 and claims through the Netsmart system from May 1, 2024.

Medicare certification layers on the license for owners who want that payer. The federal layer runs on Form CMS-855A through PECOS with the CY2026 institutional enrollment fee of $750, to CGS Administrators, Jurisdiction 15, the Home Health and Hospice MAC for Nebraska; the A/B MAC is Wisconsin Physicians Service Government Health Administrators, Jurisdiction 5. Initial Medicare enrollment for home health agencies is gated by the federal moratorium extended and modified effective November 12, 2026 under CMS-6101-N2, with no end date printed in the notice (the extension is a six-month period, running to about May 12, 2027). That federal gate belongs to the certified track only. It does not pause Nebraska licensure, and it does not govern the state Medicaid doors.

The hospice lane and the residential doors beside it

Hospice in Nebraska is two doors, not one. The state door is the hospice license under 175 NAC 16 from the DHHS Licensure Unit. The DHHS Hospice Service Licensure Application prints the initial fees: $450 for other than inpatient hospice and $650 for inpatient hospice, and the license expires June 30 of each year under 175 NAC 16-005.02. The federal door is Medicare certification under 42 CFR Part 418, with initial hospice enrollment gated by the federal moratorium under CMS-6102-N2 on the same extended frame as the home health gate. Holding one door without the other leaves a program unable to operate or unable to bill, and the Medicaid hospice benefit sits at 471 NAC 36.

The residential doors in the Nebraska packet sit in the all-homes tracks and carry their own credentials. Developmental disabilities residential habilitation runs on DHHS agency provider certification under 404 NAC through the Division of Disability and Aging, with an initial certification of six months and a two year certification after a successful review, and no certification fee published. Adult day services hold a DHHS license under 175 NAC 5, priced by capacity at 175 NAC 5 003.02: $200 for capacity 4 to 16, $250 for 17 to 50, and $300 for 51 and up. Assisted living is a residential facility license under 175 NAC 4. None of those credentials authorizes in-home agency work, and no in-home agency needs them.

Background checks and the Heritage Health roster: the requirements that decide readiness

Screening in Nebraska is door specific, and the file has to match the door. On the non-medical door, the Act's three checks, criminal history, driving record, and the Adult Protective Services and Child Protective Services registries, run on every worker before the first visit. On the home health door, aide competency is documented in the personnel file, home health aide services run under registered nurse supervision, and the Nebraska Nurse Aide Registry checks join the registry checks before the first visit, not after a survey finding. Across every door, the pattern I tell owners is the same: run the checks at hire and on a set rhythm after that, and keep the documentation where it can be produced. Registry and exclusion checks run once and never again are one of the deficiencies the Nebraska packet names.

The managed care roster matters because contracting follows enrollment. Heritage Health runs through three organizations: Nebraska Total Care, Molina Healthcare of Nebraska, and UnitedHealthcare Community Plan of Nebraska. Get the history right, because the trap on this point was printed backwards in older materials: Molina Healthcare of Nebraska started in Heritage Health on January 1, 2024, and Healthy Blue is the plan that exited, effective December 31, 2023. State Plan personal care and 1915(c) waiver services bill the Department fee for service; home health and hospice for Heritage Health members are plan claims. Contracting with a plan is separate from Medicaid enrollment, and enrollment alone puts an agency in no plan network.

What you still do today: form the business the Nebraska way

Whatever the door, the business load comes first. Form the entity with the Nebraska Secretary of State, get the EIN and the tax registrations, and put the operating accounts and liability insurance in place before the first client. Then stand up the parts a surveyor or a family will actually test: written policies and procedures covering the services you will sell, the screening and training systems for the door you are on, the service agreement that states plainly what the agency does and does not do, with skilled and medical tasks on the does not do side for a non-medical agency, and the EVV configuration for any service a payer covers. On the licensed doors, hold services until the license issues. On the no license door, the screening file, the insurance certificate, and the policies are the credential, so build them like one.

The practical warning

Three Nebraska traps cost new owners money. The first is scope drift in both directions: skilled services marketed or delivered on the no license non-medical door are the most expensive scope error in Nebraska home-based care, and budgeting the $650 home health fee for a non-medical agency is the same confusion with the money flowing the other way. The second trap is the assisted living name: an assisted living facility license under 175 NAC 4 is a residential credential, it authorizes no in-home work, and an in-home agency presented as licensed when no license covers the service has a record problem, not a marketing line. The third trap is sequencing a waiver agency build as if enrollment were open, against the Provider Bulletin 26-17 window. Add the stale plan list to the list: a Heritage Health plan built on Healthy Blue, the plan that left on December 31, 2023, is a contract plan built on a roster that no longer exists. Doors, fees, rosters, and windows: in Nebraska all four are printed somewhere primary, and all four punish guessing.

How much does a home care license cost in Nebraska?

The home health agency license costs $650 at initial licensure under 175 NAC 14-003.01, with renewals tiered by unduplicated consumer admissions at $650, $850, and $950. Do I need a license for non-medical home care in Nebraska?

Your next step

Work in door order. Decide first which door your first clients sit behind, non-medical care with no license, the home health agency license under 175 NAC 14 at $650, or the hospice license under 175 NAC 16 at $450, or $650 for inpatient hospice, because that one decision determines whether you file nothing with DHHS, file a license application and pass inspections, or run a two door hospice build. Then sequence the payer side separately: Maximus PDMS enrollment for Medicaid work, the Bulletin 26-17 six month waiver agency window that runs from October 1, 2026, Heritage Health contracting with Nebraska Total Care, Molina Healthcare of Nebraska, and UnitedHealthcare Community Plan of Nebraska, and EVV through Netsmart Mobile Caregiver+ from the first billed visit.
