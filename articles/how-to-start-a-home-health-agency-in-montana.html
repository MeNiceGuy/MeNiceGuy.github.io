How to Start a Home Health Agency in Montana

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Montana licenses home health agencies as a Home Health Agency license, issued as a health care facility license. A facility may not accept patients until it is licensed, and DPHHS first issues a six (6) month provisional license after approval of the submitted documentation., through the Montana Department of Public Health and Human Services (DPHHS), Office of Inspector General, Licensure Bureau. DPHHS letters and rules formerly cited this office as the Quality Assurance Division, Licensure Bureau.. The governing law is Montana Code Annotated Title 50, chapter 5, parts 1 through 3, including MCA 50-5-101 (definitions, including home health agency and health care facility), MCA 50-5-201 (license required, license term of 1 to 3 years), MCA 50-5-202 (license fees) and MCA 50-5-203 (application for license, at least 45 days before opening, with attestation). Administrative Rules of Montana Title 37, chapter 106, subchapter 3 sets minimum standards for all health care facilities, and DPHHS links home health agency rules from its Home Health Agency application page.. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the Montana license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The Montana license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Montana, step by step

  1. Submit a completed license application and fee to the DPHHS Licensure Bureau, using the application linked from the DPHHS Home Health Agency page. Do not submit an application earlier than 6 months prior to the desired licensure date.
  2. Submit a completed BFS Plan Submittal Form to the Licensure Bureau Construction Consultant, as directed in the DPHHS prospective provider letter.
  3. Submit policies and procedures for review and approval at least 45 days prior to the expected facility opening date.
  4. Submit a list of counties in which the home health agency plans to serve.
  5. Submit a list of all professional staff, including license numbers.
  6. Submit an attestation statement from the prospective administrator stating that he or she has reviewed the rules pertaining to home health agencies.
  7. After submission and approval of the information and documentation, the Licensure Bureau issues a six (6) month provisional license.
  8. A health care facility surveyor from the Licensure Bureau conducts an on-site survey of the facility within the provisional license period to assess compliance with home health agency regulations.

Montana home health license fees and costs

  • MCA 50-5-202: $20 for facilities with 20 beds or less
  • MCA 50-5-202: $1 per bed for facilities with 21 beds or more
  • The DPHHS prospective home health agency letter requires a completed license application and fee but does not print a separate home health agency fee amount in the letter itself

How long does it take in Montana?

  • Do not submit an application earlier than 6 months prior to the desired licensure date (DPHHS prospective home health agency letter)
  • Application at least 45 days prior to the opening of a facility (MCA 50-5-203)
  • Policies and procedures at least 45 days prior to the expected facility opening date (DPHHS prospective home health agency letter)
  • Six (6) month provisional license issued after approval of submitted documentation, with an on-site survey within the provisional period (DPHHS prospective home health agency letter)
  • Health care facility licenses may be issued for a period of 1 to 3 years (MCA 50-5-201 and ARM 37.106.310)

What catches Montana applicants

  • Certificate of need: the current DPHHS prospective home health agency letter (2025 version) states home health agencies are not required to be reviewed by the Health Planning Program and therefore a Certificate of Need (CON) review is not required. An older DPHHS letter on file (2021 version) stated a CON was required before pursuing licensure through October 1, 2021, so the 2021 statement should not be used as the current rule.
  • Definition: under MCA 50-5-101, a home health agency is a public agency or private organization, or a subdivision of one, engaged in providing home health services to individuals in the places where they live. Home health services must include the services of a licensed registered nurse and at least one other therapeutic service and may include additional support services.
  • Home health agencies are listed as health care facilities in MCA 50-5-101, and MCA 50-5-201 provides that a person may not operate a health care facility unless the facility is licensed by the department. The license is valid only for the person and premises for which it was issued and may not be sold, assigned, or transferred.
  • The health care facility list in MCA 50-5-101 and the in-home facility types on the DPHHS Licensure Bureau application pages (home health agency, home infusion therapy agency, hospice) contain no separate license category for a standalone non-medical personal care, companion, or homemaker agency.
  • Licensure and certification are separate in the DPHHS letter: the letter covers licensing only, and applicants seeking certification information are directed to the DPHHS Certification Bureau.
  • A facility may not accept patients until it is licensed (DPHHS prospective home health agency letter).

The Medicare layer, after the Montana license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.
