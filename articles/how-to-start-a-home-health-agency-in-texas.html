How to Start a Home Health Agency in Texas

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Texas licenses home health agencies as a Home and Community Support Services Agency (HCSSA) license, in the Licensed Home Health Services or Licensed and Certified Home Health category for skilled and Medicare-certified work; Personal Assistance Services (PAS) is the non-medical category only, through the Texas Health and Human Services Commission (HHSC). The governing law is Texas Health and Safety Code Chapter 142; 26 TAC Chapter 558, Licensing Standards for Home and Community Support Services Agencies. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the Texas license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The Texas license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Texas, step by step

  1. Write the service list first and check it against the PAS definition and the skilled trigger list; skilled services such as nursing, therapies, IV therapy, dialysis or furnishing medical equipment push the agency into Licensed Home Health Services, not PAS
  2. Designate an administrator and an alternate administrator who meet the qualifications in 26 TAC Chapter 558; for a PAS only agency HHSC states a high school diploma or GED with at least one year of experience or training in caring for individuals with functional disabilities, or two years of full time study at an accredited college or university in a health related field
  3. Both the administrator and the alternate administrator complete the HHSC presurvey computer based training before filing; packets are not accepted without the signed participation certificates
  4. Complete the application, Form 2021 for a parent agency, and submit everything through TULIP, the Texas Unified Licensure Information Portal, with all documents and the license fee, and confirm the portal shows payment received
  5. Once the initial license issues, begin services
  6. Enroll the first client, then file Form 2020, Notification of Readiness for Initial Survey, with the regional office no later than six months after the license effective date
  7. Prepare for and complete the initial survey, which reaches client records, policies, personnel files, and complaint and quality processes
  8. Enroll separately for Texas Medicaid through the Provider Enrollment and Management System (PEMS) through TMHP; licensure does not enroll the agency

Texas home health license fees and costs

  • HCSSA initial licensing fee for a three year license: $2,625 for a parent agency or branch and $1,500 for an alternate delivery site, as published by HHSC in its presurvey training and license fees page cited in the article
  • HCSSA renewal for a three year license: $2,625 for a parent agency or branch and $900 for an alternate delivery site
  • Reporting most changes carries a $30 standard fee; every license fee is non-refundable
  • Late fee for a three year parent agency license when renewal is not filed at least 45 days before expiration: $1,312.50, as stated in the article
  • Federal institutional enrollment fee: $750, calendar year 2026 (CMS)

How long does it take in Texas?

  • HHSC has up to 45 days to process a thoroughly complete application under 26 TAC 558.31
  • If the application is incomplete, HHSC notifies the applicant and the applicant has 30 days to respond with complete and correct information or the application is denied; after that response the license is issued or denied within 45 days
  • The HCSSA license is good for three years from the date of issuance; renewal has to be filed at least 45 days before expiration to avoid a late fee
  • Form 2020 readiness for initial survey must be filed no later than six months after the license effective date
  • A first time administrator completes 8 clock hours of training in agency administration before designation and 16 more within the first 12 months under 26 TAC 558.259

What catches Texas applicants

  • Category split: a PAS only agency is not required to have a supervising nurse and PAS cannot become Medicare certified; only the Licensed Home Health, Licensed and Certified Home Health, and hospice categories can
  • Providing personal assistance services without a license, or using the words personal assistance services to advertise unlicensed work, is a violation of Texas law, as stated in the article
  • Texas Medicaid enrollment is a separate application through PEMS, and electronic visit verification (EVV) applies to Medicaid personal care services, not to private pay work
  • HCS is not a license and is distinct from HCSSA licensure and from assisted living licensure: HCS is a Medicaid waiver contract under 26 TAC Chapter 52 plus PEMS enrollment, and no HHSC license issues for an HCS residence
  • Criminal history checks run under Texas Health and Safety Code Chapter 250; unlicensed staff, volunteers and contractors with face to face client contact are checked against the Employee Misconduct Registry and the Nurse Aide Registry before hire and annually
  • Medicare MAC for Texas cited in the research for nursing facilities: Novitas Solutions, Jurisdiction H; CMS-855A through PECOS for certified providers

The Medicare layer, after the Texas license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a home health agency in Texas?

The figures Texas publishes are these: HCSSA initial licensing fee for a three year license: $2,625 for a parent agency or branch and $1,500 for an alternate delivery site, as published by HHSC in its presurvey training and license fees page cited in the article HCSSA renewal for a three year license: $2,625 for a parent agency or branch and $900 for an alternate delivery site Reporting most changes carries a $30 standard fee; every license fee is non-refundable Late fee for a three year parent agency license when renewal is not filed at least 45 days before expiration: $1,312.50, as stated in the article Federal institutional enrollment fee: $750, calendar year 2026 (CMS) Beyond the published figures, the operating costs (clinical payroll before certification, insurance, and the reserve operating funds required by 42 CFR 489.28) are larger than every fee on the list.

How long does it take to get a home health license in Texas?

HHSC has up to 45 days to process a thoroughly complete application under 26 TAC 558.31 If the application is incomplete, HHSC notifies the applicant and the applicant has 30 days to respond with complete and correct information or the application is denied; after that response the license is issued or denied within 45 days The HCSSA license is good for three years from the date of issuance; renewal has to be filed at least 45 days before expiration to avoid a late fee Form 2020 readiness for initial survey must be filed no later than six months after the license effective date A first time administrator completes 8 clock hours of training in agency administration before designation and 16 more within the first 12 months under 26 TAC 558.259 The federal certification that follows runs on the survey calendar, and new Medicare enrollment is under a CMS moratorium to about mid-May 2027.

What do I need to start a home health agency in Texas?

The Home and Community Support Services Agency (HCSSA) license, in the Licensed Home Health Services or Licensed and Certified Home Health category for skilled and Medicare-certified work; Personal Assistance Services (PAS) is the non-medical category only (Texas Health and Human Services Commission (HHSC)), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet Texas rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in Texas?

Apply to the Texas Health and Human Services Commission (HHSC) under Texas Health and Safety Code Chapter 142; 26 TAC Chapter 558, Licensing Standards for Home and Community Support Services Agencies. The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
