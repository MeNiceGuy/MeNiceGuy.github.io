How to Start a Home Health Agency (State License and Medicare Certification, Step by Step)

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Starting a home health agency means clearing two gates, not one. Your state licenses the agency, and the federal government certifies it for Medicare. The owners who get stuck are almost never stopped by the work itself. They are stopped by the order: they spend on the federal side before the state side is real, or they build a non-medical agency and discover late that it can never bill Medicare. This page walks the whole path in the order the regulators run it, and each state page in this guide then gives you your state license, fee, and clock in its own numbers.

First, the current status you have to plan around

On May 13, 2026, CMS imposed a nationwide moratorium on new Medicare enrollment for home health agencies and hospices. On November 12, 2026, CMS extended it, and it currently runs to about mid-May 2027, with the option to extend again in six month steps. While it runs, a brand new agency cannot complete Medicare enrollment, no matter how ready it is. What still moves: the state license, the policies, the staffing, the building, and the patient census you will need the day enrollment reopens. What I tell owners is simple. Do the state and readiness work now, in the right order, and spend like the federal gate is closed until CMS says otherwise, because today it is.

What a home health agency is, in Medicare's definition

A home health agency provides skilled care in the home. Federal rules at 42 CFR Part 484, the Conditions of Participation, set the floor, and the service definition requires skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service: physical therapy, speech language pathology, occupational therapy, medical social services, or home health aide services. That skilled core is what separates home health from home care. Home care is non-medical help with daily living, licensed state by state under its own rules, and it sits in a different track entirely. If you are still deciding between the two, read Home Care vs Home Health Agency: Which License Do You Actually Need before you file anything.

The steps to start a home health agency, in order

  1. Confirm the license and any need test in your state. Most states license home health agencies through the department of health. Some states, North Carolina and New York among them, run a certificate of need or public need process that decides whether a new agency may exist at all. Your state page names the authority and the rule.
  2. Form the business. Entity, EIN, registered agent, and the NPI for the agency. Ownership has to be disclosed in full on the federal application later, so set the ownership up cleanly now.
  3. Build the operation to the Conditions of Participation. Written policies, patient rights, care planning, quality assessment and performance improvement, infection control, and the clinical record system, all written to 42 CFR Part 484 before a surveyor ever arrives. Set up OASIS data transmission, because CMS expects a new agency to demonstrate it before the initial survey.
  4. Hire the leadership your state requires. An administrator and clinical leadership, in most states a registered nurse supervising skilled services, who meet the state qualifications on paper, not just in practice.
  5. Earn the state license. File the state application, pay the state fee, and clear any state survey or review the license requires. The state pages in this guide carry each state fee and clock.
  6. Open and serve patients. This surprises people: before the federal initial survey, your agency must already be operating. CMS survey guidance requires a new agency to have provided skilled care to a minimum of 10 patients, with at least 7 of them receiving skilled care at the time of the survey. Those patients do not have to be Medicare patients. In a medically underserved area, CMS can reduce the minimum to 5 patients with 2 active.
  7. File the Medicare enrollment application. Form CMS-855A, filed through PECOS, with the enrollment application fee for institutional providers, $750 for calendar year 2026. Your Medicare Administrative Contractor reviews and approves the application before any survey is scheduled. While the moratorium runs, this is the step that waits.
  8. Choose your survey route. Either the state survey agency surveys you for certification, or you earn accreditation from an accrediting organization with CMS deeming authority for home health: ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission. Accreditation costs more and usually moves faster; the state route costs less and moves on the state calendar. Both test the same Conditions of Participation.
  9. Pass the initial certification survey. The survey reviews all Conditions of Participation against your real operation: real charts, real staff files, real patients. The participation effective date is set by the survey outcome under 42 CFR 489.13.
  10. Show the reserve, sign the agreement, then enroll with the other payers. Federal rules at 42 CFR 489.28 require a new home health agency to show reserve operating funds for its first months of operation. After certification comes the Medicare provider agreement, then Medicaid enrollment and managed care credentialing in your state, each a separate application.

Home health agency requirements, the short list

  • State home health license, and a certificate of need or need approval first where your state requires one.
  • Skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, delivered under the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484.
  • An administrator and clinical leadership who meet state qualifications, documented.
  • Policies, quality program, infection control, emergency preparedness, and patient rights processes that match how the agency actually runs.
  • OASIS transmission capability demonstrated before the initial survey.
  • A census of 10 skilled patients served and 7 active before the initial certification survey (5 and 2 in qualifying underserved areas).
  • An approved Form CMS-855A with the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), and reserve operating funds under 42 CFR 489.28.

How long does it take to start a home health agency?

There is no honest national number, because the state license clock is different in every state and the federal survey calendar is not yours to control. What is true everywhere: the state license takes months, the census requirement means you operate before you certify, and the current CMS moratorium freezes the federal finish line to about mid-May 2027 at the earliest. Owners who plan on a year or more from first filing to first Medicare payment are planning honestly. Owners who plan on a quarter are planning on being disappointed.

How much does it cost to start a home health agency?

The published federal figure is small: $750, the 2026 Medicare enrollment application fee for institutional providers. Everything around it is larger and state specific: the state license fee, accreditation fees if you choose that route, clinical payroll carried before the first payment arrives, insurance, and the reserve funds federal rules require. The full walk-through, with the verified figures and the categories owners underbudget, is on How Much Does It Cost to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How do I start a home health agency?

In order: confirm your state licenses home health agencies and whether a certificate of need applies, form the business, build your policies to the Medicare Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, earn the state license, open and serve your first skilled patients, file the Medicare enrollment application (Form CMS-855A) through PECOS, pass an initial certification survey, and only then bill Medicare. Right now one gate is paused: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The state license and readiness work can still move; the federal enrollment cannot complete while the moratorium runs.

What do I need to start a home health agency?

A state home health license where your state requires one, an administrator and clinical leadership who meet your state rules, written policies that satisfy the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service (physical therapy, speech language pathology, occupational therapy, medical social services, or home health aide services), the ability to transmit OASIS data, reserve operating funds under 42 CFR 489.28, and, before the initial survey, a census of at least 10 skilled patients served with at least 7 receiving care at survey time.

How long does it take to start a home health agency?

The state license alone takes months in most states, and the clock is printed on each state page in this guide. The federal side adds the CMS-855A review, survey scheduling, and the survey itself, and while the CMS moratorium on new home health enrollment runs (currently to about mid-May 2027), no new agency can finish the federal step at all. Owners who plan honestly budget the better part of a year or more from filing to first Medicare payment.

How much does it cost to start a home health agency?

The fixed federal figure is the Medicare enrollment application fee for institutional providers, $750 for calendar year 2026. State license fees are set by each state and printed on the state pages here. The larger costs are operating ones: clinical staff hired before revenue arrives, accreditation or survey readiness, insurance, and the reserve operating funds federal rules require a new agency to show. The cost page in this guide walks each category with the verified figures.

How do I get a home health license?

You apply to your state licensing authority, usually the department of health, under your state home health statute. Some states run a certificate of need or need test before the license application is even accepted. Your state page in this guide names the authority, the license, the fee the state publishes, and the application sequence in order.

Can a home care agency become Medicare certified?

Only if it holds the skilled license category. A non-medical home care license, no matter how clean, cannot become Medicare certified, because certification requires skilled nursing and therapy services under the Conditions of Participation. Owners who want Medicare work add the home health license track; owners who want private pay work stay in the home care track. The comparison page in this guide draws the line in full.

What is the home health agency startup checklist?

Confirm the license category and any need test. Form the entity and get the EIN and NPI. Write policies to 42 CFR Part 484. Hire the administrator and clinical supervisor your state requires. Apply for the state license. Open, admit, and build to 10 skilled patients with 7 active. File Form CMS-855A in PECOS and pay the enrollment fee. Choose the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority. Pass the initial survey. Enroll with Medicaid and the managed care plans in your state.
