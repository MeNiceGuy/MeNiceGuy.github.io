How to Start a Group Home in North Carolina

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in North Carolina. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type North Carolina licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types North Carolina licenses

Adult care home, 7 or more beds

Licensed by: DHSR Adult Care Licensure Section; G.S. Chapter 131D, 10A NCAC 13F. Serves: Adults needing residential care in a home of 7 or more beds. Initial license period six months; special care unit for Alzheimer and dementia care sits inside this track under 10A NCAC 13F .1300 with disclosure under G.S. 131D-4.6; multiunit assisted housing with services under G.S. 131D is a registration, not a license

Family care home, 2 to 6 beds

Licensed by: DHSR Adult Care Licensure Section; G.S. Chapter 131D, 10A NCAC 13G; county DSS adult home specialist door. Serves: Adults in a small residential home of 2 to 6 beds. Small home counterpart to the adult care home track

Nursing home / nursing facility, including combination homes

Licensed by: DHSR Nursing Home Licensure and Certification Section; G.S. 131E-100 to 131E-102, 10A NCAC 13D. Serves: Residents needing skilled nursing facility care. Federal layer in full: SNF (Medicare) and NF (Medicaid) paths under 42 CFR Part 483 Subpart B and Part 488, CMS-855A and CMS-1561; certification is by state survey, no accreditation deeming route stated

Hospice residential facility

Licensed by: DHSR; G.S. 131E-201(5a), 10A NCAC 13K .1100. Serves: Hospice patients in a residential setting. Beds ride the hospice agency license; no separate facility fee captured

Hospice inpatient facility or unit

Licensed by: DHSR; G.S. 131E-201(3a), 10A NCAC 13K .1200. Serves: Hospice patients needing inpatient level care. Units in hospitals meet 10A NCAC 13B and units in nursing facilities meet 10A NCAC 13D, each with the exceptions .1201 lists; federal hospice Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 418 govern

ICF/IID (community)

Licensed by: DHSR Mental Health Licensure and Certification Section; G.S. Chapter 122C, categories at 10A NCAC 27G .2100, .5600B or .5600C. Serves: Individuals with intellectual or developmental disabilities. CON required; Medicaid only, no Medicare ICF/IID benefit or enrollment path; federal certification under 42 CFR Part 483 Subpart I and Part 442, surveyed by the state

Supervised living, 10A NCAC 27G .5600A

Licensed by: DHSR MHLCS; G.S. Chapter 122C, 10A NCAC 27G .5600. Serves: Adults with mental illness. One of six supervised living categories .5600A to F sharing the 122C license; LME/MCO letter of support required under G.S. 122C-23.1 and 27G .0406

Supervised living, 10A NCAC 27G .5600B

Licensed by: DHSR MHLCS; G.S. Chapter 122C, 10A NCAC 27G .5600. Serves: Minors with developmental disabilities. Also the ICF/IID door where ICF/IID certified

Supervised living, 10A NCAC 27G .5600C

Licensed by: DHSR MHLCS; G.S. Chapter 122C, 10A NCAC 27G .5600. Serves: Adults with developmental disabilities. Also the ICF/IID door where ICF/IID certified; NC Innovations Residential Supports Levels 1 to 4 are delivered in licensed .5600 homes under the Tailored Plan contract

Supervised living, 10A NCAC 27G .5600D and .5600E

Licensed by: DHSR MHLCS; G.S. Chapter 122C, 10A NCAC 27G .5600. Serves: Minors (.5600D) and adults (.5600E) with substance use disorders. Substance use supervised living categories inside the supervised-living track

Alternative Family Living, 10A NCAC 27G .5600F

Licensed by: DHSR MHLCS; G.S. Chapter 122C, 10A NCAC 27G .5600. Serves: Individuals living in a family setting under the supervised living system. Category register inside supervised-living; the NC Innovations Alternative Family Living service is its payer door

Behavioral health residential categories under 10A NCAC 27G

Licensed by: DHSR MHLCS; G.S. Chapter 122C. Serves: Children, adolescents and adults by category. One track with a per-category register because every category shares the 122C license, the MHLCS application packet, the letter of support rule and the G.S. 131E-272 fee schedule: .1300, .1700 and .1800 child residential treatment levels; .1900 PRTF; .2100 specialized community residential centers; .3100 nonhospital medical detox; .3200 social setting detox; .3400 substance abuse residential treatment; .4100 residential recovery for mothers and children; .4300 therapeutic community; .5000 facility based crisis; .5100 community respite; .5200 residential therapeutic camps

Adult day care program and adult day health program

Licensed by: NC Division of Aging certification, county DSS supervising agency; G.S. 131D-6, 10A NCAC 06R and 06S. Serves: Adults in a certified day program, including combination programs. Certification, not a residential license; adult day health Medicaid payment under CAP/DA Clinical Coverage Policy 3K-2; adult day overnight respite under G.S. 131D-6A is a certification layer on a certified program; certificate runs 12 months and no certification fee figure is stated in the materials checked

Group homes for children in foster care, including independent living programs

Licensed by: NC Division of Social Services; G.S. 131D-10.3, 10A NCAC 70I. Serves: Children in foster care. Accreditation for three years by COA, the Joint Commission, CARF or CQL is required before licensure (effective August 1, 2011); license term is not to exceed 24 months under G.S. 131D-10.3(e); DSS publishes no license fee figure in the materials checked

Child-caring institutions, childrens camps providing foster care, and emergency and shelter care classes

Licensed by: NC Division of Social Services; G.S. 131D-10.2, 10A NCAC 70I and 70J. Serves: Children in residential child care. Class register inside the child-residential track; residential maternity homes at 10A NCAC 70F and family foster and therapeutic foster homes at 10A NCAC 70E are register entries inside that track, not facility tracks

NC Innovations Supported Living

Licensed by: NC Medicaid NC Innovations 1915(c) waiver; NCTracks enrollment. Serves: Innovations waiver participants in their own home. A service in the person own home, not a group home license; waiver renewed July 1, 2024 to June 30, 2029

How to start a group home in North Carolina, step by step

  1. For the main 122C group home track (supervised living and ICF/IID), identify the exact 10A NCAC 27G category for the population served, for example .5600A to F, .2100, or a behavioral health residential category
  2. Obtain the LME/MCO letter of support required under G.S. 122C-23.1 and 10A NCAC 27G .0406
  3. Complete the DHSR MHLCS initial application packet, Form DHHS/DHSR/MHL5001, revised 10/01/25, for the category sought
  4. Pay the G.S. 131E-272 initial licensure fee for the track (see gh_fees); construction fees are billed separately by the DHSR Construction Section and the packet does not quote a construction figure
  5. For ICF/IID, complete the Certificate of Need gate and the State Medical Facilities Plan need determination; DHSR MHLCS states there is currently no identified need for additional ICF/IID facilities
  6. Enroll in NCTracks for NC Medicaid, and for NC Innovations residential services complete NCTracks enrollment with national accreditation, then contract with the Tailored Plan or LME-MCO
  7. For DSS child residential homes, first obtain three year accreditation by COA, the Joint Commission, CARF or CQL, conduct a needs assessment with named referral agencies, and attach the accreditation letter to the Residential Child Care Facility Inquiry Form mailed to DSS Regulatory and Licensing Services in Black Mountain, NC, as stated in the research

North Carolina group home license fees and costs

  • Adult care home: initial $400 plus $19 per bed (G.S. 131E-272); renewal $360 plus $17.50 per bed (G.S. 131D-2.5(a))
  • Family care home: $350 at initial application per the DHSR ACLS procedure page; renewal $315; G.S. 131D-2.5(a) sets the annual fee for a home with six or fewer beds at $315; both figures are printed and labeled in the research conflict log
  • Nursing home: initial $470 plus $19 per bed (G.S. 131E-272); renewal $420 plus $17.50 per bed (G.S. 131E-102(b))
  • Mental health facilities (27G tracks), initial per G.S. 131E-272 as tabled in the MHLCS packet: non-residential $265; residential non-ICF/IID, 6 beds or less $350; residential non-ICF/IID, 7 beds or more $525 plus $19 per bed; ICF/IID, 6 beds or less $900; ICF/IID, 7 beds or more $850 plus $19 per bed
  • Hospice facility beds: no separate facility fee captured; beds ride the hospice agency license (statutory $400 at G.S. 131E-202(b), DHSR schedule listing $450 initial, conflict carried and disclosed in the research)
  • Adult day certification: no certification fee figure stated in the Division of Aging initial certification materials checked, so no fee is quoted
  • DSS child residential: DSS publishes no license fee figure in the materials checked, so no fee is quoted
  • NCTracks enrollment: $100 North Carolina application fee plus federal fee $750 for calendar year 2026 where required

How long does it take in North Carolina?

  • Adult care home initial license period is six months
  • Mental health facility (27G) licenses renew every year and expire at the end of the calendar year (packet; G.S. 122C-23)
  • Adult day certificate runs 12 months
  • DSS child residential license term is not to exceed 24 months (G.S. 131D-10.3(e))
  • NC Innovations waiver renewed July 1, 2024 through June 30, 2029
  • CAP/DA Clinical Coverage Policy 3K-2 amended September 1, 2025

What are the requirements to open a group home in North Carolina?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in North Carolina?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
