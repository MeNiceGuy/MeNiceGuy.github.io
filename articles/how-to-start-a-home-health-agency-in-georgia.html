How to Start a Home Health Agency in Georgia

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Georgia licenses home health agencies as a Home Health Agency license, with a separate Medicare-certified home health track in the research; the Private Home Care Provider (PHCP) license under Rule 111-8-65 is Georgias separate in-home non-residential agency license and is not the home health license, through the Georgia Department of Community Health (DCH), Healthcare Facility Regulation Division (HFRD). The governing law is PHCP boundary cited in the research: Ga. Comp. R. 111-8-65, O.C.G.A. 31-7-300 et seq.; general HFRD licensing machinery at Ga. Comp. R. 111-8-25; the expansion research lists home-health-license and medicare-certified as existing Georgia tracks checked November 11, 2026 and does not restate a home health statute citation. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the Georgia license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The Georgia license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Georgia, step by step

  1. Choose the correct Georgia door first: a licensed home health agency and Medicare-certified home health are separate existing tracks from the PHCP in-home license and from a Personal Care Home permit; exempt categories in Rule 111-8-65-.07 include licensed home health agencies and personal care home staff working inside that home
  2. For the PHCP in-home track described in the article, pick only the service categories the agency can staff under Rule 111-8-65-.10(1): nursing services, personal care tasks, or companion or sitter tasks, each priced separately
  3. Identify the administrator and, for PHCP, a Georgia licensed registered nurse for the initial application regardless of service level, as required on the DCH Private Home Care Providers Application Checklist; if nursing services are offered, employ a fully licensed Georgia registered professional nurse to supervise them under Rule 111-8-65-.10(2)
  4. Build the complete checklist packet before filing, including the notarized affidavits, city or county business license, Secretary of State entity evidence, electronic owner form, and GCHEXS satisfactory determination letters dated within twelve months of submission for the administrator and owners of ten percent or more
  5. File online through GAHLES, the Georgia Healthcare Licensing Electronic System; do not submit until every required document is complete and ready for upload, because incomplete submission may cause delay, rejection and forfeiture of application fees, and policies and procedures must be established and attested by the Affidavit of Compliance, not uploaded
  6. For PHCP, after approval the provisional license stays active for three months, inside which the provider must become operational, admit at least three clients, and email the Private Home Care Unit to request the initial survey; after a successful survey the agency receives the permanent permit
  7. Enroll separately for Medicaid through GAMMIS at www.mmis.georgia.gov after licensure; for nursing facility and ICF/IID Medicare doors, enroll on CMS-855A through PECOS with survey by DCH HFRD as Georgias state survey agency and Georgia MAC Palmetto GBA, as stated for those classes in the expansion research

Georgia home health license fees and costs

  • Application processing fee, any HFRD program: $300 on submission (Ga. Comp. R. 111-8-25-.03(8) and the DCH HFRD Schedule of Licensure Activity Fees); initial license fee is the same as the annual fee for the program
  • PHCP license fee, separate in-home track, not a home health fee: $250 for each service offered (companion or sitter, personal care, nursing), capped at $750 per year; companion plus personal care is $500 in license fees, so $300 processing plus $500 equals $800 total, and all three services equals $1,050, as stated in the article; fees are non-refundable except in extraordinary circumstances
  • Federal institutional enrollment fee: $750, calendar year 2026 (CMS)
  • Follow-up visit $250; late fee $150; permit replacement $50 (Ga. Comp. R. 111-8-25-.03(8))

How long does it take in Georgia?

  • PHCP initial review timeframe stated by DCH: thirty business days from the submission date, as stated in the article checklist summary; the PCH research separately proves HFRD publishes no processing timeline for PCH permits, and that figure must not be imported into the PCH packet
  • PHCP provisional license: three months to become operational, admit at least three clients and request the initial survey; failure to notify the department within three months results in the license being automatically voided for expiration, with a new application, full review and full fees again; extensions require approval before the deadline with documented justification
  • PHCP caregiver training clock: personal care assistants complete 20 hours of the 40 hour program before serving any client and the remaining 20 within six months of when training began (Rule 111-8-65-.09(5)(c)); at least eight hours of job related training annually after the first year
  • Continuing license fees are due October 31 of each state fiscal year under Rule 111-8-25-.03(6), collected through December 31 without penalty, with a $150 late fee per instance when fees are sixty days past due

What catches Georgia applicants

  • Each PHCP office location must be separately licensed and no license is assignable or transferable under Rule 111-8-65-.05(2); a change of ownership runs through its own application
  • PHCP services do not include physical, speech or occupational therapy, medical nutrition therapy, medical social services, home health aide services provided by a home health agency, or proxy caregiver services, under Rule 111-8-65-.03(p)4 as cited in the article
  • No surety bond in the PHCP rules or fee schedule, except a narrow bonding rule for an employee authorized unlimited access to a client personal funds for home management under Rule 111-8-65-.03(g)
  • Medicaid enrollment through GAMMIS is a separate door from HFRD licensure; no state enrollment fee figure is published in the NOW/COMP manual or DBHDD provider pages read, so no enrollment fee is quoted
  • Certificate of Need: CON is required for new or expanded skilled nursing, intermediate care, or intermingled facilities under Rule 111-2-2-.30, as stated for the nursing facility and ICF/IID classes; no CON fact for a new home health agency is stated in the research files read

The Medicare layer, after the Georgia license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

What do I need to start a home health agency in Georgia?

The Home Health Agency license, with a separate Medicare-certified home health track in the research; the Private Home Care Provider (PHCP) license under Rule 111-8-65 is Georgias separate in-home non-residential agency license and is not the home health license (Georgia Department of Community Health (DCH), Healthcare Facility Regulation Division (HFRD)), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet Georgia rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.
