How to Start a Home Care Agency in Oklahoma (One License Covers Personal Care and Skilled Care)

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 19, 2026

The Oklahoma story starts with a founder who budgeted for two licenses because every guide she read assumed two licenses. She had one budget line for a personal care license and a second line for a home health license, and she was holding her launch until she could afford both applications. When I walked through her plan with her, the second line came off the page. Oklahoma does not sell two licenses for this work. Personal care and skilled care in the home sit on a single credential, the home care agency license, and the service mix changes which rule sections a survey tests, not which license the agency holds. That one fact reshapes the budget, the timeline, and the order of work. The doors that do sit apart in Oklahoma are specific: companion or sitter help with no hands-on care carries its own license, hospice carries its own license, and Medicare certification is a federal layer on top of the state license, never a substitute for it. Here is the full picture, lane by lane, in the order a founder actually meets it.

The demand: about 742,000 seniors, nearly one in five residents

Start with why owners keep looking at Oklahoma. The Census Bureau puts the state population at 4,123,288 as of July 1, 2025, and 18.0 percent of residents are age 65 or older. Run the math and you get about 742,000 seniors, nearly one in five people in the state. The same Census QuickFacts page for Oklahoma puts median household income at $65,039 for 2020 to 2024, which is the income base a private pay agency sells into and the base the SoonerCare payer mix sits beside.

Demand alone does not open a door, and in Oklahoma the door structure is the story. One license covers the two service families most founders plan to sell, which means the preparation work, the administrator, the supervising nurse, the screening system, and the policies, is built once and surveyed once. Founders who understand that structure early spend their money on readiness instead of on a second application the state does not print.

The license question, answered directly: hands-on care takes the home care agency license

Here is the searched question this article exists to answer. Do you need a license to start a home care agency in Oklahoma? If the work includes hands-on personal care, yes, and the license is the home care agency license issued by the Oklahoma State Department of Health through its Home Services Division, under the Home Care Act, Title 63, sections 1-1960 and following of the Oklahoma Statutes, and OAC 310:662. Section 1-1962 is plain: no home care agency may operate without first obtaining a license. Home care services are the skilled or personal care services the Act defines, provided to clients at home for a fee, so one license covers an agency that fields personal care only and an agency that fields nursing and therapy.

The separate doors are narrow and named. Companion or sitter services with no hands-on care sit on the sitter or companion service license under section 1-1972 and OAC 310:662 Subchapter 8. Hospice sits on its own license under the Oklahoma Hospice Licensing Act, Title 63, sections 1-860.1 and following, and OAC 310:661. Medicare-certified home health is the same state license plus federal certification under 42 CFR Part 484. The residential types Oklahoma licenses, assisted living centers and continuum of care facilities under OAC 310:663, residential care homes under OAC 310:680, group homes for developmental disabilities licensed by Oklahoma Human Services, and the rest, are facility and program doors of their own, and no one door covers another door's services.

The main lane: the home care agency license at $1,000, filed on Form ODH 757

The sequence runs in the order the regulator works it. First, confirm the door: the services include hands-on personal care, and Oklahoma licenses that work under the Home Care Act, private pay included. Second, form the entity and build the operating record before filing: an Oklahoma place of business accessible to the public and physically located in Oklahoma, insurance, the OKSCREEN background screening system, an administrator certified by the department under section 1-1962a and OAC 310:664, a supervising registered nurse or physician and the alternate, aide certification and registry systems, service plans, and the policies OAC 310:662 requires. Build that record first so the survey is a submission exercise, not a scramble.

Third, file Form ODH 757, the Application to Operate a Home Care Agency, with the attachments the department lists and the nonrefundable $1,000 initial fee under OAC 310:662-2-1(d), at least thirty days before beginning operations. Complete applications are processed in the order received and queued for the initial licensure survey, and the agency must be ready for business at the survey. The initial license runs twelve months. Renewal aligns to July 31 at $500, the first prorated renewal is charged at $125 per quarter, and a branch office license is $25. No overall department approval timeline beyond that order-received queue is published in the sources checked for this article, and I print none.

The sitter or companion lane: a separate license for care with no hands-on work

The one home-based service family on a different license is companion or sitter help only, with no hands-on personal care. That service holds the sitter or companion service license, filed on Form ODH 1273, at $1,000 initial and $500 renewal, the same schedule as the home care agency license. The boundary is the whole license: bathing, dressing, toileting, and transfer help are hands-on personal care, and they belong on the home care agency license from the first visit. An agency that starts as a sitter service and adds hands-on care needs the home care agency license before the first hands-on visit, not after a family asks for one.

The hospice lane: its own license at $2,000 on Form ODH 924

Hospice is a separate program with a separate license, and the budget line is different. The application goes to the department on Form ODH 924, with the narrative program summary, the plan for delivery of home and inpatient hospice services, and the entity record, at a $2,000 initial license and application fee under OAC 310:661-2-5 and 310:661-2-6. Providing care before a successful on-site initial licensure survey violates the Act. The first-year license expires one year from issuance, a follow-up inspection follows at least six months of services, and no first-year license runs beyond twenty-four months. Renewal is $2,000, the late renewal fee is $50 on the terms the rule prints, and each alternate administrative office is $500, filed at least thirty days before the office begins operations.

The Medicare and SoonerCare lanes: federal certification and state enrollment sit on top of the license

Medicare runs only on the certified track. The state license is component one; the federal sequence does not substitute for it. Department guidance sets the survey predicate: a licensed agency must have provided skilled care to a minimum of ten patients before an initial Medicare survey is conducted, with at least 7 patients actively receiving skilled care at the time of the survey. Sequence the census build before requesting the survey. Enrollment runs on Form CMS-855A through PECOS to Palmetto GBA, Jurisdiction M, the Home Health and Hospice MAC for Oklahoma, with the CY2026 institutional enrollment fee of $750. Read the moratorium gate before filing: the federal Medicare enrollment moratorium (CMS-6101-N2 for home health, CMS-6102-N2 for hospice) gates initial enrollment on the certified tracks only (the extension is a six-month period, running to about May 12, 2027), and it blocks neither the state license nor any state track. The A/B MAC for Oklahoma is Novitas Solutions, Jurisdiction H, and CGS Administrators, Jurisdiction C, is the DME MAC, a different door entirely.

SoonerCare, Oklahoma Medicaid, is administered by the Oklahoma Health Care Authority. Enrollment and the provider contract run online through the authority, and managed care participation is a separate step: an enrollment does not contract the agency with a SoonerSelect entity, and an entity contract does not enroll the agency. SoonerSelect covers most SoonerCare members through Aetna Better Health of Oklahoma, Humana Healthy Horizons in Oklahoma, and Oklahoma Complete Health, with Oklahoma Complete Health also running the Children's Specialty Program. Members in the waiver, long-term services and supports, nursing facility, and ICF/IID populations are served fee-for-service, which is where most home-based Medicaid volume sits, including State Plan Personal Care and ADvantage waiver personal care. Oklahoma requires electronic visit verification for personal care services and home health care services under OAC 317:30-3-34, through an authorized and approved system, as a condition of payment: the visit record, the authorization, and the claim must agree.

Background checks and the staffing record: OKSCREEN before assignment

The personnel file is a survey document in Oklahoma, and its order matters. OKSCREEN background screening is completed before assignment, not after the first visit. Home health aides meet the certification and registry requirements the Act sets. The administrator holds department certification under section 1-1962a and OAC 310:664, and the agency names a supervising registered nurse or physician. In the files I review for owners, the recurring failure is sequencing: workers assigned before screening, certification, and training records were complete and filed. Build the personnel file system, application and identity verification, screening result, certification and registry standing, orientation and training record, competency evaluation for each assigned service, supervision and evaluation record, before the first client is admitted, and the survey reads it as a system instead of a pile.

What you still do today: form the business the Oklahoma way

The license sits on top of an ordinary business build, done in the right order. Form the entity and keep the Secretary of State record current, get an EIN from the IRS, and stand up the Oklahoma place of business the Act requires, accessible to the public and physically located in Oklahoma. Put liability insurance in place before the first client. Adopt the policies OAC 310:662 requires, covering the services the license actually carries, and set up the service agreement that states what the agency does and does not do, with hospice services and hands-on care on a sitter license on the does not do side of the line. No Oklahoma formation fee is printed in the sources checked for this article, so I print none: confirm the current filing fee on the day of filing.

The practical warning

Three Oklahoma traps cost new owners money. The first is the phantom second license: a guide that sells a separate Oklahoma home health license application is selling a door the state does not print, because personal care and skilled care share one license. The second is a pair of fee traps. Section 1-1965 lets the department set an initial fee of up to $3,000; the rule at OAC 310:662-2-1(d) sets the fee actually charged at $1,000, and a budget built on $3,000 overstates the state fee by two thousand dollars. Run the error the other way and it costs just as much: the $1,000 home care fee carried into hospice understates a $2,000 license. The third trap is reading a backlog as a moratorium. Authority provider letters from the fall of 2026 describe contracting and enrollment processing delays, with a claims exception process; no Oklahoma payer enrollment moratorium and no licensure moratorium was located in the primary sources checked. An applicant who stops filing because of a backlog story has frozen a door no authority paused.
