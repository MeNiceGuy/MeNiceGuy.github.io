How to Start a Home Care Agency in Delaware (Every Door Is Licensed, Starting With the PASA License)

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 19, 2026

The Delaware story starts with a founder who assumed small state meant simple rules. She planned a companion-only agency, light services, no nursing, no therapy, and she budgeted as if companionship sat outside licensure. It does not. In Delaware, homemaker and companion services sit inside the definition of the licensed non-medical door itself, so her light agency needed the full license, the full binder, and the full review clock. I work with owners in this state on sequence more than on any single form, because Delaware front-loads the paperwork and states a long review frame in plain terms. Here is the full picture, door by door, in the order a founder actually meets it.

Hold the headline first. Delaware licenses every home-based door through one division, and the licenses are easy to merge by mistake because the fee figures sit close together. Non-medical care, skilled care, aide-only care, and hospice each hold a separate license, at a separate fee, with a separate service ceiling. An owner who keeps the four apart files once.

The demand: about 242,000 seniors, the highest senior share in this batch

Start with why owners keep looking at Delaware. The Census Bureau puts the state population at 1,059,952 as of July 1, 2025, and 22.8 percent of residents are age 65 or older, the highest senior share among the six states in this batch. Run the math and you get about 242,000 seniors, close to one in four residents. The same Census QuickFacts page for Delaware puts median household income at $84,954 for 2020 to 2024, which is the income base a private pay agency sells into. That senior count is the population figure multiplied by the age 65 and over share, labeled as that math because the Census page publishes the two inputs, not the product.

The payer side runs through managed care for most members, which changes the startup sequence. The Delaware Medical Assistance Program (DMAP) delivers most coverage through the Diamond State Health Plan and DSHP Plus, through three managed care organizations: Highmark Health Options, AmeriHealth Caritas Delaware, and Delaware First Health. A home-based agency enrolls with DMAP first, then contracts with each organization whose members it will serve. A fee-for-service-only plan imported from another state misses the contracting step Delaware requires.

The license question, answered directly: the PASA license covers non-medical care, companions included

Here is the searched question this article exists to answer. Do you need a license to start a non-medical home care agency in Delaware? Yes. Non-medical home care is a personal assistance services agency (PASA) license under 16 Del. C. 122(3)x and 16 DE Admin. Code 3345, issued by the Division of Health Care Quality (DHCQ) of the Department of Health and Social Services, through its Office of Health Facilities Licensing and Certification. Under rule 3345, section 2.1.1, no person shall establish, conduct, or maintain a personal assistance services agency without first obtaining a license. Personal assistance services are services that do not require the judgment and skills of a licensed nurse or other professional, help with activities of daily living and instrumental activities of daily living, plus homemaker and companion services, delivered by direct care workers the agency refers. No nurse is required on this door, and the agency must not use wording that implies it provides health care services.

The other doors sit in the same division. Skilled care is a home health agency license under 16 Del. C. 122(3)o, in two types: Skilled at rule 3350 and Aide Only at rule 3351. Hospice is a hospice license under 16 Del. C. 122(3)m and rule 3380. Residential doors carry their own DHCQ licenses in the Delaware packet, including neighborhood homes for individuals with intellectual and developmental disabilities under rule 3310 at a $50 license fee for the class, group homes for persons with mental illness under rule 3305, family care homes under rule 3315, and adult day care facilities under rule 3360 at $100 application and $50 annual. No Certificate of Public Review gate sits in front of the home-based doors, because the facility definition at 16 Del. C. 9302(4) does not name home health agencies, hospice programs, or personal assistance services agencies. No one license covers another license's services.

The non-medical lane: the PASA license, binder first, survey inside 90 days

Step one is confirming the service menu is non-skilled: hands-on personal care, homemaker, and companion work only, with no nursing, no therapy, and no skilled tasks. Step two is forming the business with the Delaware Division of Corporations, holding the EIN and tax registrations and the Delaware business license, and putting the operating accounts in place. Hold spending on an office, insurance, and hiring until the binder review clears, on the DHCQ sequence. The director is a full-time director who meets the education and supervisory experience requirements DHCQ prints for the door, an associate degree plus two years of health care or home care supervisory experience on the DHCQ opening materials, and an alternate director meets the same qualifications.

Step three is the filing itself: the statement of intent, then the typed Part 1 application with the $250 nonrefundable fee at 122(3)x.4, paid by certified check or money order, and the Document Compliance Binder. The binder carries the policies and procedures manual, forms, orientation material, and the competency test with an answer key, indexed to the DHCQ Document Compliance Checklist. DHCQ states the time frame for review is 6 to 9 months or longer, and that single sentence should set the cash plan for the whole startup. Step four is building the screening systems before the first visit, covered in the background check section below. Step five is the license sequence: the first license is an initial license for 90 calendar days and may not be renewed, no PASA may provide services before DHCQ licenses it, the agency serves a first consumer and completes the unannounced on-site survey inside the initial term, and the annual license follows at the $100 annual licensure fee under 122(3)x.5, with renewal filed at least 30 calendar days before expiration. Step six, for DMAP work, is enrollment through the DMAP Provider Portal after licensure, contracting with the DSHP Plus organizations, and EVV through HHAeXchange or a certified alternate before the first billed visit.

The skilled lane: two home health types, one fee, different ceilings

Settle the licensed type first, because the type decides the service ceiling. The Skilled type at rule 3350 provides two or more home care services, one of which must be licensed nursing services or home health aide services, under practitioner orders. The Aide Only type at rule 3351 provides home health aide services and medication reminders, including tasks a licensed nurse delegates as permitted by 24 Del. C. chapter 19. All home health agency services must be supervised by a registered nurse under 122(3)o.2.C, and both types carry a full-time registered nurse as clinical director. The Aide Only type is a non-medical door for Medicare purposes and may not apply to receive Medicare payments.

File the DHCQ application with the $500 nonrefundable application fee at 122(3)o.4 and the policy file, and hold services until the license issues. The initial license runs 90 calendar days with the on-site survey inside the term, and the annual license renews each year at the $300 annual licensure fee. A provisional license carries a $300 licensure fee, and the $500 application fee is resubmitted for reinspection before an annual license issues. Trap figure: the $250 PASA application fee belongs to the non-medical door, and a home health filing budgeted at $250 is underfunded by half. Enroll with DMAP after licensure and configure EVV through HHAeXchange or a certified alternate, and hold the enforcement date the DMMA EVV page prints: effective July 1, 2025, claims for services subject to EVV that cannot be matched to a corresponding EVV visit will be denied payment.

The certified and hospice lanes: hospice never waits on Medicare in Delaware

Medicare certification layers on the Skilled license only, never on a PASA or an Aide Only agency. Enrollment runs on Form CMS-855A through PECOS to CGS Administrators, Jurisdiction 15, the Home Health and Hospice MAC for Delaware, with the federal enrollment fee of $750 for 2026. The A/B MAC is Novitas Solutions, Jurisdiction L, and it is not the home health enrollment desk; Noridian Healthcare Solutions, Jurisdiction A, is the DME desk. Read the gate before timing the filing: initial enrollment is blocked under CMS-6101-N2, effective November 12, 2026, with no end date printed in the notice (the extension is a six-month period, running to about May 12, 2027). Build to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, complete the certification survey through DHCQ or an approved accrediting organization, and hold the certification before billing Medicare.

Hospice is the Delaware fact that runs against the pattern in other states: Medicare certification is not a condition of the Delaware hospice license. Under rule 3380, section 2.3, every organization that plans to call itself a hospice or to offer hospice type or palliative care must apply for the license, whether or not it is eligible to receive Medicare or Medicaid certification. File the DHCQ application with the $100 application fee at 122(3)m. The license runs not more than one year, renewal is filed at least 30 days before expiration at the $50 annual licensure fee, and a provisional license runs not more than three months at a $50 fee. The name is licensed with the program: the term hospice, or a like term, may not be used as part of the name of any unlicensed program under section 2.1. The Medicare layer, where pursued, runs on the same federal form to the same MAC desk under 42 CFR Part 418, gated for initial enrollment by CMS-6102-N2, and the state license is sequenced on its own clock regardless.

Background checks: three checks in every direct care worker file

Every direct care worker file carries the Background Check Center criminal history check, the Adult Abuse Registry check, and the drug screening record, under 16 Del. C. 1141 to 1146 and 16 DE Admin. Code 3110, plus the OIG and SAM exclusion checks. Build the screening file before the first visit, not after the survey finding, on the PASA door and the home health doors alike. General liability insurance is held at the levels rule 3345, section 7.2 prints for a PASA, and the personnel gates on the skilled doors add the full-time RN clinical director record and the RN supervision and delegation record for aides.

The survey rhythm makes the file order matter. The initial license survey is unannounced and lands inside a 90-day window, so an agency that hires first and screens later is surveyed on the gap. The founders who clear the Delaware sequence cleanly treat the binder, the screening system, and the competency test as one build, finished before the first consumer is served, because the annual license depends on that survey record.

What you still do today: form the business the Delaware way

Form the business with the Delaware Division of Corporations, hold the Delaware business license, get the EIN from the IRS, and put the operating accounts in place. Then do the ordinary employer load in the right order, with one Delaware adjustment: sequence spending behind the binder review, because DHCQ states the review runs 6 to 9 months or longer. Written policies and procedures, the competency test with its answer key, background screening set up as a system, and a service agreement that says clearly what your agency does and does not do, with no wording that implies health care services on the PASA door. I print no Delaware formation fee here because no formation fee figure sits in the primary sources this article is built from.

The practical warning

Three Delaware traps cost new owners money. The first is the companion trap: homemaker and companion services sit inside the statutory definition of personal assistance services at 122(3)x.2, so a companion-only agency holds the full PASA license, and there is no unlicensed companion tier in Delaware. The second is the fee shuffle: the PASA application fee is $250, the home health application fee is $500, and the hospice application fee is $100, and a filing budgeted on a neighboring door figure is underfunded before it is read. The third is the assisted living name trap: in Delaware, assisted living is a residential facility license under 16 Del. C. chapter 11. It authorizes no in-home agency work, and no in-home agency needs it. Add the clock trap to all three: an owner who signs a lease and hires staff before the binder clears is paying rent against a 6 to 9 month review frame the state printed at the start.

Frequently asked questions

How do I start a home care agency in Delaware?

You start with the license for your door, all issued by DHCQ. Non-medical care files the personal assistance services agency license, binder first. Skilled care settles the Skilled or Aide Only type, then files the home health agency license. Hospice files its own license, whether or not Medicare certification is planned. What are the Delaware home care license requirements?

Delaware licenses every home-based door through DHCQ. A personal assistance services agency license (16 Del. C. 122(3)x; rule 3345) covers non-medical personal assistance, homemaker, and companion services, with a 90-day initial license and a survey inside the term. Home health agencies are licensed under 122(3)o in Skilled (rule 3350) and Aide Only (rule 3351) types, both with a full-time RN clinical director and all services RN supervised. Hospice is licensed under 122(3)m and rule 3380 whether or not the program is Medicare eligible. No Certificate of Public Review gate reaches these doors under 9302(4).

How much does a home care license cost in Delaware?

The PASA application fee is $250, nonrefundable, with a $100 annual licensure fee. The home health application fee is $500, nonrefundable, with a $300 annual licensure fee for both the Skilled and Aide Only types. The hospice application fee is $100 with a $50 annual licensure fee. Adult day care facilities, a residential door, pay $100 application and $50 annual. The federal CMS-855A fee is $750 for 2026 on the certified tracks.
