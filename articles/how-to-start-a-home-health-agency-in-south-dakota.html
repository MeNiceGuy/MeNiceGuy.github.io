How to Start a Home Health Agency in South Dakota

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

South Dakota does not require state licensure for certified home health agencies, and the Department of Health does not regulate non-medical home care providers. Entry is federal only: Form CMS-855A enrollment through the Medicare Administrative Contractor, the federal civil rights clearance, the Health Insurance Benefit Agreement, and the certification survey against the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484. This page gives you that sequence in order.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The state-side entry work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in South Dakota, step by step

  1. Complete the Medicare enrollment application, form CMS-855A, and submit it according to the directions on the form; the Medicare fee-for-service contractor for South Dakota is CGS Administrators, LLC (DOH Home Health Agencies page)
  2. Complete Office of Civil Rights clearance through the Assurance of Compliance portal, listed as a home health resource by DOH
  3. Complete the Health Insurance Benefit Agreement, listed as a home health resource by DOH
  4. For Medicaid reimbursement, contact Provider Enrollment at the number DOH lists to begin the Medicaid enrollment process

What catches South Dakota applicants

  • South Dakota does not license home health agencies at the state level and relies on Medicare certification alone: the DOH page states plainly, South Dakota does not require state licensure for certified HHAs
  • Because no state license exists, no state license is required before Medicare certification; the entry route is federal Medicare enrollment and certification
  • Non-medical home health providers who provide in-home care such as bathing, light-duty housekeeping, or meal preparation are NOT regulated by the South Dakota Department of Health, per the DOH page
  • No state licensure fee exists, so no fee is listed here

The Medicare layer

With the state-side entry handled and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

What do I need to start a home health agency in South Dakota?

Medicare certification as a home health agency, because South Dakota has no state home health license. Beyond that: an administrator and clinical leadership, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Any state gate named on this page, such as a certificate of need where the state requires one, comes first, and Medicaid enrollment is a separate application.

Do I need a state license to start a home health agency in South Dakota?

No. No state license. South Dakota does not require state licensure for certified home health agencies The operative gate is Medicare certification, which follows the federal sequence laid out on this page.
