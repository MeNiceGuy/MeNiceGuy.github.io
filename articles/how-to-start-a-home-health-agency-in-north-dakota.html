How to Start a Home Health Agency in North Dakota

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

North Dakota licenses home health agencies as a Home Health Agency license, through the North Dakota Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), Health Facilities Unit. The governing law is North Dakota Century Code chapter 23-17.3 and North Dakota Administrative Code chapter 33-03-10.1. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the North Dakota license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The North Dakota license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in North Dakota, step by step

  1. Apply to the department for a license in the form prescribed (N.D. Admin. Code 33-03-10.1-03)
  2. Obtain an initial license before accepting patients for care or treatment
  3. Renewal is issued when the agency is in compliance, as determined by periodic unannounced onsite surveys conducted by the department and other information submitted on request

How long does it take in North Dakota?

  • Initial license is valid for a period not to exceed one year and expires on December 31 of the year issued (N.D. Admin. Code 33-03-10.1-03)
  • Renewal licenses expire on December 31 of each year

What catches North Dakota applicants

  • An entity meeting the definition of home health agency in N.D.C.C. section 23-17.3-01 must obtain a license from the department to operate in North Dakota
  • A person or entity may not establish or operate an agency or use the terms home health agency or home health services without first having obtained a license (N.D. Admin. Code 33-03-10.1-03)
  • A state license is therefore required under the state rule before operating; the HHS home health page links separately to federal Medicare resources from CMS
  • No application fee is printed in N.D. Admin. Code 33-03-10.1-03 as published on ndlegis.gov, so no fee is listed here
  • No certificate of need step is printed on the HHS home health agencies page or in the chapter 33-03-10.1 sections reviewed

The Medicare layer, after the North Dakota license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get a home health license in North Dakota?

Initial license is valid for a period not to exceed one year and expires on December 31 of the year issued (N.D. Admin. Code 33-03-10.1-03) Renewal licenses expire on December 31 of each year The federal certification that follows runs on the survey calendar, and new Medicare enrollment is under a CMS moratorium to about mid-May 2027.

What do I need to start a home health agency in North Dakota?

The Home Health Agency license (North Dakota Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), Health Facilities Unit), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet North Dakota rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in North Dakota?

Apply to the North Dakota Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), Health Facilities Unit under North Dakota Century Code chapter 23-17.3 and North Dakota Administrative Code chapter 33-03-10.1. The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
