How to Start a Group Home in Connecticut

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Connecticut. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Connecticut licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Connecticut licenses

Community Living Arrangement (CLA)

Licensed by: Connecticut Department of Developmental Services (DDS). Serves: 15 or fewer individuals with intellectual disabilities; the governing statute, C.G.S. 17a-227, covers persons with intellectual disability, Prader-Willi syndrome or autism spectrum disorder. A CLA is a residential facility in which the licensee or DDS Public Division provides residential services to 15 or fewer individuals with intellectual disabilities - DDS Procedure I.D. PR 007 definition. License types under Regulations section 17a-227-02 are issued as either a community living arrangement, habilitative nursing facility or residential school, and a license may be designated to provide permanent and/or respite services. A license is issued only to a single residence, except for a residential school, condominium or apartment complex grouping stated in that regulation. Statutory basis is C.G.S. 17a-227: no person, firm or corporation shall operate a community living arrangement or community companion home for persons with intellectual disability, Prader-Willi syndrome or autism spectrum disorder without a DDS license.

Community Companion Home (CCH), also listed as Community Training Home (CTH)

Licensed by: Connecticut Department of Developmental Services (DDS). Serves: People with intellectual disability cared for in a private family home. Licensure of private family homes which provide care, treatment or lodging for people with intellectual disability - DDS Licenses Issued page. DDS procedure documents refer to CLA and Community Companion Home licensing together.

Residential Care Home (RCH)

Licensed by: Licensed as a health care institution by Connecticut Department of Public Health; payment and resident information also involve Connecticut Department of Social Services. Serves: Adults who need assistance with daily living, supervision, meals and coordination of services, but do not require 24-hour skilled nursing care. A Residential Care Home is a licensed community-based residential setting that provides housing and supportive services for adults who may need assistance with daily living, supervision, meals, and coordination of services, but who do not require 24-hour skilled nursing care. The state summary table describes an RCH as providing room, meals, supervision, medication supervision, and supportive services. The State Supplement program through DSS may help eligible individuals pay for room, board and personal care costs in an RCH, depending on age, disability status, income, assets and living arrangement - Connecticut Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program page on Understanding Residential Care Homes.

Assisted Living Services Agency (ALSA), serving a Managed Residential Community (MRC)

Licensed by: Connecticut Department of Public Health, Home Health Unit. Serves: Residents of a Managed Residential Community who require and request nursing services, including medication administration and aide assistance with activities of daily living. Connecticut splits assisted living into two parts. DPH regulates the ALSA, meaning the nursing services delivered within the assisted living facility, including medication administration and aide assistance with activities of daily living. DPH does not license the physical building or structures of an assisted living facility and has no oversight over the financial aspect of the stay. The facility where ALSA recipients reside must be registered as a Managed Residential Community (MRC) with DPH before the potential ALSA provider may apply for an ALSA license - DPH Home Health Unit page.

Mental Health Group Home - Residential Rehabilitation

Licensed by: Connecticut Department of Mental Health and Addiction Services (DMHAS). Serves: Persons receiving mental health rehabilitative services in a group home setting under the Medicaid Rehab Option. DMHAS page states Medicaid Rehab Option services are delivered in Private Non-Medical Institutions, access to mental health group home rehabilitative services is provided through that program, the Local Mental Health Authority has responsibility for determining medical necessity and developing the Master Treatment Plan, and participating facilities must be licensed, with provider qualifications listed on the page. No bed size, fee or timeline was printed in the DMHAS text fetched, so none is stated here.

How to start a group home in Connecticut, step by step

  1. For a CLA or CCH, contact the appropriate DDS Regional Office for licensure information - DDS Licenses Issued page
  2. For an ALSA, the Managed Residential Community where ALSA care will be delivered must be registered with DPH before the potential ALSA provider may apply for an ALSA license; to register an MRC and apply for an ALSA license, call DPH Home Health Unit or email the contact named on that page - DPH Home Health Unit page
  3. DDS CLA licensure and certification applications are submitted through the eLicense system, per DDS Procedure I.D. PR 007 on CLA Licensing Inspections

How long does it take in Connecticut?

  • DDS residential facility licenses must be renewed annually - Connecticut General Assembly Office of Legislative Research report on opening a group home, citing DDS Regulations section 17a-227-6

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get a group home license in Connecticut?

DDS residential facility licenses must be renewed annually - Connecticut General Assembly Office of Legislative Research report on opening a group home, citing DDS Regulations section 17a-227-6

What are the requirements to open a group home in Connecticut?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Connecticut?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
