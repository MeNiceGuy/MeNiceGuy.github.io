How to Start a Home Health Agency in New Jersey

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

New Jersey licenses home health agencies as a Home Health Agency license, through the New Jersey Department of Health, Office of Certificate of Need and Healthcare Facility Licensure, Division of Certificate of Need and Licensing. The governing law is Health Care Facilities Planning Act, N.J.S.A. 26:2H-1 et seq., in particular N.J.S.A. 26:2H-5 and 26:2H-7; Licensing Standards for Home Health Agencies at N.J.A.C. 8:42; Certificate of Need rules at N.J.A.C. 8:33. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the New Jersey license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The New Jersey license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in New Jersey, step by step

  1. Obtain a Certificate of Need first, and only under a Certificate of Need Call: N.J.A.C. 8:42-2.1(a) states a home health agency shall not be instituted, constructed, expanded, licensed to operate or closed except upon application for and receipt of a Certificate of Need issued by the Commissioner pursuant to a Certificate of Need Call as required by N.J.A.C. 8:33
  2. Apply for licensure to the Office of Certificate of Need and Healthcare Facility Licensure: general licensure by that office is mandatory prior to commencement of new or expanded services, per the Department CN-7 licensure instructions
  3. File the license application before opening: the CN-7 instructions state to file 90 days prior to the planned opening, with the completed license application form, license application fee, biennial inspection fee if applicable, floor plan if applicable, and all out-of-state track record reports
  4. Arrange the operational survey: the CN-7 instructions state to contact the inspections unit 45 days prior to the planned opening to arrange an operational survey
  5. Receive the license: a license is issued upon construction or renovation approval where applicable, compliance with all regulatory requirements based on the operational survey, a copy of the certificate of occupancy, and receipt and approval of the application for licensure; services may not begin until occupancy approval is received
  6. For Medicaid participation as a home health agency, N.J.A.C. 10:60 requires the agency to be approved by the Department of Health including Certificate of Need and licensure when applicable, certified as a home health agency under Title XVIII Medicare, and approved for participation by New Jersey Medicaid and NJ FamilyCare

How long does it take in New Jersey?

  • License application filing: 90 days prior to planned opening, per CN-7 licensure instructions
  • Operational survey contact: 45 days prior to planned opening, per CN-7 licensure instructions

What catches New Jersey applicants

  • Certificate of need is a hard gate in New Jersey, not a routine filing: a new or expanding home health agency can only be licensed under a Certificate of Need Call issued under N.J.A.C. 8:33, per the adopted text of N.J.A.C. 8:42-2.1(a) in the Department adoption document fetched
  • No HHA application fee amount was printed on the NJ Department of Health pages or forms fetched, so no fee amount is stated here; the CN-7 instructions refer to a license application fee and a biennial inspection fee without printing amounts in the text fetched
  • The older Certificate of Need Home Health Agency Policy Manual at N.J.A.C. 8:33L had expired on December 31, 1997 and was proposed again in Department material fetched; the operative rule cited in the current 8:42 adoption is the CN Call requirement at N.J.A.C. 8:33, so 8:33L is not treated as the current licensure standard here

The Medicare layer, after the New Jersey license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get a home health license in New Jersey?

License application filing: 90 days prior to planned opening, per CN-7 licensure instructions Operational survey contact: 45 days prior to planned opening, per CN-7 licensure instructions The federal certification that follows runs on the survey calendar, and new Medicare enrollment is under a CMS moratorium to about mid-May 2027.

What do I need to start a home health agency in New Jersey?

The Home Health Agency license (New Jersey Department of Health, Office of Certificate of Need and Healthcare Facility Licensure, Division of Certificate of Need and Licensing), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet New Jersey rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in New Jersey?

Apply to the New Jersey Department of Health, Office of Certificate of Need and Healthcare Facility Licensure, Division of Certificate of Need and Licensing under Health Care Facilities Planning Act, N.J.S.A. 26:2H-1 et seq., in particular N.J.S.A. 26:2H-5 and 26:2H-7; Licensing Standards for Home Health Agencies at N.J.A.C. 8:42; Certificate of Need rules at N.J.A.C. 8:33. The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
