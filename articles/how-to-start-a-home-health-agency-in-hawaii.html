How to Start a Home Health Agency in Hawaii

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Hawaii licenses home health agencies as a Home Health Agency license, through the Hawaii Department of Health, Office of Health Care Assurance (OHCA), Medicare Section. The governing law is Hawaii Administrative Rules Chapter 11-97, cited on the OHCA Opening a Home Health Agency page as Chapter 97, and listed in the current Department of Health Title 11 rules list as Chapter 11-97.1, Home Health Agencies. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the Hawaii license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The Hawaii license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Hawaii, step by step

  1. First obtain an approved Certificate of Need (CON) from the State Health Planning and Development Agency (SHPDA). Applicants interested in participating in the voluntary Medicare or Medicaid programs will need to obtain an approved CON, with a public hearing process. If the applicant is not interested in pursuing federal certification, SHPDA will issue a letter indicating there is no need to obtain a CON
  2. Submit a copy of the SHPDA response, either an approved CON or a letter indicating a CON is not necessary, along with a written request to the Hawaii Department of Health, Office of Health Care Assurance, 601 Kamokila Boulevard, Room 395, Kapolei, HI 96707, for licensure as a home health agency
  3. OHCA responds with a written acknowledgement of the application for licensure and schedules an initial onsite survey with the applicant
  4. Be able to meet all applicable licensing requirements at the time of the request, with written policies and procedures and qualified staff, and submit copies of required policies and procedures to OHCA for review prior to the initial onsite survey. Items OHCA lists for review include the policy and procedure manual, contracts for services not provided through salaried employees, licenses and credentials, legal name and tax identification number and DBA, physical location and contact information, days and hours of operation, and administrator contact information
  5. Initial onsite survey to determine compliance with licensure requirements
  6. After state licensure, the provider may voluntarily elect to participate in the Medicare or Medicaid certification program, using Medicare application forms from CMS

How long does it take in Hawaii?

  • OHCA states it may not be able to accommodate requests for initial licensure for several months from the time of the request, due to existing contractual obligations
  • For Medicare certification after a denial, OHCA states a re-application survey should not be expected for up to six months due to other workload priorities

What catches Hawaii applicants

  • Current Hawaii law as stated by OHCA requires anyone operating a home health agency in Hawaii to be licensed by the Hawaii Department of Health. Operating without a valid license may be subject to civil money penalties and imprisonment, per OHCA.
  • Licensure is based on meeting the definition of home health service, not the source of reimbursement (Medicare, Medicaid, or private pay), frequency, or volume, per OHCA. The definition includes providing direct or indirect skilled nursing services and other therapeutic services under a physician direction to homebound patients.
  • State license comes before Medicare certification in the OHCA sequence: once licensed, a provider may voluntarily elect Medicare or Medicaid certification. Upon issuance of a state license a facility can admit patients, but there will be no Medicare or Medicaid reimbursement until the provider successfully completes the Medicare certification process, per OHCA.
  • Certificate of need: a CON from SHPDA is required in the OHCA procedure for applicants interested in Medicare or Medicaid participation. Applicants not pursuing federal certification receive a SHPDA letter that a CON is not necessary, and that letter is submitted with the licensure request.
  • Fees: OHCA materials state licensure and certification fees are assessed under HAR Chapter 11-103.1, Licensure and Certification Fees for Health Care Facilities and Agencies, but the dollar amount for a home health agency was not printed on the official pages fetched in this pass, so no fee amount is stated here.

The Medicare layer, after the Hawaii license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get a home health license in Hawaii?

OHCA states it may not be able to accommodate requests for initial licensure for several months from the time of the request, due to existing contractual obligations For Medicare certification after a denial, OHCA states a re-application survey should not be expected for up to six months due to other workload priorities The federal certification that follows runs on the survey calendar, and new Medicare enrollment is under a CMS moratorium to about mid-May 2027.

What do I need to start a home health agency in Hawaii?

The Home Health Agency license (Hawaii Department of Health, Office of Health Care Assurance (OHCA), Medicare Section), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet Hawaii rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in Hawaii?

Apply to the Hawaii Department of Health, Office of Health Care Assurance (OHCA), Medicare Section under Hawaii Administrative Rules Chapter 11-97, cited on the OHCA Opening a Home Health Agency page as Chapter 97, and listed in the current Department of Health Title 11 rules list as Chapter 11-97.1, Home Health Agencies. The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
