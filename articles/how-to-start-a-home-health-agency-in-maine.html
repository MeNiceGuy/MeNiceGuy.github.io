How to Start a Home Health Agency in Maine

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Maine licenses home health agencies as a Home Health Agency license for agencies that are state licensed and federally certified, and Home Health Care Services license for agencies that are state licensed only, through the Maine Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS), Division of Licensing and Certification (DLC), Medical Facilities Unit. The governing law is Title 22, Subtitle 2, Part 4, Chapter 419, Home Health Services, and 10-144 CMR Ch. 119, Regulations Governing the Licensing and Functioning of Home Health Care Services. Federal regulations cited by DLC are 42 CFR Part 484. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the Maine license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The Maine license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Maine, step by step

  1. Complete the appropriate application and submit it with the required documentation and fees as detailed in the application form and in the appropriate state regulations, per DLC instructions
  2. For a Home Health Agency that is state licensed and federally certified or intends to be federally certified: use the Home Health Initial and Renewal Application
  3. For a Home Health Agency that is state licensed only and has no intention of being federally certified: use the Home Health Service Provider Application (online)
  4. To become federally certified generally, per DLC: submit an enrollment application, then DLC will provide additional CMS-required forms, complete these and return to DLC

Maine home health license fees and costs

  • Initial application fee $300, nonrefundable, as printed on the official DHHS Application for Home Health Care Services Agency, Form 090102 Rev 6/2024
  • Renewal application fee $600, nonrefundable, as printed on the same official application form

What catches Maine applicants

  • DLC states: These agencies must all be licensed by the State of Maine, but they have the choice of also being federally certified. Home Health Agencies are both licensed by the state and federally certified. Home Health Care Services are only licensed by the state.
  • State licensure is therefore required in Maine, and Medicare certification is presented by DLC as a choice in addition to licensure, not as a substitute for it.
  • The official Medical Facilities page fetched for this sheet does not list a certificate of need step for home health agencies.
  • Verification note: no processing timeline for home health licensure was printed on the official pages fetched, so no timeline is stated. A 6 to 9 month delay notice on the DLC site in this pass applied to Personal Care Agencies, not to home health, and was not carried over.

The Medicare layer, after the Maine license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a home health agency in Maine?

The figures Maine publishes are these: Initial application fee $300, nonrefundable, as printed on the official DHHS Application for Home Health Care Services Agency, Form 090102 Rev 6/2024 Renewal application fee $600, nonrefundable, as printed on the same official application form Beyond the published figures, the operating costs (clinical payroll before certification, insurance, and the reserve operating funds required by 42 CFR 489.28) are larger than every fee on the list.

What do I need to start a home health agency in Maine?

The Home Health Agency license for agencies that are state licensed and federally certified, and Home Health Care Services license for agencies that are state licensed only (Maine Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS), Division of Licensing and Certification (DLC), Medical Facilities Unit), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet Maine rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in Maine?

Apply to the Maine Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS), Division of Licensing and Certification (DLC), Medical Facilities Unit under Title 22, Subtitle 2, Part 4, Chapter 419, Home Health Services, and 10-144 CMR Ch. 119, Regulations Governing the Licensing and Functioning of Home Health Care Services. Federal regulations cited by DLC are 42 CFR Part 484. The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
