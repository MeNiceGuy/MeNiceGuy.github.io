How to Start a Group Home in Kansas

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Kansas. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Kansas licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Kansas licenses

Assisted Living Facility

Licensed by: Kansas Department for Aging and Disability Services (KDADS), Secretary for Aging and Disability Services, under K.S.A. 39-923 et seq.. Serves: Six or more individuals who, by choice or due to functional impairments, may need personal care and supervised nursing care to compensate for activities of daily living limitations. An adult care home classification required to be licensed by KDADS. Includes apartments for residents and provides or coordinates personal care or supervised nursing care available 24 hours a day, seven days a week, for the support of resident independence (K.S.A. 39-923 definitions). Residents may receive Medicaid payment only through an approved HCBS plan, per KDHE Medicaid policy materials.

Residential Healthcare Facility

Licensed by: Kansas Department for Aging and Disability Services (KDADS), under K.S.A. 39-923 et seq.. Serves: Six or more individuals who may need personal care and supervised nursing care to compensate for activities of daily living limitations. An adult care home classification required to be licensed by KDADS. Includes individual living units and provides or coordinates personal care or supervised nursing care available 24 hours a day, seven days a week (K.S.A. 39-923 definitions).

Home Plus

Licensed by: Kansas Department for Aging and Disability Services (KDADS), under K.S.A. 39-923 et seq.. Serves: Not more than 12 individuals who, due to functional impairment, need personal care and may need supervised nursing care to compensate for activities of daily living limitations. An adult care home classification required to be licensed by KDADS. The level of care is determined by preparation of the staff and KDADS rules. An adult care home may convert a portion of one wing to a home plus of not less than five and not more than 12 beds, separate and contiguous. Personnel providing services for residents with dementia must take annual dementia care training (K.S.A. 39-923 definitions).

Boarding Care Home

Licensed by: Kansas Department for Aging and Disability Services (KDADS), under K.S.A. 39-923 et seq.. Serves: Not more than 10 individuals who need supervision of activities of daily living but are ambulatory and essentially capable of managing their own care and affairs. An adult care home classification required to be licensed by KDADS, operating 24 hours a day, seven days a week (K.S.A. 39-923 definitions, KDADS boarding care home statutes and regulations compilation).

Intermediate Care Facility for People with Intellectual Disability (ICF/IID)

Licensed by: Kansas Department for Aging and Disability Services (KDADS), under K.S.A. 39-923 et seq.. Serves: Four or more individuals who, due to functional impairments caused by intellectual disability or related conditions, need services to compensate for activities of daily living limitations. An adult care home classification required to be licensed by KDADS, operating 24 hours a day, seven days a week (K.S.A. 39-923 definitions). Two state hospitals, Parsons State Hospital and Kansas Neurological Institute, are certified as ICF-IIDs, per KDHE Medicaid policy materials.

Nursing Facility for Mental Health

Licensed by: Kansas Department for Aging and Disability Services (KDADS), under K.S.A. 39-923 et seq.. Serves: People needing nursing facility level care for mental health, as an adult care home classification. Listed in K.S.A. 39-923 as a classification of adult care home required to be licensed by the Secretary for Aging and Disability Services. No further defining detail was printed in the definition excerpts fetched for this sheet.

Residential Supports for Adults, I/DD Waiver (including shared living and 24/7 staffed homes)

Licensed by: Kansas Department for Aging and Disability Services (KDADS), HCBS Intellectual/Developmental Disability Waiver. Serves: Adults with intellectual or developmental disabilities who live in a residential setting and do not live with someone meeting the definition of family. KDADS I/DD Waiver materials describe residential supports as help with activities of daily living and building social and adaptive skills; some individuals live in a house staffed 24/7, others in their own apartment with staff stopping by once a day, and shared living with an unrelated family is also an option. Residential supports are an I/DD Waiver service and are not offered on the newer Community Support Waiver.

Behavioral health residential facilities licensed by KDADS

Licensed by: Kansas Department for Aging and Disability Services (KDADS), under K.S.A. 39-2001 classifications. Serves: People with behavioral health needs in residential care facilities, psychiatric residential treatment facilities and related KDADS licensed settings. Kansas Department for Children and Families policy materials list, under K.S.A. 39-2001, residential care facilities, residential and day support facilities, private and public psychiatric hospitals, psychiatric residential treatment facilities, community mental health centers and crisis intervention centers licensed by KDADS. Detail by facility subtype was thin on the official pages fetched.

How to start a group home in Kansas, step by step

  1. Submit a letter of intent to KDADS for an adult care home license (K.A.R. 26-39-101 licensure of adult care homes)
  2. For new construction or conversion for a nursing facility, ICF/IID, assisted living facility or residential healthcare facility, request approval of the site at least 30 days before construction begins (K.A.R. 26-39-101)
  3. Complete the application and required forms; a new license is issued if a complete application is received and the applicant is found to qualify under K.S.A. 39-928 (K.A.R. 26-39-101)
  4. Renew annually through the Facility Home Page in the online Kansas Tracking of Adult Care Homes (KOTA) application, with the licensure renewal and fees (KDADS adult care home licensure page)

How long does it take in Kansas?

  • Adult care home licensure renewal is annual, submitted through the KOTA web application, with an electronic notice sent to the administrator before the renewal due date (KDADS adult care home licensure page)

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get a group home license in Kansas?

Adult care home licensure renewal is annual, submitted through the KOTA web application, with an electronic notice sent to the administrator before the renewal due date (KDADS adult care home licensure page)

What are the requirements to open a group home in Kansas?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Kansas?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
