How to Start a Home Health Agency in Arkansas

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

Arkansas licenses home health agencies as a Home Health Agency license, Class A (Medicare Certified) or Class B (Non-Medicare Certified), including Derivative B, on the state hospital or related institution license application, through the Arkansas Department of Health, Division of Health Facility Services (Health Facility Services); Permit of Approval from the Arkansas Health Services Permit Agency. The governing law is Arkansas Code section 20-10-801 et seq.; Rules for Home Health Agencies in Arkansas, 20 CAR part 46, sections 46-101 to 46-120. That state license is the first gate. Medicare certification is the second, and it runs on federal rules that are the same in every state: the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, the Form CMS-855A enrollment, and an initial survey of an agency that is already operating. This page gives you the Arkansas license in its own terms, then the federal layer in the order it actually runs.

One status every new owner has to plan around: CMS has a nationwide moratorium on new home health agency Medicare enrollment, imposed May 13, 2026 and extended on November 12, 2026 to about mid-May 2027. The Arkansas license work below can proceed now. The federal enrollment cannot finish while the moratorium runs, so sequence your spending accordingly.

How to start a home health agency in Arkansas, step by step

  1. Obtain a Permit of Approval (POA) before applying for a home health license: 20 CAR 46-106 states that prior to applying for a license, an agency shall obtain a Permit of Approval
  2. POA step 1: Determine the type of home health agency or health facility sought
  3. POA step 2: Review the AHSPA bed need book to determine if the county has a need
  4. POA step 3: If there is a need, access an application from the AHSPA webpage
  5. POA step 4: Recommended pre-submission conference with AHSPA staff to review the application and requirements
  6. POA step 5: Fill out the application and fully answer each applicable question
  7. POA step 6: Submit one original and one copy of the completed application with the $3,000.00 fee in accordance with the review cycle schedule, original signed in blue ink
  8. Complete the notarized Application for License to Conduct a Hospital or Related Institution, checking only one home health license class (Class A, Class B or Derivative B) and all services to be licensed, with the home health addendum
  9. Submit the notarized application with the $1,000.00 per parent agency fee, board or governing body list and any accrediting organization deemed status documentation to Health Facility Services, Arkansas Department of Health
  10. Maintain a licensed, fully operational physical location within Arkansas; the service area must be consistent with the Certificate of Need or POA and up to 100 miles from the parent agency or branch office

Arkansas home health license fees and costs

  • Home Health Agency license fee: $1,000.00 per parent agency (Arkansas Department of Health application instructions, HFS-10A Rev. 06/18/2026)
  • Permit of Approval application fee: $3,000.00, submitted to the Arkansas Health Services Permit Agency (AHSPA Permit of Approval Process page)

How long does it take in Arkansas?

  • POA applications are received only four times a year: February 1, May 1, August 1 and November 1
  • POA completeness determination within 30 days; if complete, the agency has 90 days to approve or deny the application; appeal of a denial within 30 days of receipt
  • All home health licenses expire at midnight on December 31 of the calendar year in which they are issued (application instructions)

What catches Arkansas applicants

  • State license and POA before operation: no person shall provide home health services in Arkansas without a licensed, fully operational physical location in the state (20 CAR 46-104)
  • Certificate of need equivalent: Arkansas uses a Permit of Approval; the home health rules treat the service area as consistent with its Certificate of Need or POA
  • A certified agency under the rules is a home health agency primarily engaged in skilled nursing and other therapeutic services, licensed under state law, meeting the Medicare conditions of participation under Title XVIII (20 CAR 46-103)
  • Medicare Class A is a distinct license class on the state application, separate from Class B non-Medicare certified

The Medicare layer, after the Arkansas license

With the license in hand and the agency operating, the federal sequence is: build to at least 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care, file Form CMS-855A through PECOS and pay the $750 institutional enrollment fee (2026), then pass the initial certification survey, through the state survey agency or an accrediting organization with deeming authority (ACHC, CHAP, or The Joint Commission). The full federal walk-through is on Medicare Certification for Home Health Agencies, and the national path is on How to Start a Home Health Agency.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a home health agency in Arkansas?

The figures Arkansas publishes are these: Home Health Agency license fee: $1,000.00 per parent agency (Arkansas Department of Health application instructions, HFS-10A Rev. 06/18/2026) Permit of Approval application fee: $3,000.00, submitted to the Arkansas Health Services Permit Agency (AHSPA Permit of Approval Process page) Beyond the published figures, the operating costs (clinical payroll before certification, insurance, and the reserve operating funds required by 42 CFR 489.28) are larger than every fee on the list.

How long does it take to get a home health license in Arkansas?

POA applications are received only four times a year: February 1, May 1, August 1 and November 1 POA completeness determination within 30 days; if complete, the agency has 90 days to approve or deny the application; appeal of a denial within 30 days of receipt All home health licenses expire at midnight on December 31 of the calendar year in which they are issued (application instructions) The federal certification that follows runs on the survey calendar, and new Medicare enrollment is under a CMS moratorium to about mid-May 2027.

What do I need to start a home health agency in Arkansas?

The Home Health Agency license, Class A (Medicare Certified) or Class B (Non-Medicare Certified), including Derivative B, on the state hospital or related institution license application (Arkansas Department of Health, Division of Health Facility Services (Health Facility Services); Permit of Approval from the Arkansas Health Services Permit Agency), an administrator and clinical leadership who meet Arkansas rules, policies written to the Conditions of Participation at 42 CFR Part 484, skilled nursing plus at least one other therapeutic service, OASIS transmission capability, and, before the federal survey, 10 skilled patients served with 7 receiving care. Medicaid enrollment is a separate application after licensure.

How do I get a home health license in Arkansas?

Apply to the Arkansas Department of Health, Division of Health Facility Services (Health Facility Services); Permit of Approval from the Arkansas Health Services Permit Agency under Arkansas Code section 20-10-801 et seq.; Rules for Home Health Agencies in Arkansas, 20 CAR part 46, sections 46-101 to 46-120. The steps on this page follow the sequence the authority publishes. Confirm the current application and fee on the authority site before you file, because fees and forms are updated on the state calendar.
