How to Start a Home Care Agency in Nevada (Non-Medical Care Is Licensed at $1,374, and Home Health Sits Behind a 2026 Pause)

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 19, 2026

The Nevada story starts with a founder who assumed non-medical care was the unlicensed lane, because that is how it works in the state she came from. She had attendants ready, a name picked, and a start date circled, and the filing stopped her cold: Nevada licenses the non-medical door itself. Personal care agencies hold a real license, with a real fee, a surety bond, and a survey before the first client. The skilled and hospice doors beside it carry some of the highest facility fees in this series, and in 2026 both of those doors also picked up a state licensure pause and a Medicaid enrollment moratorium with a December clock on them. In the agency records I review, Nevada is the state where reading last year's fee schedule, or another state's rulebook, costs the most money the fastest. Here is the full picture, lane by lane, with the 2026 gates stated first where they belong.

The demand: about 601,000 seniors in a fast growing state

Start with why owners keep looking at Nevada. The Census Bureau puts the state population at 3,282,188 as of July 1, 2025, and 18.3 percent of residents are age 65 or older. Run the math and you get about 601,000 seniors. The same Census QuickFacts page for Nevada puts median household income at $78,260 for 2020 to 2024, which is the income base a private pay agency sells into. Demand concentrates in Clark and Washoe counties, and that concentration matters twice in Nevada: it is where most clients are, and, as the Medicaid section below shows, it is not the whole map for plan contracting.

The license question, answered directly: every Nevada door is licensed, and two doors are paused

Do you need a license to start a non-medical home care agency in Nevada? Yes. Non-medical home care sits on the personal care agency license under NRS chapter 449B and NAC 449.396 to 449.3982, issued by the Health Care Purchasing and Compliance Division of the Nevada Health Authority, through HCQC. Skilled home health sits on the agency to provide nursing in the home license under NRS 449 and NAC 449. Hospice sits on the program of hospice care license under the same chapter. No one door covers another door's services, and an employment agency that contracts with persons to provide nonmedical services holds a different license again, under NRS 449B.105. Nevada prints no certificate of need for home health; the entry gates in late 2026 are the pause and the moratorium, not a need test.

State the gates exactly, because they are perishable. On June 5, 2026 the Nevada Health Authority announced a temporary pause on new state licenses for hospice and home-health providers, in effect for at least six months upon federal approval, with an exemption path for rural and underserved access. Nevada Medicaid Web Announcement 3940, dated June 11, 2026, implements a corresponding six-month moratorium: new home health agency, hospice, and hospice long term care group enrollments received on or after June 11, 2026 are denied for the duration, a window that runs to about December 11, 2026. Revalidations, changes of ownership, and updates to existing enrollments are not restricted, and an exemption process exists for areas where access to care concerns exist. The personal care agency door is inside neither the pause nor the moratorium. Both gates are state actions on state doors; neither one is the federal Medicare moratorium, which gates the certified tracks separately.

The non-medical lane: the personal care agency license at $1,374

The personal care build runs in a set order. Form the business. Appoint an administrator who meets NAC 449.3973: at least eighteen, a high school diploma or equivalent or a long-term care administrator license, and able to read, write, speak, and understand English. Write the policies NAC 449.3974 requires. Post the surety bond NRS 449B.185 sets by employee count: $5,000 with fewer than 7 employees, $25,000 with 7 to 25, and $50,000 with more than 25. Complete the fingerprint background checks for owners and staff, and apply through CLICS at myhealthfacilitylicense.nv.gov with the HCQC checklist uploads. The Division investigates and performs a prelicensure survey under NAC 449.0112 before the license issues, and no client is served before it under NRS 449B.485. Attendants deliver the nonmedical services NRS 449B.465 authorizes and may not deliver skilled care under NAC 449.3978.

The fee facts for this door: $1,374 for the initial license and $687 at each annual renewal under NAC 449.013(1)(n) and (2)(n), nonrefundable, plus a fee equal to 6 percent of the renewal fee on issuance and on each renewal under NAC 449.013(3), which supports the system for reporting information on cancer and other neoplasms. Two clocks decide the filing calendar. An application for a license is valid for one year after the date it is submitted under NAC 449.013(4); subsection (3) is only the 6 percent cancer fee, and the two subsections are not interchangeable. And the first license expires December 31 of the year it issues under NRS 449B.210, so a license issued late in the year renews almost at once. The personal care figure was the one fee Approved Regulation R071-26 left unchanged in July 2026. The Medicaid door on this lane is State Plan personal care under Medicaid Services Manual Chapter 3500, delivered by enrolled agencies as provider type 30, or through the self-directed model with an intermediary service organization as provider type 83, prior authorized on a Functional Assessment Service Plan, with waiver personal care lines under the Waiver for the Frail Elderly and the Waiver for Persons with Physical Disabilities.

The skilled and hospice lanes: high fees, priced by office, behind the June 2026 gates

An agency to provide nursing in the home is a medical facility under NRS 449.030 and may not operate without a license. The build runs through CLICS with the HCQC checklist, the notarized application NRS 449.040 requires, evidence of zoning approval, the administrator and clinical leadership the NAC 449 rules require, and the surety bond at NRS 449.065. The license is priced by office under NAC 449.013(1)(b) and (c), as amended by Approved Regulation R071-26, filed July 1, 2026: $5,530 for a home office and $5,733 for a branch office at initial licensure, and $2,765 and $2,867 at renewal. A branch is its own priced office; a second location is not a free extension of the home office license. The pre-increase schedule, $5,168 for a home office and $5,358 for a branch, still prints in the stale compiled code and in HCQC checklists, and R071-26 controls.

A program of hospice care is a medical facility license of its own at $7,548 at initial licensure and $3,774 at renewal under NAC 449.013(1)(f), plus the 6 percent cancer reporting fee. A facility for hospice care, the inpatient building, is a separate license under NAC 449.016(1)(h) at $4,267 plus $377 per bed; the program fee buys no building, and the building fee buys no program. Both hospice doors and the home health door sit inside the June 2026 pause and moratorium frame, so the practical sequence for a new skilled or hospice entrant is to build the file during the pause, work the access exemption process where the service area qualifies, and budget no start date the announcements do not print. Medicare certification layers on the licensed agency through Form CMS-855A in PECOS with the CY2026 federal enrollment fee of $750, and initial Medicare enrollment on the certified tracks is gated by the federal moratoria extended effective November 12, 2026 under CMS-6101-N2 and CMS-6102-N2, with no end date printed in the notices (the extension is a six-month period, running to about May 12, 2027).

Background checks, bonds, and the Medicaid map: the requirements behind the license

Nevada front-loads its trust requirements into the application itself. Fingerprint background checks for owners and staff run on the NRS 449B frame before the license issues, the surety bond tiers at NRS 449B.185 are sized to employee count and are a common deficiency when an agency grows past a tier without resizing the bond, and the prelicensure survey tests the file before any client is served. The pattern I give owners in licensed states applies with force here: screening and exclusion checks run at hire and never again are a survey finding waiting for a date, and attendants helping with medication without the training record the current rules require is the same class of problem.

Medicaid is administered by the Division of Health Care Financing and Policy. Provider enrollment runs online through Provider Flex, operated by the fiscal agent Gainwell Technologies, with revalidation at least once every five years. Managed care runs through five plans, and the service areas decide what a contract is worth: of the five plans Nevada Medicaid lists, Anthem Blue Cross and Blue Shield, CareSource, Health Plan of Nevada, Molina Healthcare, and SilverSummit Healthplan, only CareSource and SilverSummit Healthplan serve rural counties. Anthem and Molina serve urban Clark and Washoe counties, and Health Plan of Nevada serves Clark County only. A rural agency that builds its payer plan on all five names has built it on plans it cannot contract with. Electronic visit verification runs on an Open Choice Model: Sandata Technologies is the state contracted vendor and aggregator, an agency with an existing system may keep it where each visit captures the required elements, and EVV attaches to personal care provider types 30 and 83, to home health and private duty nursing under provider type 29, and to the named in-home waiver lines.

What you still do today: form the business the Nevada way

Form the entity with the Nevada Secretary of State, get the EIN and the tax registrations, and put the operating accounts and liability insurance in place before the filing, not after it. Then build the record the license will be surveyed against: the administrator appointment, the policy set for the door you are on, the bond sized to the employee count you will actually run, the fingerprint screening system, and a service agreement that keeps attendants on the nonmedical side of the line on the personal care door. On the paused doors, use the pause honestly: assemble the complete file, document the service area for any access exemption showing, and keep the application inside its one year validity window under NAC 449.013(4) so a reopening does not find a stale filing.

The practical warning

Four Nevada traps recur. The first is the wrong door filing: an agency that employs its own attendants files under NRS 449B.100, a business that contracts with persons to provide nonmedical services files under NRS 449B.105, and HCQC warns that the wrong filing delays processing and the fee is not refunded. The second is the stale fee schedule: R071-26 raised the NAC 449.013 and 449.016 fees on July 1, 2026, and a budget built on the pre-increase figures, $5,168 for a home office or $7,054 for a hospice program, underfunds the filing. The third is treating the June 2026 pause as slower processing; the pause and the Web Announcement 3940 moratorium deny new licenses and new enrollments on the doors they name, with an access exemption process, and no fee buys an exception. The fourth is the residential name trap: a residential facility for groups license under NRS 449.017, priced at $2,553 per facility plus $214 per bed at initial licensure, authorizes no in-home agency work, and no in-home agency needs it. In Nevada the license is the business, so the door, the fee year, and the gate calendar all have to be right before money moves.
