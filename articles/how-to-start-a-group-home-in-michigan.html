How to Start a Group Home in Michigan

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 15, 2026

There is no single group home license in Michigan. There is a license for each population the state serves, and the population you choose decides the authority, the rule chapter, and the funding stream. This page lists every residential type Michigan licenses, who runs each one, and the order the application actually runs in.

The group home types Michigan licenses

Adult Foster Care (AFC) Family Home

Licensed by: LARA Bureau of Community and Health Systems (BCHS). Serves: 3 to 6 adults who are aged, mentally ill, developmentally disabled, or physically disabled, requiring ongoing supervision but not continuous nursing care; the licensee must be a member of the household and live in the home. Adult Foster Care Facility Licensing Act, 1979 PA 218, MCL 400.703(5); unified rules R 400.601 to R 400.745 (filed November 3, 2025); homes for 6 or fewer are under department fire protection rules rather than Bureau of Fire Services plan review

AFC Small Group Home

Licensed by: LARA BCHS. Serves: 3 to 12 adults in the AFC population; the licensee is not required to live in the home. 1979 PA 218, MCL 400.703(7); unified rules R 400.601 to R 400.745; fee bands split at 3 to 6 and 7 to 12, and the fire safety plan review applies at 7 to 12; an increase beyond 6 in a previously licensed small group home requires application for a temporary license

AFC Large Group Home

Licensed by: LARA BCHS. Serves: 13 to 20 adults in the AFC population. 1979 PA 218, MCL 400.703(6); unified rules R 400.601 to R 400.745; fire safety plan review applies

AFC Congregate Facility

Licensed by: LARA BCHS. Serves: More than 20 adults (21 or more) in the AFC population. 1979 PA 218, MCL 400.703(3); closed to new entrants: the department shall not issue a temporary license except to replace an existing congregate facility, with capacity not exceeding the facility replaced (MCL 400.715); renewal and replacement only; also requires the MDHHS Adult Foster Care Agreement for Congregate Facilities (form DHS-3422)

AFC Camp (Adult Foster Care Camp)

Licensed by: LARA BCHS. Serves: More than 4 adults (5 or more) in a facility located in a natural or rural environment. 1979 PA 218, MCL 400.703(2); unified rules R 400.601 to R 400.745; seasonal operation; no Medicaid residential door published for AFC camps in the sources reviewed

AFC with Specialized Program Certification - developmental disability (specialized residential)

Licensed by: AFC license from LARA BCHS plus department specialized program certification; payer through the local Community Mental Health Services Program (CMHSP) and Prepaid Inpatient Health Plan (PIHP). Serves: Residents with a developmental disability in an AFC facility offering a specialized program. A certification layered on an AFC license, not a stand-alone license (MCL 400.710(3) and R 330.1801 to R 330.1809); capacity follows the underlying AFC license; this is the main DD residential Medicaid door with personal care and community living supports in specialized residential settings and Habilitation Supports Waiver services; no separate certification fee published

AFC with Specialized Program Certification - mental illness (specialized residential)

Licensed by: AFC license from LARA BCHS plus department specialized program certification; payer through CMHSP and PIHP. Serves: Residents with mental illness or serious mental illness in an AFC facility offering a specialized program. Same certification frame as the developmental disability add-on (MCL 400.710(3) and R 330.1801 to R 330.1809), built as a separate add-on track because program, staffing, and CMHSP contract requirements differ by population; no separate certification fee published

Home for the Aged (HFA)

Licensed by: LARA BCHS (a Public Health Code health facility license, not an AFC license). Serves: 21 or more unrelated, nontransient individuals age 55 or older needing room, board, and supervised personal care; also a facility for 20 or fewer age 55 or older if operated as a distinct part of a licensed nursing home. Public Health Code Part 213, MCL 333.21301 to 333.21335, definition at MCL 333.20106(3), rules R 325.1901 to R 325.1981; no upper capacity cap in the definition; admission under age 55 only on a director waiver as the statute provides; Medicaid door is the Adult Community Placement personal care supplement (with SIGMA vendor registration and Bridges enrollment for Title XIX funds through ACP) and MI Choice services, while room and board itself is not a Medicaid service

Child Caring Institution (CCI) - general residential

Licensed by: MDHHS Division of Child Welfare Licensing (DCWL). Serves: Minor children receiving care, maintenance, and supervision, usually on a 24-hour basis. Child Care Organizations Act, 1973 PA 116, MCL 722.111(1)(c), rules R 400.4101 to R 400.4666; no fixed statutory capacity cap, capacity is the number stated on the license; payer is MDHHS contract through the Child Care Fund, and a residential program must be certified as a Qualified Residential Treatment Program (QRTP) to contract with MDHHS for residential services (form DHS-5336, annual recertification on form MDHHS-5999); no fee published

Child Caring Institution - Agency Group Home

Licensed by: MDHHS DCWL. Serves: More than 4 but less than 13 minor children (5 to 12) in a small child caring institution owned, leased, or rented by a licensed agency. A sub-form inside the CCI definition at MCL 722.111(1)(c) and a CCI sub-track; the children's setting closest to a group home in Michigan law; no fee published

Children's Therapeutic Group Home

Licensed by: MDHHS DCWL. Serves: Not more than 6 children diagnosed with a developmental disability or a serious emotional disturbance. 1973 PA 116, MCL 722.111(1)(h); must provide care usually on a 24-hour basis, comply with the CCI rules, not be a private home, and not be located on a campus with other licensed facilities; no fee published

Foster Family Home (children)

Licensed by: MDHHS, with investigation and certification by a licensed child placing agency or approved governmental unit. Serves: 1 to 4 minor children placed away from their parent, legal guardian, or legal custodian in foster care, in the private home of the licensee. 1973 PA 116, MCL 722.111(1)(v)(i); steps include orientation, application, medical statements, criminal history checks, at least 12 hours of pre-licensure training before recommendation, and home evaluation and certification; payer is foster care maintenance through MDHHS or the certifying agency; no fee published

Foster Family Group Home (children)

Licensed by: MDHHS, with child placing agency or governmental unit investigation and certification. Serves: 5 to 6 minor children in foster care in the private home of the licensee. 1973 PA 116, MCL 722.111(1)(v)(ii); same process and payer frame as the foster family home; no fee published

Unlicensed CMH-monitored private residence (register entry, no license)

Licensed by: No license; monitored by the local CMHSP. Serves: 1 to 4 adults who all receive benefits from a community mental health services program. Expressly excluded from the AFC definition at MCL 400.703(4)(k); the generator screens for it and must not sell it a license packet

Assisted living (register entry, no Michigan license by that name)

Licensed by: No Michigan license by that name (LARA BCHS statement). Serves: Settings marketed as assisted or independent living. LARA BCHS states the State of Michigan does not license assisted or independent living facilities; a Home for the Aged or Adult Foster Care license may still be required if the level of care meets those licensing requirements, so settings route to the AFC tracks or the HFA track by capacity, age, and care; independent living with no foster care is unlicensed

ICF/IID (register entry, no distinct Michigan facility license)

Licensed by: No distinct Michigan facility license found. Serves: Persons at the ICF/IID level of care. ICF/IID appears in Michigan sources as a level of care for Habilitation Supports Waiver and Children's Waiver Program eligibility, not as a facility license; DD residential demand routes to the AFC and specialized residential tracks; do not build an ICF/IID license track for Michigan

How to start a group home in Michigan, step by step

  1. Main group home track (AFC, common process for all AFC types): read the AFC licensing rules (R 400.601 to R 400.745) and 1979 PA 218 before completing the online application
  2. Complete the application online on department forms with the fee prescribed in MCL 400.713a; a credit or debit card is required for the online fee (Discover and American Express not accepted, per the group home page)
  3. If the applicant is a corporation or LLC, form the corporation or LLC through LARA and obtain a Federal Identification Number from the IRS before submitting the corporate application
  4. Submit the additional application items together to LARA BCHS: Licensing Record Clearance Request (BCAL-1326A-FP) with fingerprints for the applicant, licensee designee, and owners, partners, or directors with regular direct access or on-site responsibilities; Licensing Information Request (BCHS-AFC 100) for the administrator and household members age 18 or older; and Medical Clearance Request (BCAL-3704-AFC) for each applicant and responsible person, dated not more than 6 months before license issuance
  5. For 7 or more residents, complete fire safety plan review by submitting building plans with form BCC-979 to the LARA Bureau of Construction Codes and Fire Safety for approval; complete environmental health inspections for private water supply or sewage disposal, and submit floor plans to the assigned licensing consultant for review and approval before new construction begins
  6. Complete the department investigation and on-site evaluation; the department shall issue an initial or renewal license not later than 6 months after a completed application is filed, and if it misses the deadline it returns the fee and reduces the next renewal fee by 15 percent
  7. Operate first on the temporary license for the first 6 months (not renewable); at the end of 6 months the department issues a regular license, a provisional license, or refuses licensure; the license states the maximum number of persons and any specialized program certification type and is issued to a specific person at a specific location and is nontransferable
  8. For a specialized residential program, submit the specialized program certification application to the department, pass certification inspections and investigations, meet staffing and individual plan of service requirements, and contract with the local CMHSP and PIHP for Medicaid payment
  9. For children's residential types instead, follow the PA 116 process through MDHHS: application on department forms, department investigation and on-site visit, criminal history check before issuance or renewal, and a license stating the kind of organization and the number and ages of children

Michigan group home license fees and costs

  • AFC Family Home: $100 application fee for a temporary or renewal license beginning January 1, 2022 (MCL 400.713a(3)(b)(i)); no per-bed fee published for AFC
  • AFC Small Group Home: $150 for 3 to 6 and $200 for 7 to 12 (MCL 400.713a(3)(b)(ii) and (iii))
  • AFC Large Group Home: $500 (MCL 400.713a(3)(b)(iv))
  • AFC Congregate Facility: $500, renewal only (MCL 400.713a(3)(b)(v)); no initial license fee is published for a new congregate facility, consistent with the bar on new temporary licenses
  • AFC Camp: $200 (MCL 400.713a(3)(b)(vi))
  • Specialized program certification: no fee published (neither MCL 400.713a nor R 330.1801 to R 330.1809 publishes a separate certification fee)
  • Home for the Aged: $500 per facility license plus $6.27 per licensed bed, assessed annually, plus a $2,000 initial licensure application fee (MCL 333.20161(1)(d) and (1)(j)); fees are payable when the application is submitted and are not refunded if denied or revoked, and the provisional license or temporary permit fee is the same as for a license
  • Child Caring Institution, Children's Therapeutic Group Home, Foster Family Home, and Foster Family Group Home: no fee published (no fee schedule for these types appears in MCL 722.115 or MCL 722.118 as read for the research)

How long does it take in Michigan?

  • AFC (except camp): temporary license 6 months and not renewable, then a regular license valid for 2 years, renewed biennially on application and approval (MCL 400.714 and 400.719(2))
  • AFC application decision: the department shall issue an initial or renewal license not later than 6 months after a completed application is filed (MCL 400.713)
  • AFC Camp: a regular license is effective for the specific dates of operation, not to exceed a 12-month period, and is renewed annually on application and approval (MCL 400.719(3))
  • Specialized program certification: regular certification up to 2 years; provisional certification up to 3 months, renewable once for 1 additional 3-month period; temporary certification up to 6 months, not renewable (R 330.1801)
  • Home for the Aged: a license issued under Part 201 is effective for no longer than 1 year after issuance (MCL 333.20161(4)), so HFA licensure is annual, unlike the 2-year AFC license; the MCL 333.20161 fee schedule runs until October 1, 2027
  • PA 116 children's types: a regular license is effective for 2 years and renewed biennially; foster family homes and foster family group homes in good standing and licensed at least 1 year may receive an extended license effective for 3 years (MCL 722.118); if a county juvenile agency certifies it intends to contract with a new applicant, the department shall issue or deny within 60 days after a complete application
  • Environmental health inspections for AFC private water supply or sewage disposal systems are conducted every two years

The figures Michigan publishes are these: AFC Family Home: $100 application fee for a temporary or renewal license beginning January 1, 2022 (MCL 400.713a(3)(b)(i)); no per-bed fee published for AFC AFC Small Group Home: $150 for 3 to 6 and $200 for 7 to 12 (MCL 400.713a(3)(b)(ii) and (iii)) AFC Large Group Home: $500 (MCL 400.713a(3)(b)(iv)) AFC Congregate Facility: $500, renewal only (MCL 400.713a(3)(b)(v)); no initial license fee is published for a new congregate facility, consistent with the bar on new temporary licenses AFC Camp: $200 (MCL 400.713a(3)(b)(vi)) Specialized program certification: no fee published (neither MCL 400.713a nor R 330.1801 to R 330.1809 publishes a separate certification fee) Home for the Aged: $500 per facility license plus $6.27 per licensed bed, assessed annually, plus a $2,000 initial licensure application fee (MCL 333.20161(1)(d) and (1)(j)); fees are payable when the application is submitted and are not refunded if denied or revoked, and the provisional license or temporary permit fee is the same as for a license Child Caring Institution, Children's Therapeutic Group Home, Foster Family Home, and Foster Family Group Home: no fee published (no fee schedule for these types appears in MCL 722.115 or MCL 722.118 as read for the research) The license fee is rarely the largest cost. What are the requirements to open a group home in Michigan?

The license for your population from the authority named on this page, a home that passes health and life safety inspection with local zoning cleared, a qualified administrator or manager, background checks on owners and every staff member, written policies matched to the rule chapter, and staffing that matches the residents' assessed needs. Funded placements require separate Medicaid waiver provider enrollment after the license.

How do I get licensed for a group home in Michigan?

Apply to the authority that licenses homes for your chosen population, listed with each home type on this page. The application, background checks, home inspection, and life safety survey all run through that authority, in the order the steps above follow.
