How to Start a Home Care Agency in Arkansas (Medicaid Personal Care Is Licensed, Private Pay Has No License)

By Dr. Devon Boswell, DBA · November 19, 2026

The Arkansas story starts with a founder who budgeted the right total on the wrong lines. She had set aside money for a license, money for a permit, and money for insurance, and she still stalled, because she had priced the permit as the license and the home health fee as the personal care fee. I see that exact shuffle in the records I review, and Arkansas invites it: three licensed doors, three different fee logics, and one door, private pay only care, with no license at all. Here is the full picture, door by door, in the order a founder actually meets it.

Hold the split first. Medicaid personal care is licensed in Arkansas, under its own name and its own rule part. Skilled home health is licensed separately, behind a need review that runs ahead of the application. Hospice is licensed separately again. An owner who keeps the three doors and their fees apart files once. An owner who merges them pays for the lesson twice.

The demand: about 592,000 seniors, close to one in five residents

Start with why owners keep looking at Arkansas. The Census Bureau puts the state population at 3,114,791 as of July 1, 2025, and 19.0 percent of residents are age 65 or older. Run the math and you get about 592,000 seniors. The same Census QuickFacts page for Arkansas puts median household income at $60,773 for 2020 to 2024, which is the income base a private pay agency sells into. That senior count is the population figure multiplied by the age 65 and over share, labeled as that math because the Census page publishes the two inputs, not the product.

The payer structure shapes what that demand is worth. Arkansas Medicaid personal care runs through State Plan Personal Care and the ARChoices in Homecare waiver, and beneficiaries with high behavioral health or developmental disability needs are served through the PASSE program, with Arkansas Total Care, CareSource PASSE, Empower Healthcare Solutions, and Summit Community Care as the four PASSE organizations. A founder planning Medicaid work is planning a license, an enrollment, and, for PASSE members, a separate contract with each organization served.

The license question, answered directly: Medicaid personal care takes the private care agency license

Here is the searched question this article exists to answer. Do you need a license to start a home care agency in Arkansas? For Medicaid personal care, yes: the private care agency license issued by the Arkansas Department of Health (ADH) under sections 20-10-2301 and following of the Arkansas Code and 20 CAR Part 49, which sets the minimum standards for agencies providing Medicaid personal care at 20 CAR 49-101. For purely private pay non-medical care, no Arkansas license was located in the primary sources checked for this build, because the private care agency definition is tied to personal care for which the provider seeks reimbursement through the Arkansas Medicaid Program. A private pay only plan confirms the current position with the Division of Health Facility Services before launch instead of assuming the Medicaid license covers it.

The other doors are just as specific. Skilled home health sits on the home health agency license under sections 20-10-801 and following and 20 CAR Part 46, in two classes: Class A for the Medicare-certified agency and Class B for the non-Medicare agency. Hospice sits on its own license under 20 CAR Part 47, and no agency or person may hold itself out as a hospice without it under 20 CAR 47-104. Residential models carry their own doors in the Arkansas packet, including CES Waiver group homes capped at eight beneficiaries under 20 CAR 535-502, assisted living facilities at Level I and Level II behind a Permit of Approval, and adult day programs licensed at five dollars per licensed slot. No one door covers another door's services.

The non-medical lane: the private care agency license, in six steps

The private care track covers hands-on personal care, attendant care, respite, chore, and homemaker help for Medicaid clients. Step one is confirming the door: the services include hands-on personal care for Medicaid clients, and skilled services belong on the home health door instead. Step two is forming the entity and building the operating record first: the Arkansas office, the liability coverage the statute requires at not less than $1,000,000 covering employees and independent contractors while they provide services, background check systems under the ADH criminal history rules for private care agencies, aide training records, service plans, and the policies 20 CAR Part 49 requires. Act 853 of 2025 removed the former Department of Human Services certification layer from this license and removed the multiple regional office requirement, so the current frame is one primary Arkansas office.

Step three is building to the Part 49 standards the license enforces: supervision of the care program, background checks before assignment, aide training and competency, and the client record sections the part sets. Step four is filing the annual license application with the Division of Health Facility Services on its forms under 20 CAR 49-105. The fee fact is plain and worth stating twice: NO FEE PUBLISHED for this license. The rule prescribes the annual application and the December 31 expiry and prints no fee figure, and no fee figure was located in any Arkansas primary source checked. Step five is passing the licensure survey and holding the calendar-year cycle, because the license is effective on a calendar-year basis and expires December 31. Step six is opening the Medicaid doors: Division of Medical Services (DMS) provider enrollment opens State Plan Personal Care and ARChoices waiver personal care, attendant care, and respite, and EVV runs from the first visit on the state-selected AuthentiCare system under an open, hybrid model.

The skilled lane: Permit of Approval first, then the home health license at $1,000 a year

Home health services in Arkansas include nursing care, physical, occupational, and speech therapy, medical social services, home health aide services, and personal care services delivered at a client residence. The sequence fact that decides the timeline: a Permit of Approval from the Health Services Permit Agency runs ahead of the license application for a new agency. File on the quarterly review cycle, check the need frame for the service area, complete the completeness determination the Permit Agency runs, and pay the $3,000 application fee for the permit review. That $3,000 is a review fee, not a license fee, and it is never merged into a license fee line. One named exception exists at section 20-8-106(a)(1) for a Class B cohort that was serving patients on or before June 30, 1988 in the county of its principal office. A new applicant does not plan around a 1988 legacy exception.

With the permit in hand, build the governance and clinical record, then apply to ADH with the $1,000 annual fee under section 20-10-812, plus $100 a year for each subunit. Pass the licensure survey against the Act and 20 CAR Part 46 before services begin. Private pay and commercial contracts run on the license, and Medicaid enrollment runs through DMS, with home health EVV on the state-selected Sandata system provided by HHAeXchange. Hold the December 1, 2026 date: the Department of Human Services prints full implementation of home health EVV on December 1, 2026, when claims for EVV-required services submitted without EVV compliance will be denied, after a soft launch on November 1, 2026, and edited or manually entered visits are monitored against a ten percent threshold.

The certified and hospice lanes: the federal layer runs on its own clock

The Class A form of the home health license carries Medicare certification under 42 CFR Part 484, through enrollment on Form CMS-855A in PECOS to Palmetto GBA, Jurisdiction M, the Home Health and Hospice MAC for Arkansas, with the federal enrollment fee of $750 for 2026. The A/B MAC is Novitas Solutions, Jurisdiction H, and CGS Administrators, Jurisdiction C, is the DME desk, never the enrollment desk. Read the gate before filing: initial Medicare enrollment for home health agencies is blocked under CMS-6101-N2, effective November 12, 2026, with no end date printed in the notice (the extension is a six-month period, running to about May 12, 2027). That gate reaches federal initial enrollment only; the state license sequence runs on its own clock while the federal door is gated.

Hospice repeats the permit-first order. An agency making initial application, or requesting a change in service area, submits a Permit of Approval ahead of licensure, then files the license application under 20 CAR Part 47 with the $500 annual licensure fee under section 20-7-117(e). Licensure rests on the operational and physical plant survey, the license runs for one year, and the program standard is an identifiable hospice administration with nursing, physician, social work, counseling, and volunteer services. Inpatient direct care hospices comply with 20 CAR 47-101 through 47-132. Medicare hospice certification runs under 42 CFR Part 418 on the same federal form, gated for initial enrollment by CMS-6102-N2, and no EVV door attaches to hospice.

Background checks and the worker file: screening before assignment, on every door

On the private care door, background checks run under the ADH criminal history rules for private care agencies, before assignment, alongside aide training and competency records. On the skilled and hospice doors, the personnel file carries licensure and registry standing for clinical roles, background screening, clinical records discipline, and the policies the governing rule part requires, assembled before filing rather than after a survey finding. The file order I tell owners to hold is the same across doors: application and role description, credential verification where the role requires one, screening results filed before the first assignment, orientation and training records with dates, competency evaluation for the services assigned, and the supervision record.

Enrollment discipline belongs in this section too, because it fails the same way a thin file does. DMS enrollment establishes fee-for-service participation. PASSE participation takes a separate enrollment and contract with each PASSE organization. A contract with one organization is not an enrollment, and an enrollment is not a contract with any organization. Agencies that merge those records bill their first PASSE member on a door that was never opened.

What you still do today: form the business the Arkansas way

Form the entity, get the EIN from the IRS, secure the Arkansas office the private care frame requires, and put the $1,000,000 liability coverage the statute requires in place before the first client on the Medicaid personal care door. Then stand up the ordinary load in the right order: liability insurance matched to the door you file on, written policies and procedures covering the services you will actually sell, background screening and aide training set up as systems rather than a scramble, and a service agreement that says clearly what your agency does and does not do. Pull every form from the Division of Health Facility Services or the Permit Agency on the day of filing. I print no Arkansas formation fee here because no formation fee figure sits in the primary sources this article is built from.

The practical warning

Three Arkansas traps cost new owners money, and all three are fee and sequence errors. The first is reading the $1,000 home health fee at section 20-10-812 as the private care fee: the private care rule at 20 CAR 49-105 publishes no fee figure, and a budget that carries $1,000 into the private care application is built on the wrong credential. The second is reading the $3,000 Permit of Approval fee as a license fee, or filing the license application before the permit: on the home health and hospice tracks the permit runs ahead of licensure on a quarterly cycle, and a filing in the wrong order waits a full cycle. The third is stretching the private care license to private pay only care: the definition is tied to Medicaid reimbursement, no private pay only license was located, and the honest move is to confirm the current position with the Division of Health Facility Services instead of assuming coverage the state does not print.
