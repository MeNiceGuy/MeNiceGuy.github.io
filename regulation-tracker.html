Regulation tracker

The enrollment freezes, licensing changes, and deadlines that decide whether an agency can open, enroll, or keep billing, tracked in one place for home health, home care, hospice, and group home owners. Every entry below was checked against the issuing authority's own notice, and every entry carries the date it was last verified. When an authority has not confirmed a status, the entry says so instead of guessing.

Federal

In effect

Medicare enrollment moratorium on new home health agencies and hospices, extended

Applies to: new Medicare home health agencies and hospices, nationwide

CMS has extended and modified the nationwide moratorium that began May 13, 2026. Initial Medicare enrollment of home health agencies and hospices stays closed, including enrollment through a nonexempt change in majority ownership. The modification reopens one door: agencies already enrolled may again add branch and practice locations inside their own state. The extension carries no printed end date; the six month term runs to about May 2027.

Extension effective November 12, 2026. Source: CMS notices CMS-6101-N2 and CMS-6102-N2, Federal Register, published November 16, 2026: home health notice (PDF), hospice notice (PDF). Last verified November 21, 2026.

States

Applications closed

Alabama: developmental disabilities provider applications closed

Applies to: new developmental disabilities providers and new settings, Alabama

The Alabama Department of Mental Health, Division of Developmental Disabilities, is not accepting applications for new providers or for new settings from existing providers. Applications already submitted continue to be processed, and emergency and replacement applications still go through Community Services Directors. No reopening date is stated; the Department says it will issue updated correspondence when applications reopen.

Effective September 16, 2026, no end date. Source: ADMH Memorandum 26-DD-10, ADMH Application Process (PDF). Last verified November 21, 2026.

In effect

Indiana: HCBS provider certification and enrollment moratorium

Applies to: home and community based services providers under Indiana's 1915(c) waivers

A CMS approved statewide moratorium covers HCBS providers under the PathWays for Aging, Health and Wellness, Traumatic Brain Injury, Community Integration and Habilitation, and Family Supports waivers, for a listed set of services. It blocks new certification and enrollment, and also blocks changes of ownership, added counties, and added covered services for existing agencies. The initial period is six months, and it may be extended in six month increments.

Effective August 1, 2026, initial period to about January 31, 2027. Source: Indiana Health Coverage Programs Bulletin BT2026124, bulletin (PDF). Last verified November 21, 2026.

In effect, ends December 17, 2026

Louisiana: enrollment moratoria on home health, hospice, and DME

Applies to: new home health agencies, hospices, and durable medical equipment suppliers, Louisiana

CMS approved moratoria deny initial enrollments, majority ownership changes that require new enrollment, and added practice locations for the three covered provider types. Managed care organizations are bound by the same moratoria. Providers and applications in place before June 18, 2026 are unaffected. An extension is possible with CMS approval.

Effective June 18, 2026 through December 17, 2026. Source: Louisiana Department of Health Informational Bulletin 26-13, bulletin (PDF). Last verified November 21, 2026.

Licensure suspended

Mississippi: new hospice licensure suspended by statute

Applies to: new hospice agencies, Mississippi

Mississippi law directs the State Department of Health not to process new hospice licensure applications or issue new hospice licenses, renewals excepted. The suspension sits in the statute itself, and the subsection carrying it stands repealed on July 1, 2027, which is when the suspension ends unless the Legislature acts first.

In effect now; repeal date July 1, 2027. Source: Mississippi Code Section 41-85-7(3). Last verified November 21, 2026.

In effect

Missouri: enrollment moratorium on personal care and DME

Applies to: new Consumer Directed Services and personal care providers, and DMEPOS suppliers, Missouri

A six month statewide moratorium covers new enrollments in two categories only: durable medical equipment, prosthetics, orthotics, and supplies, and Consumer Directed Services / Personal Care. Applications received before August 1, 2026 and currently enrolled providers are unaffected. It may be extended in six month increments up to one year. Provider enrollment functions moved from MMAC to the MO HealthNet Division on September 16, 2026.

Effective August 1, 2026, initial period to about January 31, 2027. Source: Missouri Medicaid Audit and Compliance, provider enrollment page. Last verified November 21, 2026.

In effect, near its scheduled end

Nevada: home health and hospice enrollment moratoria

Applies to: new home health agencies (Provider Type 29) and hospices (Provider Types 64 and 65), Nevada

Six month moratoria deny new enrollment for the three provider types; applications submitted on or after June 11, 2026 are denied. Revalidations, changes of ownership, and updates are exempt, and an access to care exemption request process exists. No calendar end date is printed; six months from June 11, 2026 runs to about December 10, 2026, so this entry is due for re-verification first.

Effective June 11, 2026. Source: Nevada Medicaid Web Announcement 3940, announcement (PDF). Last verified November 21, 2026.

Proposed rule

New Mexico: home health agency rule being repealed and replaced

Applies to: home health agencies, New Mexico

The Health Care Authority proposes to repeal and replace rule 8.370.22 NMAC, Requirements for Home Health Agencies, updating every section from licensure procedures and renewals to personnel, supervision, plan of care, records, and incident reporting. This is a proposal, not a final rule. Written comments close December 3, 2026 and the public hearing is December 4, 2026, so owners who want a say have days, not weeks.

Hearing December 4, 2026; proposed effective date December 22, 2026. Source: Notice of Hearing, New Mexico Register, Volume XXXVII, Issue 21, notice of hearing. Last verified November 21, 2026.

In effect

New York: Medicaid enrollment moratorium covering LHCSAs

Applies to: Licensed Home Care Services Agencies and other listed provider classes, New York Medicaid

The Department of Health's moratorium covers new enrollments, reinstatements, and changes of ownership for the listed provider classes, which include Licensed Home Care Services Agencies. Pending applications are discontinued; revalidations are not affected. One caution on dates: the Department's page does not print a start or end date. Law firm alerts report a term of July 27, 2026 to January 27, 2027. Those dates are reported, not confirmed on the Department's own page, and this entry will be updated when the Department publishes them.

Source: New York State Department of Health, provider revalidation page. Last verified November 21, 2026.

Deadline ahead

North Carolina: Innovations Waiver accreditation deadline, April 20, 2027

Applies to: Innovations Waiver providers of select intellectual and developmental disability services, North Carolina

Providers of the covered services under Clinical Coverage Policy 8-P must be nationally accredited and must report that accreditation. On April 20, 2027 the legacy service is end dated and removed, and providers who have not complied lose Innovations Waiver reimbursement eligibility. Note the date carefully: a May 13, 2026 webinar slide deck printed April 19, 2027, but the bulletin itself, and NC Medicaid's later pages, print April 20, 2027. The bulletin governs.

Deadline April 20, 2027. Source: NC Medicaid bulletin, April 16, 2026, Innovations Waiver accreditation requirements. Last verified November 21, 2026.

Status unconfirmed

Ohio: Medicaid enrollment moratorium past its scheduled end, no confirmation either way

Applies to: home health, hospice, private duty nursing, personal care aides, home care attendants, and waivered services providers, Ohio Medicaid

Ohio's moratorium ran from May 14, 2026 to a scheduled end of November 14, 2026, for eight provider types, with the notice stating it remains in effect until that date unless an extension is authorized by CMS. Two things keep this entry open. Ohio Senate Bill 315, effective October 6, 2026, requires a corresponding state moratorium when a federal one issues, and CMS extended the federal moratorium on November 12, 2026. And as of November 21, 2026, the Ohio Department of Medicaid has published no notice confirming that the moratorium ended or that it was extended. Do not assume Ohio enrollment is open until the Department says so.

Scheduled end November 14, 2026, outcome unconfirmed. Source: Ohio Department of Medicaid, statewide new provider moratorium notice (PDF). Last verified November 21, 2026.

Moratorium lifted

Oregon: developmental disabilities endorsement moratorium lifted, market study added

Applies to: developmental disabilities agency endorsements, Oregon

Oregon has lifted its moratorium on new endorsements for standard model agencies and 24 hour residential providers, and application reviews resumed December 2, 2025. In its place, a market study is now part of the initial application. Owners who sat out the moratorium can apply; the market study is the new gate to prepare for.

Effective November 25, 2025. Source: Oregon Department of Human Services, Permanent Administrative Order APD 16-2025, administrative order (PDF). Last verified November 21, 2026.

In effect

Texas: SEMARC employability search now required

Applies to: HCSSAs, assisted living facilities, and other HHSC regulated providers, Texas

Regulated providers, home and community support services agencies included, must now search SEMARC, the Search Engine for Multi-Agency Reportable Conduct, in addition to the Nurse Aide Registry, before putting a person to work. The duty began August 3, 2026. A hold harmless period runs through January 6, 2027, and surveyors begin checking for compliance on January 7, 2027, so the time to build the search into hiring paperwork is now, while it is still free of findings.

Duty effective August 3, 2026; compliance surveying begins January 7, 2027. Source: Texas Health and Human Services Commission, Provider Letter 2026-10 (Revised), provider letter (PDF). Last verified November 21, 2026.

How this page is kept

An entry appears here only after the underlying notice has been read at the issuing authority: CMS, a state Medicaid agency, a licensing board, or the statute itself. Every entry carries its last verified date, and any status with a scheduled end is re-checked against the authority as that date approaches, because a freeze that quietly extends is worse than no tracker at all. Nothing here is legal advice, and no entry is a guarantee for a specific agency's situation; enrollment and licensing decisions belong to the authority named in the entry. When a status changes, the entry changes, and the change is announced in my Facebook group, Run Your Home Care Agency and Group Homes, where I give free advice like this every week.
