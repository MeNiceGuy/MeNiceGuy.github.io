How this calculator works

Most startup budgets list the license fee and a few purchases, then stop. The number that decides whether a start is funded is bigger: everything you pay once, plus everything your first hires cost to bring on, plus every month of operating cost that lands before the first payment does. This calculator keeps those three blocks separate and itemized, so you can see which block is doing the damage and challenge any line in it. Change a line and the totals rebuild around it.

The arithmetic is plain addition and multiplication, on purpose. One-time costs are added line by line. Onboarding is the per-hire cost (background checks, training, uniforms and supplies) multiplied by the number of first hires. Runway is the monthly burn (rent, software, insurance installment, phone and internet, marketing) multiplied by the months until first payment. The all-in figure is the three block totals added together. There is no hidden margin, no contingency percentage, and no rounding up: if you want a cushion, add it as a line you can see.

Methodology and sources

Every figure in this tool except one is your own input. The tool estimates nothing: insurance, rent, software, and supplies vary by quote, market, and vendor, so each field waits for the number from your own paperwork. The one figure the tool supplies is the state application and license fee, and it supplies it only where that fee is verified in the state records behind this site, with the source named and the date it was last checked. Where a fee is not verified, the field stays blank with a note, because a guessed fee is worse than an empty field.

Verified fees, re-checked November 27, 2026 against the state sources named:

  • Texas: $2,625 initial HCSSA license fee for a parent agency or branch, three-year term, one fee covering one or more service categories on the same license. Source: Texas Health and Human Services Commission, 26 TAC 558.3 and HHSC Provider Letter 2021-03, Attachment 1. An alternate delivery site pays $1,500 instead.
  • Ohio: $250 application fee for the skilled home health services license and $250 for the nonmedical license. Source: Ohio Department of Health, ORC 3740.03 and OAC 3701-60-03. Hospice care program license: $600, plus a $1,625 licensure inspection fee charged by the Ohio Department of Health (OAC 3701-19-03 and 3701-19-05(C)).
  • Georgia: $300 application processing fee plus the annual licensure fee: $250 per service category for a private home care provider (Companion Sitting, Personal Care Services, Nursing Services), $1,000 for a home health agency, $1,000 for a hospice. Source: Georgia Department of Community Health, Healthcare Facility Regulation Division, Schedule of Licensure Activity Fees.
  • Florida: home health agency $1,705 biennial license fee plus the $300 biennial assessment; nurse registry $2,000 per biennium; homemaker and companion services registration $50.75; hospice $1,218 licensure fee plus the $300 biennial assessment. Source: Florida Agency for Health Care Administration application forms 3110-1011, 3110-7004, 3110-1003, and 3110-4001, and Rules 59A-18.004 and 59A-8.025, F.A.C.
  • North Carolina: $510 nonrefundable annual license fee for a home care agency and for a home assistance services provider, the same form and the same fee. Source: North Carolina Division of Health Service Regulation, G.S. 131E-138(c).

Before you rely on a fee here, confirm it against the free state checklist for your state, which walks the application in filing order with the fee in context: Texas free state preview. The licensing portal's figure for your application controls if it ever differs from the schedule.

Read this before you budget from it

This tool is general information for planning. It is not financial or legal advice, and it is not a guaranteed figure for any specific agency: fees change, quotes differ, and your own timeline to first payment is an estimate, not a promise. The most common budgeting failure the agencies I work with show me is not a wrong line item; it is a runway that is two months shorter than the wait turns out to be. Run the number again with two extra months before you call the start funded. When the total says the start is not funded yet, closing that gap in the right order is the work I do with agencies at Boswell Consulting Agency services.
